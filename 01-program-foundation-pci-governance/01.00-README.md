## What this phase is

The foundation. It establishes who is accountable, what standard applies, what the acquirer requires, and how the programme will be run — and it sets up the question the rest of the portfolio answers: **what is actually in scope?**

Phase 01 deliberately does **not** answer that question. It states a hypothesis (604 systems), writes down the twelve assumptions the hypothesis rests on, and specifies exactly what Phase 02 must do to test them. Two of those assumptions will not survive.

## The three things people get wrong about PCI DSS

This phase spends real effort on framing, because the framing is where most programmes go wrong before they start.

> **PCI DSS is contractual, not statutory.** It reaches Marketa through one instrument — the merchant agreement with Cardinal Merchant Bank. It is not a law, and no regulator enforces it.
>
> **The PCI Security Standards Council does not enforce it.** The Council writes the standard and qualifies assessors. Enforcement runs card brand → acquirer → merchant.
>
> **There is no "PCI certification" for a merchant.** A QSA produces a Report on Compliance and an Attestation of Compliance describing a point in time, for an assessed scope. Neither is a certificate, and neither is a determination of compliance with any law.

## Why 2026 is not a repeat of 2024

Marketa validated compliant in 2023 and 2024 — both under **v3.2.1**, which was retired on 2024-03-31. This assessment runs against **v4.0.1**, and it is the first in which the **51 future-dated requirements are mandatory** (they ceased to be best-practice-only on 2025-03-31).

Three of them describe controls Marketa has never operated in any form: **6.4.3** payment-page script inventory and authorisation, **11.6.1** payment-page tamper detection, and **8.4.2** MFA for *all* access into the CDE rather than just administrative and remote. As the CISO put it to the Steering Committee: this is not a repeat of a passed assessment, it is a materially different one.

## The idea the whole portfolio turns on

PCI DSS applies to the **cardholder data environment**. Every requirement in the standard follows from where that boundary is drawn, which makes scope reduction the highest-leverage activity available to a merchant — the cheapest control is the one you never have to implement, on a system that was never in scope.

Marketa has three reduction mechanisms already in place: **P2PE** at the store lane, **tokenization and a hosted checkout iframe** in e-commerce, and **DTMF masking** in the call centre. Phase 02 will show these cutting 604 believed-in-scope systems to **71 assessed components**.

The discipline is in one word: **conditional**. P2PE does not reduce scope because a terminal appears on a list. It reduces scope because the solution is deployed as validated *and* operated in accordance with the P2PE Instruction Manual. Break the condition and the systems come back. Phase 01 records every condition so Phase 02 can test it and Phase 08 can prove it to an assessor.

## The honest position

At the end of Phase 01, Marketa has a governance structure, a confirmed validation path, a calendar, and a hypothesis about scope. **Nothing has been validated.** No system has been scanned, no assumption tested, no control assessed. The value of this phase is that it makes the next one falsifiable — every claim it makes is written so that Phase 02 can prove it wrong, and two of them will.
