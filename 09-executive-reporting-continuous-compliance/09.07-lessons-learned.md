## Purpose

Eleven lessons from seventeen and a half months, **four of them uncomfortable**.

A lessons-learned document is the easiest artefact in a programme to write badly. The failure mode is well known: a list of things that went well, restated as insights, with the difficult items either absent or rendered in a register so passive that no reader can tell who did what. This document is written against that failure mode. Every lesson states **what happened**, **what it cost** and **what changes** — and the four uncomfortable ones are placed first, at length, because a retrospective that buries its difficult findings in position eight has already decided what it wants to be read.

**The four uncomfortable lessons are not the four worst outcomes of the programme.** They are the four the programme would most easily have avoided writing down. The failed segmentation test in §4.1 was a worse operational event than any of them and is not uncomfortable at all, because it was found in May by a control that was working, reported as a failure, and fixed. What makes a lesson uncomfortable is that it implicates the programme's own judgement rather than the estate's condition.

## 3. The Four Uncomfortable Lessons

### 3.1 L-01 — the close forecast was wrong because nobody checked the arithmetic

**What happened.** In 03.12 §6, in month seven of an eighteen-month programme, the register published a forecast close position of **0 High · 13 Moderate · 31 Low**. It was restated in 07.13, in 08.13 and in the Phase 08 handover, and it was reported to the Steering Committee and the Audit Committee as the programme's target risk profile.

The actual close at **2027-06-30** is **0 High · 16 Moderate · 28 Low**. **Three entries short.**

The reason is not that treatments failed. Eight entries moved to Low in Phase 09 — R-13, R-22, R-21, R-15, R-23, R-08, R-18 and R-32 — every one on evidence, four of them on an **impact** reduction argued from a change in consequence that had actually occurred. The reason is arithmetic.

| Line | Entries | Count |
|---|---|---|
| Forecast Moderate→Low, delivered in Phase 06 | R-10, R-19, R-26, R-34 | 4 |
| Forecast Moderate→Low, delivered in Phase 09 | R-08, R-18, R-21, R-22, R-23, R-32 | 6 |
| **Forecast Moderate→Low, not delivered** | **R-04, R-28, R-33** | **3** |
| Forecast High→**Low**, delivered to **Moderate 8** instead | **R-17, R-36** | **2** |
| Not forecast to move at all, but reached Low | R-13, R-15 | **+2** |
| **Net shortfall against 13 Moderate** | | **+3 → 16** |

**Under this register's own discipline — a rating moves on likelihood unless the consequence itself changed, and a likelihood of 1 means the event is not reasonably foreseeable — an entry scored 3 × 4 cannot reach Low. It can reach 2 × 4 = 8, and 8 is Moderate. Eight is a floor.**

**R-04, R-28 and R-33 are all 3 × 4. R-17 and R-36 both arrive at 2 × 4.** Every entry the forecast got wrong is an entry the arithmetic already excluded. **The Phase 03 forecast moved thirteen entries across a boundary the arithmetic does not permit them to cross**, and it was written before the scoring discipline had been tested against a single year of evidence.

**What it cost.** Nothing operationally, and a great deal in credibility, because the forecast was reported four times over eighteen months and the arithmetic was checkable on every one of those occasions by anybody with the register's own scoring rules in front of them. **The cost is that a governance forum spent a year measuring the programme against a target that could not be reached**, and that any external reader who checked the arithmetic would have found it before the programme did.

There is a compounding element. **This is the second published forecast in this programme that did not survive contact with its own evidence.** 06.12 §7 forecast R-17 and R-36 reaching Low on remediation; 08.13 §6.3 corrected it and said so. This phase corrects 03.12 §6. **Two forecasts published in advance, two missed, both reported against** — which is the portfolio's habit and is the only thing that makes this lesson survivable.

**What changes.** Three things, and only the third is a real control.

Any published forecast of a register position must state, per entry, **the score it is forecast to reach**, not merely the band. A forecast that says "R-04 moves to Low" is unfalsifiable until the day it fails; a forecast that says "R-04 moves from 3 × 4 = 12 to 2 × 4 = 8, Moderate" is checkable by anybody in ten seconds.

**No forecast may cross a band boundary without stating the mechanism** — a likelihood movement, an impact movement argued on a change in consequence that has occurred, or both — because the register's own rule is that impact is never reduced as a way of moving a score that likelihood would not move, and a band-crossing forecast that does not name its mechanism is asserting one of those two things without saying which.

**Forecasts are re-derived at every phase boundary against the current scores, and the delta is reported.** The failure here was not the original forecast, which was a reasonable guess by people who had not yet operated the discipline. **The failure was that it was restated three times without being recomputed.**

**The close position is not a failure of the programme. It is a failure of a prediction, and the programme is reporting the prediction it published rather than the one it could have written afterwards.**

### 3.2 L-02 — three of the five worst assessed outcomes came from one support agreement

**What happened.** A 306-requirement assessment produced **five exceptional dispositions**: two **Not in Place** findings and three **compensating controls**.

| Disposition | Requirement | Cause |
|---|---|---|
| **Not in Place** | **11.3.1.2** — authenticated internal scanning | **The support agreement releases no administrative credential and grants no agent installation right** |
| Not in Place | 8.6.2 — hard-coded credentials | No source rights over a vendor wrapper; no callback hook in an ERP interface library |
| **Compensating control CCW-01** | **8.3.6** — 12-character minimum | **Appliance firmware caps local passwords at ten, and the support agreement prohibits modification of appliance software** |
| **Compensating control CCW-02** | **2.2.7** — strong cryptography on management interfaces | **The management interface offers no protocol with strong cryptography, and the same agreement applies** |
| Compensating control CCW-03 | 3.4.2 — copy/relocation of PAN under remote access | Six disputes analysts and one legacy published application |

**Three of the five sit on nine devices out of seventy-one, under one support agreement with one vendor.** Add **S-12** — the only sampled population whose standardisation could not be demonstrated, and which the assessor therefore refused to sample at all, examining all nine instead — and the count is four. Add the two customized-approach account classes that cannot be gated because they authenticate locally in appliance firmware, and **the same nine devices are present in five separate places in one assessment**.

**Nobody had read the support agreement as a security control.** It was procured as a maintenance contract, it was reviewed by people evaluating service levels and price, and the terms that mattered — no credential release, no agent installation, no software modification — were not security terms in anybody's classification. **They were the load-bearing constraint on three requirements.**

**What it cost.** The 2026 Attestation was filed **Non-Compliant**. The re-assessment cost **$44,000**. A remediation ran inside a seasonal change freeze under an exemption with eighteen days of margin and no float. Two compensating controls must now be re-argued annually and **neither has a vendor roadmap**. And the fix that closed the finding was a **contract amendment executed 2027-01-08**, which means the position at **R-36** is now contractual rather than technical: Marketa can scan those nine appliances because a clause says so, and **a control that depends on a contract clause has a renewal date**. **BAU-02** — Route B, replacement of the appliance class — is the only action that removes the constraint, and it is not funded.

**What changes.** Commercial agreements covering any component in the assessed population are read as security controls, by somebody whose job is to read them that way. Three specific terms are now mandatory review points before signature or renewal on any in-scope estate: **administrative credential release**, **agent or software installation rights**, and **the right to modify or configure the software** — because those three terms determine, in advance and irreversibly, whether an entire class of requirements is achievable.

Second, **the third-party register and the assessed component register are cross-referenced**, so that any component whose in-scope obligations depend on a supplier term is visible as such. This is the same defect as **OBS-05** in a different domain: two registers, both correct, with no reconciliation between them, and a truth that lives only in the space between.

Third, and most uncomfortable: **the programme found this from the wrong direction.** The sampling methodology saw it first, in 08.02, when the assessor asked for an authenticated configuration read and Marketa could not provide one. The compensating-control documentation saw it second, in 08.07 §8. Nobody saw it in procurement, in Phase 01's constraint analysis, or in the third-party register — all three of which had the agreement available. **A commercial document is not a technical one, and the programme had no process that would read it as though it were.**

### 3.3 L-03 — the incident response plan has never been tested by the event it exists for

**What happened.** Marketa has an incident response plan at version 3.0. It covers every element 12.10.1 requires. It has a severity model, a 24/7 declaration authority requiring no approval, a named Incident Commander role, a 12.10.3 roster with monthly delivery testing verified across a full quarter, semi-annual training for the core team and annual training for the extended roster, a lessons-learned loop that produced nine plan amendments and two recorded declines, and procedures under **12.10.7** for account data discovered where it is not expected — procedures improved four separate times by four real discoveries.

It was tested on **2026-10**, under 12.10.2, against a scenario chosen by an outsider under **ADR-0027** precisely so that no internal author could choose something the plan was ready for. The scenario was a common-point-of-purchase alert arriving from the acquirer, so the response began with Marketa already behind and an external clock running. **Determination took 9 hours 20 minutes against a plan that assumed four.** Six findings came out of it.

Across the operating period the plan handled **512 incident records** — 458 closed at SEV-4, most within the hour, **5 reached SEV-2**, and **0 reached SEV-1**.

**None of them was a confirmed compromise of account data. The plan has never handled one.**

**R-43** — incident procedures not exercised against the scenarios this estate produces — **has not moved in any phase of this programme.** It was held in Phase 06, held in Phase 07, held in Phase 08 with the sentence *use is not proof*, and it is held at close. The assessor recorded the same thing independently as **OBS-12**.

**What it cost.** Nothing that has happened. **Everything that might.** The specific exposure is that the plan's most consequential assumptions have been tested only by exercise, and the exercise disproved one of them by a factor of more than two. The 9 hours 20 minutes is itself an exercise measurement, on a compressed clock with scripted injects, in a room that knew it was an exercise — **the real number is unknown and is probably worse.**

The cost is also structural. **This is the one measure on the board scorecard that money cannot move.** No line of the $4,588,000 could have bought it. **BSC-12 is red and stays red under ADR-0035**, because a scorecard that goes all green has stopped measuring something.

**What changes.** Very little, and saying so is the lesson.

**Exercising is not testing, and more exercising does not become testing.** An exercise measures a room's response to a scenario somebody wrote. It cannot measure the things that only a real event produces: an adversary who adapts, a third party that does not answer, a fact that turns out to be wrong six hours in, a General Counsel with a real notification decision, and the specific quality of judgement available to people who know it is real. Marketa will keep exercising annually because 12.10.2 requires it and because the exercises have produced nine plan amendments — **but the programme does not claim that another exercise moves R-43, and it will not accept an argument that it does.**

What does change is narrower and honest. The exercise scenario is chosen by somebody outside the response team, every year, under **ADR-0027**. The single assumption the exercise disproved has been repaired at the level of obligation rather than of intent: **NOT-1 may and must be discharged on an `undetermined` determination**, *undetermined* is a permitted answer, the Incident Commander records why a conclusion was not reached, and the outer bound from suspicion to notification is stated as **48 hours** and is a plan obligation. **The point of that change is that the 24-hour acquirer clock no longer depends on the thing the exercise proved Marketa is slow at.**

And **BAU-01** exists because the largest single dependency in that chain — Truvance's token resolution — is a third party's response time with no contractual commitment behind it.

**A clean assessment and an untested capability are entirely compatible.** An assessment tests controls against a standard. It does not test an environment against an adversary. An entity that reads its Compliant attestation as an answer to the second question has misread the instrument it just paid for.

### 3.4 L-04 — the 86.9% evidence figure decays

**What happened.** Sable Ridge raised **214 evidence requests** during fieldwork. **186 of them — 86.9% — were satisfied from artefacts that already existed.** Twenty-eight required new production, and those 28 are point-in-time observations nobody can pre-produce: a live configuration read, a witnessed restore, a witnessed POI device inspection. **Zero requests went unsatisfied.**

It is the single most quotable number the programme produced about its own discipline. It is also, in the phrasing the Phase 08 handover used, the number most likely to be misread as a property of the organisation.

**It is not a property. It is an output.** It is what happens when eleven full-time-equivalent people spend seventeen months producing evidence as the work is done, in a programme with a weekly working group, a monthly steering committee, a monthly QSA checkpoint from March, an evidence manager whose whole role is the evidence set, and a standing instruction that evidence must show that the activity occurred, when, by whom, and with what result.

**Business as usual is 4.2 FTE.** The governance forum is quarterly rather than monthly. The working group is fortnightly rather than weekly. The QSA checkpoint is quarterly. **The obligations are identical and the population watching them is roughly a third of what it was.**

**What it cost.** Nothing yet, which is the difficulty. This is a lesson about a cost that has not been incurred, and those are the ones organisations reliably fail to act on.

The mechanism of decay is specific rather than general. Evidence that is produced contemporaneously is nearly free — the record is a by-product of doing the work. Evidence that is reconstructed later is expensive and is sometimes impossible: **a cadence cannot be reconstructed.** A configuration screenshot proves a state; proving that a review happened monthly for twelve months needs twelve dated records, and there is no way to produce them in November. **The 28 items that required new production are the ones that cannot be pre-produced, and they are also the ones a programme forgets to schedule in a year when nobody is coming.**

At 4.2 FTE the pressure runs one way. Every recurring obligation still happens; what erodes first is the **artefact**, because performing a control and evidencing a control feel like the same activity to the person doing it and are not.

**What changes.** The measure exists — **M-05**, evidence requests satisfied from artefacts that already existed, annual, owned by Owen Castellanos, with a threshold at 80%. Publishing a measure is not a control, and this lesson does not claim it is.

Three things are controls. **The monthly control attestation survives programme close**, at monthly cadence, even though the governance forum around it drops to quarterly — because it is the mechanism that produces the dated record. **Every one of the 47 recurring obligations in the 2027–28 calendar names its evidence artefact**, so that "performed" and "evidenced" cannot silently become the same word. And **the 28 non-pre-producible items are scheduled explicitly**, as calendar entries with owners, rather than being left to appear when an assessor asks — which is the specific failure this lesson predicts.

The honest position is stated rather than argued away. **If M-05 falls at the 2027 assessment, the cause will not be a failure of any individual control. It will be that a programme with eleven people watching became an operation with four, and nobody costed the difference in evidence.** 09.04 §4 puts the ratio at 35.3% of the staffing and 29.2% of the spend. **The controls do not know that.**

## 4. The Other Seven

### 4.1 L-05 — the failed segmentation test

| | |
|---|---|
| **What happened** | The first segmentation penetration test, in **May 2026**, **failed**. One path proved segmentation was not effective: at store **0417** in Dayton, Ohio, a guest wireless VLAN reached the store back-office VLAN through a switch whose access point trunk permitted every VLAN. The trunk defect **P1** existed at **37 stores** from a central build image; the second precondition — a guest SSID moved from central to local switching to fix a latency complaint, in September 2025 — existed at **one**. Re-tested clean in June 2026 |
| **What it cost** | **$77,000** on the segmentation build line, and remediation across all 37 stores rather than the one that was live. Risk entry **R-14** was **raised** on the evidence under **ADR-0005**, after remediation was complete |
| **What changes** | The change that created the live path was, on its own terms, correct: a performance fix on a network classified as untrusted, by an engineer who therefore did not treat it as a segmentation change. **The classification was the defect, not the engineer.** Any change touching a network boundary is now caught by **SIG-5** in significant-change control regardless of the trust classification of the network being changed, and **CSC-8's daily assertion bounds a misclassified change to at most 24 hours** — the movement that took **R-32** to Low. The scheduling lesson is separate and equally load-bearing: **the test was scheduled in May, four months before the seasonal freeze, so that a failure was affordable.** A test scheduled in October produces the same finding at a moment when nothing can be changed |

### 4.2 L-06 — the two refused compensating controls

| | |
|---|---|
| **What happened** | A compensating control was available to be argued at **8.6.2** and at **11.3.1.2**. Both were **refused by Marketa** — in **June** and **July 2026** — on a test stated once and applied consistently. A late argument was declined three weeks before fieldwork under **DEC-710**, on the reasoning that arguing one then would tell the assessor exactly what the June and July refusals were worth |
| **What it cost** | **A Non-Compliant Attestation of Compliance**, filed 2026-12-11. **$44,000** in re-assessment fees. A monthly reporting obligation to Cardinal from 2026-12-16. An out-of-cycle Board and Audit Committee report on 2026-12-17. A remediation inside a change freeze under exemption, with eighteen days of margin and no float. **Had either worksheet been argued and accepted, the Attestation would have read compliant** |
| **What changes** | Nothing about the decision, which the programme would take again, and everything about how it is priced. **The test that distinguishes a legitimate constraint from an inconvenience is written down once and applied to every candidate**, before the assessment rather than during it — and the price of applying it is now a known figure rather than an abstraction. **A programme that has never refused a compensating control has not established that its test is real**, and this one has a receipt |

### 4.3 L-07 — the tabletop that ran 9h20m against an assumed 4

| | |
|---|---|
| **What happened** | **TT-2026-01**, the annual 12.10.2 exercise, ran a scenario chosen by an outsider under **ADR-0027**: a common-point-of-purchase alert from the acquirer. **Determination took 9 hours 20 minutes against a plan that assumed four.** Six findings, including that **nobody had defined what "determination" means** even though the 24-hour notification clock runs from it |
| **What it cost** | Nothing on the day. The exposure it revealed was that a **contractual 24-hour obligation to Cardinal rested on an internal assumption nobody had ever had to act on** — and the assumption was wrong by a factor of more than two, on a compressed exercise clock with scripted injects |

The Incident Commander records why a conclusion was not reached. The programme's response was not to improve the estimate but to **remove the dependency on it** |

### 4.4 L-08 — the 5.3.3 exemption cost more than the evidence would have

| | |
|---|---|
| **What happened** | Marketa proposed **5.3.3** — anti-malware scanning on removable electronic media — as **Not Applicable**, on the ground that removable media is prohibited by policy. The assessor refused it: **USB ports were enabled on 31 of the 71 components**. The requirement returned to the tested population and was assessed **In Place on the EDR device-control module** — a control Marketa already owned and had not thought to present. Recorded as **D-1** |
| **What it cost** | The preparation of an exclusion argument, the assessor time to refuse it, and the loss of a determination Marketa had reported internally as settled. **The exemption argument cost more effort than the evidence would have.** Two further exclusions — **3.5.1.2** and **3.5.1.3** — were refused on the parallel ruling that **absence of use is not absence of the thing**, and all three refused exclusions were assessed **In Place** |
| **What changes** | **A policy prohibition is not a technical absence**, and every proposed exclusion is now tested against the estate before it is proposed, using the same evidence the assessor would use. Seven exclusions were proposed and four accepted; the correct number to propose is the number that survive that test. And the underlying error is worth naming: **Marketa argued for an exemption from a requirement it was already meeting**, with a control it already owned, because nobody had mapped the control to the requirement |

### 4.5 L-09 — the customized approach's 51 hours against 46

| | |
|---|---|
| **What happened** | **8.3.9** — password change frequency — was met through the **customized approach**, using continuous behavioural authentication analytics rather than 90-day rotation. Phase 05 estimated **46 additional assessor hours per year**. The actual was **51**. Marketa supplied **CAC-1 to CAC-8** and the 12.3.2 analysis; **Sable Ridge derived CTP-1 to CTP-12** independently, and **CTP-7** and **CTP-11** required a second evidence round |
| **What it cost** | **51 assessor hours**, an annual re-argument of the controls matrix and the targeted risk analysis, and — under **ADR-0036** — **the whole argument again from zero in 2028**, because CTP-1 to CTP-12 are Sable Ridge's derived procedures and the next assessor derives its own, and may derive harder ones. The QSA recorded that the objective was met **for the assessed period** and carries **no forward assurance** |
| **What changes** | **The variance is recorded because the next budget uses it**, and an estimate nobody reconciles is an estimate that will be wrong again next year. More importantly, the customized approach is now treated as a **recurring liability with an annual cost line**, re-decided each year against the alternative of meeting the requirement by the defined approach — rather than as an argument won once. **A customized approach is not a solution; it is a subscription** |

### 4.6 L-10 — the capability removed rather than governed

| | |
|---|---|
| **What happened** | The 6.4.3 script inventory found **3 of 38 payment-page scripts unauthorised** — a marketing tag manager injecting two undeclared third-party scripts. The obvious response was to **govern** the capability: approval workflow, alerts, quarterly container review. Marketa **removed** it instead, under **ADR-0011**. **Payment-template publication no longer exists as a capability in the tag console**; publication accounts went from **19 to 6**, none of the six can reach a payment template, and the Content Security Policy on those templates denies the container origin so that a restored permission would still be refused by the browser |
| **What it cost** | A real business cost, paid in the open: Marketing lost a self-service capability and received a **five-business-day approval service level** through the engineering deployment path in exchange. That trade was negotiated rather than imposed |
| **What changes** | **Where the business cost is payable, remove the capability rather than govern it.** A governed capability depends on a workflow configured by the same people who use it, reviewed quarterly, by people who will one day be busy. A removed capability depends on nothing. **R-34** — payment-page script inventory drift — reached **Low** on this and on four independent reconciliation mechanisms; **RC-21**, the six remaining publication holders, is countersigned by the compliance manager because the population that can alter a payment page is the population that mattered most. The limit is stated too: **this only works where the business cost is payable**, and much of the time it is not |

### 4.7 L-11 — the second discovery run

| | |
|---|---|
| **What happened** | PAN discovery found spoken account data in approximately **11,400 call-centre QA recordings**, predating DTMF masking. It disproved assumption **A-04** — that masking had eliminated spoken account data from the call estate — because the control was forward-only and had never been applied to what already existed. **R-31 was raised to 16 on 2026-02-05**, two days after confirmation and **while remediation was already running**, under **ADR-0005** |
| **What it cost** | The deletion of the corpus, retention reduced from seven years to thirteen months and enforced at the capture layer, procedure **P-1** — any change to a capture-affecting control triggers a retrospective review of what was captured before — and DLP live from 2026-04-10. And it cost a risk entry that stayed high while everything was being fixed, which is uncomfortable to report and is the whole point |
| **What changes** | **The exit criterion is evidence, not the completion of remediation.** R-31 came down when a **second full discovery run across the same populations returned nothing**, not when the first run's findings were cleaned up. Deleting 11,400 recordings removed the recordings; it did not establish that Marketa knew what it held. **ADR-0005** was tested and declined nine times across the programme and applied twice — **R-14** and **R-31** — and both entries are Low at close. A rule that is applied every time it is invoked is a rule nobody is testing; a rule declined nine times and applied twice is a rule with a threshold |

## 6. The Sentence This Document Ends On

Eleven lessons, four of them uncomfortable, and the four have one property in common that the other seven do not.

**L-05 through L-11 are lessons about the estate. L-01 through L-04 are lessons about the programme's own judgement** — a forecast nobody checked, a document nobody read as a control, a capability nobody can prove, and a number nobody has costed the loss of.

The estate lessons were all found by instruments the programme built and pointed at itself: a segmentation test that failed, a discovery run that disproved an assumption, a tabletop designed to fail, an inventory that found three unauthorised scripts. **Every one of those instruments worked.**

**None of them was pointed at the programme.** The arithmetic error in the close forecast was visible for eleven months and was found by writing this phase. The support agreement was available in Phase 01 and was found by an assessor asking for a configuration read. **An organisation that builds good instruments and does not point one at itself will keep finding things about its estate and nothing about its judgement**, and the four uncomfortable lessons in §3 are the whole return on the one occasion this programme did.
