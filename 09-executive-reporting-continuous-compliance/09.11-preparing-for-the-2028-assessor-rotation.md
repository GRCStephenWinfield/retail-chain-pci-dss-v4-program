## Purpose

Sable Ridge Assurance, LLC assessed Marketa in 2026 and will assess it again in 2027. **In 2028 the assessor rotates.**

This document exists because the most expensive assumption available to a Level 1 merchant in year three is that year three is like year two. It is not. A new Qualified Security Assessor arrives with no memory of the arguments, no stake in the previous conclusions, and a professional obligation to reach its own. **Every judgement in the 2026 report that was a judgement rather than a fact is available to be reached differently.**

The document records **ADR-0036**, enumerates precisely what resets to zero, states the cost of the reset in the one place it can be measured, sets out a preparation plan with dated milestones through 2028, and puts on the table — without taking it — the decision Marketa must make in 2027 about whether to keep the customized approach at all.

## 1. ADR-0036 — The 2028 Assessment Is Planned as Though Nothing Carries Forward

> **ADR-0036 — The 2028 assessment is planned as though nothing carries forward.**
>
> **Decision:** Marketa plans, resources and budgets the 2028 assessment on the assumption that **no determination, procedure, rationale, worksheet or exclusion from 2026 or 2027 is available to it**. Every sampling rationale is rebuilt. Every compensating control is re-argued from the constraint. Every Not Applicable determination is re-evidenced. The customized approach is either re-argued from zero or withdrawn, and the decision on which is taken deliberately in 2027 rather than by default in 2028.
>
> **Rationale:** a merchant that plans the rotation year as a continuation plans for the cheapest possible outcome and has no answer when it does not arrive. The failure is not that the new assessor is harder; it is that the entity has budgeted assessor hours, internal hours and a fieldwork window against a report that the new assessor has not read and is not bound by. **The programme has already learned once what an unchecked assumption costs — the close forecast in 03.12 §6, examined in 09.12 §2 — and this is the same class of error applied to a professional engagement.**
>
> **Consequences:** the 2028 budget is set above the 2027 actual rather than at it. The preparation plan in §4 starts fourteen months before fieldwork. And the plan's first substantive act is performed while Sable Ridge is still engaged: **capturing the 2026 and 2027 derivations as a record of what was argued, not as an artefact to be re-used.**
>
> **Status:** Accepted. Approved by the PCI Steering Committee 2027-06-16.

### 1.1 Why the assessor rotates at all

Rotation is not obligatory under the standard, and stating why Marketa does it prevents the decision being reversed later on convenience grounds.

| Reason | Substance |
|---|---|
| **Familiarity is a benefit and a risk at the same time** | An assessor in its third year knows the estate, and knowing the estate is exactly what makes it cheaper to accept last year's rationale for this year's population. **The 8.2.2 shared-account observation — Marketa's figure of eleven against a true population of twenty — was raised in year one by an assessor with no priors** |
| **Every judgement in the report is a judgement by one firm** | Four contested determinations, three refused exclusions, three accepted compensating controls, twelve derived testing procedures and eighteen sampling rationales are all **Sable Ridge's** professional opinions. A second firm's opinion on the same estate is the only available test of the first |
| **The acquirer relationship benefits from it** | Cardinal receives an attestation each year. An attestation produced by a different assessor against the same environment, reaching a materially similar conclusion, is a stronger statement than a fourth one from the same firm |
| **It is cheaper to plan for than to be surprised by** | Rotation happens eventually — through a firm's own staffing, a commercial decision, or a Council listing change. **Planning it converts an event into a milestone** |

**The cost is stated rather than hidden: rotation is the most expensive assessment year Marketa will have**, and §3 measures one component of it precisely.

## 2. What Resets to Zero

Seven categories, and the count in each is exact because every one of them was enumerated in the 2026 report and can be counted from it.

| Resets | Count | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **CTP-1 to CTP-12** — the derived testing procedures for the 8.3.9 customized approach | **12** | **They are Sable Ridge's procedures.** The standard obliges the assessor to derive testing procedures for a customized approach; it does not oblige, or permit, the next assessor to adopt the last one's. **The next assessor derives its own, and may derive harder ones** |
| **All 18 sampling rationales — S-01 to S-18** | **18** | Sampling is permitted only where standardisation and centralised management are **demonstrated to the assessor who is doing the sampling**. A rationale accepted by Sable Ridge is a record of a conversation with Sable Ridge. **Four populations were sampled at or below 5.8%** — S-04, S-05, S-17 and S-18 — and those four will attract the most scrutiny |
| **All 3 compensating controls — CCW-01, CCW-02, CCW-03** | **3** | A worksheet is validated against **a stated constraint, a stated population and a stated set of controls**, and every one of those can move. CCW-01 and CCW-02 were re-validated at the 2027-02-18 re-assessment rather than carried forward, and they survived only because the firmware and the management interface had not changed |
| **All 4 Not Applicable determinations — 1.5.1, 3.3.2, 3.5.1.1, 4.2.1.2** | **4** | Each was **tested** rather than accepted, and each must be tested again. **The three refusals of 2026 — 5.3.3, 3.5.1.2 and 3.5.1.3 — may be refused again on the same reasoning, or the four that were accepted may not be** |
| **The scope boundary itself** | **604 → 71** | The reduction rests on **P2PE, tokenization and DTMF masking**, and each of those is a third party's validation status on the day the new assessor looks. Verition's solution listing, Truvance's Level 1 attestation and Cadence's Level 1 attestation are three documents held by three companies Marketa does not control |
| The four contested determinations **D-1 to D-4** | **4** | Three went against Marketa and one for it. **A new assessor may contest a different four**, and the three that were resolved are resolved only in the sense that Marketa now has the evidence to answer them quickly |
| The 17 assessor observations | **17** | Adopted or declined in writing under **ADR-0034**. **A declined observation is a position, and the next assessor may raise the same subject and not accept the reason** |

### 2.1 The scope boundary is the one that should worry a reader

Everything else in that table is an argument Marketa can rehearse. The scope boundary is not an argument at all — it is a dependency.

| Dependency | What it removes from the assessed population | What could change it |
|---|---|---|
| **Verition P2PE validation** | 1,914 lanes and 482 store servers from the card-data path | A lapse in the solution listing, a change in the P2PE Instruction Manual, or a store-level condition failure. **Three stores were found non-conforming in February 2026** |
| **Truvance tokenization and hosted checkout** | The token-to-PAN mapping and the e-commerce card field | A change in the provider's AOC **scope statement** — which has already happened once and was detected at MT-1 review before the provider raised it |
| **Cadence DTMF masking** | Spoken account data from a 310-agent contact centre and its recording estate | A telephony routing or platform change moving the suppression point downstream — condition **MOTO-C2**, and the exposure scored at **R-23** |

**Any one of those three moving materially changes what the 2028 assessment is.** A new assessor's first question is always the scope, and the scope's whole argument is three companies' current validation status.

## 3. Fifty-One Hours, Spent Again, From Zero

> **The 2026 assessment cost 51 assessor hours on one requirement because Marketa chose the customized approach. In 2028 that 51 hours is spent again, from zero, by somebody who has never seen the argument.**

| Element | 2026 | 2028 |
|---|---|---|
| Marketa's controls matrix | **CAC-1 to CAC-8** at Appendix E1 | Rebuilt against the population as it then is, and re-evidenced |
| Marketa's 12.3.2 targeted risk analysis | **TRA-10**, reviewed annually at **CAL-28** | Current, and read for the first time by a new reader |
| The assessor's derived testing procedures | **CTP-1 to CTP-12**, derived by Sable Ridge | **Derived from scratch by the incoming assessor.** Marketa cannot supply them, propose them, or point to the last set |
| Assessor hours | **51 actual against 46 estimated** | Planned above 51, because the incoming assessor has no familiarity to amortise |
| Evidence rounds | **CTP-7 and CTP-11 required a second round** — behavioural baseline coverage across all 61 accounts, and an instance of the response action actually firing | Assume at least two procedures require a second round again, and possibly different ones |
| The limitation recorded | The objective was met **for the assessed period**, with **no forward assurance** | Identical. The limitation is a property of the mechanism, not of the assessor |

**Fifty-one hours is not the cost of the customized approach. It is the annual, non-amortising, non-transferable cost of the customized approach**, and it is the single cleanest number in the portfolio for what the approach is worth arguing about. Marketa recorded the variance against its 46-hour estimate for exactly this reason: an estimate nobody reconciles is an estimate that will be wrong again next year, and the 2028 budget uses the actual.

## 5. The 2027 Decision — Whether to Keep the Customized Approach

Marketa must decide, at the milestone dated **2028-04-19** and on the analysis prepared through 2027, whether to continue meeting **8.3.9** through the customized approach or to return it to the defined approach.

**This document does not take that decision.** It sets out both arguments in the form in which they will be put, because the decision belongs to the year it is taken, by the people who will be accountable for it, against evidence that does not yet exist — a second full year of behavioural analytics operation, the state of the residual single-factor population, and the disposition of an assessor nobody has met.

### 5.1 What 8.3.9 is, restated

The customized approach replaces the defined approach's password change frequency with **continuous behavioural authentication analytics**. It is scoped to the residual in-scope population where a password remains the sole factor — **connected-to and vendor-managed systems only**, because 8.4.2's multi-factor authentication removes password-only user access to the cardholder data environment. It is the programme's **only** customized approach, against **one** of 306 sub-requirements.

### 5.2 The argument for keeping it

| Argument | Substance |
|---|---|
| **It is the better control, and that is not a rhetorical claim** | Ninety-day rotation on a residual population of vendor-managed systems produces predictable passwords and a scheduled administrative burden. Continuous behavioural analytics produced **three real firings** in the assessed period: two false positives cleared within the hour, and **one genuine credential-sharing case** that a rotation schedule would never have surfaced |
| **Withdrawing is not free either** | The defined approach must then be implemented and evidenced across the same population, inside the same freeze calendar, and the assessor will test the implementation rather than the argument. **A withdrawal is a project, not a deletion** |
| **The capability now exists and is paid for** | The analytics platform, the baselines across all 61 accounts and the response action are built, running and evidenced. Abandoning the approach abandons none of that, but it does convert an assessed control into an unassessed one |
| **It is one requirement of 306** | The blast radius of the argument is bounded. If the new assessor rejects the customized approach, 8.3.9 is a finding on one sub-requirement with a remediation route that already exists |
| **The portfolio's own standard** | Marketa built the approach because the defined approach was worse for this population, not because it was easier. **Withdrawing it because a new assessor might be difficult would be choosing the weaker control for an assessment reason**, which is the exact behaviour every ADR in this programme has refused |

### 5.3 The argument for withdrawing it

| Argument | Substance |
|---|---|
| **Fifty-one hours, every year, forever** | It does not amortise. It is not transferable between assessors. **It is the single largest per-requirement assessor cost in the programme**, on a requirement that would otherwise be routine |
| **It carries no forward assurance, and that is recorded in the report** | The objective was met **for the assessed period**. Every year begins with the argument unproven. A control whose compliance status resets annually is a control with a permanent open question attached |
| **The risk is concentrated and the failure mode is severe** | **A 12.3.1 analysis justifies a number; a 12.3.2 analysis justifies a control.** If a frequency analysis is wrong, the frequency is a finding. **If the customized approach fails, the requirement is not met at all**, because there is no defined-approach implementation underneath it |
| **The population it serves may be shrinking** | The approach applies only where a password remains the sole factor. As multi-factor coverage extends across connected-to and vendor-managed systems, the population the argument protects gets smaller — and at some point the annual cost is being paid for a handful of accounts |
| **A new assessor may derive harder procedures** | **CTP-7 and CTP-11 already required a second evidence round** with an assessor that had helped derive them. A different firm may derive twelve procedures Marketa has never seen, at a point in the calendar where there is no room to answer them |

### 5.4 What the decision needs, and what it must not be

| The decision needs | The decision must not be |
|---|---|
| A second full year of analytics operation, including how many firings occurred and how many were genuine | **Taken by default.** An approach kept because nobody scheduled the conversation is an approach nobody has decided to keep |
| The current size of the residual single-factor population, with a trajectory | **Taken in 2028 under time pressure**, three months before fieldwork, by whoever is in the room |
| The TRA-10 review of 2028-03-15, complete and read | **Taken to please an assessor**, in either direction |
| A costed implementation plan for the defined approach, so that *withdraw* is an option with a price rather than a preference | Presented as obvious. **It is not obvious, and a document that concluded here would be pretending to knowledge it does not have** |

### 5.5 A decision framework, not a recommendation

To make the 2028-04-19 decision a decision rather than a discussion, three tests are recorded now. Each has a threshold nobody has yet measured against, which is the point.

| Test | Retain if | Withdraw if |
|---|---|---|
| **Does the control still do something a rotation schedule would not?** | The analytics produced at least one genuine detection in the second year that a 90-day rotation could not have produced | Two consecutive years with no genuine detection. **A control whose only output is false positives is an expensive way to satisfy a requirement** |
| **Is the population it protects still material?** | The residual single-factor population remains large enough that rotation across it would be a real operational burden with a real predictability cost | The population has shrunk to a size where the defined approach is trivially implementable — at which point the argument is being paid for out of habit |
| **Can the argument be made cold?** | The matrix, the analysis and the evidence can be handed to a reader who has never seen them and be understood without narration | It takes a conversation to explain. **An argument that needs its author present is an argument that will not survive an assessor rotation, which is precisely the scenario** |

**Nothing in this section takes the decision.** The tests are written now because the people who will apply them in April 2028 should not also be the people who choose the tests, and because a threshold set in advance is a threshold that cannot be adjusted to the answer somebody wants.

## 6. What Does Carry Forward

Stating the reset without stating its limits would overclaim in the other direction. Four things genuinely survive an assessor rotation, and they are the four the programme spent eighteen months building.

| Carries forward | Why |
|---|---|
| **The evidence discipline** | **186 of 214 requests satisfied from artefacts that already existed** is a property of how Marketa works, not of who was asking. A new assessor asks different questions of the same index |
| **The environment** | Nine zones, 71 assessed components, six baselines, the assertion layers, the script control and the tamper detection are all still there. **The assessment resets; the controls do not** |
| **The operating history** | A control with two years of dated records is a stronger control to present than the same control with one, whoever is reading |
| **The habit of publishing the uncomfortable thing first** | The 2026 report contains two Not in Place findings Marketa identified itself and refused to argue away. **That record is available to be shown to a new assessor, and it is the most persuasive thing in the file** |

### 6.1 And the thing that carries forward whether Marketa wants it to or not

**The 2026 Non-Compliant attestation is on file with Cardinal Merchant Bank and was never withdrawn — ADR-0032.** A new assessor performing its scoping will see two attestations for the 2026 cycle, in that order, and will ask about them.

That is not a liability to be managed. It is the strongest single piece of evidence in the file: **an entity that identified its own two worst findings five and four months before the assessor arrived, refused a compensating control for each, filed the finding twenty days early, remediated inside a change freeze with eighteen days of margin, and left the original record standing.** The answer to the question is the whole answer, and it takes one paragraph.

## 7. What This Planning Does Not Achieve

| Limitation | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **A plan is not a result** | Every milestone in §4 is a commitment made eighteen months in advance by people who will not all still hold the same roles |
| **The incoming assessor is unknown** | Nothing here predicts its interpretations. It predicts only that the interpretations will be its own, which is the whole basis of ADR-0036 |
| **Three of the resets are outside Marketa's control** | The scope boundary rests on three providers' validation status. Marketa can verify it monthly under MT-1 and cannot determine it |
| **The customized approach decision is deferred, and deferral has a cost** | Every month the decision is not taken is a month of preparation spent on both options. **The plan bounds that cost by giving the decision a date** |
| **Budget is a forecast** | The 2028 assessment is budgeted above the 2027 actual on the reasoning in ADR-0036. **The programme has already published one forecast that was wrong and reported against it** — 09.12 §2 — and this is another forecast |

## 8. The One-Sentence Version, for the Governance Forum

> **Plan the 2028 assessment as a first assessment of a well-run estate, not as the third year of a relationship — because that is what it will be, and the only variable Marketa controls is whether it planned for it.**

Twelve derived testing procedures, eighteen sampling rationales, three compensating controls, four Not Applicable determinations and one customized approach are, between them, the entire difference between a routine assessment and a difficult one — and **not one of them carries forward.** What carries forward is the estate, the operating history, the evidence discipline and a filing that says Marketa reported two findings it could have argued away. **Those four are worth more to a new assessor than any document Sable Ridge produced**, and they are the only part of the preparation that cannot be done in the fourteen months §4 allows.
