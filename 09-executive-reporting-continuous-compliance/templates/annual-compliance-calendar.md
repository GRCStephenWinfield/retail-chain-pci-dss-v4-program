## Sections to include

| Section | Typical content |
|---|---|
| **Governance** | Steering or governance forum, working group, audit committee, provider review |
| **Acquirer obligations** | Every C-number, with its contractual deadline — not the internal target date |
| **External testing** | ASV quarters (and the **30-day** attestation window, which is the deadline people miss), segmentation and application penetration testing |
| **Internal testing** | Vulnerability scanning cycles, controlled execution, unannounced availability tests |
| **Targeted risk analyses** | One line per analysis. **Stagger the review dates** — fourteen analyses all falling in the same month is fourteen analyses reviewed badly |
| **Annual set-pieces** | Scope confirmation (12.5.2), policy review, awareness cycle, incident response test, the assessment itself |
| **Device and physical** | POI inspection cycles, media destruction certification |

## Two rules worth writing on the calendar itself

**Service-provider requirements are not on a merchant's calendar.** 11.4.6, 12.5.2.1, 12.5.3, 12.4.2 and their relatives never applied. Putting them on the calendar makes the calendar look thorough and makes it wrong.

**A date the entity does not control is a date the entity cannot meet.** An item written against a vendor's renewal date, a provider's publication cycle or a third party's availability should say so on its face — Marketa closed one open item **40 days late** for exactly this reason, and the lesson is recorded rather than the date adjusted.
