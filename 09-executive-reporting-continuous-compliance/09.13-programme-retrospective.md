## Purpose

Seventeen point six months, from kickoff on **2026-01-12** to close on **2027-06-30**. Nine phases, 306 assessed sub-requirements, two attestations, **$4,588,000** against a **$4,600,000** budget, and a risk register that came down from nine High entries to none and missed its own published close forecast by three.

This document is the retrospective: the arc of the programme in one narrative, the five decisions that determined how it ended, the things Marketa's own instruments found that the assessment did not, eleven lessons of which four are uncomfortable, three things a reader should take away, and — the section that justifies writing a retrospective at all — **what a second programme would do differently, in specifics rather than platitudes.**

It is written for a reader who was not there and who will have to do this somewhere else.

## 1. The Arc

### 1.1 From 1,842 to 71

| Position | Number | What it was |
|---|---|---|
| Systems in the enterprise estate | **1,842** | Everything Marketa runs |
| **Systems initially believed in scope** | **604** | The starting position, before analysis. Not a measurement — **a belief**, held by competent people, and wrong by a factor of eight |
| Systems confirmed in the cardholder data environment | **8** | Payment operations, chargebacks, settlement reconciliation and refunds, in Columbus and AWS |
| Connected-to and security-impacting systems | **63** | Directory, SIEM, jump hosts, patching, endpoint console, backup, vulnerability scanner |
| **Total assessed system components** | **71** | The population the Report on Compliance describes |
| **Scope reduction** | **604 → 71, −88.2%** | Achieved through **P2PE, tokenization and DTMF masking** — three architectural decisions, none of them a security control in the ordinary sense |

**The single most consequential number in the programme is 604 → 71, and it was produced in Phase 02, in the first eight weeks.** Everything afterwards — the segmentation build, the baselines, the identity work, the monitoring, the assessment itself — was performed against a population that had already been reduced by 88.2%. A programme that had assessed 604 components would have been a different programme with a different budget and a different outcome, and it would have been assessing components that had no business being in scope.

The reduction was not free and the register prices it. **R-05** is a P2PE condition failing. **R-09** is Truvance being hosted checkout, gateway and the single token vault across all three channels simultaneously. **R-23** is the DTMF suppression point moving downstream. All three are still Moderate or Low at close, all three are permanent, and the tabletop found a fourth cost nobody had scored: **604 to 71 put the primary account number somewhere else; it did not put the obligation to answer questions about the primary account number somewhere else.**

## 2. The Five Decisions

Five decisions shaped how this programme ended, and every one of them could have gone the other way. Four were taken before anybody knew which requirements would be uncomfortable, which is the only reason they held.

| # | Decision | When | What it prevented |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **ADR-0004 — no "Not Tested" disposition is permitted in Marketa's Report on Compliance** | **Phase 01, January 2026** | Not Tested is the disposition used when an entity and its assessor agree that a requirement was not examined. It is legitimate and it is also the softest available landing for an inconvenient requirement. **Taken ten months before anybody knew that 8.6.2 and 11.3.1.2 would be the two problems**, it removed the option in advance. The report contains **Not Tested: 0 of 306** |
| **2** | **The two refused compensating controls — 8.6.2 in June 2026 and 11.3.1.2 in July 2026** | **Phases 05 and 06** | A compensating control worksheet was available for both. Both were refused on a test stated once and applied consistently: **a compensating control addresses a constraint that prevents the requirement being met, not a cost that makes meeting it inconvenient.** Refusing them converted two accommodations into two findings, and the findings are what got fixed |
| **3** | **DEC-710 — declining a late compensating-control argument three weeks before fieldwork** | **October 2026** | The last opportunity to avoid a Non-Compliant filing. It was declined on the CISO's reasoning that **arguing one then would tell the assessor exactly what the June and July refusals were worth.** A programme that refuses an accommodation in June and produces one in October has not applied a test; it has run out of time |
| **4** | **ADR-0031 — the Non-Compliant attestation is filed early, not late** | **December 2026** | Filing late after an attempt to close the gaps would have converted a compliance problem into an operational one, by creating a commercial incentive to push a settlement-path change through the seasonal freeze at peak trading. **Filed 2026-12-11, twenty days ahead of the 31 December deadline, with an honest Part 4** |
| **5** | **ADR-0032 — the 2026 attestation is not withdrawn** | **February 2027** | The revised attestation of 2027-02-18 is **additive**. The Non-Compliant record of 2026-12-11 stands, both are filed, and neither is withdrawn. **A record that is replaced when it becomes inconvenient is not a record** |

### 2.1 What the five have in common

**Four of the five were decided before the situation they governed existed.** ADR-0004 in January, the two refusals in June and July, ADR-0031 as a standing position on filing. Only ADR-0032 was taken after the fact, and it was taken to preserve a record rather than to create one.

That is the whole mechanism. **A decision taken in advance is a decision taken by people who do not yet know which way it will cut**, and it is the only kind that survives a December in which a Chief Financial Officer is about to sign a Non-Compliant attestation. Every one of the five had a moment at which reversing it would have produced a more comfortable outcome, and the reason none was reversed is that each was written down, with a rationale, in a document somebody would have had to visibly contradict.

### 2.2 Four decisions that went the other way, and what they cost

A retrospective that lists only the decisions it is proud of has described a different programme. Four significant decisions went the pragmatic way, each was recorded with its residual at the time, and **three of the four produced a register entry that is still Moderate at close.**

| Decision | Taken | The residual, recorded at the time | What it actually cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **No second enforcement plane in the store fabric** | Phase 03, on cost and space across 482 stockrooms | **R-03** — three store zones separated by one switch's configuration | **The residual materialised.** Store 0417's guest wireless reached the store back-office zone in nineteen minutes, and the remediation ran across 37 stores at **+$77,000** against the segmentation build line |
| **A single provider across all three channels** | Predates the programme; re-examined in Phase 02 and retained | **R-09** — Truvance is hosted checkout, gateway and the single token vault simultaneously | **BAU-01.** The provider declined a response-time commitment, and no control Marketa builds reduces the consequence of a compromise at that provider |
| **The customized approach at 8.3.9** | Phase 05, because the defined approach was the weaker control for the residual population | The approach carries **no forward assurance** and re-argues annually | **51 assessor hours against 46 estimated**, every year, non-amortising, resetting entirely at assessor rotation |
| **The seasonal change freeze accepted as a constraint rather than contested** | Phase 01, **CON-01** | Four months a year in which store-side change is impossible, at peak volume | The whole calendar is built backwards from **30 September**, and the 2027 remediation had to run inside the freeze under an exemption with **eighteen days of margin and no float** |

**None of the four was wrong.** Each was a business decision taken with the residual written down, which is the only defensible way to take one — and the reason the register contains eight entries that no control will ever reach is that Phase 03 priced the trades rather than presenting them as unmixed goods.

## 3. What the Programme's Own Instruments Found That the Assessment Did Not

The assessment examined 71 components across 15 days, tested 306 sub-requirements and reached 297 In Place. **Every one of those conclusions is about whether a control exists, operates and can be evidenced.** Three of the programme's most valuable findings came from instruments that asked a different question, and none of them would have appeared in a Report on Compliance.

| Instrument | What it found | Why the assessment could not have found it |
|---|---|---|
| **The application penetration test, September 2026** | **An unauthenticated administrative interface on a cardholder data environment component, open for 176 days**, reached by a tester from an assumed-compromise position on 2026-09-16. It was produced by three individually correct decisions intersecting: authentication enforced at a reverse proxy, a management connector bound to all interfaces by a platform vendor's new default in a minor release, and an approved firewall rule permitting the platform's management port range from the administrative zone. **Nobody was careless and nothing was misconfigured against its own intent** | **An assessor tests controls against a standard; a penetration tester tests an environment against an attacker.** Every control involved was operating correctly. Marketa could establish that nothing else reached the interface across the whole 176-day window — **a complete enumeration of 47 sessions, 41 the tester's and 6 the platform's own health checks** — because inline prevention with flow recording retains every session for fifteen months. **Retention is what converts *we found nothing* into *there was nothing*** |
| **11.6.1 payment-page tamper detection** | **Two of Marketa's own release failures on live payment pages**, including security-header regressions on payment templates where the edge configuration is changed by a different team, on a different cadence, from the template code. Twenty-one of the 214 alerts found a control that was not operating as intended rather than a page that had changed | The requirement obliges detection at least weekly. **Marketa's own releases are the most common source of unauthorised change to its own payment pages**, and no annual assessment observes a release. The mechanism found them at a median of nine minutes |
| **TT-2026-01, the annual tabletop of 2026-06-24** | **The disproof of a load-bearing assumption.** The scenario was designed by Internal Audit and disclosed to nobody, including the CISO: a 07:40 telephone call from Cardinal reporting approximately 1,900 accounts with a common point of purchase at Marketa's e-commerce channel, with no Marketa alert. Asked which accounts were affected, the room reached the correct answer in fifty-five minutes: **Marketa does not hold that information.** The token-to-account mapping is Truvance's, and the agreement carried **no response-time commitment** for the enumeration | A QSA testing 12.10 tests whether the plan exists, covers the required elements and has been exercised. **It does not test whether the plan works on the worst morning.** The exercise found the gap because it started from somebody else's evidence, and **every scenario the security team had ever built started with a Marketa alert** |

**All three findings share a property.** Each was produced by an instrument that could fail, run by somebody who wanted it to. The penetration test was scoped so that failure was possible. The tamper detection reports its false-positive rate at 15.9% rather than engineering it away. The tabletop was designed by a person with a standing brief to find the seam, in front of the Audit Committee Chair, with the explicit acceptance that it might make the security function look bad. **A test that cannot embarrass anybody has not been designed to find anything.**

### 3.1 And the symmetry — three things the assessment found that the programme's instruments did not

The argument only works if it runs both ways, and it does. **An entity is not usually wrong about the things it already knows are broken. It is wrong about the things it is confident about**, and the value of an independent assessor is concentrated entirely in that second category.

| What the assessor found | What Marketa believed | Why no internal instrument caught it |
|---|---|---|
| **Two of nine network security control ruleset backups held on an unrestricted file share** — 1.2.8, found on the assessor's second working day | The requirement was asserted **In Place** | **The assumption that a backup destination inherits the protection of its source.** No assertion covered the destination, because nobody had thought of the destination as part of the control. Corrected 2026-11-05 and disclosed under **ADR-0029** |
| **USB mass-storage capability enabled on 31 of 71 components** while policy prohibited removable media — 5.3.3, proposed by Marketa as Not Applicable | That a policy prohibition described the estate's capability | **A policy prohibition is not a technical absence.** The requirement was returned to the tested population and assessed **In Place on the EDR device-control module** — a control Marketa already owned and had not thought to present. **The exemption argument cost more effort than the evidence would have** |
| **The nine appliances' built-in administrative accounts governed under the appliance inventory rather than the enterprise identity store**, so Marketa's own 8.2.2 shared-account figure of **eleven understated the true population of twenty** | That the shared-account population was fully enumerated | **Two registers, one population, and nothing reconciling them.** Adopted as an assessor observation; both registers reconciled **2027-04-17** |

**All three are the same failure in three shapes: a control's boundary drawn where the team drew it rather than where the exposure is.** No dashboard finds that, because a dashboard is built by the same people who drew the boundary.

## 4. Eleven Lessons

**Two series, and the distinction matters.** **L-01 to L-04** are the four uncomfortable lessons carried unchanged from **09.07**, where each is set out at length with its evidence; they are restated here because a retrospective that omitted them would be a different document. **ML-01 to ML-07** are the seven **method** lessons this retrospective adds — statements about how the programme was run rather than about what it found — and they are complementary to 09.07 §4's seven operational lessons rather than a replacement for them.

### 4.1 The seven method lessons

| # | Lesson | The evidence behind it |
|---|---|---|
| **ML-01** | **Scope is the highest-leverage work in the programme, and it is finished before most programmes have started.** | **604 → 71 in eight weeks.** Everything after Phase 02 was performed against a population 88.2% smaller. The reduction is also the source of three permanent register entries, which is the honest accounting |
| **ML-02** | **Evidence built as the work is done is the only cheap evidence, and the saving is measurable.** | **186 of 214 assessor requests (86.9%) satisfied from artefacts that already existed**, 0 unsatisfied. The 28 that required new production are point-in-time observations nobody can pre-produce |
| **ML-03** | **A control published is not a control operating, and a register that moves on delivery is a register that flatters.** | **Phases 04 and 05 built most of the programme's controls and moved zero risk entries**, and said so both times. The six, five, seven and eight movements afterwards mean something because of it |
| **ML-04** | **Publish the forecast, the exit criterion and the reversal condition before you know the answer.** | R-14's exit criterion was published in Phase 03 before the first test ran, and its clock was reset by a real recurrence. R-13's reversal condition was published with its movement. R-42's likelihood of 1 was published with an expiry date **and actually expired on it** |
| **ML-05** | **A frequency without a targeted risk analysis is a habit with a number attached.** | **Fourteen analyses**, six mandatory elements each, staggered review dates, and **four trigger sets that fired** in the operating period. TRA-01 changed a cadence from the assumed annual to six-monthly, and the calendar was amended |
| **ML-06** | **A system that has failed is not a reliable narrator of its own state.** | **9 of 21 critical security control failures were invisible to the failing system's own health reporting** and were found only by an independent secondary detection — a synthetic probe, an injected event, a canary file change, a synthetic authentication |
| **ML-07** | **An accommodation never lowers a rating, and naming the difference keeps a register honest.** | R-17 and R-36 carried **eleven interim measures between them across 2026** and neither score moved by a point until the requirements were met. The SNMPv2c compensating position was recorded as an accommodation of R-15, not a treatment of it — **only CAP-05 treated it** |

### 4.2 The four uncomfortable ones — L-01 to L-04, carried from 09.07

| # | Lesson | Why it is uncomfortable |
|---|---|---|
| **L-01** | **The close forecast was wrong because nobody checked the arithmetic.** 03.12 §6 forecast thirteen Moderate entries reaching Low, and **under the register's own scoring discipline an entry at 3 × 4 cannot reach Low** — it can reach 2 × 4 = 8, and 8 is Moderate. **Eight is a floor.** R-04, R-28 and R-33 are all 3 × 4; R-17 and R-36 both arrive at 2 × 4 | It was published in month seven of an eighteen-month programme, restated unchanged five times by five later documents, **and none of the five checked it against the model 03.10 had published three weeks earlier.** The register closes at **0 · 16 · 28** against **0 · 13 · 31**, three short, and 09.12 §2 reconciles it line by line. **The failure was not in the delivery. It was in a prediction nobody audited** |
| **L-02** | **Three of the programme's five worst assessed outcomes came from one support agreement that nobody had read as a security control.** The Verition support agreement produced **11.3.1.2 Not in Place** — no credential release, no agent installation rights on nine components — **CCW-01**, the ten-character local password cap against 8.3.6's twelve, and **CCW-02**, a management interface offering no protocol with strong cryptography. **Three of the five: two Not in Place and three compensating controls** | It is a **commercial document, not a technical one**, and it sat in a contract repository that no security review touched. The constraint predated the programme by years. The remediation was a contract amendment executed on 2027-01-08 by the General Counsel — **and it removed the finding, not the constraint.** Marketa can scan those nine appliances because a clause says so, and **R-36's residual is now contractual rather than technical**, with a renewal date and **BAU-02** attached |
| **L-03** | **The incident response plan has never been tested by the event it exists for, and no amount of exercising changes that.** **512 incident records, 5 SEV-2, 0 SEV-1, no confirmed compromise of account data.** The plan has been activated for a rogue access point, two file-integrity escalations, three page-integrity incidents, twenty-one critical security control failures and a penetration test Critical, and **none of those was a breach** | **R-43 has not moved in a single phase of this programme**, and it is the register's most honest entry. It could be argued down — the plan has been exercised eleven times and tested by an exercise designed to break it — and it is held because the event it exists for has not happened. **It is also the one red measure on the board scorecard, and ADR-0035 keeps it there.** A scorecard that goes all green is a scorecard that has stopped measuring something |
| **L-04** | **The 86.9% evidence figure decays.** It is the output of a programme with eleven people watching. **Business as usual has four.** The 28 requests that required new production are the ones that cannot be pre-produced — and they are also the ones a programme forgets to schedule in a year when nobody is coming | **09.08 §6.5 names the four activities that will be done less**, and the first of them is proactive evidence quality assurance. The decay has a measurement attached to it — the proportion of the next assessor's requests satisfiable from existing artefacts, reported against 86.9% at the pre-fieldwork readiness review — but a measurement is not a mitigation. **The number will fall, and the programme has said so in advance rather than explaining it afterwards** |

### 4.3 Two lessons that pull against each other

Presenting eleven lessons as though they are mutually consistent would be dishonest. Two of them are in tension, the tension is real, and every entity running this programme will have to resolve it for itself.

| Tension | The pull | The counter-pull |
|---|---|---|
| **ML-02 and L-04** — evidence built as the work is done is the only cheap evidence, **and the number decays** | Build the index continuously and the assessment is cheap. **86.9% is the payoff of seven phases** | The discipline that produces 86.9% costs eleven people's attention. **At 4.2 FTE it will not be sustained**, and the honest plan is to measure the decay quarterly rather than to promise it will not happen |
| **ML-03 and ML-04** — a register must not move on delivery, **and a forecast must be published in advance** | Nothing moves until it has operating history. Phases 04 and 05 moved zero entries and were right to | A forecast published in month seven is a prediction about ratings that have no evidence behind them yet. **L-01 is what happens when the two collide**, and the resolution is not to stop forecasting but to forecast against the arithmetic floor rather than against the plan |

### 4.4 The lesson that is not on the list, because it is not repeatable

**Some of this programme's outcome was luck, and a retrospective that converts luck into method is the least useful document a programme can produce.**

| What went well | The part that was method | The part that was luck |
|---|---|---|
| The 176-day interface was found before anybody else found it | An assumed-compromise penetration test, scoped so that failure was possible | **That it was found in September rather than in month nineteen.** The test date was chosen for the assessment calendar, not for that finding |
| Nothing else reached the interface across 176 days | Fifteen months of immutable flow recording at the Z3-to-Z1 crossing, built for a different purpose | **That the recording covered the whole window.** Twelve months of retention would have left the first fifty-nine days unanswerable |
| The remediation completed inside the freeze with eighteen days of margin | A credible plan, an approved exemption, and two parallel routes | **That the vendor executed a contract amendment on 2027-01-08.** Marketa had no instrument to compel it, which is the same gap **BAU-01** describes at a different provider |
| No SEV-1 in 512 incident records | Segmentation, monitoring, detection and a scope reduction that removed the primary account number from three channels | **That nobody came.** 0 SEV-1 is evidence that none was detected and declared, and the four techniques the controlled execution did not catch describe exactly the compromise that would not have appeared |

## 5. Three Things a Reader Should Take Away

### 5.1 Scope is the control

**Not a preliminary to the control work — the control work.** Marketa removed 1,914 lanes, 482 store servers, a 310-agent contact centre and an entire e-commerce card field from the assessed population by making three architectural decisions, and every subsequent control was cheaper, smaller and more defensible because of it. The corollary is the part most programmes skip: **a scope reduction is a dependency on somebody else's validation status**, and Marketa's rests on three companies' documents, verified monthly under MT-1, re-tested at every assessment, and capable of moving without notice.

### 5.2 Publish the number before you know what it is

The programme published a close forecast in month seven, exit criteria before the tests that would meet them, reversal conditions attached to contested movements, an expiry date on a temporary likelihood, a 46-hour estimate for the customized approach, and a Part 4 remediation date on an attestation filed twenty days early. **It missed two of those forecasts and reported against both.** A forecast published in advance and missed in public is worth more than a position described accurately after the fact, because only the first one can be checked — and an organisation that has been checked once behaves differently the next time.

### 5.3 A Compliant attestation answers a narrower question than anybody wants it to

**An assessment tests controls against a standard. It does not test an environment against an adversary.** Sable Ridge examined 71 components across 15 days and concluded that 297 of 306 requirements were met, three more by compensating control and four did not apply. **1,284 of the 1,654 indexed artefacts were Marketa's own documents.** The assessor tested them, performed 34 independent actions of its own, and reached an opinion — which binds neither the acquirer nor any card brand and establishes nothing about statutory compliance. **The programme's own instruments answered the harder question, and their answers were less comfortable.** A clean assessment and an untested capability are entirely compatible.

## 6. What a Second Programme Would Do Differently

Ten specifics. None of them is *communicate better* or *engage stakeholders earlier*.

| # | Change | Why, from this programme's record |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Model every register entry's minimum reachable score before publishing a close forecast.** For each entry, compute the lowest score the scoring discipline permits — the floor at likelihood 2 where impact cannot be argued down — and forecast against that, not against optimism | **L-01.** Thirteen entries were forecast across a boundary the arithmetic does not permit. The check takes an afternoon and would have produced a forecast of eleven or twelve, which the programme would have met |
| **2** | **Read the three largest support and service agreements as security controls in Phase 01, with the General Counsel in the room.** Extract every clause that grants or withholds credential access, agent installation, scanning rights, firmware roadmap visibility and incident response times | **L-02.** One support agreement produced three of the five worst assessed outcomes, and it was discovered as a security constraint in Phase 05, sixteen months after it was signed. **The tabletop found the same class of gap in a second agreement — Truvance's — in June 2026, and OI-07-01 still did not close** |
| **3** | **Run an assumed-compromise penetration test in month three, not month nine.** | The unauthenticated administrative interface was open for **176 days**, and 176 days is roughly the gap between the platform upgrade of 2026-03-24 and the test of 2026-09-16. **An earlier test finds it earlier; that is the entire argument** |
| **4** | **Have Internal Audit design the first tabletop, from an external inject, in year one.** | The 2025 exercise was designed by the security team against a scenario the security team considered likely, and the plan performed well. The 2026 exercise was designed by somebody with a brief to find the seam and **disproved a load-bearing assumption in fifty-five minutes.** A year was lost between those two exercises |
| **5** | **Give every targeted risk analysis an identifier, an owner and a review date at the moment it is created.** | **Three of the fourteen lived inside other documents** — 04.06 §3.1, 05.06, 06.11 — with no separate identifier, no analysis owner and no review date, until they were consolidated in Phase 07. **An analysis that lives inside another document is an analysis that will not be reviewed, because nothing raises it** |
| **6** | **Start the evidence index at kickoff against the four-part standard, and measure the satisfiable-from-existing ratio quarterly.** | **86.9% was discovered in November 2026.** A quarterly measurement would have shown the trajectory, would have identified the 28 unproducible items eighteen months earlier, and is the only instrument that would detect the decay L-04 predicts before an assessor does |
| **7** | **Schedule every quarterly obligation in the first half of its quarter.** | The Q1 2026 ASV scan failed with three findings and was rescanned clean in eleven days, which is a pass for the quarter — **and it worked only because there was room.** A quarter that closes with only a failing scan cannot be repaired retrospectively |
| **8** | **Negotiate provider incident response-time commitments at contract inception, not at renewal under a compliance deadline.** | **Truvance declined at the 2027 renewal.** Marketa asked for a response-time commitment on incident-driven token resolution from the provider that holds the token-to-PAN mapping for all three channels, from a position of no leverage, eight months after discovering it needed one. **BAU-01 carries the item to 2028-03-31** |
| **9** | **Budget the customized approach at its true annual, non-amortising cost from the start, and give the keep-or-withdraw decision a date.** | **51 assessor hours against 46 estimated, on one requirement of 306, resetting every year and entirely at assessor rotation.** The estimate was reconciled and the variance recorded, which is right; the decision about whether the approach is worth it was never given a date until 09.11 §5 gave it one |
| **10** | **Make the compliance manager role permanent in month three, and fund business as usual before the programme ends rather than at its close.** | The role was created for the programme and funded from the programme's internal labour line. **A programme-funded role ends with the programme**, and the 47 recurring obligations it holds have nowhere to go. Marketa made the appointment at close, which worked; **making it in month three would have removed the risk entirely and cost nothing** |

### 6.1 What a second programme would keep exactly as it is

The list above is longer than this one, which is the normal shape of a retrospective and is also misleading. Six things worked, worked for stated reasons, and would be repeated without amendment.

| Keep | Why |
|---|---|
| **Removing the Not Tested disposition in month one** | It cost nothing when it was taken and it was decisive eleven months later. **The cheapest decisions in a programme are the ones taken before anybody has a stake in the answer** |
| **Raising a risk on a disproved assumption, and keeping it High after complete remediation** | **R-14** and **R-31** were both High in a document published after both instances were fully closed. A register that netted discovery off against remediation would have shown two closed items and would have been describing an organisation that had learned nothing |
| **Publishing exit criteria and reversal conditions before the tests that would meet them** | R-14's clock was reset by a real recurrence and the reset was reported. R-42's likelihood of 1 expired on its published date. **A movement with a stated reversal condition is a movement somebody can hold you to** |
| **Reporting the failures of the programme's own instruments as positive figures** | **21 control failures**, a **15.9%** tamper false-positive rate, **4 undetected techniques**, and **the 38-minute proof-of-life result rather than the 4**. An entity reporting zero of any of these is reporting a detection gap |
| **Letting Internal Audit design the incident exercise and inviting the Audit Committee Chair to watch** | It made the security function look worse and the plan better. **ADR-0027** records that this was the point |
| **Refusing to reduce an impact score to move a rating** | Seven impact reductions in eighteen months, each on a consequence that had actually changed, and **none at R-04, R-28 or R-33 — where three reductions would have delivered the published forecast** |

## 7. What This Retrospective Does Not Claim

| Limitation | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **A retrospective is written by the people who ran the programme** | Naomi Bhatt owns this document and Raymond Voss approved it. The independent view is Rosa Delgado's internal audit — **7 recommendations, 0 Critical** — and the assessor's, which produced **17 observations, 4 contested determinations and 3 refused exclusions** |
| **Eighteen months is one cycle** | Almost every lesson here rests on a single instance. **One assessment, one assessor, one seasonal peak, one tabletop, one segmentation failure.** ML-04 is the exception, and it is the lesson with the most evidence behind it |
| **The counterfactuals are unprovable** | Nobody knows what would have happened if DEC-710 had gone the other way, or if the compensating controls had been argued in June. **The five decisions in §2 are recorded as decisions, not as proofs** |
| **The programme succeeded on the metric it set and missed the metric it forecast** | Both are true simultaneously, and a retrospective that reported only the first would be the kind of document this programme spent eighteen months refusing to produce |
