## 2. The point the Chief Executive made

Diane Okafor's question was whether the Board was being told something it should have been told earlier. The answer, on the record:

> "The Audit Committee was told in March that this was the likely outcome, and told why the alternative was worse. Nothing in December is new except the dates."

**A December that surprises a board is a March that was not honest.** The March 2026 Audit Committee minute recorded that two requirements were expected to be Not in Place at assessment, that a compensating control would be refused for each, and that the Committee preferred that outcome to a compliant attestation obtained by an argument nobody believed.

## 3. What the Audit Committee Chair asked

Priya Raghunathan asked one question and it was the right one: **what would have had to be true for this to have been avoided?**

The answer was not a control. It was a **support agreement signed years before the programme began**, which provided neither credential release nor agent installation rights on nine appliances, and which nobody had read as a security control. Three of the programme's five recorded assessment exceptions trace to it.

## 4. What the Chief Financial Officer said he was signing

Recorded verbatim because it had been recorded verbatim in November and in October, and the consistency is the point:

> "A non-compliant attestation with two findings, a dated remediation plan, and a re-assessment we expect to pass. That is what we have."
