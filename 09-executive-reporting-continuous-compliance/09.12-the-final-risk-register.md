## Purpose

03.11 published **44** risks on 2026-07-15 — **9 High, 21 Moderate, 14 Low** — and 03.12 §6 published a forecast of where they would be at programme close. Every phase since has reported against that baseline: nothing in Phase 04, nothing in Phase 05, six movements in Phase 06, five in Phase 07, seven in Phase 08.

This document reports the close position as at **2027-06-30**, the day the programme ends. It contains the **eight final movements**, the reconciliation of the close against the published forecast, the argument for the register's only non-expiring likelihood of 1, the complete account of the seven impact reductions the programme made in eighteen months, and the sixteen entries that remain Moderate with the reason each of them does.

It opens with the number, because the number is the thing.

> **The register closes at 0 High · 16 Moderate · 28 Low, against a published forecast of 0 High · 13 Moderate · 31 Low.**
>
> **Three entries short. The forecast was wrong, the programme is reporting against the forecast it published rather than the one it could have written afterwards, and §2 sets out exactly why — because the arithmetic is exact and the error is checkable.**

## 2. The Forecast, and the Miss

**Published forecast, 03.12 §6, restated unchanged in 04.12, 05.12, 06.12, 07.12, 08.13 and 08.14: 0 High · 13 Moderate · 31 Low.**

**Actual close, as at 2027-06-30: 0 High · 16 Moderate · 28 Low.**

### 2.1 The reconciliation

03.12 §6 forecast **thirteen** Moderate → Low entries: R-04, R-08, R-10, R-18, R-19, R-21, R-22, R-23, R-26, R-28, R-32, R-33, R-34.

| Line | Entries | Count |
|---|---|---|
| Forecast Moderate→Low, **delivered in Phase 06** | R-10, R-19, R-26, R-34 | 4 |
| Forecast Moderate→Low, **delivered in Phase 09** | R-08, R-18, R-21, R-22, R-23, R-32 | 6 |
| Forecast Moderate→Low, **not delivered** | **R-04, R-28, R-33** | **3** |
| Forecast High→**Low**, delivered to **Moderate 8** instead | **R-17, R-36** | **2** |
| **Not forecast to move at all, but reached Low** | **R-13, R-15** | **+2** |
| **Net shortfall against 13 Moderate** | | **+3 → 16** |

### 2.2 Why it was missed — and the reason is a single arithmetic fact

> **Under this register's own discipline — a rating moves on likelihood unless the consequence itself changed, and a likelihood of 1 means the event is not reasonably foreseeable — an entry scored 3 × 4 cannot reach Low. It can reach 2 × 4 = 8, and 8 is Moderate. Eight is a floor.**
>
> Every entry the forecast got wrong is a 3 × 4 entry or an entry that arrives at 2 × 4. **R-04, R-28 and R-33 are all 3 × 4. R-17 and R-36 both arrive at 2 × 4.** The Phase 03 forecast moved thirteen entries across a boundary the arithmetic does not permit them to cross, and it was written in **month seven of an eighteen-month programme, before the scoring discipline had been tested against a single year of evidence.**
>
> **The close position is not a failure of the programme. It is a failure of a prediction, and the programme is reporting the prediction it published rather than the one it could have written afterwards.**

### 2.3 The five entries, one by one

| Entry | Score at close | The arithmetic | What would actually move it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **R-04** — configuration drift returns to the store estate faster than assurance detects it | **Moderate 12** — 3 × 4 | Likelihood 3 is defensible and could fall to 2 on a second clean year; **2 × 4 = 8 is Moderate.** Impact 4 is a property of a store fabric with one enforcement plane | A second consecutive year with no segmentation-determining divergence across 482 stores, which is an operating cycle the programme does not contain |
| **R-28** — automated log review fails silently behind a healthy dashboard | **Moderate 12** — 3 × 4 | Same arithmetic. The mechanism that would reduce it is the mechanism that keeps discovering it is needed: **four CSC-9 failures in the period, three found only by secondary detection, and F-07's rule reporting itself healthy for 57 hours** | A full year with no silent-rule failure, and the fourth undetected technique's rule validated — **BAU-03** |
| **R-33** — scope creep enters the estate without a scope determination | **Moderate 12** — 3 × 4 | Same arithmetic. **27 significant changes** in the period, all classified, and the TRA-14 trigger fired once on a marketing subdomain repoint that had **not** been classified as significant | Two annual scope confirmations with no unclassified significant change, which is 2028 at the earliest |
| **R-17** — embedded credentials in legacy batch integrations | **Moderate 8** — 2 × 4 | Arrived at 2 × 4 on remediation and re-test. **Impact 4 holds because both accounts still reach settlement-path data**; removing a credential from a file does not change what the account can do if it is obtained another way | Nothing available in this programme. A likelihood of 1 would require the claim that the event is not reasonably foreseeable, and a vault is a system that can fail |
| **R-36** — nine vendor-locked appliances not authenticated-scanned | **Moderate 8** — 2 × 4 | Arrived at 2 × 4 on the contract amendment and the clean scan. **The residual changed shape rather than disappearing: the exposure is now contractual, not technical** | **BAU-02** — replacement of the appliance class. A right to scan that rests on a clause has a renewal date |

### 2.4 Two forecasts published in advance, two missed, both reported against

**06.12 §7 forecast R-17 and R-36 reaching Low on remediation, and 08.13 §6.3 corrected it in the open** — a remediation that closes a requirement takes a risk to Moderate when impact is held at 4, and leaves it there. **This phase corrects 03.12 §6 in the same way.**

| Forecast | Published | Corrected | By whom |
|---|---|---|---|
| R-17 and R-36 move **High → Low** on remediation | **06.12 §7** | **08.13 §6.3** | The phase after the one that would have benefited from the prediction being right |
| The close reconciles at **0 · 13 · 31** | **03.12 §6**, restated five times | **09.12 §2** — this section | The phase that has to report the actual number |

**That is the portfolio's habit and this is its last exercise.** A programme that publishes a forecast and then quietly restates it as though it had been met has learned nothing and has taught its readers to stop checking. The forecast was published in July 2026 so that Phase 09 would report against a commitment rather than against a blank page, and 03.12 §8 said in advance that missing it was a legitimate outcome and would be reported as one. It was, and it is.

### 2.5 The honest counter-argument, stated

A reader may object that the programme is being hard on itself: eight entries moved in four months, two of them were never forecast to move at all, and the composition of the miss is three entries that need another operating cycle rather than three entries that failed. That objection is fair and it does not change the number.

| The objection | The answer |
|---|---|
| "Thirteen was always optimistic; the delivery was fine" | Then the forecast should have said eleven. **A forecast is a commitment about a number, not about effort** |
| "R-13 and R-15 arrived early and offset the shortfall" | They did, and the reconciliation counts them. **The shortfall is +3 after crediting both** |
| "The band boundary is arbitrary — 8 and 6 are not very different" | The boundary is the model 03.10 published and every movement in this programme has been scored against it. **A band boundary that moves when it is inconvenient is not a band boundary** |
| "R-17 and R-36 came off High, which is what mattered" | It is, and it is the headline in 09.14. It is also not what the forecast said |

## 3. The Likelihood-1 Argument at R-15

**08.13 §5.2 said: *a likelihood of 1 that expires is the only kind this register will carry.*** R-15 breaks that sentence, and the phase argues it rather than hoping nobody checks.

### 3.1 What R-15 describes, and what CAP-05 did to it

R-15 as written describes **a legacy pre-shared key remnant** — 214 store appliances at 138 stores sharing one static WPA2-PSK that could not be revoked for one holder, attributed to one holder, or rotated without a field operation across 138 sites. **CAP-05 took the population from 214 devices at 138 stores to zero on 2027-06-12.** The key was decommissioned and the service set identifier was withdrawn.

| Term | R-42's likelihood of 1 | R-15's likelihood of 1 |
|---|---|---|
| What produced it | A specific event on a specific date could no longer occur — evidence unavailable to an assessor in November 2026, with 214 of 214 requests answered | **The condition the entry describes no longer exists in the estate** |
| What kind of claim it is | A control performed and a window closed | **The removal of the thing.** Not a control reducing a probability |
| Does it expire? | **Yes** — it reverted to 2 on 2027-01-01 when a new evidence year began with no history behind it, exactly as published | **No.** There is no date on which 214 devices return by the passage of time |
| Was the expiry honoured? | **Yes**, on the published date, without anybody arguing for an extension | Not applicable |

**08.13's sentence was a statement about the register as it then stood, not a rule.** At the time it was written, R-42 was the only likelihood of 1 the register had ever carried, and it was a temporary one; the sentence recorded the discipline that made it temporary. A rule that forbids a likelihood of 1 for a condition that has been physically removed would be a rule requiring the register to describe an estate that no longer exists.

### 3.2 The qualification — R-15 does not close

**R-15 stays on the register at Low 4.** It is not closed, not removed, and not re-worded.

> **The estate can reacquire a static pre-shared key the same way it acquired this one — a store refit, a vendor default, a temporary workaround that outlives its reason.**
>
> **A likelihood of 1 records that nothing is there now. It does not record that nothing can arrive.**

That is why the entry stays, why its treating controls stay, and why the wireless detection obligations under 11.2.1 and 11.2.2 continue quarterly in the 2027–28 calendar. The entry's continued presence at Low 4 is the mechanism that makes anybody look. **An entry closed on 2027-06-12 would have deleted the only standing instruction to check.**

### 3.3 The reversal condition, published

> **If any static pre-shared key is found in the store estate at any subsequent wireless detection cycle, R-15's likelihood returns to 3 without further argument and the entry returns to Moderate 12.**

That is how every contested movement in this portfolio has been handled — R-13's reversal condition in 08.13 §3.4, R-27's return-to-High condition in Phase 07, R-14's 90-day clock reset on 2026-07-03, R-42's published expiry. **A movement with a stated reversal condition is a movement somebody can hold you to.**

## 4. The Seven Impact Reductions in the Whole Programme, Named

**Impact has never been reduced in this programme as a way of moving a score that likelihood would not move.** Seven impact reductions were made in eighteen months, and every one was argued on **a change in consequence that had actually occurred**. All seven are named here, in one table, for the first and last time.

| # | Risk | Phase | Impact | The change in consequence that justified it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **R-10** — a provider's validation lapses inside the compliance year | **06** | 4 → 3 | **The suspension rule recorded in advance.** A provider lapse became a defined change in the assessed position — reliance suspended, the requirements brought into Marketa's own assessment — rather than an improvised argument under deadline pressure. The consequence of a lapse is now a known operational sequence instead of an open question |
| **2** | **R-14** — segmentation controls in the store estate are not effective | **07** | 4 → 3 | **The path the penetration test actually proved ran Z7 to Z5** — guest wireless into a connected-to store zone — while the Z3-to-Z1 crossing into the cardholder data environment is separately enforced at an inline prevention point with fifteen months of flow recording. The consequence of the proved failure is smaller than the consequence that was scored |
| **3** | **R-31** — historical account data persists in unstructured stores | **07** | 4 → 3 | **The historical corpus was destroyed and evidenced**, not merely controlled. Approximately 11,400 recordings and 2,187 documents no longer exist, and a certificate of destruction is a different kind of statement from an access control |
| **4** | **R-23** — the DTMF suppression point moves downstream | **09** | 5 → 3 | **The Cadence renewal of 2027-03-14** added pre-notification and rollback obligations for any change to the suppression point, and the daily MOTO-C2 assertion now **fails closed**: the channel stops accepting card payments rather than accepting them unmasked. The worst case changed from *spoken PAN in Marketa's recording estate* to *a MOTO channel that will not take a card* |
| **5** | **R-08** — account data as a by-product of an unmapped process | **09** | 4 → 3 | Process-level data mapping completed **2027-04-30** across all 61 procedures, and the collector-side quarantine rule applies at **every** inbound path. **An unmapped process now produces account data into a quarantine rather than into a store** |
| **6** | **R-18** — privileged access from a general-purpose workstation | **09** | 4 → 3 | The privileged access workstation rollout completed **2027-05-29** across all **178** in-scope administrative personnel. **A compromised general-purpose workstation no longer holds a privileged session to steal**; an adversary must compromise a second, hardened device |
| **7** | **R-32** — a boundary-affecting change classified as routine | **09** | 4 → 3 | **CSC-8's daily assertion bounds a misclassified change to at most 24 hours** rather than indefinitely. A boundary that is wrong for a day is a different consequence from a boundary that is wrong until somebody notices |

### 4.1 Why the final phase carries four of the seven

The distribution looks odd — none in Phases 03, 04, 05 or 08, one in Phase 06, two in Phase 07, four in Phase 09 — and the explanation is a single sentence that has been true throughout and only becomes visible at the end.

> **Architecture changes consequences; operations change likelihood.**
>
> **The last four months are when the architectural changes landed** — a contract clause, a data map, a dedicated workstation estate, and a daily assertion that bounds a window.

| Phase | What it built | What that does to a score |
|---|---|---|
| 03, 04, 05 | Zones, baselines, identity, access, physical controls | **Nothing, at the time.** A control published in July has no operating history, and 04.12 and 05.12 both moved zero entries and said so |
| 06 | A year of monitoring, testing and provider operation | Likelihood, six times — plus **one** consequence change, R-10's suspension rule |
| 07 | Policy, risk analysis, incident response, and the completion of two treatments | Likelihood, five times — plus **two** consequence changes, both on evidence about what a failure would actually reach |
| 08 | An independent test of the same controls | **Likelihood only, seven times.** The From I and To I columns are identical in all seven rows, which is a checkable claim and was made in that form deliberately |
| **09** | **The four things that change what an event costs** — a contract obligation with a rollback, a complete process map with a quarantine, a hardened privileged estate, and a daily bound on a misclassification | **Four consequence changes and four likelihood changes**, in the phase where the architecture finished |

**The distribution is evidence that the discipline held rather than evidence that it slipped.** A programme willing to reduce impact for convenience would have spread the reductions evenly across eighteen months and used them to move the entries that would not move otherwise. This one made none at all for five phases, and then made four in the phase where four consequences actually changed — and did **not** make them at R-04, R-28 or R-33, which are the three entries where an impact reduction would have delivered the published forecast.

**That is the whole argument in one line: the programme could have hit its forecast by reducing three impact scores, and did not.**

## 7. The Closing Statement

**Forty-four entries. None closed, none removed, none ever added.**

| Property | Statement |
|---|---|
| **The total never changed** | 44 at the baseline, 44 at every interim position, 44 at close. A register that grows by one entry every time a control produces a finding stops describing exposure and starts describing activity; a register that closes an entry every time a control works stops describing exposure at all |
| **ADR-0005 was tested and declined nine times** | The rule obliges a new entry when discovery **disproves an assumption**, not whenever a control or an assessor produces a finding. Nine candidates were tested against it across the programme — including the 1.2.8 backup share, the 5.3.3 port-state finding, the 8.3.9 forward-assurance limitation and the seventeen assessor observations — and **every one was recorded as an instance of an existing entry rather than as a new one** |
| **Two entries were raised on evidence, and both are now Low** | **R-14**, raised 2026-05-18 when the first segmentation penetration test failed, and **R-31**, raised 2026-02-05 when PAN was found in call recordings. Both were raised **after** complete remediation, both were High in a document published after the instance was closed, and **both are at Low 6 at programme close** — R-14 on its own published exit criterion and a second clean annual test, R-31 on a destroyed and evidenced corpus |
| **No High risk was ever accepted** | The acceptance route exists in 03.10 §5, requires written acceptance by the Chief Financial Officer with an expiry date, and **was never used.** Nine High entries entered this programme and every one left it by treatment |

And the sentence the register earns, which is a smaller claim than a reader might expect and is the only one the evidence supports:

> **The register is three entries worse than the programme said it would be, and it is three entries worse in a way that is arithmetically inevitable, publicly reconciled, and could have been avoided by reducing three impact scores that nothing in the environment justified reducing.**
>
> **The programme chose the number it could defend over the number it had promised.**
