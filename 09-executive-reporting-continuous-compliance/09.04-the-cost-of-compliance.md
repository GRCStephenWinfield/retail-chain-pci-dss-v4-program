## Purpose

This document states what the PCI DSS v4.0.1 compliance programme cost, line by line, against the **$4,600,000** envelope approved in the charter; expresses that cost in the three framings an executive audience can actually use; states the **business-as-usual steady state** the programme hands over; and then — in §6 — says what the money bought and what it did not.

Every figure here is a **cost of assurance**. **No fine, fee schedule or penalty amount is stated anywhere in this programme**, and none is stated here. The cost of *not* complying is described in §8 only as **categories** assessed against the acquirer under the card brand programmes and passed through to Marketa under the merchant agreement, with **no amount**, because Marketa does not hold that number, could not verify it, and would be inventing it if it published one.

Neither happened, and the $12,000 that remains is not a saving worth reporting as one.

## 2. The Three Framings

A total of $4,588,000 is not a number an executive can act on. Three framings are, and each one answers a different question.

| Framing | Figure | Arithmetic | The question it answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **As a share of revenue** | **0.11%** | $4,588,000 ÷ **$4,200,000,000** | *Is this proportionate to the business?* |
| **Per card transaction** | **$0.067** | $4,588,000 ÷ **68,400,000** transactions | *What does it cost to accept a card safely?* |
| **Per applicable sub-requirement** | **$14,993** | $4,588,000 ÷ **306** applicable sub-requirements | *What does a single line of the standard cost to reach and evidence?* |

### 2.1 What each framing is good for, and where it misleads

**0.11% of revenue** is the framing the Board used, and it is the right one for a proportionality question. It is also the framing most likely to be misread, in two directions. Read downward it invites the conclusion that compliance is cheap, which is only true because Marketa's revenue is large; the same programme at a $400M retailer is 1.1% of revenue and a different conversation entirely. Read upward it invites the conclusion that the number is a permanent tax, which it is not — **this was a first-year v4.0.1 programme with a scope reduction, a segmentation build and fifty-one future-dated requirements in force for the first time**, and the steady state in §4 is less than a third of it.

**$0.067 per transaction** is the framing operations understood immediately, because six and a bit cents on a transaction is a comparable unit to interchange, to the cost of a receipt, to a chargeback handling cost. Its weakness is that it implies linearity. **The cost is not per-transaction; it is per-environment.** Marketa's transaction volume could double without materially moving the programme cost, and could halve without reducing it at all, because the cost driver is 71 components, 482 stores, 1,914 terminals, three channels and six providers — not 68.4 million authorisations.

**$14,993 per applicable sub-requirement** is the framing that survives the most scrutiny and is the least comfortable, because it makes the scope argument in §6.1 unavoidable. Every requirement in the population costs, on average, roughly fifteen thousand dollars to reach and evidence. **The single largest financial lever available to any merchant is therefore not efficiency in meeting requirements; it is having fewer components in the population that has to meet them.** Its weakness is that the average conceals an enormous range — **8.3.9's customized approach alone consumed 51 assessor hours against 46 estimated**, and dozens of sub-requirements were evidenced from artefacts that already existed at effectively zero marginal cost.

| Type | Lines | Actual | Share of total |
|---|---|---|---|

| **Technology** — tooling and build | Security tooling; segmentation build | **$2,160,000** | The two capital lines combined |

**The assurance lines — the QSA, the ASV and the penetration testers — are $635,500, under fourteen per cent of the programme.** That ratio is worth stating to any executive who believes a compliance programme is an audit. The audit is the smallest part of it. The rest is building the thing the audit looks at, and staffing the people who build it.

### 3.1 Where the assurance money went

**$635,500** bought independent opinion. It is the smallest block in the programme and the only one Marketa did not write itself, and the composition matters because each of the three lines answers a different question.

| Line | Actual | What it bought | The question it answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **QSA — Sable Ridge Assurance, LLC** | **$429,000** | The annual assessment; monthly interim checkpoints from 2026-03; ROC and AOC production; and the February 2027 re-assessment. **15 business days** of on-site fieldwork, four assessors at peak, across Columbus, Austin, Ashburn and **28 stores**; **47 interviews across 39 individuals**; **61 process observations**; **34 independent testing actions**; a **741-page** report with **1,654 evidence references**; and **51 assessor hours** on one requirement because Marketa chose the customized approach | *Does a qualified independent party agree that these controls exist, operate and can be evidenced?* |
| **ASV — Sable Ridge Scanning Services** | **$28,000** | Four quarterly external scans plus rescans, with independence from the QSA practice documented under **ADR-0003** | *Is the external perimeter clean, four times a year, on somebody else's scanner?* |
| **Penetration testing — Ironwood Security Labs** | **$178,500** | The segmentation test, the segmentation re-test, the application test, retesting of remediated findings, and the second annual segmentation test of 2027-05-21 | *Does the boundary hold against somebody trying?* |

**The third line is the only one that answers a question about an adversary**, and it is the line that produced the programme's most expensive single finding: the failed segmentation test of May 2026, and behind it the **$77,000** of remediation across 37 stores. **An assessment tests controls against a standard; a penetration test tests an environment against somebody trying to get in**, and the two lines are not substitutes at any price.

## 4. The Business-as-Usual Steady State

The programme closes on **2027-06-30** under **ADR-0033**. What follows it is a standing operation with a named owner for every recurring obligation.

| Dimension | Programme | Business as usual |
|---|---|---|
| **Resourcing** | **11.4 FTE planned · 11.9 average · 12.7 at peak, October 2026** | **4.2 FTE** |
| **Annual cost** | **$4,588,000** over 17.6 months | **$1,340,000 per year** |
| Governance | PCI Steering Committee **monthly**, chaired by Raymond Voss | **PCI Governance Forum, quarterly**, same chair |
| Working group | **Weekly** | **Fortnightly** |
| QSA checkpoint | **Monthly** from 2026-03 | **Quarterly** |
| Programme management | Owen Castellanos, 1.0 FTE dedicated to the programme | Owen Castellanos, permanent **Payment Card Compliance Manager** |
| 12.1.4 accountability | Naomi Bhatt | **Naomi Bhatt, retained** |
| Recurring obligations | Built as the work was done | **47 dated obligations** across 2027–28 |

**4.2 FTE is 35.3% of the 11.9 the programme averaged. $1,340,000 is 29.2% of what the programme spent.** Both are ratios derived from the figures above rather than independently budgeted, and both understate the difficulty, because **the controls do not know that the resourcing changed.**

A quarterly ASV scan takes the same effort in a year when nobody is coming as in a year when somebody is. Four POI inspection cycles across 1,914 terminals in 482 stores cost the same whether or not an assessor will observe 96 of them. Fourteen targeted risk analyses must be reviewed annually whether or not anyone will read them. **The obligations are the same size; the population watching them is a third of what it was.**

**09.08** is the document that makes this concrete: it names, obligation by obligation, which of the 47 are genuinely cheaper to run than to build, and which are not. Three general observations belong here because they are financial rather than operational.

| Observation | Why it is a financial statement |
|---|---|
| **Build cost and run cost are not proportional, and the ratio differs per obligation** | Segmentation cost $967,000 to build and costs one annual penetration test to maintain. Payment-page script governance cost far less to build and costs a monthly reconciliation, a weekly evaluation and a continuous monitoring subscription forever. **A programme that budgets BAU as a percentage of build will underfund the second class and overfund the first** |
| **The 86.9% evidence figure is a labour subsidy that expires** | 186 of 214 assessor requests were satisfied from artefacts that already existed, because seven phases of eleven people produced evidence as the work was done. At 4.2 FTE the incentive to produce evidence contemporaneously weakens, and the cost reappears as assessment-season labour. **It does not disappear; it moves** |
| **The one line that cannot be reduced is the one nobody wants to spend** | The assurance lines are the smallest share of the programme and the first place a run-rate discussion looks. They are also the only lines that produce an opinion Marketa did not write itself |

| Unit | Count | Cost basis |
|---|---|---|
| Assessed system components | **71** — 8 CDE, 63 connected-to | The population the standard was applied to |
| Systems believed in scope before analysis | **604** | The population the programme avoided applying it to |
| Enterprise estate scanned for account data | **1,842** systems, 41 network shares, 6 email archives | Scanned to prove absence, not because they were in scope |
| Stores | **482** across 38 states | 9.5.1 and segmentation obligations |
| POI terminals | **1,914** | Four inspection cycles a year |
| Payment channels | **3** — card-present, e-commerce, MOTO | Each with a different third party and a different control set |
| Third-party service providers | **6** | 12.8 governance, quarterly review, annual 12.8.4 validation |

**The 604 is the number that matters financially, and it never appears in a budget.** It is the population the programme did not have to assess, and the difference between assessing 604 components and assessing 71 is not a 8.5-fold difference in cost — it is a difference in kind, because 604 in-scope components would have meant every store back-office server inside the cardholder data environment, with configuration standards, authenticated scanning, log review, access control and change management applied to each of them, in 482 buildings, forever.

## 6. What the Money Bought, and What It Did Not

### 6.1 What it bought

**A scope reduction from 604 to 71, and the reduction is permanent in a way the controls are not.** Through P2PE at the terminal, tokenization at Truvance and DTMF masking at Cadence, the population of systems that must meet the standard fell by **88.2%**. At an average of $14,993 per applicable sub-requirement, the arithmetic of not having to apply the standard is the strongest financial argument in this document. **Phase 02 ran before Phases 04 to 07 for this reason and not for convenience**, and the charter's first principle — *the cheapest control is the one that is not needed because the system holds no account data* — is the only principle in the programme with a dollar figure attached to it.

The qualification is stated because it is a live financial exposure. **The reduction rests on three third parties' validation status.** Verition's P2PE solution must remain listed by the Council; Truvance and Cadence must remain validated Level 1 service providers. If any of those lapses, the scope reduction that depends on it lapses with it, and Marketa acquires an assessment population it has never costed. **That is the concentration recorded at R-09 and it does not reduce.**

**A validated position, twice stated.** Non-Compliant at 2026-12-11 and Compliant at 2027-02-18, both filed, neither withdrawn. What the money bought is not a certificate — there is no PCI DSS certification for a merchant — but a Qualified Security Assessor's opinion across **306 applicable sub-requirements**, **71 components**, **482 stores**, **1,914 terminals**, **15 business days of on-site fieldwork**, **1,654 indexed evidence artefacts**, **47 interviews across 39 individuals**, **61 process observations** and **34 independent testing actions performed by the assessor itself**. Marketa can put that in front of Cardinal Merchant Bank, and it did, twenty days early.

**An evidence base that answered 86.9% of assessor requests from artefacts that already existed.** **186 of 214** requests were satisfied without new production; **28** required it, and those 28 are point-in-time observations nobody can pre-produce — a live configuration read, a witnessed restore, a witnessed POI inspection. **Zero requests went unsatisfied.** This is the most direct financial evidence the programme produced that building evidence as the work is done is cheaper than assembling it in November, and it is the figure **09.07 §3.4** treats as an uncomfortable lesson precisely because it will not recur on its own.

**Four unexpected PAN locations found and removed**, each of which drove a 12.10.7 procedure improvement: approximately 11,400 call recordings containing spoken account data from before DTMF masking, 2,187 scanned dispute documents, an application debug log on a decommissioned host, and a store manager's emailed spreadsheet holding 34 records. Every one of them existed before the programme started, and none of them was known.

**A defect at 37 stores found in May rather than in November**, and remediated for **$77,000** with four months of margin before the seasonal freeze.

### 6.2 What it did not buy

**It did not buy an incident response capability proved against a real event.** Marketa has an incident response plan at version 3.0, a 24/7 declaration authority, a tested notification path, a severity model, an annual test under 12.10.2, a tabletop designed by an outsider that ran **9 hours 20 minutes** against an assumed four, and **512 incident records with 5 SEV-2 and 0 SEV-1**. It has never handled a confirmed compromise of account data. **R-43 has not moved in any phase of this programme**, and the board scorecard measure for it — **BSC-12** — is red and stays red under **ADR-0035**.

No part of the $4,588,000 could have bought this, and that is the point rather than a criticism of the spend. **An assessment tests controls against a standard. It does not test an environment against an adversary.** The capability that would matter most on the worst morning is the one with the least evidence behind it, and no line item corrects that.

**It did not buy a contractual commitment from Truvance.** Marketa's obligation **C5** requires notification of a suspected or confirmed compromise to Cardinal within **24 hours**. Determining whether account data was reached, in a tokenized estate, requires Truvance to resolve tokens against the mapping only Truvance holds. **OI-07-01** asked for a contractual response-time commitment on that resolution, at renewal, with the General Counsel leading. **Truvance declined.**

Money did not solve it and could not have. Marketa is one merchant negotiating with the provider that operates the single token vault across all three of its channels; the commercial leverage runs the other way, and the alternative — moving the vault — is a multi-year architectural programme whose own cost would dwarf this one. What Marketa has instead is an operational runbook, named escalation contacts and a tested path. **What it does not have is an obligation**, and the item is not closed, not re-worded and not quietly reclassified. It becomes **BAU-01**, and it appears on the board scorecard as **BSC-09**, amber.

**It did not buy the removal of three compensating controls.** CCW-01 (8.3.6), CCW-02 (2.2.7) and CCW-03 (3.4.2) are live at close, and **none of the three has a vendor roadmap behind it.** The **$44,000** remediation removed the 11.3.1.2 finding by obtaining a contract amendment; it did not change a single byte of appliance firmware. The nine appliances still cap local passwords at ten characters and still offer no management protocol with strong cryptography. **The remediation removed the finding, not the constraint**, and the residual at **R-36** changed shape from technical to contractual rather than disappearing. Route B — replacement of the appliance class — is a capital decision carried into business as usual as **BAU-02**, and it has not been funded.

**It did not buy certainty about 2028.** **ADR-0036** plans the next assessment as though nothing carries forward, because nothing does. The twelve derived testing procedures **CTP-1 to CTP-12** are Sable Ridge's and the next assessor derives its own. All eighteen sampling rationales must be demonstrated again, to the assessor doing the sampling. All three compensating controls can be re-argued and can move. All four Not Applicable determinations can be refused, and the three that were refused in 2026 can be refused again. **The 51 assessor hours that 8.3.9's customized approach consumed in 2026 will be spent again, from zero, by somebody who has never seen the argument.**

### 6.3 The honest ledger

| The money bought | The money did not buy |
|---|---|
| A scope reduction from **604 to 71** — but resting on three third parties' validation status | An incident response capability proved against a real compromise of account data |
| A validated position, twice stated and neither withdrawn | A contractual response-time commitment from **Truvance** |
| An evidence base answering **86.9%** of assessor requests from existing artefacts | The removal of any of the three compensating controls |
| Four unexpected PAN locations found, removed and evidenced | A change to the firmware on nine appliances |
| A segmentation defect at 37 stores found in May for **$77,000** | Any assurance that carries into the 2028 assessment |
| **0 High** risks across a 44-entry register | The three Moderate entries the close forecast promised would be Low |

### 6.4 What it bought that does not appear in any line

Three outcomes of the programme have no budget line and are not attributable to a spend, and each is worth more than several lines that are.

**A refusal that was priced.** Two compensating-control arguments were available and were refused, in June and July 2026, before anybody knew what refusing them would cost. The cost turned out to be **$44,000**, a non-compliant filing and five months of tail. **A programme that has never refused a compensating control has not established that its test is real**, and this one now has a receipt for its integrity that it can show to its own people the next time the argument comes round.

**Two risks raised on evidence after remediation was complete.** **R-14** was raised when the first segmentation test failed; **R-31** was raised when account data was found in call recordings, disproving the assumption that DTMF masking had eliminated spoken account data historically. Both were raised **while remediation was already running**, under **ADR-0005**, which was tested and declined nine times across the programme. Neither raise cost money. Both cost something harder to price, which is the willingness to make a register look worse in the month you are fixing the thing.

**An organisation that knows what its clean numbers are worth.** Awareness at 97.1% did not predict two agents who would have destroyed evidence. POI coverage at 99.56% did not predict two of nine failed identity tests. A green log-review dashboard did not predict a rule reporting itself healthy for **57 hours 5 minutes**. Every one was found by a second instrument, and the second instruments are all small, adversarial and cheap. **The most valuable thing the programme bought is the habit of pointing a second instrument at a number it likes.**

## 7. What the Cost Would Have Been Without the Scope Work

This programme cannot state that counterfactual as a number, and it does not.

What it can state is the shape. **604 systems believed in scope became 71 assessed components.** Had that analysis not run, or had it run and failed, the population subject to configuration standards, authenticated vulnerability scanning, daily log review, access control, change management, file integrity monitoring and annual assessment would have included **482 store back-office servers** and the network infrastructure behind them. Every one of the 306 applicable sub-requirements that attaches to a system component would have attached to each of them, in 482 buildings, under a seasonal change freeze that closes four months of every year to store-side change.

**No responsible figure is available for that, so none is given.** The observation that is available is the one at §5: the 604 never appears in a budget line, and it is the largest financial decision the programme made.

## 8. The Cost of Not Complying

This programme states no amount here, and the reason is not squeamishness.

**PCI DSS is contractual, not statutory.** The PCI Security Standards Council writes the standard and **does not enforce it**. Enforcement runs from the card brands to the acquirer and from the acquirer to the merchant. **Marketa's counterparty is Cardinal Merchant Bank, N.A.**, and Marketa's exposure arises under the merchant agreement rather than under any published schedule available to Marketa.

The exposure is therefore describable only as **categories**:

| Category | Description |
|---|---|
| **Brand-programme amounts assessed against the acquirer** | Assessed by the card brands against **Cardinal**, and passed through to Marketa under the merchant agreement's indemnity and pass-through provisions. **The amounts are set by the brands, are not published to merchants, and vary by programme, by circumstance and by negotiation** |
| **Forensic investigation** | A brand-mandated investigation by a PCI Forensic Investigator, engaged on the brand's terms and at the entity's cost — obligation **C10** |
| **Card reissuance and fraud recovery** | Amounts claimed by issuers through the brands' recovery processes, driven by the count of accounts placed at risk |
| **Operational and contractual consequence** | Increased validation obligations, additional reporting conditions of the kind Cardinal imposed on 2026-12-16, and in the extreme the acquirer's contractual remedies |
| **The consequence with no category** | **Reputational damage, and the loss of the ability to accept payment cards on current terms** |

**No amount is stated for any of these**, because Marketa does not hold those figures, could not verify them, and publishing an estimate would create a false precision that a board would then plan against.

What the programme *can* state with precision is one thing, and it is the only comparison in this document that carries a real warning: **the most expensive outcome available to a breached retailer is a forensic report concluding that requirements attested to as In Place were not in place.** That is not a compliance cost. It is a credibility cost, and it is the entire reason the two findings were reported rather than argued, the reason **ADR-0004** removed the Not Tested option in Phase 01, and the reason the **$44,000** re-assessment fee is the cheapest line in this table.

## 9. Standing Framing

Every figure in this document is a **cost of assurance**. **No fine, fee schedule or penalty amount is stated anywhere in this programme.** The **$44,000** re-assessment fee is a professional fee for assessor time, drawn against a contingency line that exists for exactly that purpose.

**PCI DSS is contractual, not statutory**; the Council writes the standard and does not enforce it; **there is no PCI DSS certification for a merchant**; a Report on Compliance and an Attestation of Compliance are a **point-in-time attestation about an assessed period** whose author's opinion binds neither Cardinal Merchant Bank nor any card brand; and **no Attestation of Compliance is a determination of compliance with any law.** Marketa is privately held, is not an SEC registrant, and has no SOX control population.
