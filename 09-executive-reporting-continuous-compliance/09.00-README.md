## What this phase is

The programme ends here. Phase 09 reports the assessment outcome to the Board, disposes of everything the assessment left open, hands every recurring obligation to a named person, and closes the risk register.

It also does the thing the previous eight phases were building the right to do: **it reports against a forecast the programme published in month seven, and reports that the forecast was missed.**

### Why it was missed — the arithmetic, which is exact

> **Under this register's own discipline — a rating moves on likelihood unless the consequence itself changed, and a likelihood of 1 means the event is not reasonably foreseeable — an entry scored 3 × 4 cannot reach Low. It reaches 2 × 4 = 8 and stops. Eight is a floor.**

| Line | Entries | Count |
|---|---|---|
| Forecast Moderate → Low, **delivered in Phase 06** | R-10, R-19, R-26, R-34 | 4 |
| Forecast Moderate → Low, **delivered in Phase 09** | R-08, R-18, R-21, R-22, R-23, R-32 | 6 |
| Forecast Moderate → Low, **not delivered** | **R-04, R-28, R-33** — all three scored **3 × 4** | **3** |
| Forecast High → **Low**, delivered to **Moderate 8** | **R-17, R-36** — both arrive at **2 × 4** | **2** |
| Not forecast to move, but reached Low | **R-13, R-15** | **+2** |
| **Net** | | **+3 → 16 Moderate** |

The forecast was written in **month seven of an eighteen-month programme**, before the scoring discipline had been tested against a single year of evidence, and it moved thirteen entries across a boundary the arithmetic does not permit them to cross.

**This is not a failure of the programme. It is a failure of a prediction — and the programme is reporting the prediction it published rather than the one it could have written afterwards.** 06.12 §7 forecast R-17 and R-36 reaching Low and 08.13 §6.3 corrected it; this phase corrects 03.12 §6. Two forecasts published in advance, two missed, both reported against.

**Four impact reductions here; seven in the whole programme, all named** — R-10 (Phase 06), R-14 and R-31 (Phase 07), R-23, R-08, R-18 and R-32 (Phase 09). The final phase carries four because **architecture changes consequences and operations change likelihood**, and the last four months are when the architectural changes landed: a contract clause, a data map, a dedicated workstation estate, and a daily assertion that bounds a window.

## The three things this phase will not tidy

**One open item did not close, because a provider said no.** Marketa asked **Truvance** — the provider on whom the entire e-commerce and tokenization scope reduction depends — for a contractual response-time commitment on incident-driven token resolution. **Truvance declined at renewal.** The programme cannot close **OI-07-01**, does not close it, and does not re-word it into something closable. It becomes **BAU-01** with a named owner and the next renewal date.

**One board measure is red, and stays red.** *Incident response capability proved against a real compromise of account data* — red because **it has never happened**. 512 incident records, 5 SEV-2, 0 SEV-1, no breach. **R-43 has not moved in any phase of this programme.** **ADR-0035** prevents the measure being retired as unmeasurable: a scorecard that goes all-green has stopped measuring something.

**Two open items closed late, and are reported as late.** OI-06-04 by **34 days** and OI-06-08 by **40**. Neither was re-dated. A table in which nothing is ever late is a table nobody has to read.

## What business as usual actually costs

The programme ran at **11.9 FTE**. Business as usual is **4.2**. Some of that difference is real — build work does not recur, and several controls genuinely cost less to run than to construct.

**Some of it is not.** **09.08 §6** names the obligations where the honest answer is that less will be done, rather than that the same will be done more efficiently. As the CFO put it at the transition meeting: *"I want the record to show that I asked what stops being done, and that I was given a list rather than a reassurance."*

**ADR-0033** closes the programme on a fixed date with a named business-as-usual owner for every recurring obligation — because a programme allowed to drift into business as usual is one nobody ever declares finished, and its obligations quietly become nobody's.

## The honest position

**Marketa Retail Group holds a compliant Attestation of Compliance dated 2027-02-18, and a non-compliant one dated 2026-12-11 that has not been withdrawn.** Both are on the record and both describe the periods they describe.

Four things this programme does not claim at its close:

**That compliance is a state.** It is a point-in-time attestation about an assessed period. **There is no PCI DSS certification for a merchant**, and the 2028 assessment resets the customized approach, all eighteen sampling rationales, all three compensating controls and all four Not Applicable determinations to zero.

**That the environment is secure.** An assessment tests controls against a standard; it does not test an environment against an adversary. **The programme's own instruments answered the second question and their answers were less comfortable** — a penetration tester reached an unauthenticated administrative interface that had been open for 176 days, tamper detection caught two of Marketa's own release failures on live payment pages, and a tabletop designed by somebody who wanted it to fail disproved a load-bearing assumption of the incident response plan.

**That the incident response capability works.** It has never been tested by the event it exists for. That is why one board measure is red and **R-43 has not moved in nine phases**.

**That the register reached its forecast.** It did not. **0 High · 16 Moderate · 28 Low against 0 · 13 · 31 — three entries short**, and 09.12 §3 publishes the arithmetic rather than the excuse.

**PCI DSS is contractual, not statutory.** The Council writes the standard and does not enforce it — enforcement runs from the brands to the acquirer to the merchant, and Marketa's counterparty is **Cardinal Merchant Bank, N.A.**, not Visa. **No Attestation of Compliance is a determination of compliance with any law.** Marketa is privately held, is not an SEC registrant, and has no SOX control population. **No fine, fee schedule or penalty amount is stated anywhere in this programme** — every figure quoted is a cost of assurance.
