## Context

An assessed entity reviews the draft Report on Compliance before it is issued. The purpose of that review is to catch errors of fact — a wrong count, a misnamed system, an owner who left in March. The review is also, in practice, the last opportunity an entity has to influence how it is described.

Marketa's draft ran to 741 pages and described two requirements it does not meet.

## Decision

**Marketa's review is limited to factual accuracy.** A factual error is a statement about Marketa that is wrong. A characterisation is the assessor's judgement about what a fact means. The first may be corrected; the second may not be touched.

Thirty-one comments were raised. **Twenty-four were accepted as factual corrections. Seven were declined**, and all seven sought to change characterisation rather than fact.

## Consequences

The report says things about Marketa in language Marketa would not have chosen, and that is the point. **A ROC an entity has edited for tone is a document about the entity rather than an assessment of it**, and its value to an acquirer, a brand or a future assessor falls to approximately nothing.

One consequence was uncomfortable enough to record separately: a proposed softening of the 8.6.2 language was raised in the review meeting and withdrawn in the same meeting, on the reasoning that if the assessor declined it Marketa would have created a record of trying to soften a finding — and if he agreed, something worse.
