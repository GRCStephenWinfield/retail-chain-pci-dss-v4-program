# ADR-0031 — The Non-Compliant AOC Is Filed Early, Not Late

## Context

Obligation **C3** requires the executed Attestation of Compliance to reach Cardinal Merchant Bank **on or before 31 December**. Marketa's attestation would record a non-compliant status with two requirements in Part 4.

There was time. Twenty days separated the report's issue from the deadline, and a programme minded to use them could have attempted a late compensating-control argument, a partial remediation, or a re-scoping exercise — any of which might have moved one finding and none of which would have moved both.

## Decision

**File on 2026-12-11**, the day the report is issued, twenty days ahead of the deadline, with an honest Part 4 and dated remediation.

## Consequences

Cardinal receives the position twenty days earlier than it is entitled to, and receives it in a form it can act on: two named requirements, one date, a monthly reporting commitment, and a re-assessment already booked. Cardinal acknowledged on 2026-12-16 and required monthly remediation status until the re-assessment.

The alternative was priced honestly in the Steering Committee: **an attestation filed on 31 December after three weeks of unsuccessful argument arrives at the same status, later, with less credibility, and with a remediation plan written under deadline pressure.** Nothing about the position improves by being disclosed at the last legal moment.

There is a second-order consequence worth naming. **Filing early is only available to a programme that knew its position early.** Marketa knew in June and July respectively — this decision is cheap because two earlier and much harder ones were taken.
