## Purpose

Marketa Retail Group operates 482 stores, 1,914 point-of-interaction terminals, 71 assessed system components, 142 application and system accounts and a workforce of roughly 34,000 that grows by 6,800 every October. **No assessor examines all of it, and no assessment is expected to.**

Sampling is what makes a Level 1 merchant assessable. It is also the single mechanism by which an assessment can be quietly hollowed out, because a sample chosen badly — or chosen by the wrong party — produces a report about a curated subset and a conclusion about an estate.

This document records the sampling that Sable Ridge Assurance performed for the 2026 assessment: **eighteen populations**, the sample drawn from each, the percentage that represents, and the basis. It also records the four rules that governed the exercise, all of which are the assessor's rules rather than Marketa's, and the one population where **sampling was not permitted at all** — a refusal that told the assessor something about Marketa's environment before a single testing procedure had been executed.

The table in §6 is reproduced from the assessor's sampling rationale as recorded in Part I Section 3 of the Report on Compliance. **Marketa saw it for the first time when it was issued.**

## 1. What Sampling Is, and What It Is Not

| Sampling **is** | Sampling **is not** |
|---|---|
| A method of testing a **standardised** population efficiently, by examining a subset and reasoning about the whole | A method of reducing the assessment's workload to fit a schedule |
| Available only where the entity can **demonstrate** standardisation and centralised management | Available on the entity's assertion that its estate is uniform |
| Justified in writing by the assessor, per population, in the ROC | Justified by the fact that the population is large |
| A conclusion about the population, drawn from the sample, that the assessor is professionally accountable for | A conclusion about the sample only |
| Re-derived at every assessment | Inherited from the previous year's report |

The operative asymmetry is this: **the whole population is being assessed; only part of it is being examined.** If the sample fails, the requirement fails for the population — not for the sampled items. That is why the precondition in §2 matters more than the arithmetic in §6.

## 2. Standardisation and Centralised Management Must Be Demonstrated **Before** Sampling Is Permitted

This is the rule most frequently applied backwards. An entity presents a large population, the assessor samples it, and standardisation is inferred from the fact that the sampled items looked alike. That is a conclusion drawn from the sample being used to justify the sample.

The correct sequence, and the one Sable Ridge followed:

| Step | What the assessor did |
|---|---|
| **1. Establish the population** | Take the population from the entity's own register — the 71-component inventory, the 482-store estate register, the 142-account enumeration, the change record set — and reconcile it independently rather than accepting a count |
| **2. Test the claim of standardisation** | Examine the mechanism that makes the population uniform: the six Phase 04 baselines, the store build image and its six variants, the credential architecture, the change control process. **A claim of standardisation is a claim about a mechanism, and the mechanism is what gets tested** |
| **3. Test the claim of centralised management** | Establish that the uniformity is maintained centrally and that drift is detected — configuration management, the drift assertions, the monthly register reconciliation against four sources |
| **4. Only then, sample** | And record the basis in the ROC, per population |
| **5. Include items that test the claim** | Selecting only representative items tests the average. The sample deliberately included known-difficult cases — **store 0417**, both 8.6.2 accounts, components on all six baselines and the un-baselined SaaS control planes |

**Where step 2 or step 3 failed, no sample was drawn.** That is S-12, and §8 is about it.

### 2.1 What the assessor tested standardisation against

| Population | The standardisation mechanism examined | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Connected-to components (S-02) | **BL-WIN 18 · BL-LNX 15 · BL-NET 11 · BL-AWS 6 · BL-DB 3 · BL-APP 12**, plus 6 un-baselined SaaS control planes | Demonstrated per baseline group. The six un-baselined control planes were **not** sampled as a group and each was examined |
| Stores (S-04) | A single store build image with **six variants** by store format and age, deployed and maintained centrally, with the image itself a **SIG-10** significant change | Demonstrated. All six variants represented in the sample |
| POI terminals (S-05) | One P2PE-validated solution from one vendor, one PIM, one quarterly inspection procedure across all 1,914 | Demonstrated |
| Distribution centres (S-06) | One operating model, one network design, one inventory system | Demonstrated |
| Application and system accounts (S-13) | Four credential architectures, centrally governed, 93 of 142 vault-brokered and 31 holding no static credential | Demonstrated, and sampled **stratified** rather than uniformly |
| Change records (S-17) | One change control process, one significant-change definition **SIG-1 to SIG-10** used by three separate requirements | Demonstrated |
| **Vendor-locked appliances (S-12)** | **None available to examine.** §8 | **Not demonstrated** |

## 3. The Rationale Is the Assessor's, and It Is Written Down

PCI DSS obliges the assessor to document the sampling rationale in the ROC — the population, the sample size, the selection method, and why the sample is representative. It is not a note in a working paper. It is a section of the report a reader can disagree with.

| Element recorded per population | Why it is in the report rather than the file |
|---|---|
| The population and how its size was established | A sample percentage is meaningless without a defensible denominator. Three of the eighteen denominators were re-derived by the assessor rather than accepted |
| The sample size and how it was determined | So that a reader can judge whether 34 of 63 is a sample or a gesture |
| The selection method, including any stratification | **S-13, S-14, S-17 and S-18 are stratified.** Stratification that is not disclosed looks like selection bias |
| Why the sample is representative of the population | The standardisation and centralised-management argument, per population |
| Any deliberate inclusion | **Store 0417**; the two 8.6.2 accounts; the six un-baselined SaaS control planes |
| Where no sample was drawn, why | Small population, heterogeneous population, or standardisation not demonstrated |

## 4. The Entity Cannot Choose the Sample

Marketa supplied populations. Marketa did not supply selections.

| What Marketa provided | What Sable Ridge did with it |
|---|---|
| The 71-component register, the store estate register, the account enumerations, the change record set, the personnel record set | Reconciled each against an independent source before drawing from it — an assessor-directed enumeration for the account populations, the store estate register against the property schedule, the change set against the ticketing system's own export |
| Logistical constraints — trading hours, settlement windows, which store has a district manager on site | Accepted as scheduling input. **Rejected as selection input** |
| A proposal, made once early in planning, to nominate stores that had recently passed an internal inspection | **Declined.** Recorded as declined |
| The list of components carrying known interim measures | Used to ensure those components were **in** the sample, not out of it |

The declined proposal is worth recording rather than omitting. It was made in good faith — the stores in question were operationally tidy and the visits would have been efficient — and it was exactly the wrong thing to offer. **An entity that nominates its best sites has not saved the assessor time; it has invalidated the sample.**

Two further consequences follow, and both were stated in the engagement plan:

**Marketa was not told the store list in advance of the district-level schedule.** Individual store teams learned of the visit when the district schedule was issued, and were not briefed on what would be asked.

**Marketa could not remove an item from a sample.** One sampled component — a vendor-hosted control plane — was mid-way through a support case at the time of the store sweep, and Marketa asked whether it could be substituted. The answer was no, and the assessor examined it in the state it was in.

## 5. Where There Is No Sample

Sampling is a concession to size and uniformity. Remove either and it is unavailable.

| Condition | Populations | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Small population** | S-01 (8 CDE components), S-06's basis, S-07 (2 offices), S-09 (1 call centre), S-10 (6 templates) | Examined in full. Eight components is not a population you sample; it is a population you examine |
| **Heterogeneous population** | S-08 (2 data centres) | One owned, one co-located. **They are not the same control set**, and a sample of one would have tested whichever one was chosen and concluded about the other |
| **The inventory is itself the control** | S-11 (38 scripts), S-15 (24 role definitions) | 6.4.3 requires every script executing in the consumer browser on a payment page to be inventoried and authorised. **Sampling an inventory tests the sample, not the inventory** — a script absent from a sampled subset is exactly the script the requirement exists to catch |
| **One per zone** | S-16 (9 network security control rulesets) | Nine zones, nine rulesets, each a distinct configuration. Sampling would have left zones untested |
| **Standardisation not demonstrated** | **S-12 (9 vendor-locked appliances)** | **No sampling permitted.** §8 |

## 7. Notes on Individual Populations

### 7.1 S-01, S-02 and S-03 — the composite

S-03 is not an independent sample. It is the arithmetic combination of the eight CDE components examined in full and the thirty-four connected-to components sampled from sixty-three, and it exists in the table because the ROC states component coverage as a single figure. **59.2% of the assessed estate was examined directly**, and the reason it is not higher is that the connected-to population is genuinely standardised across six baselines with centralised configuration management and continuous drift assertion — the conditions under which sampling is permitted.

The thirty-four were drawn to cover every baseline group, both data centres, both cloud regions, and — deliberately — **all six un-baselined SaaS control planes**, which are the components with the weakest standardisation argument in the estate and were therefore examined individually rather than treated as a sampled group.

### 7.2 S-04 and S-05 — the store estate

Twenty-eight stores out of 482 is 5.8%, and the number that makes it defensible is not the percentage but the **six build variants**. Marketa's store estate is not 482 unique environments; it is one build image with six variants driven by store format and construction age, deployed and maintained centrally, where a change to the image is a **SIG-10** significant change affecting 482 networks at once.

| Selection dimension | How the 28 were spread |
|---|---|
| Build variant | All six represented |
| Geography | Clustered into three regional groups for travel, but spanning multiple states within each |
| Store format and age | Across the range, including the oldest variant, which carries the most legacy network equipment |
| Trading volume | High, medium and low volume sites, not only flagship stores |
| **Known-difficult site** | **Store 0417, Dayton OH** — §7.3 |

Ninety-six POI terminals were observed inside those 28 stores, which is an average of between three and four terminals per store against an estate average of 4.0 lanes per store. Terminal observation was performed at the lane, during trading, on the device as installed.

### 7.3 Store 0417, Dayton OH

Store 0417 is where the **first segmentation penetration test failed in May 2026**: a store back-office VLAN reachable from the guest Wi-Fi VLAN through a misconfigured trunk. It was remediated and the re-test in June 2026 passed.

**The assessor put it in the sample deliberately, and said so.** The reasoning recorded in the sampling rationale is that a site with a documented control failure is the site where the remediation can be tested rather than read about, and that an entity's remediation of a known failure is more informative about its control environment than the ninety-eight per cent of sites that never failed.

Marketa did not propose it, could not have excluded it, and would not have chosen it. **That is the entire argument for §4 compressed into one store.**

### 7.4 S-13 and S-14 — stratified account sampling

Neither account population was sampled uniformly, because a uniform sample of 142 accounts of which 31 hold no static credential at all would have been dominated by the least interesting class.

| Population | Strata | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **S-13** — 142 application and system accounts, 38 sampled | Four credential types: cloud workload identities holding no static credential; certificate and mTLS credentials; vault-brokered passwords; **embedded credentials** | The two embedded credentials — `batch-icq` on CDE-05 and `gl-extract` on CDE-04 — are **both in the sample**. A stratified sample over a stratum of two is a census of that stratum, and that is the correct treatment of the population carrying a Not in Place finding |
| **S-14** — 61 customized-approach accounts, 24 sampled | Stratified across the **19 components** carrying them, and across the five account classes P1 to P5 | Includes the P2 local fallback class, which exists to be used when central services are unavailable and is the class the customized approach's own analysis identifies as its most uncomfortable factor |

Note the distinction between the two rows and the customized approach itself: **CTP-6 in the derived procedure set examines the baseline confidence report for all 61 accounts, not a sample.** A sampling rate of 39.3% governs the assessor's account-level testing; it does not govern a procedure that the assessor derived on a full-population basis. The sample is a floor, not a ceiling.

### 7.5 S-17 — change records

Forty-five change records out of 1,109 in the period, stratified as **12 significant and 33 standard**. The twelve significant changes were drawn from the population classified significant under **SIG-1 to SIG-10**, which is the same definition driving 11.3.1.3 scanning and 11.4.5 segmentation testing — so a sampled change record carries three requirements' evidence at once.

The assessor tested two things per record: that the change followed the process, and that its **classification** was correct. The second is the more valuable test, because a change misclassified as standard never triggers the scope-delta review, the post-change scan or the segmentation test, and therefore never appears in any of the three populations an assessor would otherwise look at. **Zero exceptions were found across the 45.**

### 7.6 S-18 — personnel records

Seventy records from a population of 40,412 is 0.2%, and it is the smallest percentage in the table by two orders of magnitude. It is also the population where percentage is least informative, because the requirements being tested — **12.6.3** awareness and **12.7.1** screening — are satisfied by a process operating across a population, not by a property of each record.

| Stratum | Sampled | What it tested |
|---|---|---|
| Screening | 30 | 12.7.1 across three screening tiers and 38 states, including the enhanced tier applied to in-scope administrative personnel |
| Awareness | 40 | 12.6.3 completion, including role-based modules |
| Employment class | Permanent, **seasonal** and contractor represented in both strata | The seasonal intake is the population an annual assessment most easily misses, and November is when it exists |

Sampling the seasonal population in November is a scheduling decision that was made in Phase 01 and paid off here. **A seasonal workforce assessed in June is a workforce of zero.**

## 8. S-12 — The Population That Could Not Be Sampled

Nine vendor-locked POS back-office appliances, held under constraint **CON-03** since programme start. All nine examined. **No sampling permitted, and the reason is the finding.**

### 8.1 What the assessor asked for, and what he could not be given

| Step | Position |
|---|---|
| The assessor asked for the **build standard** for the appliance class | There is none Marketa holds. The build is the vendor's |
| The assessor asked for the **configuration baseline** the nine are maintained against | There is none. The appliances are not covered by any of the six Phase 04 baselines; they are the platform Marketa cannot configure |
| The assessor asked how **drift** from a known-good state is detected | It is not, in the sense the question means. Firmware version is observed from the management console monthly and reconciled against the vendor's current release. That establishes which release is running; it does not establish what state the appliance is in |
| The assessor asked for the **authenticated configuration read** he would use to compare two of the nine | Not available. Marketa holds no administrative credential for the appliances, and the support agreement provides neither credential release nor agent installation rights |
| **Conclusion** | **Standardisation could not be demonstrated by any mechanism Marketa controls.** Sampling is unavailable. All nine were examined by every method available — unauthenticated scan output, console-observed firmware version, physical inspection, network position, vendor advisory correspondence and the vendor's quarterly patch attestation |

### 8.2 What that told the assessor before any testing began

This is the point of the section and it deserves stating flatly.

**The refusal to permit sampling was not a consequence of the 11.3.1.2 finding. It preceded it, and it predicted it.**

Sampling requires the entity to demonstrate that the population is standardised and centrally managed. Marketa could demonstrate neither — not because the appliances are inconsistent, but because **Marketa has no instrument that could tell it whether they are.** The same absence produces both results:

| The sampling refusal says | The 11.3.1.2 finding says |
|---|---|
| Marketa cannot demonstrate what state these nine devices are in | Marketa cannot enumerate what is installed on these nine devices |
| Therefore the assessor must examine all nine | Therefore authenticated scanning is incomplete on nine of 71 components |

**Both statements have the same cause: an administrative credential Marketa does not hold, on nine devices, under a support agreement nobody negotiated for.**

An assessor who is told on day one of planning that a population cannot be sampled has been told that the entity's visibility into that population is deficient. Everything that follows — the Not in Place finding at 11.3.1.2, the two compensating control worksheets at 8.3.6 and 2.2.7, the two customized-approach account classes that cannot be gated, the unknown Medium and Low vulnerability population that TRA-11.3.1.1 explicitly declines to rank — is the same nine devices arriving in a different requirement.

**08.07 §8 makes the same observation from the other end of the assessment.** Two of the three accepted compensating controls and one of the two Not in Place findings trace to these nine devices and this one support agreement. The sampling methodology saw it first.

### 8.3 What examining all nine achieved

| Achieved | Not achieved |
|---|---|
| Every appliance physically inspected, its network position verified, its console-visible configuration observed, its unauthenticated scan output examined | An enumeration of installed software on any of the nine |
| The management VLAN isolation tested from a position the assessor selected | Any assurance about the unpatched state of the appliance firmware |
| The break-glass authentication controls in **CCW-01** observed on the devices they apply to | A demonstration that the nine are configured alike |
| The 10-character firmware password cap on the **built-in local administrative account** of each of the nine verified by attempted set, rather than accepted from a vendor datasheet | Anything that would have made sampling available |

**Examining a population in full is not the same as understanding it.** Nine of nine were examined and the assessor's conclusion about their internal state is that it is not observable — which is precisely what a Not in Place finding at 11.3.1.2 records.

## 9. What the Sampling Does Not Establish

| Claim | Position |
|---|---|
| That the unsampled 454 stores are compliant | **No.** The conclusion is that the population is compliant, drawn from a sample of a demonstrably standardised population. The assessor is professionally accountable for that inference; it is not a certainty about any individual unvisited store |
| That 5.8% is a strong store sample | It is a defensible sample **because of the build standardisation**, not because 28 is a large number. Remove the single centrally-maintained build image with six variants and the same 28 stores would support nothing |
| That the sample percentages are comparable across populations | They are not. 0.2% of personnel records and 100% of payment page scripts are the correct treatments of two populations with nothing in common |
| That a sample can substitute for an inventory | **No** — S-11 and S-15 exist to make the point. Where the requirement is that a complete inventory exists and is authorised, the inventory is the control and the whole of it is the evidence |
| That the same sample would be drawn next year | No. Sampling is re-derived at every assessment, by whichever assessor performs it, and **the 2028 assessor rotation will re-derive it from scratch** |
| That Marketa influenced any selection | The one proposal Marketa made was declined and is recorded as declined in §4 |
