## Purpose

**One requirement out of 306 was met by the customized approach**, and this document records what happened when it was assessed.

Requirement **8.3.9** applies where a password is the only authentication factor for user access. Marketa's population is **61 component-local accounts on 19 of the 71 assessed system components**, held by **17 named individuals** — a residual population, because 8.4.2's multi-factor authentication on all access into the cardholder data environment removes password-only user access to all eight CDE components. Neither defined-approach option was adopted: 90-day rotation was rejected on measured evidence that it reduced credential strength on this population by roughly half, and the dynamic-analysis option was rejected because **51 of the 61 accounts authenticate locally in firmware with no external decision point**, so no real-time access determination exists to claim.

The division of labour is the thing that makes a customized approach credible, and it held throughout.

| Party | Contribution |
|---|---|
| **Marketa Retail Group** | The **Appendix E1 controls matrix** — CAC-1 to CAC-8 — and the **12.3.2 targeted risk analysis**, approved by the Chief Information Security Officer |
| **Sable Ridge Assurance, LLC** | The **testing procedures — CTP-1 to CTP-12** — derived independently and documented in **Appendix E2**, then executed at fieldwork |

**The entity does not write its own test.** Marketa supplied what it built and what it believed about it; Sable Ridge determined what would have to be true for the Customized Approach Objective to be met, and how each of those things would be tested.

**Result: In Place via the customized approach.** Two of the twelve procedures required a second evidence round.

## 1. The Objective, and What It Governs

Requirement 8.3.9's Customized Approach Objective, quoted unchanged from 01.05 §7 and from 05.06 §6:

> **"Access by user accounts cannot be compromised via the use of a known or guessed password."**

Three properties of that wording governed the whole assessment, and Sable Ridge's derived procedures map to them.

| Property | Consequence for the testing |
|---|---|
| It is about **compromise of access**, not about a password's age | No procedure tests rotation. Rotation is one mechanism among several and the objective does not privilege it |
| It covers **known** and **guessed** — two distinct threat paths | Credential *disclosure* and credential *derivation* are tested separately. **CTP-4 and CTP-10 address the first; CTP-3, CTP-8 and CTP-5 address the second** |
| It says **cannot be compromised**, not *is less likely to be* | The bar is an outcome, so the procedures include the assessor performing the compromise attempts himself rather than examining records of Marketa performing them |

### 1.1 The population the assessor confirmed

**CTP-1 exists to establish that the population is what Marketa says it is**, because a customized approach applied to an under-counted population is an approach applied to the wrong thing. The assessor directed an independent enumeration on all nineteen components and reconciled it to the register of 71.

| Class | Components | Accounts | Why a password is the sole factor at the component |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P1** | The 9 vendor-locked POS back-office appliances | **36** | Vendor firmware supports local password authentication only — no external authentication integration of any kind, and the support contract prohibits modification |
| **P2** | Local fallback accounts on six components | **12** | Accounts of last resort, used when the central authentication path is unreachable. **By definition they cannot depend on the path they exist to survive** |
| **P3** | A storage array management local realm | **4** | The running firmware release does not support external authentication integration |
| **P4** | Vendor support tiers on two vendor-hosted control planes | **6** | The provider's support-tier login sits outside Marketa's federation and the provider offers no multi-factor option on that tier |
| **P5** | A hypervisor management local realm | **3** | Local realm retained for cluster recovery; the SSO realm is the normal path and exists to be bypassed only when SSO is what has failed |
| **Total** | **19 of 71 components** | **61** | Held by **17 distinct individuals** |

The assessor found no password-only user account outside the declared scope, and confirmed under CTP-2 that the **145 federated identities** are genuinely excluded because multi-factor authentication is enforced on all twenty access paths — which is to say that 8.3.9's applicability condition genuinely fails for them rather than being argued away.

### 1.2 Why neither defined-approach option was available

Both rejections were made in Phase 05 on evidence, and the assessor examined the evidence rather than the conclusion.

| Defined-approach option | Marketa's position | What Sable Ridge examined |
|---|---|---|
| **Option A — change at least every 90 days** | Rejected on measured evidence. Under rotation, **71%** of changes on the P1 and P2 populations differed from their predecessor only by an incremented character, a case change or a single substitution; measured credential strength was roughly **half** what it is now; **4 of 61** credentials matched a breached corpus, against 0 today; and help-desk-assisted resets across the enterprise fell by **84%** when rotation stopped | The two offline credential audits, the corpus screening records, the service desk volumes and the physical inspection findings — the measurements themselves, not the argument built on them |
| **Option B — dynamic posture analysis with automatic real-time access determination** | Rejected because **51 of the 61 accounts authenticate locally in firmware with no external decision point.** The analytics observe the authentication after it has been admitted; they do not gate it | The component-level authentication behaviour on sampled components in each class, confirming that no hook, callout or policy agent exists or can be inserted |

**Claiming Option B would have been the cheaper route and would have been false for 84% of the population.** The programme's record of refusing it is itself evidence the assessor examined, because an entity that would overclaim here would overclaim in its controls matrix.

## 2. The Appendix E1 Controls Matrix — CAC-1 to CAC-8

Supplied by Marketa. Assessed by Sable Ridge against the procedures in §3.

| ID | Implemented control | How it meets the objective | What the assessor examined | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **CAC-1** | **16-character minimum**, numeric and alphabetic, machine-generated on request, across all 61 accounts **in the appliance user realm and the equivalent local realms** — exceeding 8.3.6's 12-character floor | Addresses **guessing**. Raises the online and offline search space beyond feasible derivation for a targeted attacker | Enforced setting read on all 19 components; assessor-directed set-and-test on a disposable account at each; the two components that truncate above 32 characters examined against the material threshold | **Met.** 19 of 19 enforce **in the appliance user realm** |
| **CAC-2** | **Known-compromised credential screening** at the point of set, with a **daily** re-run of the full corpus against the stored population and forced reset on match | Addresses **known** passwords — the half of the objective rotation never touched | Job execution record for the full period; assessor-supplied corpus credential rejected at set; assessor-planted credential detected on the daily re-run and force-reset inside one business day | **Met.** 0 of 61 matched at any point |
| **CAC-3** | **Per-account behavioural baselining** — source address, source device, time of day, session duration, command profile, concurrency — with a risk score computed on every authentication event | Detects use by someone other than the holder, which is the realised form of a compromised password | Baseline confidence report for **all 61 accounts**, not a sample, across the full period; exception handling for any account below the confidence threshold | **Met — after a second evidence round.** CTP-7 |
| **CAC-4** | **Automatic session termination and account suspension** above the risk threshold, at every enforcement point Marketa controls | Bounds the value of a compromised password to a window of seconds. **Explicitly post-admission, and described as post-admission** | Assessor-performed anomalous authentication with measured intervals to detection, alert and termination; the three real threshold breaches in the operating period | **Met — after a second evidence round.** CTP-11 |
| **CAC-5** | **Spray and stuffing detection across the whole population** — a correlation rule firing on distributed low-rate failures against multiple identifiers, below any single account's lockout threshold | Addresses the guessing path that per-account lockout is structurally unable to see | Assessor-generated distributed failure pattern across identifiers he selected, at a rate below the lockout threshold; log source health for the test window | **Met** |
| **CAC-6** | **Network reachability constraint** — all 19 components reachable only from a brokered session or a named management subnet | An attacker must already hold a position that itself required multi-factor authentication. **Carries more of the objective than any other control in the matrix** | Assessor-attempted reach from two positions **he selected**; boundary logs; the annual segmentation test evidence; the monthly reachability probes | **Met**, with the conditionality recorded — this is the control class that failed at store 0417 in May 2026 |
| **CAC-7** | **Rotation on event, not on calendar** — forced change on any indicator of compromise, corpus match, holder departure or role change, suspected disclosure, or completion of a third-party engagement touching the account | Retains rotation's one genuine benefit — invalidating a credential that may be known — without the four measured pathologies of calendar rotation | All **14** event-driven rotations in the period examined individually: trigger, elapsed time, and component-side evidence that the change occurred. Qualifying triggers that produced no rotation searched for | **Met.** 14 of 14; no qualifying trigger without a rotation |
| **CAC-8** | **Quarterly offline credential-strength audit** under dual control, with artefacts destroyed under 9.4.7 | Measures the population's actual strength instead of assuming the policy produced it. Also the compensating measure for the 3 components that cannot enforce password history natively | Both audit reports; the compute budget applied; the dual-control record; the Halberd certificate of destruction; interview on methodology and on what result would have triggered escalation | **Met.** 0 of 61 recovered at a 72-hour compute budget |

### 2.1 How the eight combine

| Half of the objective | Controls | The argument the assessor tested |
|---|---|---|
| **"…via the use of a known password"** | CAC-2, CAC-7, CAC-8, CAC-3 | A password becomes known through corpus exposure, disclosure or reuse. CAC-2 tests every credential against the corpus daily — a test rotation never performs. CAC-7 invalidates on any indicator. CAC-8 verifies empirically that the population is not derivable. CAC-3 catches use by a person who is not the holder |
| **"…via the use of a guessed password"** | CAC-1, CAC-5, CAC-6, plus 8.3.4 lockout | CAC-1 removes derivation as a practical path. CAC-5 sees the distributed attack lockout is blind to. CAC-6 requires the attacker to already be somewhere that required multi-factor authentication before a single guess is possible |
| **"cannot be compromised"** — the outcome bar | CAC-4, CAC-6, CAC-3 | For **51 of 61 accounts admission cannot be gated**, so a correct password admits a session for a measured interval before termination. The claim is that reaching a position to try a password requires multi-factor authentication, that the password is neither derivable nor known, and that use by the wrong party is detected and terminated in seconds |

**The residual is stated in the matrix rather than argued away**, and Sable Ridge's assessment says so: the objective is met by a combination whose weakest limb is a post-admission window, and the entity's own documentation describes it as post-admission.

## 4. The Two Procedures That Required a Second Evidence Round

Ten of the twelve were satisfied from evidence produced during the first pass. Two were not, and both failures were evidentiary rather than control failures — which is a distinction worth making carefully, because it is exactly the distinction an entity is tempted to make when it is not true.

### 4.1 CTP-7 — behavioural baseline coverage across all 61 accounts

| Element | Position |
|---|---|
| What the procedure requires | The baseline confidence report for **all 61 accounts**, across the whole operating period — not a sample |
| What Marketa produced first | The baseline confidence position for the accounts within the **S-14 sample of 24**, drawn from the assessor's own sampling of the population |
| Why that was insufficient | **The sample is a floor on the assessor's testing, not a ceiling.** Sable Ridge derived CTP-7 on a full-population basis deliberately: an account without a usable baseline is an account outside the control, and a sampled report cannot identify one. Sampling a coverage question answers a different question |
| What was produced on the second round | Per-account monthly baseline confidence for all 61 across the full period, the degradation exception records, and the handling of every account that fell below the threshold at any point |
| Outcome | **Passed.** All 61 carried a usable baseline; the exception handling was evidenced |
| The lesson recorded | This is the same error as one of the six re-requests in 08.03 §5.3 — **a sample supplied where a population was asked for** — and it happened in the one place where the procedure had said "all 61" in writing since June |

### 4.2 CTP-11 — the response action when a score breaches threshold

| Element | Position |
|---|---|
| What the procedure requires | The configured response action, **and real instances of it firing**, traced from score to alert to action to closure — plus the degraded-state position |
| What Marketa produced first | The configured action set, the alert definition, and the four controlled quarterly tests in which the assessor's own or Marketa's own anomalous authentications were detected and terminated |
| Why that was insufficient | A controlled test proves the mechanism can fire. **It does not prove what the organisation does when it fires unexpectedly**, which is the response action the procedure asks about. Marketa could show the alert; it could not, initially, show an instance of the alert firing outside a test and being worked |
| What was produced on the second round | **Three real firings from the operating period**, each traced end to end |
| Firing 1 | A false positive, cleared within the hour |
| Firing 2 | A false positive, cleared within the hour |
| Firing 3 | **A genuine credential-sharing case** — an account used by a person who was not its holder. Session terminated, account suspended, credential rotated under CAC-7, and the matter handled as a personnel and access issue rather than as a technical one |
| Outcome | **Passed** |

The third firing is the most valuable single piece of evidence in this document, and it is worth saying why. 05.06 §10 recorded 23 anomalous events in the measurement window with **zero genuine security concerns**, and stated plainly that this is not evidence the control works — a detection control that has never seen a real event has an unmeasured true-positive rate. **CTP-11's second round produced the first genuine true positive in the control's history**, and it produced it because an assessor's independently derived procedure asked for something the entity's own matrix had not thought to evidence.

That is the argument for the customized approach in one sentence: **the entity describes what it built, the assessor decides what would have to be true, and the gap between the two is where the real finding lives.** The July dry run made the same point in the opposite direction, producing a finding against Marketa when the degraded-state position for the local fallback account class turned out never to have been documented.

| Field | Position |
|---|---|
| Requirement | **8.3.9** — password change frequency where a password is the only authentication factor for user access |
| Approach | **Customized**, under 12.3.2 and Appendix D |
| Population | **61 accounts on 19 of the 71 assessed components**, held by **17 named individuals**. No CDE component is in scope of the approach |

| Fallback if it had failed | **None.** A customized approach cannot be combined with a compensating control for the same requirement. Failure would have been a third Not in Place finding |

### 5.1 How it is recorded in the Report on Compliance

A customized approach is not recorded by a sentence saying the requirement was met differently. It occupies its own structure inside the report, and the structure is what makes it reviewable by somebody who was not in the room.

| Location in the ROC | Content |
|---|---|
| Part II, Requirement 8, at sub-requirement 8.3.9 | The disposition — **In Place** — with the approach identified as **customized** and a cross-reference to Appendix E |
| **Appendix E1** | The controls matrix **as supplied by Marketa**, unedited by the assessor: the implemented controls, how each meets the objective, what could undermine each, and the entity's own testing and results |
| **Appendix E2** | The testing procedures **as derived by Sable Ridge**, with the method, the evidence obtained and the result for each of the twelve |
| Part I Section 3 | The scope description recording that one requirement of 306 was met by the customized approach, and the population it applies to |
| Part I Section 6 | The evidence references for the customized approach, tagged by class alongside the rest of the 1,654 |
| The 12.3.2 analysis | Referenced from the Requirement 12 section as well as from Appendix E, because 12.3.2 is itself an assessed sub-requirement |

**Keeping E1 and E2 in separate appendices is not a formatting choice.** It is the physical expression of the division of labour: the entity's account of what it built sits next to, and is not merged with, the assessor's account of what he tested. A reader can compare the two and see where they diverge — which is exactly what happened at CTP-11, where the assessor's procedure asked for something the entity's matrix had not thought to evidence.

## 6. What It Cost — 51 Hours Against 46

Phase 05 estimated **46 additional assessor hours per year** for the customized approach at fieldwork. **The actual figure for 2026 was 51.**

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Phase 05 estimate — 05.06 §11 | **46 assessor hours** |
| Actual, 2026 fieldwork | **51 assessor hours** |
| Variance | **+5 hours, +10.9%** |
| Assessor hours **before** fieldwork, to derive and agree the procedures | ≈ 18, across two checkpoints, as estimated and not re-measured here |
| Comparison — 8.3.9 under the defined approach | ≈ 3 hours |

**The variance is recorded because the 2027 budget uses it**, and because an estimate that is never compared to an actual is a number that will be repeated indefinitely.

### 6.1 Where the five hours went

| Cause | Effect |
|---|---|
| **CTP-7's second evidence round** | Full-population baseline evidence for 61 accounts across the period, re-examined after the first response was judged insufficient |
| **CTP-11's second evidence round and its extension** | The extended procedure was not in the June estimate; obtaining and tracing three real firings end to end was work the original derivation did not contemplate |
| The remainder | Within the estimate. Ten of twelve procedures were executed once and closed |

### 6.2 What the variance says about the estimate

It says the estimate was good and the shape of the overrun was predictable. **Both extra hours came from the two procedures that required a second round, and both second rounds came from an evidence gap rather than a control gap.** An entity that wants to close the variance in 2027 does not need better controls; it needs to hold full-population baseline evidence in the form the procedure asks for, and to maintain a traced record of every threshold breach as a matter of routine.

The 2027 budget therefore carries **51 hours, not 46**, and the recurring entity-side cost stated in 05.06 — roughly 62 hours a year to refresh the analysis, the matrix, the evidence architecture and the derivation — is unchanged.

## 7. The Limitation the QSA Recorded

Sable Ridge recorded a limitation on the determination, and it is reproduced here in full rather than summarised.

> **The Customized Approach Objective was met for the assessed period. A customized approach carries no forward assurance.**

| Element | Position |
|---|---|
| What the determination covers | The assessed period, on the evidence obtained, against procedures derived for this implementation |
| What it does not cover | Any period after **2026-12-11**. **An In Place determination on a customized approach is not a licence to continue** |
| Re-argument | The approach must be **re-argued at every assessment**, not merely re-evidenced. The targeted risk analysis is refreshed, the controls matrix is refreshed, senior management re-approves, and the procedures are re-derived |
| **The 2028 assessor rotation** | Grant Whitfield derived these twelve procedures. **A successor QSA is not bound by them and will derive their own — from scratch.** That is correct: the derivation is the assessor's professional judgement, not a Marketa artefact |
| The consequence of the rotation | A fresh derivation may ask questions this one did not, and may reach a different view of whether the objective is met. The July dry run and CTP-11's extension both demonstrate that an independently derived procedure finds things the entity's own matrix did not anticipate |
| The four conditions | The analysis makes the approach conditional on four things remaining true — constrained network reachability, usable per-account baselines, daily corpus screening, and a population not exceeding 80 accounts or 25 components. **Any one failing is a trigger for immediate re-analysis, not a matter for the annual review** |
| The dependency the programme will not smooth over | **CAC-6 carries more of the objective than any other control in the matrix, and it is the same class of control that failed at store 0417 in May 2026.** The approach depends on a control the programme has already seen fail once |

**A customized approach is a rental, not a purchase.** It is re-argued annually, it has no fallback, its cost recurs, its testing is not portable between assessors, and the entity that adopts one has accepted that a disagreement about whether an objective is met is a Not in Place finding with no intermediate outcome available.

## 8. What This Assessment Does Not Establish

| Claim | Position |
|---|---|
| That the objective is achieved absolutely | **No.** For 51 of 61 accounts admission cannot be gated, and the residual post-admission window is measured, reported and recorded in both the matrix and the ROC |
| That the customized approach is stronger than rotation in general | **No.** It is stronger for **this** population — small, named, individually baselined, behind a tested reachability constraint. Remove any of those properties and the argument does not hold |
| That the determination carries into 2027 | **No.** §7. The determination is for the assessed period |
| That three real firings prove the detection rate | No. Three firings, two of them false positives, is the first genuine operating evidence the control has produced. **It is a beginning, not a base rate** |
| That the entity influenced the testing | The procedures are Sable Ridge's, derived independently, extended by the assessor without Marketa's agreement being sought, and executed by the assessor. Marketa reviewed them for factual accuracy about its environment and **made no request to soften any of them** |
| That a customized approach is a lighter route | **It is the heaviest route available.** 51 assessor hours against approximately 3 for the defined approach, roughly 62 recurring entity hours a year, no compensating-control fallback, and no portability. **One out of 306 is the honest ratio** |
