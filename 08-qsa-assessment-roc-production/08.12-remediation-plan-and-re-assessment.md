## Purpose

The Attestation of Compliance was filed **Non-Compliant** on **2026-12-11** with two requirements Not in Place and a Part 4 target date of **2027-01-31**. This document records what was done between those dates, how it was verified, what the re-assessment on **2027-02-18** covered, what it concluded, what it cost, and — the sentence the whole phase turns on — **what the remediation did not change.**

Two corrective action plans were executed. One was an engineering sequence bounded by a vendor release cycle. The other was a contract negotiation with a replacement programme running behind it in case the negotiation failed. **Neither was certain when the AOC was filed**, which is why the filing said 2027-01-31 rather than *shortly*.

**The numbering is out of order, and the reason is worth a sentence.** The 8.6.2 remediation became a *corrective action plan* only when the Attestation's Part 4 was drafted in December; by then **CAP-05** — the legacy pre-shared key programme raised in Phase 03 — and **CAP-06** — 11.3.1.2, raised in Phase 06 at the moment the finding was recorded — already existed, so the 8.6.2 plan takes **CAP-07** and supersedes open item **OI-06-02** — itself the Phase 06 successor to OI-05-01. **The out-of-order numbering is itself evidence that the 11.3.1.2 finding was recorded before this one was formalised**, even though 8.6.2 was identified first: the earlier finding was carried as an open item with a dated plan for five months and was only converted into a numbered corrective action plan when the form demanded one.

**Row 8 is the row a remediation plan usually omits.** Six interim measures were built around two credentials that no longer exist in a file. Three of them address a class of exposure that survives the fix and were kept; one addressed the specific defect and was retired. **A remediation that leaves every interim measure in place has not been finished, it has been abandoned in the safe position**, and the maintenance burden of unnecessary controls is paid every year afterwards.

**Milestone 5 is the milestone that mattered.** For the whole of 2026 the programme could describe what the nine appliances disclosed and what release they ran, and could not describe what was installed and unremediated inside them. The first authenticated scan closed a blind spot that had been recorded, ranked and reported since programme start, and it produced findings — which is the expected result, because a population nobody has been able to scan properly is not a population that has been clean.

### 3.2 Route B — the platform replacement

| Field | Position |
|---|---|
| What it is | Replacement of the vendor-locked back-office appliance class across the store estate |
| Status at the filing | **Costed, scoped and held as the fallback** if the negotiation did not deliver the term |
| Was it needed for the finding? | **No.** Route A closed 11.3.1.2 on 2027-01-30 |
| Was it dropped? | **No. Retained on the 2027 roadmap** |
| Why | **A control that depends on a contract clause has a renewal date.** The amendment secures credential release for the term of the agreement; it does not secure it forever, and the next renewal is another negotiation |

### 3.3 Why both routes were run

| Reason | Statement |
|---|---|
| **Neither was certain** | The amendment required a counterparty to agree to something it had declined to include at the original signing. Marketa held no right to compel it and knew that in December |
| **The date had no float** | **Eighteen days** separate the remediation date from the re-assessment. A negotiation that failed in the third week of January leaves no time to start anything |
| **A costed fallback is leverage** | A replacement programme that is scoped and priced changes the conversation from *please* to *this is what we will do instead*. **The fallback's existence is part of why Route A succeeded** |
| **The constraint outlives the finding** | Route A removes the finding. It does not remove the dependency on a commercial relationship, and the fallback is the only thing that does |
| **CON-03 was recorded at programme start** | The constraint was known in Phase 01, ranked in Phase 04, produced a finding in Phase 06, and carried two compensating controls into the report. **A constraint with that history deserves a structural answer, not only a contractual one** |

## 4. The Sentence the Phase Turns On

> **The remediation removed the finding, not the constraint.**

The amendment gave Marketa the right to obtain credentials and install an agent. **It did not change the firmware.**

| What changed | What did not |
|---|---|
| Marketa can now perform authenticated internal vulnerability scanning on all nine appliances, and has | The appliance firmware still caps local passwords at **10 characters**, against 8.3.6's minimum of **12**, with no vendor roadmap to change it |
| **11.3.1.2 is In Place** | **8.3.6 is still met only by compensating control — CCW-01 survives** |
| The vulnerability state of nine components is now observable | The management interface still supports **no protocol offering strong cryptography**, so **2.2.7 is still met only by compensating control — CCW-02 survives** |
| The finding closes and **R-36** moves | The dependency on a single vendor's support agreement is unchanged, and the next renewal is a live event |

| Measure | At the 2026 assessment | At the 2027 re-assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Compensating controls | **3** — CCW-01, CCW-02, CCW-03 | **3** — CCW-01, CCW-02, CCW-03 |
| Not in Place | **2** | **0** |
| Requirements met by compensating control on the nine appliances | **2** | **2** |

**The compensating-control count does not fall, and it was never going to.** An entity reporting that its remediation programme reduced its findings to zero *and* its compensating controls to zero has usually mistaken a contractual accommodation for an engineering change. **Marketa's nine appliances are the same nine appliances**, running the same firmware, on the same support agreement, in the same 482 stores. What changed is that Marketa can now see inside them.

### 5.1 Why sixteen and not two

The obvious re-assessment scope is the two findings. Sable Ridge declined it, and the reasoning is the most useful methodological point in this document: **a remediation is a change, and a change to a CDE component is exactly the event the rest of the standard exists to govern.** Fixing 8.6.2 required two deployments to settlement-path components inside a change freeze. Fixing 11.3.1.2 introduced privileged credentials for nine previously unmanaged appliances and installed an agent on each. Both remediations could have broken something.

The fourteen were selected by the assessor, not proposed by Marketa, from four areas:

| Area | Why it could have been affected |
|---|---|
| **The surrounding application-and-system-account population under 8.6** | Two accounts changed their authentication mechanism. The interactive-use governance, the inventory, and the rotation regime around them all had to be re-evidenced in their new form |
| **The remaining internal scanning requirements under 11.3.1** | Nine components entered authenticated scanning mid-cycle. Coverage, ranking and remediation-window evidence for those nine had to be re-tested rather than assumed |
| **The two compensating controls on the same nine appliances — 8.3.6 and 2.2.7** | New administrative credentials and a new agent on the appliances change the population the worksheets describe. **A compensating control is validated against a stated environment, and the environment moved** |
| **Change management over the two deployments and the agent rollout** | Three changes were made inside a freeze under an exemption. The exemption, the approvals, the testing and the back-out evidence are themselves assessable |

**The two compensating controls were re-validated rather than carried forward**, and that is the row that matters most. CCW-01 and CCW-02 survived because the constraint survived — but survival was tested, not assumed, and a worksheet that had ceased to describe the environment would have failed.

### 5.2 The revised outcome

| Disposition | 2026-12-11 | **2027-02-18** | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| **In Place** | 297 | **299** | **+2** — 8.6.2 and 11.3.1.2 |
| **In Place with Compensating Control** | 3 | **3** | **No change** — CCW-01, CCW-02, CCW-03 |
| **Not Applicable** | 4 | **4** | No change — 1.5.1, 3.3.2, 3.5.1.1, 4.2.1.2 |
| **Not Tested** | 0 | **0** | No change — **ADR-0004** |
| **Not in Place** | **2** | **0** | **−2** |
| **Total** | **306** | **306** | The applicable population is unchanged |
| **Compliance status** | **Non-Compliant** | **Compliant** | |

The arithmetic is deliberately dull: **297 + 2 = 299**, and everything else stands still. **The population did not change, the compensating controls did not change, the Not Applicable determinations did not change, and the Not Tested column is still zero.** A revised report in which several columns move at once is a report that has re-assessed more than it says it has.

### 5.3 What the revised report says about the 2026 result

> **ADR-0032 — The 2026 Attestation of Compliance is not withdrawn or replaced. The revised attestation is additive.**

| Question | Position |
|---|---|
| Is the 2026 ROC superseded? | **No.** It stands as the report of an assessment covering a period ending 2026-11-20 |
| Is the 2026 AOC withdrawn? | **No.** It was accurate when signed and remains accurate about the period it describes |
| What does the revised AOC attest to? | That **as at 2027-02-18**, on re-testing of 16 sub-requirements, all 306 applicable sub-requirements are met and the entity is **Compliant** |
| What does the record show? | **That Marketa was non-compliant on 2026-12-11 and compliant on 2027-02-18.** Both are true, both are filed, and the first is not erased by the second |
| Why does it matter? | Because the alternative — quietly replacing the December filing with a February one — would make the programme's most defensible decision invisible. **The honest non-compliant filing is the thing worth keeping** |

### 5.4 The freeze exemption — what was approved, and what it was not

Both remediations required changes inside the **October–January seasonal change freeze**, and the freeze is not a guideline. It exists because a change to a settlement-path component in December is the most expensive change Marketa can make.

| Field | Position |
|---|---|
| Approved by | **PCI Steering Committee**, **2026-12-17**, the same day as the Board and Audit Committee report |
| What was approved | Deployment of the two 8.6.2 remediations, and the appliance credential and agent work, **in the January window** |
| What was not approved | Any change before the January window; any change to the payment acceptance path; any relaxation of the change-control process itself |
| Conditions attached | Full change records with back-out plans; two full nightly cycles observed after each deployment; deployment outside trading hours; **the exemption expires with the freeze, so nothing rides on it in February** |
| Why the exemption was requested rather than the freeze ignored | **A remediation delivered by breaching a control is not a remediation.** The change-management requirements were themselves re-assessed on 2027-02-18, and an undocumented exception would have produced a new finding while closing an old one |

### 5.5 Monthly reporting to Cardinal

Cardinal's acknowledgement of **2026-12-16** attached a condition: **monthly remediation status reporting until the re-assessment.** Three reports were made.

| Report | Position reported |
|---|---|
| **January** | Freeze exemption approved 2026-12-17; **CAP-06 Route A amendment executed 2027-01-08**; credential release expected mid-January; both CAP-07 deployments scheduled inside the January window; **no variance against the 2027-01-31 date** |
| **February** | **Both plans complete** — CAP-07 on 2027-01-29, CAP-06's clean confirmatory scan on 2027-01-30; re-assessment booked for **2027-02-18** |

**Every report stated a position against a date rather than a percentage of progress.** A status report that says *on track* is a status report that cannot be wrong until it is too late, and Cardinal's condition was met with the same discipline the Part 4 plan was written with.

## 7. What the Re-Assessment Cost

| Item | Amount | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **Re-assessment fee** | **$44,000** | **A professional fee** — the assessor's charge for re-testing 16 sub-requirements and issuing a revised ROC and AOC |
| Drawn against | The **$187,000** contingency line in the **$4,600,000** programme budget | The line exists for exactly this |
| Original QSA engagement | **$385,000** | The annual assessment |
| **Fines, penalties or brand assessments** | **None stated** | **No fine, fee schedule or penalty amount appears anywhere in this programme** |

**The $44,000 is a fee, not a penalty**, and the distinction is not cosmetic. A penalty is imposed by a counterparty for a breach. **A re-assessment fee is what an assurance firm charges to do more assurance work**, and Marketa received a re-tested opinion on sixteen sub-requirements and two revised deliverables for it. It was budgeted before the finding existed, drawn from a contingency line that was sized in Phase 01, and reported to the Steering Committee as a planned drawdown rather than as an overrun.

The comparison worth making is not fee against no fee. **It is $44,000 against the alternative of an optimistic attestation** — a compensating control argued into a worksheet that a competent assessor would refuse, or a requirement quietly recorded as Not Tested. The cost of that route is not visible until a forensic investigator reads the attestation after an incident and finds it described controls that were not in place, which **01.04 §6 identifies as the aggravating factor in the most expensive outcome available to a breached retailer.**

### 7.1 What the remediation bought, in operational terms

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| Two accounts authenticating from a credential readable to anyone with configuration access on CDE-04 and CDE-05 | **Two accounts authenticating through CT-14 at run time**, with no credential in any file, any repository or any backup of either |
| Six interim measures maintained monthly around two accounts, including a two-person manual rotation | **Three measures retained** because they address a class rather than a defect; the manual rotation retired |
| Nine components whose installed and unremediated state was unknown, scanned weekly for what they disclosed | **Nine components inside the authenticated scanning programme**, with per-target authentication asserted, inside the same quarterly cycle as the other 62 |
| A vulnerability ranking exercise that could not rank what it could not see — recorded as a limitation in **TRA-11.3.1.1** | A ranking population that now includes the nine. **The trigger to re-perform the analysis fired on CAP-06's closure**, exactly as it was written to |
| Two High risks, twelve months unmoved, eleven interim measures between them, neither accepted | **Two Moderate risks with completed treatments and eighteen days of operating history** |

## 8. What the Remediation and Re-Assessment Do Not Establish

| Limitation | Statement |
|---|---|
| **The re-assessment covered 16 sub-requirements, not 306** | The other **290** stand on the 2026 assessment and its period. **The revised report is not a fresh annual validation**, and the next full assessment is due on the normal annual cycle |
| **The constraint survives** | **CCW-01 and CCW-02 remain**, on the same nine appliances, under the same support agreement, against the same firmware. Route B is retained on the 2027 roadmap for that reason |
| **A contractual right is not a permanent right** | The credential-release term has a renewal date, and a renewal is a negotiation. **The residual on R-36 changed shape rather than disappearing: the exposure is now contractual rather than technical** |
| **Nine appliances now hold privileged credentials that did not exist before** | Closing 11.3.1.2 created a new administrative credential population and an agent estate to manage. Both are vaulted and monitored, and both are new maintenance the 2027 programme owns |
| **The 2026 record stands** | **ADR-0032.** Marketa was non-compliant on 2026-12-11 |
| **Compliance is not a state** | The revised attestation describes 2027-02-18. **The next assessment tests the year that follows it**, with an assessor rotation due in **2028** and a customized approach that must be argued from scratch when it happens |
