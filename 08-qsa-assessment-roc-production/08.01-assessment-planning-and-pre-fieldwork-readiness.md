## Purpose

Seven phases built an environment and the evidence that it operates. This phase submits both to somebody whose professional obligation is to disbelieve them until tested.

This document records what was agreed with **Sable Ridge Assurance, LLC** before a single testing procedure was executed: the pre-fieldwork scope re-confirmation performed in October 2026 inside the seasonal change freeze; the assessment plan; the assessment period and the boundaries the ROC will state; the assessment team and Marketa's counterpart roles; the logistics of assessing a 482-store estate in fifteen business days; and — the section that matters most and is most often absent from a programme's own record — **what an entity being assessed may and may not do while the assessment is running.**

It introduces **ADR-0029**, taken on **2026-10-23** and therefore before fieldwork opened, which governs everything the assessment found and Marketa fixed while the assessor was still in the building.

Nothing in this document is an assessment outcome. The outcomes are in 08.05, 08.06 and 08.07, and they were not known when this plan was agreed.

## 1. The Pre-Fieldwork Scope Re-Confirmation — October 2026

**07.06 is the document the assessment opens with**, and 07.06 §9 says the uncomfortable thing about itself: a confirmation is a statement about a date. Scope was correct on 2026-07-14. The estate changed the next morning. The pre-fieldwork delta exists because of that, and it is **a delta, not a second full confirmation.**

| Attribute | Position |
|---|---|
| What it is | A re-verification of the 12.5.2 determination **limited to what changed** between the annual confirmation and the start of fieldwork |
| What it is not | A repeat of the seven-element confirmation. Repeating it would have produced a second document of equal weight and a question about which one the ROC relies on |
| Performed by | Owen Castellanos, with Marcus Hale for the register reconciliation and Trevor Kim for the network and component position |
| Independent challenge | Rosa Delgado, Director of Internal Audit, on the two elements 07.06 §6 identified as most exposed to owner bias |
| Window | **October 2026**, deliberately inside the seasonal change freeze |
| Input | The change record population between the annual confirmation and the delta date, filtered through **SIG-1 to SIG-10**; the monthly 12.5.1 reconciliations; the condition register |
| Output | A signed delta statement supplied to Sable Ridge before fieldwork, and the register position the assessor tested against |

### 1.1 Why the delta runs inside the freeze

The October-to-January seasonal change freeze under **CON-01** is normally described as a constraint, and in this one instance it is an advantage. A scope confirmation performed while the estate is changing is obsolete by the time it is signed. A scope confirmation performed during a period in which store-side change is prohibited and settlement-path change requires an exemption is a statement about an estate that is holding still.

That is the whole reason fieldwork is in early November. The programme did not schedule an assessment around a trading peak by accident: **it scheduled the assessment into the only three weeks of the year when the assessed population is stable, the seasonal workforce is fully provisioned and observable, and the store estate is operating at the volume the controls were designed for.**

### 1.2 What the delta re-verified

| Element | Basis | Delta position |

| Account data flows | Re-traced from observed captures, not from diagrams | **Seven named flows across three channels, three of which carry account data to a Marketa component.** The eighth flow — the store special-order payment link — remains removed under the SIG-3 change recorded in 07.06 §5 |
| Locations | 482 stores, 4 distribution centres, 2 corporate offices, 2 data centres, 1 call centre | Unchanged. No new location, merchant identifier or sales channel |

| The thirty-five scope conditions | The P2PE, tokenization and DTMF-masking conditions holding up the 604-to-71 reduction | Re-verified. The **two conditions qualified in July** were re-checked specifically, and their qualifications were carried forward unchanged rather than closed on the strength of three further months |

The delta did not produce a change to the assessed population, and the programme resisted the temptation to present that as a result. **A delta returning "no change" during a change freeze is the expected outcome, not an achievement**, and the value of running it is that the assessor tested a scope statement dated three weeks before fieldwork rather than four months before it.

### 1.3 What the delta deliberately did not do

| Not done | Why |
|---|---|

## 3. The Assessment Period and Its Boundaries

The single most misread line in any ROC is the period the report covers. A ROC is a point-in-time attestation supported by evidence of operation over a defined window, and the window is not the same for every control.

| Boundary | Position |
|---|---|
| Assessment period | The twelve months preceding fieldwork, aligned to the programme's operating year from the **2026-01-12** kickoff |
| Fieldwork window | **2026-11-02 to 2026-11-20** |
| Report date | **2026-12-11** |
| What the ROC attests to | That the assessed controls were **In Place at the conclusion of the assessment**, supported by evidence of operation across the stated period |
| What it does not attest to | Any state before the period, any state after **2026-12-11**, or any system outside the 71 |
| Controls with a shorter operating history | Recorded individually, with the actual operating window stated rather than implied — **11.6.1's rests on 140 days**, and eleven register movements rest on one operating cycle each |
| Quarterly obligations | Four internal scan cycles and four ASV cycles fall inside the period, including the **Q1 ASV failure and its clean rescan eleven days later** |
| The freeze | October to January. Its effect on remediation capacity is a stated feature of the period, not an excuse offered inside it |

**A control that began operating in August cannot evidence twelve months of operation in November, and no amount of documentation makes it do so.** Marketa's position throughout has been to state the operating window per control and let the assessor weigh it. Where the window is short, the ROC says so.

**The single-point-of-contact model is the single highest-value piece of assessment logistics available to an entity of this size.** Fourteen people answering an assessor's questions in parallel produce fourteen versions of the environment. One person routing 214 requests produces one, and the discrepancies surface inside Marketa before they surface in front of the assessor.

What the model must not become is a filter. Owen Castellanos routed requests and scheduled interviews; **he did not answer technical questions on behalf of the people who own the controls, and he was not present for every interview.** An assessor who only ever hears the compliance manager's account of an environment has interviewed one person forty-seven times.

## 5. Logistics — 482 Stores in Fifteen Days

The store estate is the part of Marketa's environment that cannot be assessed from Columbus, and it is the part where a plan either works or produces a finding about the plan.

| Constraint | Consequence for the schedule |
|---|---|
| **482 stores across 38 states** | Physical assessment of the whole estate is not possible in any assessment. The sample is 28, derived in 08.02 |
| **1,914 POI terminals** | 96 observed within the sampled stores. Terminal observation is done in the store, at the lane, during trading hours |
| Trading pattern | Store visits scheduled outside peak trading hours where possible, and never during a delivery window, so that the assessor observes normal operation rather than a store rearranged for a visit |
| Travel | Three assessors, three days, twenty-eight stores, clustered into geographic groups so that travel does not consume the observation time |
| The freeze | No store-side change is permitted, so what the assessor sees is what has been running since October rather than something deployed for the visit |
| **Store 0417, Dayton OH** | Deliberately included in the sample by the assessor. §5.1 |
| Advance notice | Stores received the visit schedule at the district level. **Individual store teams were not briefed on the questions**, and the awareness and device-inspection interviews were conducted with whoever was on shift |

### 5.1 Store 0417

Store 0417 in Dayton, Ohio is the site where the **first segmentation penetration test failed in May 2026** — a store back-office VLAN reachable from the guest Wi-Fi VLAN through a misconfigured trunk. It was remediated and re-tested clean in June.

Marketa did not propose it for the sample and could not have excluded it. **Grant Whitfield selected it explicitly**, and told Marketa why: an entity that has had a segmentation failure at a named site has a site where the remediation can be observed rather than attested. The assessor tested the remediated configuration at the store, not the change record describing it.

That selection is the clearest available illustration of why §6's rule exists. **A sample chosen by Marketa would not have contained store 0417.**

### 5.2 What the store schedule cost

| Item | Position |
|---|---|
| Assessor days consumed by the store sweep | Three assessors across three days of Week 3 |
| Stores per assessor per day | Between three and four, including travel |
| Terminals observed | **96** across the 28 stores |
| Witnessed POI inspection | One, performed by the store team under the 9.5.1 procedure with the assessor observing |
| Store-level findings | **None.** Recorded in 08.04, and recorded there without celebration — it is the outcome a quarterly inspection cycle across all 1,914 terminals is supposed to produce |

## 6. What the Entity May and May Not Do During an Assessment

This is the section a programme writes for itself, and the reason to write it down is that every line in it is a temptation under time pressure.

### 6.1 What Marketa may do

| Permitted | Basis and limit |
|---|---|
| **Remediate a deficiency found during fieldwork, and have the requirement assessed In Place** | Provided the correction is complete, tested by the assessor, and **recorded in the ROC as corrected during the assessment**. This is **ADR-0029** and §7 states it in full |
| Supply additional evidence after an initial response is judged insufficient | Six requests were re-requested on that basis. Producing better evidence is not the same as producing a better answer |
| Correct a factual error in the assessor's understanding of the environment | With evidence. A correction unsupported by evidence is an assertion, and an assertion is what the assessment exists to test |
| Challenge a determination **on fact** | Four determinations were disputed. **One went Marketa's way on evidence and three did not** — 08.04 §7 |
| Ask how a testing procedure was derived | Particularly for the customized approach, where the procedures are the assessor's own work. Asking is legitimate; asking for a softer one is not |
| Decline to speculate | An interviewee who does not know an answer says so and the request is routed. **A guess in an interview becomes an assessor's note about an inconsistency** |
| Observe the assessor's testing | Marketa personnel accompanied all independent assessor testing actions, for operational safety on production systems |

### 6.2 What Marketa may not do

| Prohibited | Why |
|---|---|
| **Choose the sample** | The sample is the assessor's, derived from a documented rationale recorded in the ROC. An entity that selects its own sample has selected its own result. **08.02 is written by reference to a sample Marketa saw for the first time when it was issued** |
| **Comment on, negotiate or soften a conclusion** | The conclusions are the assessor's professional opinion. Marketa's review of the draft ROC is limited to factual accuracy — **ADR-0030**, introduced at the reporting stage and applied without exception |
| Write its own testing procedures for the customized approach | Sable Ridge derived **CTP-1 to CTP-12** and documented them in Appendix E2. **An entity that writes its own test has written its own assessment** |
| Brief interviewees on expected answers | Interviews test whether a control is understood by the people who operate it. A rehearsed answer tests whether they can be rehearsed |
| Withhold, curate or delay evidence | Every request is logged, including the ones Marketa would rather not have received. **Zero requests went unsatisfied**, and that figure is only meaningful because the log records refusals as well as responses |
| Present a remediation completed after fieldwork as In Place | The boundary that makes ADR-0029 a discipline rather than a loophole. §7 |
| Direct which individuals are interviewed | The assessor selects. Marketa schedules |
| Describe itself as certified, or the AOC as a compliance determination under law | Standing framing. **There is no PCI DSS certification for a merchant**, and no attestation is a determination of compliance with any law |

### 6.3 The rule underneath all of them

> **An assessment is not a negotiation about what the environment is. It is a test of what the environment is, performed by somebody the entity does not control, against a population the entity does not select.**

Everything in §6.1 preserves the entity's ability to be accurate. Everything in §6.2 preserves the assessor's ability to be independent. A programme that blurs the two produces a report that describes a relationship rather than an environment.

## 7. ADR-0029 — Remediation During Fieldwork Is Disclosed, Never Absorbed

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Identifier** | **ADR-0029** |
| Decision | **A deficiency identified during fieldwork and corrected before the conclusion of the assessment may be assessed In Place, and is recorded in the Report on Compliance as corrected during the assessment. The correction is disclosed in the ROC's finding text. It is never absorbed into an unqualified In Place.** |
| Status | Approved |
| **Date** | **2026-10-23** — agreed at the assessment planning stage, **ten days before fieldwork opened** and before any deficiency existed to be argued about |
| Decided by | Naomi Bhatt, with Grant Whitfield's confirmation of the assessor's own position |
| Applies to | Every in-flight correction. **Nine occurred** — 08.04 §6 |

**The date is the point.** A rule about how findings are disclosed is worth nothing if it is written after the first finding, because by then it is a decision about a specific embarrassment rather than a decision about conduct. ADR-0029 was settled on **2026-10-23**, alongside the pre-fieldwork scope delta and the declaration of the two known gaps, at a moment when nobody in the room knew what the assessor would find. **The 1.2.8 correction on 2026-11-05 is the first thing the decision governed, not the reason it exists.**

### 7.1 The reasoning

There are three positions an entity can take when an assessor finds something on day four that can be fixed by day six.

| Position | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Fix it and say nothing | The ROC records an unqualified In Place for a control that was not in place when the assessor arrived. **The report is then inaccurate about the period it covers**, and the entity has spent its credibility on a detail |
| Refuse to fix it during fieldwork, to avoid the disclosure | A deficiency is left running in production so that a report reads more cleanly. **This is the worst of the three and it is the one entities most often choose** |
| **Fix it, and disclose that it was fixed during the assessment** | The requirement is met at the conclusion of the assessment, the reader knows when it was met, and the environment is better on the day the assessor leaves than it was on the day they arrived |

Marketa takes the third, and records it as an ADR because it is a decision that has to survive the moment somebody wants an unqualified line in a board report.

### 7.2 The boundary

**A requirement corrected during fieldwork can be assessed In Place. A requirement corrected after the ROC cannot.**

That is the whole of the rule and it is not arbitrary. Fieldwork is the window in which the assessor is present, can test the correction themselves, and can evidence the tested state. Once the assessment concludes, a correction is a statement by the entity about a period the assessor is no longer observing — which is precisely what a remediation plan is, and precisely why **8.6.2 and 11.3.1.2 are Not in Place with dated plans rather than corrected findings.**

The distinction is the difference between the nine and the two. All eleven are disclosed. Nine were fixed while somebody was watching.

### 7.3 The worked example the ADR was written against

**Requirement 1.2.8.** Configuration files for network security controls are secured from unauthorised access and kept consistent with active configurations. On **2026-11-04**, the assessor found that **two of nine** NSC ruleset backups were held on a file share whose access control was not restricted to the network engineering group.

Marketa had asserted 1.2.8 In Place. It was not. The backups were relocated to the restricted repository and the share permissions corrected on **2026-11-05**; the assessor re-tested the access control himself, examined the share's access log for the preceding period, and assessed **1.2.8 In Place with the correction disclosed in the finding text.**

That instance is also **D-4** in 08.04 §7 — one of the four determinations where Marketa's initial position and the assessor's differed. **Marketa was wrong.** The programme records it that way, in this document and in the ROC.
