## Purpose

Two of the 306 applicable sub-requirements were assessed **Not in Place**: **8.6.2** and **11.3.1.2**. This document records how each is written into **Part II** of the Report on Compliance and into **Part 4** of the Attestation of Compliance, what the assessor said, why neither was resolved by a compensating control, what the risk register does with them, and the point that is easy to lose in the paperwork.

**Both findings were identified by Marketa — in Phase 05 and Phase 06 respectively — months before the assessor arrived.** Sable Ridge confirmed them. It did not discover them. §7 makes that case with dates, because it is the difference between a programme that assesses itself and one that waits to be told.

## 2. How a Not in Place Is Recorded in Part II

The template gives a Not in Place the same four elements as any other disposition, and adds nothing. There is no severity field, no mitigating-circumstances field and no place to record how hard the entity tried.

| Element | What it contains for these two findings |
|---|---|
| **Assessment finding** | **Not in Place** |
| **Testing procedures performed** | The full set the requirement defines, executed against the sampled population. **A Not in Place is reached by performing the test, not by declining it** |
| **Evidence reference** | Indexed artefacts, including the two source files, the account inventory, the scanner authentication assertions per target, and the support agreement |
| **Narrative** | What the assessor observed, why the requirement is not met, and what the entity has in place instead — **recorded as what it is, not as mitigation of the finding** |

The remediation plan does not live in Part II. **It lives in Part 4 of the Attestation of Compliance**, and the two documents therefore say different things about the same fact: the ROC says the requirement was not met, and the AOC says what will be done about it and by when.

## 3. 8.6.2 — What the Assessor Wrote

> **8.6.2 — Not in Place.** The assessor examined the population of 142 application and system accounts and identified two accounts capable of interactive login whose authentication credentials are stored in cleartext within files on the system that invokes them: `batch-icq` on CDE-05, where the credential is assigned within a Korn shell wrapper supplied by the platform vendor, and `gl-extract` on CDE-04, where the credential is held in a Java properties file read at process initialisation. The assessor confirmed by direct file examination that the credential values are present and readable to any principal holding read access to the file, and confirmed by account testing that both accounts can be used for interactive login. The entity has implemented restricted repository access with read logging, file integrity monitoring on both files, source-host restriction, out-of-window authentication alerting, brokered and recorded interactive activation, and monthly credential rotation under two-person change control. The assessor examined the operating records for these measures and found them effective for their stated purposes, with no unauthorised file reads and no out-of-window authentications in the period. **These measures reduce the likelihood that an embedded credential is obtained and used. They do not cause the credential to be absent from the file, which is what the requirement obliges.** The requirement is therefore Not in Place. A corrective action plan with a completion date of 2027-01-31 is recorded in Part 4 of the Attestation of Compliance.

### 3.1 What the assessor tested, and what it produced

| Testing action | Result |
|---|---|
| Examined the account inventory and the interactive-login determination for all **142** accounts | **136 of 142** prevented from interactive use and verified quarterly by attempted login; **6 documented exceptions**, of which two are these accounts |
| Read both files directly on the sampled components | Credential values present in cleartext in both |
| Attempted interactive authentication with each account under change control | Both succeeded, confirming the requirement's condition is engaged |
| Examined the restricted-repository access log, the file-integrity alert record and the out-of-window alert record for the period | **0 unauthorised file reads, 0 out-of-window authentications, 4 file-integrity alerts — all four from the approved monthly rotations** |
| Examined the vendor's release notes and the ERP interface upgrade scope | Confirmed the remediation path is real and dated, and that the constraint is the vendor's release cycle rather than Marketa's engineering capacity |
| Examined the change-freeze policy and the exemption route | Confirmed the **2027-01-31** date is bounded by the October–January freeze, not chosen for convenience |

The fifth and sixth rows are the ones that made a dated Part 4 credible. **An assessor who accepts a remediation date without examining whether the entity can actually meet it has written a wish into a compliance document.**

## 4. 11.3.1.2 — What the Assessor Wrote

> **11.3.1.2 — Not in Place.** The assessor examined the internal vulnerability scanning programme across the 71 assessed system components and confirmed authenticated scanning with per-target authentication assertion on 62. Nine components — the vendor-supplied point-of-sale back-office appliances deployed across the store estate — are scanned without authentication. The entity relied on the requirement's allowance for systems that are unable to accept credentials for authenticated scanning, and provided documentation of the constraint maintained since programme inception. The assessor tested the basis of that reliance and established that the appliances expose an administrative interface and are technically capable of credentialed assessment; the administrative credential is held by the vendor, and the support agreement in force provides neither for its release to the entity nor for the installation of an assessment agent. **The allowance addresses a system that is unable to accept credentials. It does not address a system whose credentials the entity has not obtained. The first is a property of the technology; the second is a property of a contract.** The entity performs weekly unauthenticated scanning, monthly firmware reconciliation against the vendor's current release, quarterly vendor patch attestation under Requirement 12.8.5, and network isolation of the appliance population. The assessor examined each and found them operating as described. **None establishes the installed and unremediated vulnerability state of the nine components, which is the objective of the requirement.** The requirement is therefore Not in Place. A corrective action plan with a completion date of 2027-01-31 is recorded in Part 4 of the Attestation of Compliance.

### 4.1 What the assessor tested, and what it produced

| Testing action | Result |
|---|---|
| Examined the scan configuration and **per-target authentication assertions** for every component in every cycle | **62 of 71** authenticated. A platform that reports "scanned" without asserting authentication conceals exactly this gap |
| Examined the nine appliances' administrative interfaces | **Technically capable of credentialed assessment.** This is the finding that defeated the documentation allowance |
| Read the support agreement | No credential release; no agent installation right |
| Examined the accommodating measures — weekly unauthenticated scans, monthly firmware reconciliation, the quarterly vendor attestation, the isolation ruleset | All operating as described, none establishing installed vulnerability state |
| Examined **CAP-06**, its two routes and its costed fallback | A renewal negotiation with a costed platform replacement behind it, dated to a commercial cycle |
| Included **store 0417 (Dayton OH)** in the store sample deliberately | The site of the failed segmentation test. The appliance population there was examined directly rather than by extension |

## 5. Why Each Is a Finding Rather Than a Compensating Control

08.07 sets out the three tests a compensating control must pass and applies them to the five candidates — three accepted, two refused. The refusals are restated here because a reader arriving at the findings will ask the question immediately.

| Test | **8.6.2** | **11.3.1.2** |
|---|---|---|
| **Addresses the risk the original requirement addresses** | **No.** The intent of 8.6.2 is that the secret is **not present in a readable file**. No arrangement of permissions, monitoring, alerting or rotation makes a secret absent from a file it is in, and none of it addresses readability by anyone holding configuration access | **No.** The intent of 11.3.1.2 is to know what is installed and unremediated on the component. Unauthenticated scanning establishes what the component discloses; firmware reconciliation establishes what release it runs; a vendor attestation is a statement about the vendor's process |
| **Goes above and beyond other PCI DSS requirements** | **No.** Credential rotation is already owed under 8.6.3. Restriction of access to the files is already owed under **Requirement 7**, non-interactive use and account governance under **8.6.1**, and change detection under **11.5.2**. **A compensating control cannot be assembled from requirements the entity already owes** | **No.** Network isolation is already owed under Requirement 1. Advisory monitoring is already owed under 6.3.1. Vendor assurance is already owed under 12.8 |
| **Commensurate with the additional risk imposed by the deviation** | Not reached | Not reached |

Two further arguments were made internally for 8.6.2 and refused before any worksheet was drafted:

| Argument | Why it failed |
|---|---|
| *"Rotate it monthly and it is effectively controlled."* | 8.6.2 does not prohibit **stale** credentials; it prohibits **hard-coded** ones. A credential rotated monthly and written back into the same shell script is a hard-coded credential with a fresh value |
| *"Restrict the file so tightly that nobody can read it."* | The requirement is not satisfied by narrowing the readership of a cleartext secret. **Anyone with configuration access to the component reads it**, and that population is not empty |

And for 11.3.1.2, the argument that came closest and still failed:

> **More frequent unauthenticated scanning detects a different class of vulnerability. It is not a substitute for authenticated scanning; it is a different test.** Increasing the frequency of a test that cannot see inside the appliance produces more observations of the same blind spot.

**The distinguishing test was stated once, in Phase 05, and applied consistently in Phase 06, at fieldwork and again in Appendix C.** Three worksheets passed it and two did not, and the two that did not are the two findings.

### 5.1 The three routes that were available and not taken

For each finding there were three ways to produce a report without it. All three are legitimate-looking, all three are used, and all three were rejected before fieldwork rather than during it.

| Route | What it would have required | Why it was not taken |
|---|---|---|
| **Argue a compensating control** | A worksheet at Appendix C for 8.6.2 or 11.3.1.2, drafted to the six sections and validated by the assessor | Both fail the tests in §5. **Marketa could have produced a worksheet; a competent QSA would have rejected it, and a QSA who accepted it would have been wrong.** The programme declined to spend the assessment's credibility on the one control where the gap is unambiguous |
| **Record the requirement Not Tested** | An argument that the population could not be examined within the assessment window | **ADR-0004**, taken in Phase 01, makes the disposition unavailable in this programme. **08.08 §5** sets out what it is normally used for |
| **Redefine the population** | Excluding the two accounts from the 8.6.2 population as vendor-managed, or the nine appliances from the assessed population as store equipment | **The 306 and the 71 have been published since Phase 02 and have not moved.** A denominator that shrinks when a requirement becomes uncomfortable is not a denominator |

The fourth route — the one that was actually offered — was a **late compensating-control argument three weeks before fieldwork**, when there was still time to attempt it. The Steering Committee declined it under **DEC-710**, on the CISO's reasoning: *we refused a compensating control for 8.6.2 in June and for 11.3.1.2 in July, on the record, for reasons that have not changed. Arguing one now because the assessment is three weeks away would tell the assessor exactly what our earlier refusals were worth.*

### 5.2 The two findings are not the same shape

| Dimension | **8.6.2** | **11.3.1.2** |
|---|---|---|
| What is wrong | **A secret exists where it should not.** The defect is present and readable | **A fact is unknown.** The defect, if any, is unobserved |
| What an attacker gains | A credential, if they can reach the file | Nothing directly. **The risk is that something unremediated persists because nobody can see it** |
| What fixes it | **Engineering** — two vendor upgrades and a vault integration | **A contract clause.** No engineering was required of Marketa at all |
| What Marketa controlled | The deployment date, bounded by a vendor release and a change freeze | **Nothing that mattered.** The counterparty could have declined |
| Why the remediation is not a lesson about the technology | The platform was always capable of retrieving a credential at run time; the vendor's release cycle governed when | **The appliances were always capable of credentialed assessment.** The support agreement governed whether |

**Both findings are properties of a platform Marketa does not control, and neither could be fixed by configuration** — which is the single sentence 05.07 §5.1 used to describe them together, and it survived the assessment unchanged.

## 7. Marketa Found Both of These, and the Assessor Confirmed Them

This is the point worth making, and it is checkable against documents written months before Sable Ridge arrived.

| Event | Date | Document |
|---|---|---|
| **8.6.2 determined Not in Place by Marketa** | Phase 05 | **05.07 §5.1** — "Requirement 8.6.2 is assessed Not in Place" |
| Compensating control for 8.6.2 considered and **refused by Marketa** | **June 2026** | 05.07 §5.3 |
| **11.3.1.2 determined Not in Place by Marketa** | Phase 06 | **06.05 §5** — 62 of 71 authenticated, nine impossible |
| Compensating control for 11.3.1.2 drafted and **refused by Marketa** | **July 2026** | 06.05 §5.5 — three of four tests fail |
| Both carried into the assessment undressed, with **R-17** and **R-36** held at High 16 and **neither accepted by the CFO** | Phase 07 | **07.13 §5.1** |
| A late compensating-control argument offered to the Steering Committee three weeks before fieldwork and **declined** | Pre-fieldwork | **DEC-710**, 07.00 |
| **Sable Ridge confirms both** | **2026-11-02 → 11-20** | This document |
| Both recorded in the ROC and in AOC Part 4 | **2026-12-11** | 08.09, 08.11 |

**The assessor's contribution was confirmation, not discovery**, and confirmation is worth having: an entity's own determination that a requirement is not met is a self-assessment, and a self-assessment is exactly the thing an independent assessment exists to test. Sable Ridge tested both determinations and could have disagreed in either direction — it could have found 8.6.2 worse than Marketa described, or found that the 11.3.1.2 documentation allowance did reach the nine. **It did neither. It reached the same conclusion by its own testing, five months after Marketa reached it for 8.6.2 and four months after Marketa reached it for 11.3.1.2.**

Two things follow that a reader should not have to infer:

| Consequence | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Nothing in the ROC was a surprise to the Steering Committee** | The findings were reported monthly from Phase 05 onwards, the AOC's non-compliant status was described to the Audit Committee in March, and the CFO stated on the record in Phase 07 exactly what he expected to sign |
| **The four determinations where the assessor's initial position differed from Marketa's were elsewhere** | **5.3.3, 3.5.1.3, 10.4.2 and 1.2.8** — none of them the two findings. **The disagreements were about requirements Marketa believed it met, or believed did not reach it**, which is where disagreements usually are. **One of the four went Marketa's way; three did not** |

### 7.1 The one Marketa got wrong

Symmetry requires this row. Of the four contested determinations, **1.2.8** went against Marketa on a matter of fact rather than of interpretation: two of nine network security control ruleset backups were held on an unrestricted file share, and Marketa had asserted the requirement In Place. **Marketa was wrong.** The exposure was corrected on **2026-11-05**, during fieldwork, and the requirement was assessed In Place **with the correction disclosed under ADR-0029**.

A programme that identifies its own two most serious findings months in advance and still gets a configuration wrong on the assessor's second working day is a normal programme. **The claim being made here is not that Marketa's self-assessment was complete. It is that its self-assessment was honest**, and 1.2.8 is the evidence for the distinction.

| Exit condition | **CAP-07 complete and re-tested** | **CAP-06 complete and re-tested** |

Two properties of these two rows are the programme's own standard applied to itself:

**Neither has ever been reduced by an accommodation.** Six interim measures operate on the two embedded credentials and five operate on the nine appliances. Every one of them is recorded as an accommodation — a measure that reduces consequence or likelihood of exploitation without treating the cause — and **an accommodation has never lowered a rating in this programme**. 06.12 §2 defines the term and 06.12 §3.3 applies it to R-36 by name.

**Neither has been accepted.** A formal risk-acceptance route exists, it sits with the Chief Financial Officer, and **in twelve months it has never been used for a High risk.** Accepting R-17 or R-36 would have been the cheapest possible response to an uncomfortable assessment: the risk stays exactly where it is, and the register stops recording it as an outstanding obligation. The route was available at every monthly Steering Committee from June 2026 onwards and was not taken.

## 9. The Date, and the Absence of Float

| Date | What it is |
|---|---|
| **2026-12-11** | ROC and AOC issued. Both findings live, both dated |
| **2026-12-17** | Steering Committee approves the **change-freeze exemption** for the 8.6.2 deployments |
| **2027-01-31** | **Both remediations due.** The last day of the October–January seasonal change freeze |
| **2027-02-18** | **Re-assessment.** Eighteen days later |

The two findings share a date for one reason, and it is not coordination for its own sake. **The seasonal change freeze runs October to January**, no change is permitted to a settlement-path component inside it, and the 11.3.1.2 work touches vendor-locked appliances across the store estate at the trading peak. Neither remediation could begin before the freeze lifted, and both therefore land on the same day.

**Eighteen days is the entire margin.** If either remediation slips, the re-assessment either slips with it or proceeds and returns the same finding. There is no version of the plan in which a delay is absorbed, and the programme has said so in every document that has touched the date since Phase 05 rather than discovering it in February.

### 9.1 What would have happened if either had slipped

The plan was written with the failure case stated, because a remediation plan that describes only success is a forecast rather than a plan.

| Scenario | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **CAP-07 slips** — a vendor release delayed, or a deployment failing its post-change cycles | The re-assessment either moves with it or proceeds and returns **8.6.2 Not in Place** again. A variance would be reported to Cardinal in the **monthly remediation status report**, against a date already on file |
| **CAP-06's Route A fails** — the vendor declines the amendment | **Route B**, the costed platform replacement, becomes the plan. It does not complete by February, so the finding persists into a longer remediation with a revised date agreed with Cardinal |
| **The re-assessment finds something else** | The 14 related sub-requirements were selected by the assessor precisely because the remediations could have broken them. **A new finding at the re-assessment would have produced a new Part 4 row**, not a quiet extension |
| **Nothing slips** | What happened. Both complete ahead of the date — 2027-01-29 and 2027-01-30 — and are re-tested on 2027-02-18 |

**The variance would have been reported in every case**, because the plan was already on file with the acquirer. That is the second-order benefit of filing early with an honest Part 4: **a slip against a published plan is a reportable variance; a slip against a plan nobody has seen is a surprise.**

## 10. What These Findings Do Not Mean

| Misreading | The position |
|---|---|
| That Marketa's cardholder data was exposed | Neither finding is an incident. **8.6.2 is a credential in a file on a restricted host; 11.3.1.2 is an absence of visibility.** No account data compromise occurred in the period, and none is asserted |
| That the assessment failed | The assessment succeeded. **It produced an accurate result**, which is what an assessment is for. A report with 306 In Place entries and no adverse findings would have been a less useful document and, in this environment, a false one |
| That the compensating controls could have saved the position | They could not, and **that is a finding about the controls rather than about the argument.** Every candidate control was either already owed under another requirement or did not address the risk the requirement addresses |
| That the interim measures are worthless | They are not. Six measures on the credentials and five on the appliances materially reduce the probability of exploitation, and the assessor examined and confirmed each of them. **They are recorded as accommodations, and an accommodation is not a control that closes a finding** |
| That a non-compliant filing is a programme failure | **01.04 §5 states the opposite in advance.** A merchant filing a non-compliant Attestation with a credible dated plan is in a materially better position than one that files late, files nothing, or files optimistically and is later found by a forensic investigator to have attested to controls that were not in place |
| That the findings close on 2027-01-31 | The remediation completes then. **The findings close when the assessor re-tests them on 2027-02-18** and the revised report records the result — which is 08.12's subject |
