## Purpose

Fieldwork ended at the closing meeting on **2026-11-20**. The Report on Compliance was issued on **2026-12-11**. This document records what happened in the twenty-one days between those two dates: what the report is, how it is structured, how it was produced and quality-assured, what Marketa was permitted to say about it, and what it does and does not establish.

The Attestation of Compliance — the document that is actually filed with Cardinal Merchant Bank — is dealt with separately in **08.11**. They are produced together and they are not the same instrument, and the difference is the subject of §1.

## 1. What a Report on Compliance Is, and What It Is Not

| It is | It is not |
|---|---|
| **A qualified security assessor's report** on whether each applicable PCI DSS sub-requirement was met, recorded requirement by requirement with the testing performed and the evidence relied on | **A certificate.** There is no PCI DSS certification for a merchant. The Council issues nothing to Marketa; the assessor issues an opinion |
| **A point-in-time assessment** of a **defined scope** over a **defined period** — here, the 71 assessed system components, assessed 2026-11-02 to 2026-11-20 against an operating period running to that date | **A statement about tomorrow.** A requirement In Place on 2026-11-20 can fail on 2026-11-21, and nothing in the report says otherwise |
| **The assessor's professional opinion**, formed on the assessor's own testing and reported in the assessor's own words | **A negotiated document.** Marketa's review is limited to factual accuracy — **ADR-0030** |
| **The supporting document** behind the Attestation of Compliance, provided to Cardinal on request | **The routine filing.** The AOC is what is filed; the ROC is provided on request and is not routinely distributed |
| **A record of a defined assessed population** — 306 applicable sub-requirements, 71 components, 18 sampled populations | **A guarantee of security.** It is a compliance assessment, and a compliant entity can still be compromised |
| Binding on **nobody** as a matter of law | **Binding on the acquirer or the brands.** Cardinal forms its own view; the brands form theirs through Cardinal. **The assessor's opinion binds neither** |

The last row is the one most often misread inside an organisation, and it is worth stating in the terms a Chief Financial Officer would use. **The ROC is evidence Marketa offers to Cardinal in support of a contractual obligation.** It is not a permission, not a licence, and not a defence. Cardinal may accept it, may query it, and may require more. What the report gives Marketa is a credible, independent, dated account of the state of 306 requirements — and, in 2026, an account that says two of them were not met.

## 2. The Template Structure

The report is produced on the **PCI DSS v4.0.1 ROC Template for merchants**, published by the Council. The template is not optional and its structure is not the assessor's to rearrange.

| Part | Section | Content | Marketa's material |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Part I — Assessment Overview** | **1** Contact information and summary of results | Assessed entity and QSA company details; the assessment date range; the summary of findings across all twelve requirements; the compliance determination | Marketa Retail Group, Inc.; **Sable Ridge Assurance, LLC**; lead QSA **Grant Whitfield**; fieldwork **2026-11-02 → 2026-11-20**; **In Place 297 · CCW 3 · N/A 4 · Not Tested 0 · Not in Place 2** |
| | **2** Business overview | The entity's business, payment acceptance channels and transaction volumes | National specialty retail; **482 stores** across 38 states; **1,914 POI lanes**; e-commerce; MOTO through a **310-agent** call centre; **68.4M** annual card transactions |
| | **3** Description of scope of work and approach taken | How scope was determined and validated; segmentation; sampling and its rationale; the customized approach; the use of another assessor's work | The **604 → 71** reduction; **9 zones** with segmentation validated by penetration test; the **18 sampled populations**; the **8.3.9** customized approach; **P2PE, tokenization and DTMF masking** as the scope-reducing arrangements |
| | **4** Details about reviewed environments | Network diagrams, data-flow diagrams, the system component inventory, facilities, and the third parties relied upon | **8 CDE** and **63 connected-to** components; **2 data centres**, **4 distribution centres**, **2 offices**, **1 call centre**; **6 third-party service providers** |
| | **5** Quarterly scan results | The four ASV scans in the period, with the attestation of scan compliance for each | **4 quarterly scans**; **Q1 failed with 3 findings and rescanned clean in 11 days**; Q2–Q4 passed first time. **The Q1 failure appears in the report** |
| | **6** Evidence / documentation retained | The assessor's record of what was examined and retained, indexed | **1,654 indexed evidence artefacts** |
| **Part II — Findings and Observations** | **Requirements 1 to 12** | Every applicable sub-requirement, with the assessment finding, the testing procedures performed, the evidence relied upon and the assessor's narrative | **306** sub-requirements across the twelve, in the population distribution recorded in 08.05 |
| | **Appendix C** | Compensating Controls Worksheets | **CCW-01 (8.3.6)**, **CCW-02 (2.2.7)**, **CCW-03 (3.4.2)** — six sections each |
| | **Appendix E** | Customized approach: **E1** the entity's controls matrix; **E2** the assessor's derived testing procedures | **CAC-1 to CAC-8** supplied by Marketa; **CTP-1 to CTP-12** derived by Sable Ridge, at **8.3.9** |

**Two structural properties of the template are worth naming**, because both constrain what an entity can do to a report.

First, **Part II is per sub-requirement, not per control.** An entity that has built a beautiful control covering nine requirements still has to see it reported nine times, and each of those nine can be assessed differently. There is no place in the template to argue a control's general excellence.

Second, **Appendix C and Appendix E are the assessor's sections in different ways.** A Compensating Controls Worksheet is drafted by the entity and validated by the assessor, and it appears in the report only if the assessor accepts it. **Appendix E2 is written by the assessor outright**: the entity supplies the controls matrix at E1 and does not write the test that will be applied to it.

## 4. How a Requirement Is Reported in Part II

Every one of the 306 carries four elements, and only the first is a single word.

| Element | Content | Who writes it |
|---|---|---|
| **Assessment finding** | In Place · In Place with Compensating Control · Not Applicable · Not Tested · Not in Place | **The assessor** |
| **Testing procedures performed** | Which of the requirement's defined testing procedures were executed, against which sampled population | **The assessor** |
| **Evidence reference** | The indexed artefacts relied upon, by reference number into the Part I §6 index | **The assessor**, from Marketa's produced evidence |
| **Narrative** | What the assessor observed and why the finding follows from it, including any correction made during the assessment and any limitation on the conclusion | **The assessor** |

Three narrative conventions in this report are Marketa's own doing rather than the template's, and each was decided before fieldwork:

| Convention | Consequence in the report |
|---|---|
| **ADR-0029 — remediation during fieldwork is disclosed, never absorbed** | Each of the **nine in-flight corrections** is recorded in the narrative as *corrected during the assessment*. **1.2.8** is the worked example: two of nine network security control ruleset backups were found on an unrestricted file share, corrected **2026-11-05**, and assessed In Place **with the correction disclosed** |
| **ADR-0004 — no Not Tested findings** | Two requirements are recorded as **Not in Place** rather than avoided, and the Not Tested column contains **0** |
| **Customized approach limitation stated** | The 8.3.9 narrative records that the objective was met **for the assessed period**, and that a customized approach carries **no forward assurance** |

### 4.1 Part I §3 is the longest section, and it is the one an acquirer reads

The scope description is where a Report on Compliance is won or lost, and it is the section a reader outside the programme should turn to first. Every disposition in Part II is a statement about a population, and Part I §3 is where the population is defined.

| What Part I §3 must establish | Marketa's material | Where it came from |
|---|---|---|
| **How the cardholder data environment was identified** | PAN discovery across **1,842 of 1,842** systems, 41 network shares and 6 email archives; **4 unexpected locations found and remediated** | Phase 02 |
| **What reduces the scope, and whether the reduction is valid** | **P2PE** at 1,914 lanes, **tokenization** through Truvance, **DTMF masking** through Cadence — each depending on a provider's validation status, each verified against the published registries rather than on the provider's word | Phases 02 and 06 |
| **How segmentation was validated** | **9 zones**; segmentation validated by penetration test; **the first test failed and the failure is in the report** | Phase 03 |
| **How the assessed population was arrived at** | **604 believed in scope → 71 assessed**, a reduction of **88.2%**, with the reasoning rather than the result | Phase 02 |
| **Why each sample is defensible** | **18 populations**, standardisation demonstrated before sampling, rationale per population, **selection by the assessor** | 08.02 |
| **What was excluded and why** | Systems out of scope, and the annual re-verification that keeps them there | 07.06 |

**A scope description that asserts a reduction without evidencing the mechanism is the defect a competent acquirer looks for.** Marketa's runs long because the reduction is large: an entity claiming an 88.2% reduction is claiming that the requirements do not reach 533 systems, and that claim is only as good as P2PE, tokenization and DTMF masking are.

### 4.2 Where the seventeen observations sit

| Property | Position |
|---|---|
| What they are | Matters the assessor raised that **affected no disposition** — practices that are compliant and improvable, evidence that is sufficient and fragile, and controls that work and depend on one person |
| Where they appear | In the assessor's observations, communicated to Marketa. **They are not findings, they do not appear in Part 4 of the Attestation, and they do not affect the compliance status** |
| What Marketa owes for them | **Nothing contractually. That is precisely the problem** |
| Where they go | **Phase 09.** An observation that reappears the following year as a finding is an observation somebody filed and nobody read |

Twenty-one days from closing meeting to issue, of which **five were the QSA firm's own quality assurance** and three were Marketa's review window. The sequencing is deliberate: **Marketa's comments were received before internal QA ran, not after**, so that the independent reviewer saw the report the entity had already challenged.

### 5.1 What the internal quality assurance is for

Sable Ridge's QA is a firm obligation rather than a courtesy to the client, and it examines the report against the engagement rather than against the entity's preferences.

| QA question | Why the firm asks it |
|---|---|
| Does every finding have evidence indexed against it, and does the evidence support the finding? | A finding with a thin evidence trail is the finding that fails a Council quality review |
| Are the sampling rationales sufficient to justify each sample, and is the sample demonstrably the assessor's selection? | **A sample the entity chose is not a sample** |
| Is every Not Applicable supported by the assessor's own testing rather than the entity's assertion? | Four N/A determinations, four tests, four narratives |
| Are the Appendix C worksheets complete across all six sections, and does each compensating control genuinely go above and beyond other PCI DSS requirements? | The commonest defect in a ROC is a compensating control assembled from requirements the entity already owes |
| Are the Appendix E2 testing procedures the assessor's, and are they capable of establishing whether the objective was met? | An entity-written test at E2 invalidates the customized approach |
| Is the compliance determination consistent with the findings, and is the Part 4 action plan in the AOC consistent with the Not in Place entries? | A non-compliant report with a compliant attestation is the error that ends engagements |

## 6. Marketa's Factual Review — 31 Comments

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Comments raised | **31** |
| **Accepted** — factual corrections | **24** |
| **Declined** | **7** |
| Declined comments that sought to change a **fact** | **0** |
| Declined comments that sought to change a **characterisation** | **7** |

The twenty-four accepted comments were of four kinds, and none of them touched a disposition:

| Kind | Example of the class |
|---|---|
| **Identifier and naming corrections** | A component referred to by its hostname in one narrative and by its inventory identifier in another; a provider's legal entity name given in its trading form |
| **Date corrections** | A policy quoted at its approval date where the effective date was the relevant one for the assessed period |
| **Attribution corrections** | A control described as owned by the security function where the accountable owner is in Store Operations |
| **Population and figure corrections** | A count quoted from an earlier draft of a Marketa register rather than the version in force during the assessed period |

**Every one of the twenty-four was a correction the assessor could verify from evidence already in the index.** That is the test a factual comment has to pass, and it is why they were accepted quickly.

### 6.1 The seven that were declined

All seven sought to change how something was described, not what was described. They are recorded here in the form they were made, because a portfolio that reports only the comments it won is not reporting a review.

| # | What Marketa asked for | Why it was declined |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | That the 8.6.2 narrative describe the credential exposure as *residual* rather than as a requirement not met | The requirement is not met. **A characterisation of severity is not a substitute for a disposition** |
| **2** | That the 11.3.1.2 narrative lead with the accommodating measures before the finding | The finding leads. The measures are recorded, in the narrative, where the assessor put them |
| **3** | That the phrase describing the nine appliances as **contractually** rather than technically constrained be softened, on the ground that it read as criticism of a commercial decision | It is the assessor's determination of the cause, and the determination is the reason the documentation allowance did not reach the nine |
| **4** | That the 1.2.8 in-flight correction be reported without naming the exposure that was corrected | **ADR-0029** governs, and the correction is disclosed with what it corrected |
| **5** | That the Q1 ASV scan failure be described as a scope-management matter rather than a failed scan | It was a failed scan, and it was also a scope-management matter. The report says both |
| **6** | That the 5.3.3 narrative omit the rejected Not Applicable proposal, since the requirement was ultimately In Place | The determination history is part of the assessment record. **A proposal the assessor refused is not deleted by the entity's later compliance** |
| **7** | That the customized approach limitation be expressed as applying to future assessments generally rather than to Marketa's 8.3.9 specifically | The limitation is specific because the objective was evidenced for a specific period on a specific population |

### 6.2 ADR-0030 and the conversation that produced it

> **ADR-0030 — Marketa's review of the ROC is limited to factual accuracy. Characterisation is the assessor's.**

The decision was taken on **2026-11-27**, before the draft arrived on 2026-12-01 — which is the only time it can be taken honestly. It was tested once, on **2026-12-02**, when the General Counsel asked whether the language on the two Not in Place findings could be moderated — a reasonable question from a lawyer reading a document that would be seen by the acquirer.

He was told he could not, on three grounds recorded at the time:

| Ground | Statement |
|---|---|
| **It is not Marketa's report** | The opinion is the assessor's, and an entity that edits an assessor's characterisation has an entity's report with an assessor's name on it |
| **It would not survive contact with the evidence** | Both findings are supported by evidence Marketa produced itself and by determinations Marketa published in **05.07** and **06.05** months earlier. **Softer language in the ROC than in Marketa's own documents is a discrepancy the acquirer can find** |
| **The finding is the point** | The programme's position since Phase 01 is that a non-compliant filing with a dated plan is better than an optimistic one. Softening the language would concede the argument the filing is making |

**He did not press it.** The exchange is recorded because the ADR without the instance is a policy, and the ADR with the instance is a practice.

## 7. Distribution, Classification and Retention

| Question | Position |
|---|---|
| **What is routinely filed with Cardinal** | The **AOC**. The full ROC is provided **on request only** and is not routinely distributed |
| **Who may receive the ROC** | Cardinal Merchant Bank on request; the card brands **through Cardinal**; Marketa's General Counsel. It is not shared with customers, partners or store management |
| **Classification** | Confidential — Cardholder Data Environment. The report contains the component inventory, the network and data-flow descriptions, the sampling detail and two documented weaknesses, and is therefore a target document in its own right |
| **Retention** | ROC, AOC, scan reports, penetration test reports and Compensating Controls Worksheets retained for a **minimum of three years**; underlying evidence per Requirement 10.5.1 and the records policy |
| **Version control** | The **2026-12-11** report is the issued version. The **2027-02-18** revised report is issued as a separate document and **does not replace it** — **ADR-0032** |

### 7.1 The revised report of 2027-02-18

| Field | Position |
|---|---|
| What it is | A **revised Report on Compliance and Attestation of Compliance**, issued the same day as the re-assessment |
| What it covers | **16 sub-requirements re-tested** — the two findings plus 14 the assessor judged could have been affected. The remaining **290** stand on the 2026 assessment |
| What it concludes | **In Place 299 · CCW 3 · N/A 4 · Not Tested 0 · Not in Place 0 = 306. Compliant** |

## 8. What the Report Does Not Establish

| Limitation | Statement |
|---|---|
| **It is a point in time** | The assessment covers a defined period ending **2026-11-20**, across a defined population of **71 components**. It says nothing about 2027, and Marketa's own **14 open items at the report date** are the visible proof that the environment continued to change after the assessor left. **Eleven is the count at the Phase 09 close**, after three close at or before the 2027-02-18 re-assessment |
| **It is not a certification** | **There is no PCI DSS certification for a merchant.** A ROC and an AOC are an attestation about an assessed period, and any document or supplier claiming to certify a merchant against PCI DSS is describing something that does not exist |
| **It binds nobody** | The assessor's opinion **binds neither Cardinal Merchant Bank nor any card brand.** Cardinal reaches its own conclusion, and the brands reach theirs through Cardinal |
| **It is not a legal determination** | **No Attestation of Compliance is a determination of compliance with any law.** PCI DSS is contractual; the obligation reaches Marketa through the merchant agreement |
| **It rests on sampling** | All eighteen populations were sampled or examined in full; **four were sampled at rates between 0.2% and 5.8%** — S-04 at 5.8%, S-05 at 5.0%, S-17 at 4.1% and S-18 at 0.2% — and **08.02 §5 states the rationale for each**. **A sample supports an opinion; it does not enumerate a population** |
| **It rests on evidence Marketa produced** | **186 of 214** evidence requests were satisfied from artefacts that already existed. That is a strength of the programme's record-keeping and it is also a dependency: the assessor tested the evidence, and could not test what was never created |
| **It does not assure the assessed period's future** | The **8.3.9 customized approach** is the sharpest case. The objective was met for the assessed period; it carries **no forward assurance**, must be re-argued at every assessment, and starts from nothing when the assessor rotates in **2028** |
