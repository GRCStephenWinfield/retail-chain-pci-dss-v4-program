## Purpose

The Report on Compliance records **four Not Applicable determinations** out of a population of **306** applicable sub-requirements. Four is a small number, and a small number invites the assumption that the entry was easy to obtain. It was not. Every one of the four was tested by Sable Ridge rather than accepted from Marketa, one of them was actively challenged, and a **fifth determination Marketa proposed was refused outright**.

07.13 §6.2 left this phase an explicit instruction: **name the four, and do not conflate them with the requirements that were never in the 306 in the first place.** This document does that, and it separates four dispositions that entities routinely collapse into one:

| Disposition | What it means | Count in this assessment |
|---|---|---|
| **Not Applicable** | The sub-requirement **is** in the applicable population for a merchant of this shape, and the assessor determined, on tested evidence, that the condition it depends on does not exist in this environment | **4** |
| **Out of population** | The sub-requirement never applied to an entity of this type at all. It is a **service-provider** requirement, or an Appendix that addresses an entity Marketa is not. It was never among the 306 and is therefore **not** an N/A | **14 named** |
| **Superseded** | The sub-requirement existed as an option and was replaced by another as the sole route. The operative requirement is the successor; the predecessor is not assessed and is **not** an N/A | **1** — 6.4.1 → **6.4.2** |
| **Not Tested** | The assessor did not test, and therefore cannot state a result | **0** — **ADR-0004** |

The distinction matters because three of the four are load-bearing in different directions. **Not Applicable is a finding**: the assessor has examined the environment and asserted the absence of a condition, and if the condition later appears the assertion fails. **Out of population is a scoping statement** about the kind of entity Marketa is. **Superseded is a versioning statement** about the standard. **Not Tested is an admission**, and it is the disposition an entity reaches for when it does not like the answer.

## 1. The Four Not Applicable Determinations

| # | Sub-req | Subject | Basis of determination | Test applied by Sable Ridge |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **NA-1** | **1.5.1** | Controls on computing devices that connect to both untrusted networks and the CDE | **No such device exists.** All administrative access to the CDE transits a hardened jump host inside the assessed population, and the jump host has no untrusted-network path | Netflow review across the **15-day fieldwork window** plus egress rule inspection on **all 9 zones**. The claim was tested, not accepted |
| **NA-2** | **3.5.1.1** | Keyed cryptographic hashes used to render PAN unreadable | **The mechanism is not used.** Marketa stores no PAN at rest; the token-to-PAN mapping is Truvance's | Data-store examination against the 02.09 register and the second discovery run; confirmation that no hashing function operates on PAN anywhere in the assessed population |
| **NA-3** | **3.3.2** | Sensitive authentication data stored electronically prior to completion of authorization | **No such data is stored, and no such storage point exists.** **Marketa does not perform authorization** — Verition operates the P2PE decryption environment and hands authorisation to Truvance — and Marketa stores no sensitive authentication data at any point, before or after | Data-flow examination across all three acceptance channels; the P2PE and gateway boundaries traced to the point of authorisation; the second full discovery run across 1,842 systems, 41 shares and 6 archives returning no track, code or PIN block data |
| **NA-4** | **4.2.1.2** | Wireless networks transmitting PAN or connected to the CDE | **No such network exists.** P2PE terminals are wired; store wireless is a separate zone with no CDE path | Wireless inventory reconciled against the segmentation ruleset; the store-fabric zone boundary tested as part of the segmentation evidence |

**Three of the four — 3.5.1.1, 3.3.2 and 4.2.1.2 — are conditional-clause requirements**, opening with a condition ("where X is used", "if Y is present"), and the determination in each is that the condition is not met. **The fourth, 1.5.1, is a factual absence**: the requirement applies unconditionally to any entity that has such a device, and Marketa's position is that it has none.

That difference is worth holding onto, because it changes what the assessor has to do. A conditional-clause N/A is discharged by establishing that a mechanism is not in use. **A factual-absence N/A is discharged only by establishing that something does not exist anywhere in the population**, which is a materially harder assertion and is why NA-1 consumed the most fieldwork time of the four.

### 1.1 NA-1 — 1.5.1, and why an absence claim is the expensive one

1.5.1 addresses the dual-homed device: a computing device that is on an untrusted network and can also reach the cardholder data environment. It is one of the requirements v4 tightened, and it is the one entities most often dispose of by assertion — *we do not allow that* — which is a statement about policy rather than about the network.

Marketa's claim was architectural. Administrative access into the CDE is brokered: every session originates from a hardened jump host on an isolated management VLAN, the jump host has no route to an untrusted network, and no laptop, workstation or mobile device holds a path into the CDE from anywhere else.

| What the assessor did | What it produced |
|---|---|
| Examined the **egress rules on all 9 zones**, not only the CDE perimeter | The claim depends on the absence of a path in either direction; a rule review confined to ingress would have proved nothing |
| Reviewed **netflow across the 15-day fieldwork window** for any flow pairing an untrusted source with a CDE destination via a common intermediate host | The evidence that no device was operating in the prohibited configuration during the observed period |
| Examined the jump host build against **BL-WIN** and the management VLAN's rule set | Confirmed the broker itself is not the dual-homed device the requirement contemplates |
| Interviewed four administrators on how they connect when working remotely | The path an administrator would actually use under pressure is the path that matters, and the answer was consistent across four people asked separately |

The determination is **Not Applicable**, and the assessor's note records the boundary of it: the finding is that no such device existed in the assessed population during the assessed period. **It is not a statement that one cannot be created.** A laptop with a second interface bridged to a CDE segment would make 1.5.1 applicable again on the day it appeared, which is why the annual scope confirmation in 07.06 carries the condition and re-verifies it.

### 1.2 NA-2 — 3.5.1.1, and the requirement that has nothing to operate on

3.5.1 obliges an entity that stores PAN to render it unreadable, and lists the mechanisms. **3.5.1.1 governs one of those mechanisms** — keyed cryptographic hashes — by requiring that the hash be over the entire PAN with associated key-management under Requirement 3.6 and 3.7.

Marketa stores no PAN at rest. The scope work in Phase 02 established it, the second full discovery run across 1,842 systems, 41 network shares, 6 email archives and the restored backup sets returned clean, and the token-to-PAN mapping sits with Truvance Payments as the tokenization provider. There is no hashing function operating on a primary account number anywhere in the assessed population, because there is no primary account number at rest for it to operate on.

**Not Applicable**, on a conditional clause, and the shortest of the four determinations to reach.

### 1.3 NA-3 — 3.3.2, and the requirement that has no storage point to govern

3.3.2 obliges an entity that stores sensitive authentication data electronically **prior to completion of authorization** to encrypt it with strong cryptography. It is one of the requirements a merchant is most often assumed to owe, because most merchants sit somewhere in an authorisation flow.

**Marketa does not.** The payment topology places **Verition** in the P2PE decryption environment, from which authorisation is handed to **Truvance**, which routes to **Cardinal**. Marketa is not a party to the authorisation exchange in any of the three acceptance channels: the card-present path is encrypted at the point of interaction by a SRED-capable PIN pad, the e-commerce path is a hosted checkout iframe served by Truvance, and the call-centre path is masked by Cadence before it reaches an agent desktop.

| What the assessor examined | What it established |
|---|---|
| The data flows for all three acceptance channels, traced to the point at which authorisation completes | **No point in any flow is inside Marketa's environment before authorisation completes** |
| The P2PE solution listing and the Instruction Manual obligations | The encryption boundary begins at the terminal, not at a Marketa system |
| The second full discovery run — 1,842 systems, 41 network shares, 6 email archives and the restored backup sets | **No track data, card verification code or PIN block anywhere in the estate**, before or after authorisation |
| The four unexpected PAN locations from Phase 02, re-examined for sensitive authentication data specifically | All four contained primary account numbers; **none contained sensitive authentication data** |

**Not Applicable**, on a condition that is absent from the environment in both limbs — there is no pre-authorisation storage point, and there is no sensitive authentication data to put in one. The requirement that governs post-authorisation storage of the same data, **3.3.1**, is a different matter and was **tested**: Marketa stores none, and the requirement is In Place.

### 1.4 NA-4 — 4.2.1.2, and the zone that has no path

4.2.1.2 requires that wireless networks transmitting PAN, or connected to the CDE, use industry best practice to implement strong cryptography for authentication and transmission.

| Condition the requirement depends on | Marketa's position | How it was tested |
|---|---|---|
| A wireless network **transmitting PAN** | None. The P2PE point-of-interaction terminals across 1,914 lanes are **wired**. There is no wireless payment path in the store estate | POI connectivity observed directly in the **28 sampled stores** across **96 terminals**; the wired path confirmed at the lane |
| A wireless network **connected to the CDE** | None. Store wireless is a separate zone. There is no route from it into the cardholder data environment | The zone boundary examined in the segmentation ruleset; wireless inventory reconciled against the 1,612 authorised access points maintained under 11.2.2 |

**Not Applicable.** Marketa still owes **11.2.1** — quarterly detection of unauthorised wireless access points across 488 facilities — and **11.2.2**, the maintained inventory of the 1,612 authorised ones. Both are In Place. **An N/A at 4.2.1.2 does not reduce the wireless testing obligation**, and the two are frequently confused: one asks what the wireless network must do if it carries PAN, and the other asks whether an unauthorised one has appeared. The second applies to everybody.

Fourteen named sub-requirements and two Appendices, none of which is an N/A, and none of which appears in the ROC's Not Applicable count. **A requirement that never applied to an entity of this type is out of population, not Not Applicable** — the position 06.13 and 07.13 both stated in advance so that this phase could not quietly improve its own numbers.

### 2.1 Why the distinction is not pedantry

An entity that records service-provider requirements as N/A produces a report with a large Not Applicable column, and the column then has to be defended. Three consequences follow, and all three are avoidable:

| Consequence | Why it bites |
|---|---|
| **The applicable population becomes unstable** | If 12.5.2.1 is "applicable but not applicable", the denominator of every coverage statement in the programme becomes a matter of opinion. **306 is a number the programme has used since Phase 01 and has not adjusted** |
| **The assessor must test each N/A** | Every Not Applicable entry obliges the assessor to document why. Manufacturing eleven extra ones creates eleven extra tests, each of which can fail |
| **It misdescribes the entity** | A merchant whose report is dense with service-provider N/As looks like an entity that does not know which requirements are addressed to it — which is precisely the impression a Level 1 merchant filing its first v4.0.1 assessment cannot afford |

## 3. Superseded — 6.4.1 Is Not an N/A

| Field | Position |
|---|---|
| Predecessor | **6.4.1** — public-facing web applications protected by either an annual and post-change vulnerability assessment **or** an automated technical solution |
| Successor | **6.4.2** — an automated technical solution deployed in front of public-facing web applications, as the **sole route** from 2025-03-31 |
| Disposition | **Superseded.** 6.4.1 is not assessed, is not Not Applicable, and is not among the four |
| Recorded under | **ADR-0010** |
| Consequence for the mapping | Where Phase 03 or Phase 04 documents mapped a control to "6.4.1", the operative requirement in this assessment is **6.4.2**, and the ROC records it there |

The difference between *superseded* and *not applicable* is the difference between a requirement that no longer exists in the version being assessed and a requirement that exists and does not reach this environment. **Marketa's web application firewall is In Place at 6.4.2.** Recording an N/A at 6.4.1 would suggest the control was absent, which is the opposite of the fact.

## 4. Three Exclusions Proposed by Marketa and Refused by the QSA

**Marketa proposed seven exclusions. The assessor accepted four and refused three.** The four accepted are the Not Applicable determinations in §1. The three refused are recorded here, because a document that reports only the exclusions it won is a sales document.

| Proposed exclusion | Marketa's argument | Outcome | Where it ended |
|---|---|---|---|
| **3.5.1.2** — disk-level or partition-level encryption on removable electronic media | Not Applicable; disk encryption is not the 3.5.1 mechanism | **Refused** | **Tested and assessed In Place** |
| **3.5.1.3** — disk-level or partition-level encryption used to render PAN unreadable | Not Applicable; same argument | **Refused** — recorded as **D-2** | **Tested and assessed In Place** |
| **5.3.3** — anti-malware scanning of removable electronic media | Not Applicable; removable media is prohibited by policy | **Refused** — recorded as **D-1** | **Tested and assessed In Place** on the EDR device-control module |

All three ended the same way, and the coincidence is the point: **every requirement Marketa tried to exclude turned out to be met.** The exclusions were not attempts to hide a gap. They were attempts to avoid producing evidence, and in all three cases the evidence existed.

### 4.1 The rule the assessor applied

Stated once, at the closing meeting, and applied to every conditional requirement in the population:

> **A conditional requirement is Not Applicable only where the condition is absent from the assessed environment entirely. Where the condition is present but not used for the purpose the requirement contemplates, the requirement is tested — and the test may pass trivially.**

| Requirement | Is the condition absent from the environment? | Disposition |
|---|---|---|
| **3.5.1.1** — keyed cryptographic hashes | **Yes.** No hashing function operates on a primary account number anywhere in the assessed population | **Not Applicable** |
| **3.3.2** — sensitive authentication data stored before authorization completes | **Yes.** Marketa does not perform authorization and stores no such data at any point | **Not Applicable** |
| **3.5.1.2 · 3.5.1.3** — disk-level and partition-level encryption | **No. Marketa runs disk-level encryption on components inside the assessed population**, and it is visible in the build standards, the endpoint console and the evidence set | **Tested — In Place** |

### 4.2 3.5.1.2 and 3.5.1.3 — the exclusions a good reader will look for

Both sub-requirements open with the same conditional clause, which is exactly why a reader who accepts an N/A at one and not the other should be suspicious of the report. **Marketa proposed both as Not Applicable, on the ground that disk encryption is not how it renders PAN unreadable — there being no stored PAN to render. The assessor declined both.**

| Position | Statement |
|---|---|
| **Marketa's argument** | Disk-level encryption exists for device-loss and media-disposal purposes under 9.4 and the endpoint standard, and is not relied upon anywhere as the means of satisfying 3.5.1 |
| **Why the assessor declined** | **The technology is present in the assessed population.** An assessor who sees full-disk encryption on laptops and servers and accepts an exclusion at the two requirements governing it has taken the entity's characterisation of its own purpose as a substitute for a test |
| **What was tested instead** | That **logical access to the encryption is managed independently of the native operating-system authentication mechanism**, and that **decryption keys are not associated with user accounts** — the two substantive limbs of 3.5.1.3 — and the equivalent evidence for removable media at 3.5.1.2 |
| **Result** | **Both In Place.** The test passed on evidence Marketa already held from the endpoint standard and the key inventory |
| **What it cost** | Two evidence items and part of an afternoon — **less than the exclusion argument had already cost** |

The generalisable sentence, and the one this phase carries: **absence of use is not absence of the thing.** It is the same ruling that defeated the 5.3.3 proposal in §4.3 and the same distinction that defeated the 11.3.1.2 documentation argument at 06.05 §5.4 — a claim about how an entity uses a capability is not a claim that the capability is missing.

### 4.3 5.3.3 — a policy prohibition is not a technical absence

This is the determination the phase is most instructive about, and it is recorded as **D-1**.

| Field | Position |
|---|---|
| Sub-requirement | **5.3.3** — anti-malware scanning, or continuous behavioural analysis, for **removable electronic media** when inserted, connected or logically mounted |
| Marketa's proposal | **Not Applicable.** Removable media is **prohibited by policy**. The acceptable use policy prohibits it, the topic-specific policy repeats it, awareness training covers it, and there is a documented disciplinary consequence |
| Sable Ridge's ruling | **Rejected** |
| The ruling in one sentence | **A policy prohibition is not a technical absence** |
| The evidence that settled it | **USB ports remained enabled on 31 of the 71 assessed components.** A prohibited action that the estate is still capable of performing is a prohibited action, not an impossible one |
| Consequence | 5.3.3 returned to the tested population and assessed **In Place**, on the **EDR device-control module** — which scans on mount, blocks unapproved device classes, and logs every insertion event to the SIEM |
| Effect on the counts | **No change.** 5.3.3 was already inside the 306; it moved from a proposed N/A to a tested In Place. Requirement 5 stands at **21 of 21 In Place** |

### 4.4 Why Marketa made the argument, and why it was wrong

The argument was not frivolous, which is why it is worth recording rather than quietly dropping. Marketa's reasoning ran: the requirement's condition is the use of removable media; Marketa does not use removable media; therefore the condition is not met.

The flaw is in the second step. **"Marketa does not use removable media" was a statement about what Marketa permits, not about what Marketa can do.** The assessor's test was mechanical and took less than an hour:

| Step | What Sable Ridge did | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Asked for the technical enforcement that implements the policy | The endpoint device-control policy was produced |
| 2 | Asked which components the enforcement covered | It covered the managed endpoint estate; it did not cover every component in the assessed population |
| 3 | Examined port and device-class state across the 71 | **31 of 71 had USB mass-storage capability enabled** |
| 4 | Asked what happens if a device is inserted into one of the 31 | **The EDR device-control module scans it** — which is the answer to 5.3.3, arrived at from the opposite direction |

So the requirement was met all along, by a control Marketa had already deployed and had not thought to present, because it had gone looking for an exemption instead. That is the useful lesson and it is stated plainly: **the N/A argument cost more effort than the In Place evidence would have.**

### 4.5 The general rule the ruling establishes

| Claim | Is it an N/A basis? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "The mechanism does not exist in our environment" | **Yes**, if tested — **NA-2, NA-3 and NA-4** are all of this kind | The condition the requirement depends on is factually absent from the assessed population |
| "The device or path does not exist" | **Yes**, if the absence is tested across the whole population — **NA-1** | An absence claim is discharged only by examining everywhere the thing could be |
| "The mechanism exists but we use it for another purpose" | **No** | **This is the argument that failed at 3.5.1.2 and 3.5.1.3.** The requirement is tested and the test may pass trivially, but the entity does not get to characterise its own purpose in place of evidence |
| "We prohibit it" | **No** | A prohibition is a control, and a control is evidence of applicability. **You cannot prohibit something that could not happen** |
| "We have never seen it happen" | **No** | An absence of observations is not an absence of capability, and it is usually an absence of monitoring |
| "The vendor says it is not possible" | **No, without testing** | A vendor statement is a claim about a product; the assessor tests the deployment. This is the same reasoning that defeated the 11.3.1.2 documentation argument at 06.05 §5.4 |

## 5. Not Tested Is Zero, and Why That Is a Decision

| Field | Position |
|---|---|
| Not Tested in this ROC | **0 of 306** |
| The rule | **ADR-0004** — no requirement in this programme is recorded as Not Tested |
| Where it was set | **Phase 01**, ten months before fieldwork, before anybody knew which requirements would be uncomfortable |
| What it cost | **Two Not in Place findings** and a non-compliant Attestation of Compliance |

"Not Tested" is a legitimate ROC disposition. It exists for the case where an assessor genuinely did not test something — most often because it sits outside the assessed scope, or because the entity's environment could not be observed within the assessment window. Used honestly, it is a statement of fact about the assessment.

**Used dishonestly, it is the disposition an entity reaches for when it does not like the answer**, and the pattern is recognisable. A requirement looks likely to fail. Testing it would produce a Not in Place, and a Not in Place produces a non-compliant Attestation. So the requirement is characterised as out of scope, or the population it applies to is redefined until it is empty, or the evidence is not produced and the item quietly drops out of the sample. The report then contains no adverse finding, and it also contains no information.

Three properties of this programme make that route unavailable, and all three were built in advance:

| Property | Where it was set | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **ADR-0004** | Phase 01 | A gap is recorded as a gap. There is no third option to reach for |
| **The 306 is fixed** | Phase 02 addenda | The population cannot be shrunk to exclude an inconvenient requirement, because the denominator is published and has not moved since |
| **The assessor selects the sample** | 08.02, the sampling rationale | **The entity cannot choose what is examined**, so a requirement cannot fall out of the report by not being asked about |

The consequence is visible on the face of the ROC. **Marketa's report contains two Not in Place findings and zero Not Tested entries, and those two facts are the same decision seen from two sides.**

## 6. How the Four Appear in the Report

Each Not Applicable determination is recorded in **Part II** of the ROC against its own requirement, not in a summary table, and each carries three elements the assessor writes rather than the entity:

| Element | Content for a Marketa N/A |
|---|---|
| **The condition that is absent** | Stated in the terms of the requirement — the mechanism, network or device class the requirement depends on |
| **How the assessor established the absence** | The testing action, the population examined, and the period. For NA-1 this is the netflow review and the nine-zone egress inspection; for NA-3 it is the data-flow tracing to the point of authorisation in all three channels; for NA-4 it is the observed wiring at 96 terminals in 28 stores |
| **The reporting instruction** | Recorded as **Not Applicable**, with the assessor's determination narrative. **No N/A in this report is recorded on the entity's assertion alone** |

| Requirement | Sub-reqs assessed | In Place | CCW | **N/A** | Not in Place |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | 26 | 25 | 0 | **1** — 1.5.1 | 0 |
| **3** | 28 | 25 | 1 | **2** — 3.3.2, 3.5.1.1 | 0 |
| **4** | 10 | 9 | 0 | **1** — 4.2.1.2 | 0 |
| **All others** | 242 | 238 | 2 | **0** | 2 |
| **Total** | **306** | **297** | **3** | **4** | **2** |

The four N/A entries fall in **three requirements**, all of them in the data-protection group, and that is not a coincidence: Requirements 1, 3 and 4 are where a scope reduction shows up. Marketa's four Not Applicable determinations are the visible face of the **604 → 71** reduction, and they exist because the primary account number is somewhere else.

## 7. What These Determinations Do Not Establish

| Limitation | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **An N/A is a point-in-time determination about an assessed period** | NA-1 states that no dual-homed device existed during the assessed period across the assessed population. It does not state that one cannot exist tomorrow, and the annual scope confirmation is the control that re-verifies it |
| **Two of the four depend on the payment topology holding** | **NA-2** is downstream of the fact that Marketa stores no PAN; **NA-3** is downstream of the fact that Marketa is not a party to authorisation and stores no sensitive authentication data. **If either changed — stored PAN reappearing, or an acceptance channel moving authorisation inside Marketa's environment — the determination would fail** , which is why 12.10.7, the second discovery run and the **C7** material-change notification are the controls that protect them |
| **An N/A does not reduce a neighbouring obligation** | 4.2.1.2 Not Applicable leaves **11.2.1** detection and **11.2.2** inventory fully applicable; 3.5.1.1 Not Applicable leaves the whole of Requirement 3's key-management population where it was; and **3.3.2 Not Applicable leaves 3.3.1 tested and In Place** |
| **The out-of-population list is a statement about entity type, not about good practice** | Marketa performs the substance of **3.6.1.1, 12.4.1 and 12.4.2 voluntarily**, and each is recorded as voluntary so that a later reader cannot mistake a discretionary practice for an obligation, or drop it believing it was one |
| **The three refusals could recur** | Any requirement disposed of by a policy prohibition, or by a claim about how a present technology is used, is exposed to the same test. The estate still has **31 of 71 components with USB mass-storage capability enabled**, and the control that answers 5.3.3 is the EDR module rather than the policy |
