## Purpose

Fifteen business days, four assessors, four locations and twenty-eight stores. This document records what happened during the on-site assessment: the schedule, the evidence actually gathered by class, the forty-seven interviews, the store sweep, the nine deficiencies found and corrected while the assessor was still in the building, the seventeen observations that changed nothing about any disposition, the four determinations where Sable Ridge's position and Marketa's differed, and the closing meeting.

It is deliberately not a results document. **08.05 carries the dispositions.** What is here is the process that produced them, including the parts of it that were uncomfortable — two of the four disputes went against Marketa, and one of those two was a control Marketa had asserted was In Place and was not.

**The store sweep is the last thing an assessor should do and the first thing an entity wants to schedule**, because it is the most logistically painful. Sable Ridge set the order and Marketa accommodated it.

### 3.2 What the interviews found

Nothing that changed a disposition, and two things worth recording.

**Store personnel described the device inspection procedure in their own words rather than in the procedure's words.** That is the outcome an entity wants and it is not the outcome an entity gets by circulating a document. It is the product of the seven communication methods reaching a population that is 87% deskless, and of a POI-specific module completed at 98.1%.

**Two interviewees, asked what they would do on finding account data where it should not be, answered with the negative instruction first** — report it, and change nothing. That phrasing exists in the awareness content because of a Phase 07 finding in which two of nine call-centre agents said they would have deleted a recording containing spoken account data. The interviews are the first external evidence that the amendment took.

### 5.1 Store 0417

The site of the failed segmentation test in May 2026, included in the sample by the assessor's own selection. The assessor tested the remediated trunk configuration from a position on the guest network, examined the switch configuration against the intended state, and reviewed the change record and re-test report. **The remediation held.** The value of the visit was not that it passed; it was that the pass was observed at the site rather than inferred from a re-test report written six months earlier.

### 5.2 The two witnessed events

**The witnessed POI inspection.** At a sampled store, a member of the store team performed a 9.5.1 inspection under the documented procedure while the assessor observed. The team member checked the serial number against the register, examined the tamper-evident seals, checked the cable runs and the device housing, and recorded the result. She had not been told the inspection would be observed and did not consult the procedure document during it. **That is the evidence the quarterly inspection cycle across 1,914 terminals cannot itself provide**: whether the person holding the checklist knows what they are looking at.

**The witnessed restore.** Performed during Week 2 rather than in the store estate, and recorded here because it is the other half of the same principle. The assessor selected a backup set, required the restoration to be performed in his presence, and verified the restored data himself. Marketa's evidence includes five backup sets proved restorable; **a record of a restore is not a restore**, and the request that produced this observation was one of the six re-requests in 08.03 §5.3.

### 5.3 What the store sweep did not establish

Twenty-eight stores is 5.8% of the estate. The conclusion drawn is about the population and it rests on the build standardisation argument in 08.02 §7.2, not on the number 28. **No store-level finding across 28 stores is the outcome a quarterly inspection cycle across all 1,914 terminals is designed to produce**, and reporting it as an achievement would be reporting that a control worked as specified.

### 6.1 What the nine have in common

Eight of the nine are **evidence and record-keeping defects on controls that were operating**. Only IFC-1 is a control defect: the backups genuinely sat where they should not have, and anyone with access to that share could have read the ruleset configuration of two of the nine network security controls.

That distribution is worth stating rather than hiding behind, and it cuts both ways.

| Reading | Position |
|---|---|
| The optimistic reading | Seven phases of building produced an environment where the assessor's findings were overwhelmingly about the record rather than the control |
| **The uncomfortable reading** | **Eight of nine defects were invisible to Marketa's own assurance until an assessor looked.** Internal audit reviewed the programme in October and raised seven recommendations, none Critical; none of these nine was among them |
| The correct reading | Both. A record defect on an operating control is a small thing that becomes a large thing at the moment somebody needs the record — which is what a 9.4.7 certificate, an inspection log signature and a review attestation all are |

### 6.2 The alternative Marketa did not take

Every one of the nine could have been left uncorrected until after the assessment, producing nine findings and a cleaner-looking sequence of remediation dates. Or seven of them could have been corrected quietly and reported as unqualified In Place entries.

**ADR-0029 removes both options.** The correction happens, and the correction is disclosed.

## 7. The Four Disputed Determinations

Four determinations where Sable Ridge's initial position and Marketa's differed. **One went Marketa's way and three did not.**

A determination log in which the entity wins most of the arguments is a log kept by the entity. Marketa proposed **seven exclusions** across the assessment; **four were accepted and three were refused**, and the three refusals are the reason the four that survived are worth anything.

| # | Item | Initial position | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| D-1 | **5.3.3** | Marketa proposed N/A | **Rejected.** Returned to population, assessed In Place |
| D-2 | **3.5.1.2 and 3.5.1.3** | Marketa proposed Not Applicable | **Declined.** Absence of use is not absence of the thing. Both tested and assessed In Place |
| D-3 | **10.4.2** monthly review of 11 components | QSA held it should be daily | **Held** — the assessor tested TRA-10.4.2.1's reasoning, not its conclusion |
| D-4 | **1.2.8** | Marketa asserted In Place | **Marketa was wrong.** Two of nine backups exposed; corrected in flight and disclosed |

### 7.1 D-1 — 5.3.3, and the rule it produced

Marketa proposed that **5.3.3** — anti-malware scanning of removable electronic media — was Not Applicable, on the ground that removable media is prohibited by policy.

The QSA rejected it, and the ruling is the most quotable thing in the assessment: **a policy prohibition is not a technical absence.** The prohibition is real, it is in the acceptable use policy, and it is communicated. It is also enforced by people, and the requirement is about what happens when the prohibition is not observed.

The assessor then tested the claim rather than arguing it, and found that **USB ports remained enabled on 31 of the 71** assessed components. A policy prohibiting the use of removable media on 31 components that will accept removable media describes an intention, not an environment.

5.3.3 returned to the tested population and was assessed **In Place**, on the EDR platform's device-control module, which scans removable media on connection across the estate. **Marketa lost the argument and gained a requirement it already met.** Had the N/A been accepted, the applicable population would have been 305 with a control operating unassessed inside it, and the entity would have talked itself out of credit for a control it was already paying for.

### 7.2 D-2 — 3.5.1.2 and 3.5.1.3, where Marketa argued for the weaker outcome

Marketa proposed **3.5.1.2 and 3.5.1.3 as Not Applicable**. Both carry the same conditional opener — *if disk-level or partition-level encryption is used to render primary account numbers unreadable* — and Marketa's case was that the condition is not satisfied.

**The case was a good one, and it is worth setting out properly rather than as a strawman.** Marketa stores no primary account numbers at rest; the token-to-PAN mapping is Truvance's. Full-disk encryption exists on laptops and servers as a device and media protection control, deployed years before the programme and for reasons that have nothing to do with account data. It therefore renders nothing unreadable *for the purposes of 3.5.1*, because there is nothing at rest for it to render. On that reading the requirement's condition is unmet and both sub-requirements fall away. **This is the purpose-not-presence argument, and most entities in Marketa's position make it.**

**Sable Ridge declined it**, on the same reasoning that produced the 5.3.3 rejection eight days earlier and stated as a single rule applied across the assessment:

> **A conditional requirement is Not Applicable only where the condition is absent from the assessed environment entirely. Where the condition is present but not used for the purpose the requirement contemplates, the requirement is tested — and the test may pass trivially.**

Disk-level encryption is **not absent**. It runs on components inside the assessed population, and an assessor who accepts an entity's account of what a present technology is *for* has accepted an assertion about intent in place of a test. So both were tested: logical access to the encryption is managed independently of native operating system authentication, and decryption keys are not associated with user accounts. **3.5.1.2 and 3.5.1.3 were assessed In Place.**

That outcome is better for Marketa than the one Marketa asked for, and the programme records it in those terms because the point is easily missed. **In Place is a stronger statement than Not Applicable.** A Not Applicable entry says a requirement never engaged; an In Place entry says it engaged and was met on evidence. **Marketa argued for the weaker outcome and was given the stronger one** — and the four Not Applicable determinations that survived are stronger for the two that did not, because each of the four now rests on a condition that is genuinely absent rather than merely unused.

The paired lesson sits with **NA-3**. Requirement **3.3.2** is Not Applicable because Marketa does not perform authorization at all — Verition operates the P2PE decryption environment and hands authorisation to Truvance — and stores no sensitive authentication data at any point. **That is an absence. Full-disk encryption is not.** Requirement 3 therefore keeps two Not Applicable entries and the applicable population is unchanged at 28.

### 7.3 D-3 — 10.4.2, and the difference between testing a conclusion and testing an analysis

**10.4.2** obliges logs of all other system components — those not in the daily 10.4.1 population — to be reviewed periodically, at a frequency defined by a targeted risk analysis under 12.3.1. Marketa's **TRA-10.4.2.1** determined **monthly**, risk-weighted by component class, for **11 of 71** components, with the other 60 reviewed daily.

The assessor's initial position was that the eleven should be reviewed daily.

What resolved it is the point of the entry. **The assessor did not test whether monthly was the right answer. He tested whether the analysis that produced it was performed.** The six mandatory elements were present: the assets, the threats in Marketa's environment rather than in general, the frequency with an explicit examination of a shorter one, the trigger set, the detective backstop covering the gap between reviews, and the determination with an accepting authority and a review date. The trigger set included the specific condition that would invalidate the conclusion — any component in the monthly population producing a detection the daily population would have caught within hours — and it had not fired.

**Held.** A 12.3.1 analysis justifies a number, and an assessor's role is to establish that the number has an analysis behind it, not to substitute his own preference for the entity's. Had the analysis been absent, boilerplate, or silent on the shorter alternative, the outcome would have been the opposite.

### 7.4 D-4 — 1.2.8, where Marketa was wrong

Marketa asserted **1.2.8** In Place. Configuration files for network security controls are to be secured from unauthorised access and kept consistent with active configurations.

**Two of the nine** ruleset backups were on an unrestricted file share. The assertion was wrong. It was not wrong through carelessness about the requirement — the version-controlled intended configurations were correctly held, the consistency limb was met, and seven of the nine backups were in the right place — it was wrong because nobody had examined where the backups actually were since a storage migration.

Corrected **2026-11-05**, re-tested by the assessor, and assessed **In Place with the correction disclosed** under ADR-0029.

The programme records D-4 as the most useful of the four, because it is the only one that changed the environment. D-1, D-2 and D-3 changed a determination on the page. D-4 moved two files.

### 7.5 The pattern

| Dispute | Who prevailed | On what |
|---|---|---|
| D-1 | **The assessor** | A tested fact — 31 of 71 components with enabled ports — against a policy assertion |
| D-2 | **The assessor** | The presence of a technology in the assessed population, against the entity's account of what that technology is for |
| D-3 | **Marketa** | The presence and quality of a documented analysis, not the attractiveness of its conclusion |
| D-4 | **The assessor** | A system-evidence read against an entity's belief about itself |

**Every one of the four was decided on evidence and none on argument.** The one Marketa won was won with an artefact produced six months earlier by a control that was operating — a targeted risk analysis nobody was compelled to write in that form. The three it lost were lost to things the assessor looked at directly: enabled ports on 31 components, full-disk encryption running on assessed hosts, and two backup files on the wrong share.

**Three of four going against the entity is the healthier result**, and the programme prefers to say so rather than present a scoreline. Two of the three cost Marketa nothing — 5.3.3 and 3.5.1.2/.3 all came back as In Place — and the third moved two files out of an unrestricted share. **An assessment in which the entity's proposals are mostly upheld is an assessment in which the entity did the assessor's thinking.**
