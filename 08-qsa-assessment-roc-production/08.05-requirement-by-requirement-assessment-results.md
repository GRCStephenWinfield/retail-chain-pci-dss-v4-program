## Purpose

Three hundred and six applicable sub-requirements, assessed across fifteen business days against seventy-one system components, four hundred and eighty-two stores and three payment channels. This document carries the result of every one of them.

It opens with the master disposition table exactly as it appears in the Report on Compliance, states why the **Not Tested** column is zero and what that cost, records that **fifty-one future-dated requirements were in force for the first time** in Marketa's history and names the ones that consumed the most assessor effort, and then walks the twelve requirements one at a time — what the assessor actually tested, what the evidence was, and which phase of the programme built it.

**08.06** carries the customized approach at 8.3.9 in full. **08.07** carries the three compensating controls. **08.08** carries the four Not Applicable determinations and the superseded 6.4.1. This document states their outcomes and points at them; it does not reproduce them.

**306 is the applicable population, not the standard.** Requirements that never applied to a Level 1 merchant of this shape — 3.6.1.1, 8.3.10, 8.3.10.1, 10.7.1, 11.4.6, 11.5.1.1, 12.4.1, 12.4.2, 12.5.2.1, 12.5.3, 12.9.1, 12.9.2, Appendix A1 and Appendix A3 — **were never within the 306 and are not among the four Not Applicable entries.** A requirement that never applied to an entity of this type is out of population, not N/A, and conflating the two inflates the applied population and misdescribes the assessment.

## 2. Not Tested Is Zero

Every one of the 306 was tested or determined inapplicable on evidence. **Nothing was deferred, scoped away, or left in a column that requires no conclusion.**

That is **ADR-0004**, taken in Phase 01 before anybody knew what it would cost, and it cost exactly what an honest rule costs: two Not in Place findings on the face of the Attestation of Compliance, and an Attestation filed as **non-compliant**.

| The rule | The consequence |
|---|---|
| No requirement may be recorded as Not Tested | Both known gaps had to be assessed rather than avoided |
| No gap may be presented as anything other than what it is | A compensating control was drafted for each and **refused** in both cases rather than argued into acceptance |
| The rule binds before the outcome is known | Adopted 2026-01, applied 2026-11 |

**A "Not Tested" entry is the compliance equivalent of an unaudited balance.** It converts an unknown into a blank space in a report that a reader will assume was populated. The programme's position from the first month was that a merchant who cannot say whether a requirement is met has said something about the requirement, and it is not favourable.

Three further disciplines follow from the same rule and are recorded here because each was a live temptation during fieldwork.

**The fifth Not Applicable was proposed and rejected.** Marketa proposed N/A at **5.3.3**. The QSA rejected it — §5.5 — and the requirement returned to the tested population. Had the programme been willing to hold requirements outside the population, it would have had 305 applicable and one control operating unassessed.

**Neither Not in Place finding was reframed.** Both were available to be argued into a documentation allowance or a compensating control, and both refusals are on the record with their reasoning.

**No requirement was assessed on a shorter operating period than it actually had.** Where the operating window is short — 11.6.1's 140 days, the eleven register movements resting on one cycle each — the window is stated in the ROC rather than implied.

## 3. Fifty-One Future-Dated Requirements, In Force for the First Time

v4.0 introduced **64 new requirements — 13 effective immediately and 51 future-dated to 2025-03-31.** Marketa validated as Compliant in 2023 and 2024 under v3.2.1. **2026 is its first assessment under v4.0.1 and the first with all fifty-one in force.**

This is the single most important framing fact about the whole assessment. Every one of the fifty-one is a requirement with no prior assessment history at this entity, no established evidence pattern, and — for the assessor — no previous year's ROC to compare against.

The thirteen that carried the most assessor effort:

| Requirement | What it now requires | Where the effort went |

That is not coincidence. A requirement in force for the first time is a requirement no vendor roadmap, no support agreement and no legacy integration was designed against.

## 4. The Twelve Requirements

### 4.1 Requirement 1 — Install and Maintain Network Security Controls

**26 applicable · 25 In Place · 1 Not Applicable · 0 Not in Place**

The assessor examined the **nine network security control rulesets**, one per zone, in full and without sampling — nine zones, nine distinct configurations, and a sample would have left zones untested. Each ruleset was read at the console during Week 1, compared against the version-controlled intended configuration, and reconciled to the business justification for every rule. Rule-level justification is the limb entities most often fail, and the assessor tested it by selecting rules rather than accepting the register. The six-monthly ruleset reviews under 1.2.7 were examined for both cycles, with the reviewer's decisions visible rather than a completion summary. Network diagrams and the account-data flow diagram were tested against the seven flows re-traced from observed captures at the October scope delta rather than against the February drawings — **the diagram limb of 1.2.3 and 1.2.4 is an accuracy requirement, and a current diagram of a stale environment fails it.** The segmentation position built in **Phase 03** carried Requirement 1's most consequential testing: the assessor tested reachability from assessor-chosen positions, examined the drift assertions DA-1 to DA-6 across the period, and visited **store 0417**, the site of the May 2026 segmentation failure, to test the remediated trunk configuration at the store rather than in a change record.

**1.2.8 is the requirement Marketa got wrong** — two of nine ruleset backups on an unrestricted file share, found 2026-11-04, corrected 2026-11-05, re-tested by the assessor, assessed **In Place with the correction disclosed** under ADR-0029. It is **D-4** in 08.04 §7.4.

**1.5.1 is Not Applicable** — no computing device connects both to an untrusted network and to the CDE. The assessor **tested the claim rather than accepting it**, through netflow review across the fifteen-day window and egress rule inspection on all nine zones. 08.08 carries the determination in full.

### 4.2 Requirement 2 — Apply Secure Configurations

**16 applicable · 15 In Place · 1 Compensating Control · 0 Not in Place**

**Phase 04** built six configuration baselines — BL-WIN 18 · BL-LNX 15 · BL-NET 11 · BL-AWS 6 · BL-DB 3 · BL-APP 12, plus six un-baselined SaaS control planes, totalling the 71. The assessor examined the baselines themselves against the applicable industry hardening standards, then examined configuration on the 42 sampled components against the baseline each belonged to, then examined the drift detection that keeps them there. Vendor default accounts and settings under 2.2.2 were tested by attempted authentication with default credentials on sampled components rather than by examining a checklist. The six un-baselined control planes were examined individually — they are the weakest standardisation argument in the estate and were not treated as a sampled group. Wireless defaults under 2.3.1 and 2.3.2 were tested at sampled stores during the Week 3 sweep, where the store wireless zone is separate from the store back-office and carries no CDE path.

**2.2.7 carries CCW-02.** Non-console administrative access to the nine vendor-locked back-office appliances cannot be encrypted with strong cryptography, because the management interface supports no protocol that offers it. The compensating control — management reachable only from a single hardened jump host on an isolated management VLAN, full session recording, no other route, monthly rule attestation — was **accepted**, and the assessor tested it by attempting to reach the management interface from two positions he selected. 08.07 carries the worksheet.

### 4.3 Requirement 3 — Protect Stored Account Data

**28 applicable · 25 In Place · 1 Compensating Control · 2 Not Applicable · 0 Not in Place**

Requirement 3 is where **Phase 02's** work is assessed, and the assessment of it is mostly the assessment of an absence. Marketa stores no primary account numbers at rest; the token-to-PAN mapping is Truvance's. The assessor tested that absence rather than accepting it: he examined the PAN discovery methodology across **1,842 systems, 41 network shares and 6 email archives**, examined the four unexpected-PAN findings and their remediation, and examined the **second full discovery run that returned zero**, including the restored backup sets. Data retention and disposal under 3.2.1 were examined against the retention schedule and the destruction certificates. Truncation, masking and display controls under 3.3 and 3.4.1 were observed in the applications that display truncated account numbers — the chargeback application, the settlement reconciliation views and the call-centre desktop. Cryptographic key management under 3.6 and 3.7 was examined against the ten key classes K-01 to K-10 and the per-termination-point inventory, with **3.7.7** (prevention of unauthorised key substitution) and **3.7.8** (key custodian acknowledgement) examined against signed acknowledgements.

**3.4.2 carries CCW-03** — technical controls preventing copy or relocation of account data during remote access. Six disputes analysts reach one legacy published application in the virtual desktop estate that cannot enforce clipboard and print blocking. **3.5.1.1 and 3.3.2 are Not Applicable** — keyed cryptographic hashes are not used anywhere, and Marketa does not perform authorization at all, so no sensitive authentication data exists to be stored prior to it. **3.5.1.2 and 3.5.1.3 were proposed as Not Applicable and declined — D-2 — and both were tested and assessed In Place**, on evidence that logical access to the disk-level encryption is managed independently of native operating system authentication and that decryption keys are not associated with user accounts. **A conditional requirement is Not Applicable only where the condition is absent from the environment entirely**, and full-disk encryption is present on assessed components whatever it was deployed for.

### 4.4 Requirement 4 — Protect Account Data with Strong Cryptography During Transmission

**10 applicable · 9 In Place · 1 Not Applicable · 0 Not in Place**

The assessor examined the **4.2.1.1 trusted key and certificate inventory** — maintained per termination point rather than per issuer, which is the property that lets an entity answer "where do we use it" in hours — and tested the enforced protocol and cipher suite position on sampled termination points rather than accepting the inventory. **TLS 1.2 and TLS 1.3 only**; TLS 1.0 and 1.1 were removed in the period as a SIG-7 significant change, and the assessor examined the change record and confirmed the removal by test. Certificate validity, trust and expiry monitoring were examined across the inventory. **4.2.2** — account data sent by end-user messaging technologies — was examined against Marketa's outright prohibition, and the ROC records the position accurately: the requirement obliges such transmissions to be secured with strong cryptography and does not literally prohibit them, so **Marketa's prohibition is a stricter policy choice** and is described as one. Enforcement was tested against the DLP controls on the messaging estate.

**4.2.1.2 is Not Applicable** — no wireless network transmits account data or connects to the CDE. P2PE terminals are wired; store wireless is a separate zone with no CDE path. Tested at sampled stores during the Week 3 sweep, not accepted from a diagram.

### 4.5 Requirement 5 — Protect All Systems and Networks from Malicious Software

**21 applicable · 21 In Place · 0 exceptions**

**Phase 04** delivered the anti-malware position and **v4.0.1 inverts the v3.2.1 default**: coverage is required everywhere except components a **5.2.3** evaluation excuses. The assessor examined the evaluation, the components it excused, and **TRA-01**, which sets the periodic re-evaluation frequency at six-monthly with event triggers — a cadence the Phase 01 calendar had assumed annual and which was amended when the analysis concluded otherwise. Deployment, currency and scanning behaviour were examined on sampled components and observed operating. **TRA-02** sets the periodic scan frequency where continuous behavioural analysis is not in use.

**5.3.3 is the requirement this assessment will be remembered for internally.** Marketa proposed it Not Applicable on the ground that removable media is prohibited by policy. The QSA rejected the proposal on a principle stated once and applied consistently — **a policy prohibition is not a technical absence** — and then tested the claim rather than debating it, finding **USB ports enabled on 31 of the 71** assessed components. The requirement returned to the tested population and was assessed **In Place** on the EDR platform's device-control module, which scans removable media on connection. Marketa lost the argument and gained an assessed control it was already paying for. **D-1** in 08.04 §7.1.

**5.4.1** — automated mechanisms to detect and protect against phishing — is a future-dated requirement with no v3.2.1 ancestor, and the assessor examined the mechanisms operating on the messaging estate together with the reporting path and the response, rather than treating the awareness programme as the control.

### 4.6 Requirement 6 — Develop and Maintain Secure Systems and Software

**36 applicable · 36 In Place · 0 exceptions**

The largest applicable population of any requirement and the one where **Phase 04** did the most work. The assessor examined the **6.3.1** vulnerability identification and ranking model, including its two hard override rules and the environment-aware modifiers that downgraded 74 findings and upgraded 31 in the period, and tested the ranking against sampled findings rather than accepting the model description. **6.3.2** — the inventory of bespoke, custom and third-party software components — was examined against the software bill of materials, which repeatedly identified affected applications faster than the scanner did. **6.3.3** patch timeframes were examined against the elective analysis **TRA-11**, reconciled with **TRA-07** so that two entity-defined timeframes cannot disagree. **6.5** change control was tested through the 45 sampled change records, twelve of them significant under SIG-1 to SIG-10, with the assessor testing classification as well as process — **zero exceptions across 45**. **6.5.5** — live account data never used for testing — was examined against the test data controls in 04.07.

**6.4.2 is the operative requirement, not 6.4.1**, whose option was replaced as the sole route from 2025-03-31 and which the ROC records as superseded under ADR-0010. The assessor examined the automated technical solution deployed in front of the public-facing web applications, its detection and prevention configuration, and its operating record.

**6.4.3** is the requirement that consumed the most effort in this requirement family. The assessor did not accept the script inventory: he loaded all **6 payment page templates** himself and enumerated the scripts executing in the browser, then reconciled his enumeration to the **38-script inventory SCR-01 to SCR-38**, examined the written authorisation and business justification for each, and examined the integrity assurance method. The programme's own history is in the evidence: **3 of 38 scripts were unauthorised at first inventory**, injected by a marketing tag manager, and were removed. Two scripts remain served from a third-party origin rather than the Marketa tag proxy and are tracked as OI-06-03 — recorded as **OBS-03**, affecting no disposition.

### 4.7 Requirement 7 — Restrict Access by Business Need to Know

**17 applicable · 17 In Place · 0 exceptions**

**Phase 05** built the access model and the assessor tested it against the role catalogue rather than against individual grants. The **24 role definitions** were examined in full — the catalogue is the control, and sampling it would test the sample — with each role's entitlement set traced to a documented business need and an approving authority. Default-deny under 7.2.5 and 7.3 was tested on sampled systems by attempting access outside a role's entitlement. Two in-flight corrections landed here: **IFC-4**, an account whose owner field had reverted to a team address, and **IFC-5**, a review packet closed without the reviewer's attestation recorded. Both corrected during fieldwork and disclosed.

### 4.8 Requirement 8 — Identify Users and Authenticate Access

**46 applicable · 44 In Place · 1 Compensating Control · 1 Not in Place**

The largest and most consequential requirement in the assessment. It carries a compensating control, the customized approach, and one of the two findings.

The assessor examined unique identification under 8.2.1 across the 42 sampled components and the assessor-directed enumeration under CTP-1, group and shared account governance under 8.2.2 against the **20 shared accounts** — the 11 enterprise break-glass identities with their dual-custody construction, plus the **9 appliance built-in administrative accounts** the assessor reconciled into the population, recorded as **OBS-05** — and the joiner-mover-leaver process under 8.2.4 and 8.2.5 — including the **6,800-person seasonal intake**, which is the population an annual assessment most easily misses and which exists in November. **8.3.6**'s twelve-character minimum was tested by attempted set rather than by policy examination. On the nine vendor-locked appliances — **examined in full under S-12, not sampled** — the assessor attempted the set on the **built-in local administrative account** of each and confirmed the 10-character cap on that account's firmware-resident definition, while separately confirming that the appliance **user realm** on the same devices enforces the 16-character minimum applied to the 36 named engineer accounts. **CCW-01 covers the nine built-in accounts and nothing else.**

**8.4.2 — MFA for all access into the CDE** — is future-dated, in force for the first time, and was tested across **all 20 access paths** rather than sampled, because a path not covered is the path that matters. **8.5.1**'s MFA implementation properties — not susceptible to replay, no bypass without documented exception, at least two distinct factors, all factors required before access — were tested by observation of live authentications rather than by policy examination.

**8.3.9 is met via the customized approach.** Sable Ridge executed the twelve testing procedures it derived, across the **61 password-only accounts on 19 components**. **08.06 carries it in full**, including the two procedures that required a second evidence round.

**8.6.2 is Not in Place.** Hard-coded credentials remain in two legacy batch integrations — `batch-icq` on CDE-05 in a Korn shell wrapper, `gl-extract` on CDE-04 in a Java properties file. The assessor read both files. **A compensating control was drafted and refused in June 2026** on the ground that the intent of 8.6.2 is that the secret is not present in a readable file, and no arrangement of permissions, monitoring, alerting or rotation makes a secret absent from a file it is in. The interim measures — restricted repository, file integrity monitoring, single-source-host restriction, out-of-window alerting, monthly rotation under two-person change control — produced **0 unauthorised file reads and 0 out-of-window authentications** in the period, reduce the risk, and do not meet the requirement. **R-17 remains High 16.** Remediation **2027-01-31**; re-assessment **2027-02-18**.

**8.6.1 and 8.6.3 are In Place**: interactive use prevented on 136 of 142 and verified quarterly by attempted login, six documented exceptions with identity confirmed before credential release and full attribution across nineteen recorded activations; and **TRA-8.6.3** setting frequency and complexity per account class, with 214 rotations executed and 3 automatic rollbacks.

### 4.11 Requirement 11 — Test Security of Systems and Networks Regularly

**23 applicable · 22 In Place · 1 Not in Place**

**11.3.1.2 is Not in Place.** Authenticated internal scanning is complete on **62 of 71** components and impossible on the **nine vendor-locked back-office appliances**. ### 4.12 Requirement 12 — Support Information Security with Organisational Policies and Programs

**21 applicable · 21 In Place · 0 exceptions**

**Phase 07** built the whole of it, and the assessor examined it in Week 1 rather than last — a policy set examined at the end of an assessment is examined against conclusions already reached. The policy architecture was examined as published and disseminated rather than as authored: one overarching policy approved by the Chief Executive Officer, fourteen topic-specific policies, three programme standards and sixty-one operational procedures, reaching a population that is **87% deskless** through four dissemination mechanisms, with the annual review's **nine amendments and seventeen sections deliberately unchanged with reasons**.

**12.3.1** was the effort centre. All **fourteen targeted risk analyses** were examined individually against six mandatory elements — the asset and consequence, the threats in Marketa's environment rather than in general, the frequency **with an explicit examination of a shorter one**, the trigger set, the detective backstop, and the determination with an accepting authority and a review date.
