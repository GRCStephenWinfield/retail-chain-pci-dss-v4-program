## Purpose

An assessment is an evidence exercise wearing the clothes of a control exercise. The controls were built between January and July. What happened in November was that somebody asked, two hundred and fourteen times, for proof that they existed and operated — and the programme's whole evidence architecture existed to make the answer to most of those questions a retrieval rather than a project.

This document records the evidence repository built across seven phases, the assessor request log and its outcome, what the twenty-eight newly-produced items were and why none of them could have been prepared in advance, the four classes of evidence PCI DSS testing procedures draw on and the strict limits of each, the retention and confidentiality position over an evidence set that describes the cardholder data environment in operational detail, and how evidence is referenced inside the Report on Compliance.

The headline is a ratio: **186 of 214 requests — 86.9% — were satisfied from artefacts that already existed.** That number is the payoff for seven phases of building evidence at the same time as controls, and it is stated here with its limits attached, because a high retrieval rate is a statement about preparation and not about compliance.

## 1. The Evidence Repository

### 1.1 What it is

| Attribute | Position |
|---|---|
| Origin | Established in Phase 01 alongside the programme charter, not assembled in Phase 08 |
| Structure | Indexed by **requirement**, by **system component**, by **control identifier** and by **date**, so that the same artefact is reachable from four directions |
| Contents | The seven phases' narrative documents; configuration exports; scan and rescan reports; penetration test reports and retests; access review packets; change records; training and screening records; provider agreements and responsibility matrices; incident records; targeted risk analyses; the risk register and its movement history; policy versions and approvals |
| Custody | Owen Castellanos, with a named deputy. **One custodian, one index** |
| Assessor access | Read-only accounts for the Sable Ridge engagement team, provisioned for the duration of the assessment and revoked on issue of the ROC |
| Provenance | Every artefact carries its source system, the date produced, the person who produced it, and — where it is an export — the query or command that produced it |
| Integrity | Artefacts are immutable once indexed. A corrected artefact is a new version with the superseded one retained |

The provenance rule is the one that earns its keep at fieldwork. **An assessor confronted with a spreadsheet of user accounts asks where it came from, and an entity that cannot answer has produced a spreadsheet rather than evidence.** Every export in Marketa's repository carries the command or query that generated it, which converts the question from a discussion into a re-run.

### 1.2 Built as the controls were built

The seven phases each closed with their evidence indexed rather than described, and the effect compounds.

| Phase | What it deposited |
|---|---|
| **01** | The charter, the scope assumptions and constraints **CON-01 to CON-03**, the acquirer obligation set **C1 to C10**, the compliance calendar, **ADR-0001 to ADR-0004** |
| **02** | The PAN discovery results across 1,842 systems, 41 shares and 6 archives; the four unexpected-PAN findings; the 604-to-71 derivation; the thirty-five conditions; the 71-component register |
| **03** | The nine-zone segmentation design; the failed segmentation test and its re-test; the drift assertions **DA-1 to DA-6**; the 44-entry risk register baseline |
| **04** | Six configuration baselines across 71 components; the vulnerability ranking model; change control and **SIG-1 to SIG-10**; the 6.4.3 script inventory **SCR-01 to SCR-38** |
| **05** | The access control model, the 20 MFA-enforced paths, the access review cycles, the 142 application and system accounts, the 8.3.9 customized approach and its controls matrix, the 9.5.1 inspection programme across 1,914 terminals |
| **06** | Twelve months of logging and monitoring evidence, four internal scan cycles and four ASV cycles, the penetration tests, the 11.6.1 payment page monitoring, six provider files with signed responsibility matrices |
| **07** | The policy suite, fourteen targeted risk analyses, awareness and screening records, the annual scope confirmation, 512 incident records, the incident response plan and its test, the 12.10.7 procedures |

**Nothing in that list was produced for the assessment.** Every row is an artefact a control produced while operating, indexed at the point of production. That is the mechanism behind §2's ratio, and it is the only mechanism that produces it — an entity that indexes evidence in October is not preparing for an assessment, it is reconstructing a year.

## 2. The Request Log

Every assessor request routed through the single point of contact and was logged, whether it was satisfied, re-requested, or would have been refused.

| Measure | Count | Share |
|---|---|---|
| **Total requests raised by Sable Ridge** | **214** | 100% |
| **Satisfied from artefacts that already existed** | **186** | **86.9%** |
| **Requiring new production** | **28** | 13.1% |
| Re-requested after the first response was judged insufficient | **6** | 2.8% |
| **Unsatisfied** | **0** | 0% |

### 2.1 What each figure means and does not mean

| Figure | Means | Does not mean |
|---|---|---|
| **186 satisfied from existing artefacts** | The evidence was produced by the control at the time it operated, indexed, and retrievable | That the evidence was *sufficient* — sufficiency is the assessor's judgement, and six of the responses were judged insufficient on first pass |
| **28 requiring new production** | Twenty-eight things could not exist until an assessor asked for them. §3 | That the programme had gaps in its evidence architecture. All twenty-eight are point-in-time by nature |
| **6 re-requested** | The first response answered a narrower question than the one asked, or answered it with the wrong class of evidence. §5.3 | That the underlying control was deficient. None of the six became a finding |
| **0 unsatisfied** | Every request was answered. The log records refusals as well as responses, and there were none | That every answer was the answer Marketa wanted to give. **Two of the responses evidenced the two Not in Place findings** |

**Zero unsatisfied is only meaningful because the log has a column for it.** A request log that cannot record a refusal cannot record a zero.

### 2.2 The response cycle

| Element | Position |
|---|---|
| Routing | Every request to Owen Castellanos, logged with a timestamp, the requesting assessor, the requirement, and the population it relates to |
| Assignment | To the named control owner, not to a team address |
| Target | Same business day for a retrieval; next business day for anything requiring production or a live observation to be scheduled |
| Escalation | To Naomi Bhatt where a request touched a production settlement path, a provider agreement, or personnel data |
| Legal review | **Frank Mueller** reviewed requests touching personnel records and the 38-state analysis, and cleared every one |
| Closure | Closed by the assessor, not by Marketa. **An entity that closes its own evidence requests has a log of what it sent, not a log of what was accepted** |

## 3. The Twenty-Eight

The twenty-eight requests that required new production share one property: **the evidence did not exist before the request because it could not have.** They are point-in-time observations, live reads, and witnessed events — the class of evidence an assessor obtains by being present, which is the reason a Level 1 merchant assessment is on-site.

| Class | Why it could not have been pre-produced | Representative instance |
|---|---|---|
| **Live configuration reads** | The evidence is the state of a system at the moment the assessor observes it. A configuration export produced in September evidences September. **The assessor's own read is the only artefact that evidences the assessed state** | Ruleset reads on the nine network security controls, performed at the console with the assessor watching, and compared against the version-controlled intended configuration |
| **Assessor-directed enumerations** | Marketa's own enumeration is the population Marketa believes it has. The assessor's independently-directed enumeration is the population that exists | The user-account enumeration across the 19 components carrying the 8.3.9 population, run under **CTP-1** with the assessor specifying the command |
| **Set-and-test demonstrations** | The evidence is that a control refuses something. A refusal cannot be captured in advance without a refusal having been attempted | Attempted password set below the minimum on each sampled component; attempted set of a credential known to be present in a public breach corpus |
| **Witnessed operational events** | The evidence is that a procedure performed by Marketa personnel, unrehearsed, produces the documented outcome | §3.1 |
| **Assessor-selected negative tests** | The assessor chooses the position from which the test is run. Marketa cannot choose it in advance without choosing the result | Attempted reach of a management interface from two network positions the assessor selected, neither of them the brokered path |
| **Interview records** | An interview is created by being conducted | 47 interviews with 39 distinct individuals |

Two of the twenty-eight were singular witnessed events, and both were requested precisely because they cannot be evidenced by a document.

### 3.1 The two witnessed events

**A witnessed restore.** The assessor selected a backup set and required a restoration to be performed and verified in his presence. Marketa's incident response evidence includes five backup sets proved restorable and proved immutable against the highest-privileged credential available; that evidence is a record of restorations Marketa performed. **A record of a successful restore is a record; a restore is a restore.** The distinction is the whole reason the request was raised.

**A witnessed POI inspection.** During the store sweep, a store team performed a 9.5.1 device inspection under the documented procedure, with the assessor observing and without the team having been briefed on what would be asked. The programme's inspection evidence covers 1,914 terminals on a quarterly cycle; what it cannot show is whether the person holding the checklist knows what a tampered device looks like. Twenty minutes of observation in a store can show that, and nothing produced in Columbus can.

### 3.2 Why 86.9% is the right ratio rather than a target to beat

An entity that pre-produced **100%** of an assessor's evidence would have produced something impossible, and the impossibility is instructive.

| If the ratio were higher | The reading |
|---|---|
| 95% or above | The assessor is not performing point-in-time observation, live testing or independent enumeration — which is to say, the assessment is a document review |
| **86.9%** | The documentary and system-evidence load was pre-built; the observational, testing and interview load could not be and was not |
| Substantially lower | The entity is reconstructing its year during fieldwork, which consumes the assessor's time, produces artefacts with weak provenance, and generates the re-request cycle §5.3 describes |

**The number to manage is not the ratio; it is the twenty-eight.** Twenty-eight new productions across fifteen days, none of them a scramble, all of them scheduled by the assessor, is what a prepared entity looks like.

## 4. The Four Classes of PCI Evidence

Every PCI DSS testing procedure directs the assessor to *examine*, *observe*, *interview*, or *test*. The four verbs are not interchangeable, and the most common evidentiary failure in an assessment is answering one with another.

| Class | What it is | What it **can** prove | What it **cannot** prove |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Documentation** — examine | Policies, procedures, standards, analyses, records, reports, agreements, configurations exported to file | That a thing is **defined**, **approved**, **recorded** or **retained**. That a decision has an owner, a date and an authority behind it | That anything is **done**. A policy is evidence of an intention. A procedure is evidence of a design. Neither is evidence of an operation |
| **Observation** — observe | Watching a process, a person or a system in operation, in place, at the time | That a process **happens**, that a control **is configured as described**, that a physical state **is as claimed** | That it happens when nobody is watching. An observation is a sample of one moment, and the observed party knows they are observed |
| **Interview** — interview | Structured questioning of the personnel who own or operate a control | Whether the control is **understood** by the people responsible for it; whether the documented process is the process they describe; where the real exceptions are | Anything about the control's operation as fact. **An interview is evidence about a person's account, never about the system.** It is corroboration, and it is the class most often over-weighted |
| **System evidence** — test / examine system components | Configuration reads, log content, scan output, code, cryptographic settings, the results of the assessor's own tests | What the system **is actually doing**, independent of what anybody says or writes. The only class that survives an entity that is mistaken about itself | Whether the state is representative over time. A configuration read is the same point-in-time limitation as an observation, unless it is corroborated by change records and monitoring evidence |

### 4.1 How the classes combine

No single class carries a requirement. The pattern PCI DSS testing procedures use — and the pattern Sable Ridge applied — is that a control is established by **documentation that defines it, system evidence that shows it operating, observation or testing that shows it operating in the assessor's presence, and interview that shows it is understood.**

| Requirement class | Where the weight sits | The failure mode when the weight is misplaced |
|---|---|---|
| Policy and governance — Requirement 12 | Documentation, corroborated by interview | Weighting interview over documentation produces a control that exists in the room and nowhere else |
| Configuration and cryptography — Requirements 2, 3, 4, 8 | **System evidence**, corroborated by documentation | Weighting documentation produces a baseline document and an unknown estate |
| Physical and procedural — Requirements 9, 12.10 | **Observation**, corroborated by documentation and interview | Weighting documentation produces an inspection procedure nobody performs |
| Detection and response — Requirements 10, 11 | System evidence and **assessor testing**, corroborated by records | Weighting records produces an alert catalogue with no evidence it fires |

Marketa's own experience supplies the cleanest illustration and it comes from Phase 07 rather than from fieldwork. **Twelve monthly contact verifications passed while the General Counsel's after-hours number was a full voicemail box.** The documentation was complete, the records were consistent, and the control did not work — because nobody had ever *observed* the path the control existed to protect. Documentation and system records agreed with each other and were both wrong about the world.

### 4.2 The class of evidence the two Not in Place findings rest on

| Finding | Evidence class | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **8.6.2** | **System evidence.** The credentials are present in a Korn shell wrapper on CDE-05 and a Java properties file on CDE-04, and the assessor read the files | Nothing in the documentation class could have overturned it. No policy, monitoring record or rotation log makes a secret absent from a file it is in |
| **11.3.1.2** | **System evidence, and its absence.** Per-target authentication assertions exist for 62 of 71 components and cannot exist for nine | The finding is evidenced by what the scanner could not produce. **An absence is evidence when the control is an enumeration** |

## 5. Requests in Practice

### 5.1 Distribution across the fieldwork

| Week | Focus | Character of the requests |
|---|---|---|
| **Week 1** — Columbus HQ and Columbus data centre | Requirements 1, 2, 3, 4 and the Requirement 12 documentation set | Heavily documentary and system-evidence; the largest single volume of the three weeks, and the highest retrieval rate |
| **Week 2** — Austin technology office, call centre, Ashburn co-location | Requirements 6, 7, 8, 10, 11 | The live-read and assessor-testing peak, including the customized approach procedures and the witnessed restore |
| **Week 3** — store estate | Requirements 5 and 9 | Almost entirely observation and interview, conducted in stores, with the witnessed POI inspection |

### 5.2 The requests Marketa would rather not have received

Two requests produced the evidence for the two Not in Place findings, and both were answered the same day.

| Request | Response |
|---|---|
| The contents of the credential-bearing files on CDE-04 and CDE-05, and the read-access log for the restricted repository holding them | Supplied. The credentials are in the files. The read-access log shows **0 unauthorised reads** in the period, which reduces the exposure and does not touch the requirement |
| Per-target authentication assertions for every target in all four internal scan cycles | Supplied. **248 authenticated component-scans across 62 components, four cycles, and nine components with no assertion possible.** The twelve authentication fallbacks in the period were supplied unprompted alongside |

Neither response was curated and neither improved Marketa's position. The second is the more instructive: the twelve fallbacks were volunteered rather than requested, because a scanner that silently degrades to an unauthenticated check and reports "scanned" is the precise failure 11.3.1.2 exists to prevent, and an entity that conceals its fallback count has concealed the only measure of how authenticated its authenticated scanning is.

### 5.3 The six re-requests

None of the six concerned a control that was deficient. All six concerned an answer that was narrower than the question.

| Pattern | What happened | The corrected response |
|---|---|---|
| **A record where an operation was asked for** | A restoration record was supplied where the request was for a restoration | The witnessed restore in §3.1 |
| **A sample where a population was asked for** | Baseline confidence reported for the sampled accounts where the procedure asks for **all 61** | The full-population report, which is what **CTP-6** was derived to require |
| **A policy where a configuration was asked for** | The password standard supplied where the request was for the enforced setting on named components | Per-component configuration reads |
| **A summary where the underlying records were asked for** | An access review completion summary where the request was for the review packets | The packets, with reviewer identity and decisions |
| **A current state where a period was asked for** | The present share permissions where the request was for the access history over the period | The access log for the period — this is the request that produced the **1.2.8** finding |
| **An owner's account where system evidence was asked for** | An interview answer offered in place of a log query | The log query |

**Five of the six are the same error in different clothes: answering with a weaker class of evidence than the one the procedure requires.** §4 is written because of them. The sixth — the access history request — is the one that found something, and it is recorded as **D-4** and as an in-flight correction under **ADR-0029**.

## 6. Retention and Confidentiality

The evidence set assembled for this assessment is a detailed operational description of the cardholder data environment: network positions, component names, account populations, credential architectures, detection rules, known deficiencies and their dates. **It is the most useful single document set an attacker could obtain about Marketa**, and it is treated accordingly.

| Control | Position |
|---|---|
| Classification | **Confidential — Cardholder Data Environment**. The highest handling class in the programme |
| Access, Marketa side | The custodian, the named deputy, the CISO, and control owners for their own material. Access is by named individual, logged, and reviewed |
| Access, assessor side | Read-only accounts for the named engagement team, provisioned for the assessment and **revoked on issue of the ROC** |
| Transmission | No evidence artefact left the repository by email. Assessors worked in place |
| **Account data** | **No artefact in the evidence set contains a primary account number.** Where a record necessarily referenced account data — the 12.10.7 findings, the call recording archive, the dispute file share — the artefact records counts, locations and remediation, never values |
| Personnel data | Screening and awareness records reviewed by **Frank Mueller** before release, and supplied in the minimum form that satisfies the testing procedure |
| Retention, Marketa | Retained through the re-assessment and the following assessment cycle, so that the 2027 assessor can see what the 2026 assessor saw. Superseded versions retained alongside |
| Retention, Sable Ridge | Under the QSA company's own quality programme, which governs its retention of assessment working papers and is outside Marketa's control |
| Destruction | Under **9.4.7**, with a certificate of destruction from **Halberd Data Destruction** where physical media is involved |
| Provider material | The six providers' AOCs, responsibility matrices and agreement extracts are held under the confidentiality terms of the agreements they came from |

**The single most under-managed asset in a compliance programme is the compliance evidence itself.** An entity that spends a year restricting access to the cardholder data environment and then circulates a complete description of it by email has moved the problem rather than solved it.

## 7. How Evidence Is Referenced in the ROC

The Report on Compliance is **741 pages** and carries **1,654 evidence references**. Every reference resolves to exactly one indexed artefact, and the index is the same one Marketa built across seven phases.

| Element | Position |
|---|---|
| Reference scheme | Each artefact carries a stable identifier assigned at indexing, and the ROC cites the identifier rather than a filename. A filename changes; an identifier does not |
| Where references appear | Part I Section 6 records the evidence and documentation retained; Part II carries the per-requirement references inside each testing procedure's response |
| Granularity | Per testing procedure, not per requirement. A sub-requirement with four testing procedures carries references against each |
| The four classes | Every reference is tagged with its class — documentation, observation, interview or system evidence — so that a reader can see **which class carries a given conclusion** |
| Interviews | Referenced by role and date. The 47 interviews are attributable to 39 distinct individuals, and the mapping is in the evidence index rather than the report body |
| Assessor's own artefacts | The 34 independent assessor testing actions produce artefacts held by Sable Ridge and referenced in the ROC. **Marketa does not hold the assessor's working papers** |
| Reconciliation | The 1,654 references reconcile to the **1,654 indexed evidence artefacts** in 08.04's evidence-class table. One artefact, one identifier, one reference target |

The class tagging is the feature worth defending. A reader of a ROC who cannot tell whether an "In Place" rests on a policy document or on a configuration read cannot judge the conclusion. **Tagging the class does not make a weak conclusion stronger; it makes a weak conclusion visible**, which is the correct outcome for a report whose audience includes an acquirer.

## 8. What the Evidence Position Does Not Establish

| Claim | Position |
|---|---|
| That 86.9% pre-existing evidence indicates a strong control environment | **No.** It indicates a strong evidence architecture. An entity can index bad news efficiently, and Marketa did — twice |
| That 0 unsatisfied requests means every answer was accepted | No. **Six were judged insufficient and re-requested**, and the log records that separately for exactly this reason |
| That the repository is complete | It is complete against 214 requests from one assessment team. A different assessor asks different questions, and **the 2028 rotation will** |
| That indexed evidence proves operation | Only system evidence and assessor testing prove operation, and only for the moment observed. §4 states the limits of each class deliberately |
| That the evidence set is safe because it is classified | It is classified, access-controlled, logged and never emailed. It is also the most attractive internal document set in the company, and the programme treats that as a standing exposure rather than a solved problem |
