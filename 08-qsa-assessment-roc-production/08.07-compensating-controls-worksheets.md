## Purpose

**Five compensating controls were drafted in this programme. Three were accepted and two were refused.**

That ratio is the document. A compensating control is the mechanism PCI DSS provides for an entity that cannot meet a requirement as stated because of a legitimate technical or documented business constraint, and that can demonstrate a different control addressing the same risk to the same rigour. It is not a mechanism for describing a gap in more agreeable language, and the difference between the two is decided by a test that this document states once and applies five times.

This document carries the three accepted worksheets in full against all six Appendix C sections, the two refusals with the reasoning that produced them, and — at the end — an observation about where they all came from that the programme would rather not have to make.

**A compensating control is not a customized approach.** The customized approach at 8.3.9 meets the requirement's stated objective by a different means and is recorded **In Place**; it is not among the three counted here. The two cannot be combined for the same requirement, which is why the customized approach carries no fallback.

## 2. The Test, Stated Once

Every one of the five was measured against the same four questions, in the same order. The order matters: a control that fails the first question never reaches the fourth.

| # | Question | What it excludes |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | Is there a **legitimate technical or documented business constraint** preventing the requirement being met as stated? | Inconvenience, cost preference, and "we have not got to it yet". A constraint is a property of the technology or a documented business fact, not a project position |
| **1** | Does the control **address the risk the original requirement addresses**, meeting its intent and rigour? | Controls that reduce a neighbouring risk. This is where most refusals happen, and both of Marketa's did |
| **2** | Does it go **above and beyond** other PCI DSS requirements? | **A compensating control cannot be assembled from requirements the entity already owes.** Network isolation is already required by Requirement 1; advisory monitoring by 6.3.1; provider assurance by 12.8 |
| **3** | Is it **commensurate with the additional risk** imposed by not adhering to the requirement? | Controls that are real but too light for the exposure they are asked to carry |

**The three accepted worksheets pass all four. The two refused fail at question 1, and the 11.3.1.2 draft fails at question 2 as well.**

Two further disciplines applied throughout, and both are stated because they are the ones an entity under deadline abandons first:

**A compensating control is assessed, not asserted.** Each of the three was validated by the assessor performing tests himself, not by examining Marketa's description of the control.

**A compensating control is a standing obligation, not a one-off.** Section 6 of each worksheet — Maintenance — is the section entities write last and the section that determines whether the control still exists in 2027.

## 3. CCW-01 — Requirement 8.3.6

**Requirement 8.3.6** obliges passwords and passphrases to be a minimum of **12 characters**, containing both numeric and alphabetic characters.

### 3.1 Section 1 — Constraints

| Element | Position |
|---|---|
| Population | The **built-in local administrative account on each of the 9 vendor-locked POS back-office appliances — 9 accounts**, one per appliance, held under constraint **CON-03** since programme start |
| The account | Created by the firmware at first boot. **Not removable and not renamable.** It is the platform's account of last resort and exists whether or not anybody uses it |
| The constraint | The credential field of **that account's firmware-resident definition** is capped at **10 characters**. The cap is a property of the built-in account's own definition, not of a configurable password policy, and cannot be raised by any setting Marketa can reach |
| Vendor position | **No roadmap.** The vendor has no committed release that lengthens the field, and the support agreement prohibits modification of appliance software |
| Why the requirement cannot be met | 8.3.6 requires a minimum of 12. The built-in account's definition permits a maximum of 10. **There is no configuration that satisfies both** |
| Constraint class | **Legitimate technical constraint**, verified by the assessor rather than accepted from a datasheet — he attempted a password set above 10 characters **on the built-in administrative account of each of the nine** and observed the rejection |

### 3.1.1 Reconciliation with 05.06 — two account definitions on one device

The nine appliances carry two distinct account definitions, and every apparently contradictory result about them resolves once the two are kept apart.

| | **The built-in local administrative account** | **The named engineer accounts** |
|---|---|---|
| Count | **9** — one per appliance | **36** across the nine — class **P1** of the 8.3.9 population |
| Where it lives | The firmware's own built-in account definition. Not removable, not renamable | The appliance **user realm**, which the firmware exposes for named accounts |
| Credential length | **Capped at 10 characters** by the built-in definition | **16-character minimum enforced**, machine-generated, under **CAC-1** |
| Holder | **Shared.** Released from the vault to one authenticated individual at a time for a bounded window | An individual account per engineer per appliance |
| Governing requirement | **8.3.6**, met with **CCW-01**; shared-account governance under **8.2.2** | **8.3.9**, met by the customized approach; 8.3.6 met directly at 16 characters |
| What the assessor found | Set above 10 characters rejected on all nine | **CTP-3: 19 of 19 components enforce the minimum in the appliance user realm** |

**Both results are true because they are results about different account definitions on the same device.** A 10-character cap on the built-in account and a 16-character enforcement in the user realm are not in tension; the firmware simply applies a different, fixed definition to the account it created itself.

**The consequence for the 8.2.2 population, and the observation it produced.** The nine built-in accounts are **shared and password-only**, which places them squarely in the 8.2.2 shared-account population. **That population is therefore 20 — the 11 enterprise break-glass identities recorded in 05.04 §4 plus the 9 appliance built-ins — not 11.** Phase 05's eleven counted the identities held in the enterprise identity store; the nine appliance built-ins were governed under the appliance inventory instead, and no reconciliation existed between the two registers. **Marketa's own shared-account figure understated the true population by nine, and nobody had noticed until the assessor reconciled the two registers.**

That is a finding about a register rather than about a control — the nine accounts were vaulted, alerted on and reconciled throughout, which is why 8.2.2 is In Place and why CCW-01 was accepted. It is recorded as **OBS-05**, one of the seventeen assessor observations in 08.04 §8, and it is the most useful of the seventeen: an entity that governs the same account class in two places without a reconciliation between them will eventually report a number that is true of one register and false of the estate.

### 3.2 Section 2 — Objective

The objective of 8.3.6 is that a password should be **infeasible to guess or derive** within the time an attacker has, whether online against a live authentication endpoint or offline against a captured credential store. Length is the requirement's chosen instrument; **resistance to derivation is its purpose.**

The compensating control is therefore not asked to produce a longer password. It is asked to make the guessing or derivation of a 10-character credential useless or detectable to a degree commensurate with the four characters it cannot have.

### 3.3 Section 3 — Identified Risk

| Risk introduced by the deviation | Assessment |
|---|---|
| An offline attack against a captured credential store completes faster against a 10-character credential than a 12-character one | The dominant risk. Bounded by the fact that Marketa holds no copy of the appliance credential store and an attacker would need the appliance itself |
| An online guessing attack against the appliance's local authentication | Bounded by appliance-side lockout and by the reachability constraint in **CCW-02** — an attacker must already be on the isolated management VLAN |
| Credential disclosure — written down, shared under operational pressure, or reused | The realised threat for a shorter credential a human might memorise. **Addressed by the credential never being memorised** |
| Undetected use | The material risk. A built-in administrative account on an appliance nobody monitors is the classic long-lived foothold. **This is what the alert and adjudication limb exists for** |
| Register entry | Contributes to **R-36**'s exposure surface on the same nine devices, and is reported alongside it |

### 3.4 Section 4 — Definition of Compensating Controls

| Control limb | Implementation |
|---|---|
| **Break-glass only** | The account is not used in normal operation. Every routine administrative action uses a named engineer account in the appliance's user realm. The built-in account exists for platform recovery and vendor-directed procedures |
| **Vaulted** | The credential is held in the enterprise credential vault. **No human holds it, no document records it, and it exists in no configuration file** |
| **Single-use with post-use rotation** | Release is to an authenticated, multi-factor-verified individual for a bounded window, and the credential is **rotated immediately on window close**, whether or not it was used. A released credential is therefore valid for one occasion |
| **Every authentication alerts** | Any authentication by the built-in account on any of the nine raises a **SIEM alert**, adjudicated by security operations **within 15 minutes**. There is no suppression, no business-hours exception and no volume threshold |
| **Quarterly reconciliation** | Every authentication in the quarter is reconciled to an approved change record or vendor-directed procedure. **An authentication with no corresponding record is an incident**, not a query |

**How the four limbs together address the risk.** A 10-character credential that no human knows cannot be disclosed, written down or reused. A credential valid for a single bounded window cannot be derived offline and used later, because by the time derivation completes the credential has been rotated. A credential whose every use alerts within 15 minutes and is reconciled to a change record each quarter cannot be used undetected. **The residual — offline derivation inside the release window, by an attacker already on the isolated management VLAN — is what the reachability constraint in CCW-02 bounds.**

### 3.5 Section 5 — Validation of Compensating Controls

| What Sable Ridge did | Result |
|---|---|
| Attempted a password set above 10 characters on the **built-in administrative account** of each of the nine appliances | Rejected on all nine. The constraint is a fact of that account's firmware-resident definition, verified rather than accepted. The same assessor separately confirmed under CTP-3 that the appliance **user realm** on the same devices enforces the 16-character minimum |
| Observed a vault release end to end — request, approval, multi-factor verification of the individual, bounded window, use, and post-use rotation | Performed as documented; the rotation occurred on window close |
| Attempted to obtain the credential outside the vault — configuration files, documentation, the appliance management console | Not present anywhere the assessor could reach |
| Examined **every** built-in-account authentication in the operating period against its SIEM alert and adjudication record | Every authentication alerted; every alert adjudicated inside 15 minutes |
| Examined the quarterly reconciliations for the period | Every authentication reconciled to an approved change record or a vendor-directed procedure. **No unreconciled authentication** |
| Tested the alert by causing an authentication himself | Alert raised and adjudicated within the stated interval |
| **Determination** | **Accepted. 8.3.6 assessed In Place with a compensating control** |

### 3.6 Section 6 — Maintenance

| Element | Position |
|---|---|
| Owner | Trevor Kim, Director of Infrastructure &amp; Network |
| Review | At least annually, and on any firmware release for the appliance class |
| Ongoing validation | The quarterly reconciliation is the control's own evidence and is a standing deliverable, not an assessment artefact |

## 4. CCW-02 — Requirement 2.2.7

**Requirement 2.2.7** obliges all non-console administrative access to be **encrypted using strong cryptography**.

### 4.1 Section 1 — Constraints

| Element | Position |
|---|---|
| Population | The management interfaces of the **same 9 vendor-locked POS back-office appliances** |
| The constraint | The management interface **supports no protocol offering strong cryptography.** There is no configuration that enables one, no alternative management service, and no vendor release that adds one |
| Vendor position | Modification of appliance software voids support. The interface is the vendor's, on the vendor's stack |
| Why the requirement cannot be met | The requirement obliges the session to be encrypted with strong cryptography. **The endpoint cannot offer it**, so no client configuration on Marketa's side can produce it |
| Constraint class | **Legitimate technical constraint**, verified by the assessor from the negotiated protocol and cipher suite at the interface rather than from the vendor's documentation |

### 4.2 Section 2 — Objective

The objective of 2.2.7 is that **administrative credentials and administrative session content cannot be observed or altered in transit** by a party with access to the network path.

The compensating control cannot make the protocol strong. It is therefore asked to make the network path unavailable to any party who could observe it, and to make any use of that path fully attributable and reviewable.

### 4.3 Section 3 — Identified Risk

| Risk introduced by the deviation | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Credential interception in transit | The direct risk. A weakly-protected administrative session exposes the credential to anyone on the path |
| Session content interception — configuration disclosure | An attacker who reads a management session learns the appliance's configuration, including its network position |
| Session manipulation — injection or hijack | The more serious variant, because the appliance sits on the store-estate boundary |
| Relationship to other findings | The appliances' internal state is already unobservable — **11.3.1.2**, R-36. A management path exposure on the same devices compounds an existing blind spot rather than creating an isolated one |

### 4.4 Section 4 — Definition of Compensating Controls

| Control limb | Implementation |
|---|---|
| **Single hardened jump host** | Management of all nine is reachable **only** from one hardened jump host, itself built to the applicable baseline, patched on the standard cycle, and reached by a multi-factor-authenticated brokered session |
| **Isolated management VLAN** | The path from the jump host to the nine traverses a management VLAN with no other host, no user workstation, no server and no route to or from any other segment |
| **Full session recording** | Every management session is recorded end to end and retained. **The recording is of the session, not of the connection metadata** |
| **No other route exists** | Enforced at the network security controls and asserted continuously by the drift assertions. Any rule change affecting the management VLAN is a segmentation-affecting change under **SIG-4** |
| **Monthly rule attestation** | The rules enforcing the isolation are attested monthly by a named individual against the intended configuration, independently of the six-monthly 1.2.7 review |

**How the limbs together address the risk.** The requirement's risk is observation or alteration by a party on the path. **The compensating control removes the path**: the only network on which the weakly-protected session exists carries exactly two endpoints, one of which is a hardened host reached only through a multi-factor-authenticated broker. An attacker positioned to observe the session must first hold the jump host — at which point the session encryption is not the exposure. Full session recording supplies the attribution and reviewability the protocol cannot.

### 4.5 Section 5 — Validation of Compensating Controls

| What Sable Ridge did | Result |
|---|---|
| Examined the negotiated protocol and cipher suite at the management interface of sampled appliances | Confirmed the constraint. No strong option offered or negotiable |
| **Attempted to reach the management interface from two network positions he selected**, neither of them the jump host | Both blocked at the boundary and logged. No alternate route located |
| Read the management VLAN configuration and the enforcing rules at the console | Consistent with the intended configuration; no additional host, no additional route |
| Examined the jump host's build against its baseline, its patch state and its authentication path | Consistent with the baseline; brokered access with multi-factor authentication enforced |
| Sampled session recordings across the period and reconciled the recording count to the session count | Every session recorded; no session without a recording |
| Examined all monthly rule attestations for the period | Present, signed by a named individual, and reconciled to the change records for any rule change |
| **Determination** | **Accepted. 2.2.7 assessed In Place with a compensating control** |

### 4.6 Section 6 — Maintenance

| Element | Position |
|---|---|
| Owner | Trevor Kim, Director of Infrastructure &amp; Network |
| Review | At least annually, and on any change to the management VLAN, the jump host or the appliance firmware |
| Ongoing validation | Monthly rule attestation; continuous drift assertion; the annual segmentation penetration test as external corroboration |
| Conditions under which the worksheet ceases to be valid | Any additional host on the management VLAN; any route added to or from it; loss of session recording coverage; a firmware release offering strong cryptography, which would remove the constraint entirely |
| **Survival of the 2027 remediation** | **CCW-02 survives the re-assessment.** The contract amendment changes what Marketa may obtain; it does not change what the management interface supports |
| The standing dependency | The isolation is enforced by the same class of store-network configuration that failed at **store 0417** in May 2026. **This worksheet's central limb is a control the programme has seen fail once**, which is why the monthly attestation is monthly and not annual |

## 5. CCW-03 — Requirement 3.4.2

**Requirement 3.4.2** obliges technical controls to prevent the **copy or relocation of primary account numbers** when using remote-access technologies, except where explicitly authorised with a documented business need.

### 5.1 Section 1 — Constraints

| Element | Position |
|---|---|
| Population | **6 disputes analysts**, reaching **1 legacy published application** in the virtual desktop estate |
| The constraint | The published application **cannot enforce clipboard and print blocking.** Its presentation layer predates the virtual desktop platform's redirection controls, and enabling the platform's blocking at the session level breaks the application's own rendering |
| Business constraint | The application is the system of record for historical dispute cases. Its retirement is a data migration, not a configuration change, and the analysts' work cannot pause |
| Why the requirement cannot be met | The requirement asks for a technical control preventing copy and relocation. **The one technical control the platform offers cannot be applied to this application without disabling it** |
| Constraint class | **Legitimate technical constraint with a documented business constraint behind it.** The assessor examined both: the platform behaviour and the business record of the retirement dependency |

**Where the account data is.** Marketa stores no primary account numbers at rest, and the disputes population handles truncated numbers in normal operation. The exposure this worksheet addresses is the historical dispute record — the same population that produced one of the four unexpected-PAN findings, a legacy dispute file share of **2,187 scanned documents**, remediated in Phase 02.

### 5.2 Section 2 — Objective

The objective of 3.4.2 is that **account data reached through a remote-access session cannot leave that session** — to a local drive, a clipboard, a printer, a screenshot or a file transfer — where it would land outside the controls that protect it.

The compensating control cannot block the clipboard. It is asked to remove every destination the clipboard could reach, and to make any attempt at relocation visible.

### 5.3 Section 3 — Identified Risk

| Risk introduced by the deviation | Assessment |
|---|---|
| An analyst copies account data to the local endpoint | The direct risk, and the one the requirement names |
| Account data printed to a local or network printer | A paper copy is outside every technical control in the programme |
| Account data transferred to removable media or a personal cloud location | The relocation path with the widest reach |
| Inadvertent relocation — a legitimate action with an unintended destination | More likely than malice. Six named analysts working under time pressure on customer disputes |
| Relationship to programme history | Realised once already, in a different form: the **2,187-document dispute file share** found by PAN discovery in Phase 02. **This is a risk with an instance behind it, not a theoretical one** |

### 5.4 Section 4 — Definition of Compensating Controls

| Control limb | Implementation |
|---|---|
| **100% session recording** | Every session by every one of the six analysts to the published application is recorded in full and retained. Not sampled, not metadata — the session |
| **No local drive mapping** | Client drive redirection is disabled at the session policy for this published application. **The clipboard has nowhere on the endpoint to write to** |
| **No print queue** | No printer is published to the session and no print queue exists within it. Print is not blocked at the application; **there is nothing to print to** |
| **DLP on the egress path** | Data loss prevention inspects the egress path from the virtual desktop estate for account data patterns, using the same pattern set as the PAN discovery tooling, and blocks on match |
| **Weekly review of a 10% session sample** | A weekly review of 10% of sessions by a named reviewer, examining what was displayed, what was attempted and what left |

**How the limbs together address the risk.** The requirement wants copy and relocation prevented. **The control removes every destination rather than blocking the action**: nothing to copy to, nothing to print to, and an inspected egress path that blocks account data patterns. The clipboard remains functional within the session, where its contents cannot leave. Session recording and the weekly sample supply the detection limb for anything the first three miss.

### 5.5 Section 5 — Validation of Compensating Controls

| What Sable Ridge did | Result |
|---|---|
| Entered a live session as an assessor and **attempted to copy content to the local endpoint** | No destination available; the paste target did not exist on the endpoint |
| **Attempted to map a local drive** within the session | Redirection unavailable; no client drive presented |
| **Attempted to print** from within the session | No printer available; no queue present |
| Attempted an egress transfer carrying an account-data pattern | Blocked by the DLP policy, and the block event recorded |
| Reconciled the session recording count to the session count for the period, across all six analysts | Every session recorded. **No session without a recording** |
| Examined the weekly review records for the period, including reviewer identity and findings | Performed weekly; a named reviewer; findings recorded, none involving relocation of account data |
| Interviewed the disputes analysts on what they may and may not do, unprompted by the compliance function | Consistent accounts, consistent with the control |
| **Determination** | **Accepted. 3.4.2 assessed In Place with a compensating control** |

### 5.6 Section 6 — Maintenance

| Element | Position |
|---|---|
| Owner | Elena Marchetti, VP Payment Operations, with Trevor Kim for the platform limbs |
| Review | At least annually, and on any change to the published application, the session policy, the analyst population or the DLP pattern set |
| Ongoing validation | Weekly 10% session review; continuous DLP event monitoring; session recording coverage reconciled monthly to the session count |
| Conditions under which the worksheet ceases to be valid | Growth of the analyst population beyond the named six; publication of a printer or a mapped drive to the session; loss of recording coverage; a DLP policy exception for the estate |
| The exit route | **Retirement of the legacy published application** and migration of the historical dispute record. That work removes the constraint and the worksheet with it, and it is a data migration rather than a configuration change |
| Why the exit is not dated here | The migration is not part of either 2027 remediation and does not affect the re-assessment. **The worksheet stands, with an owner and a review date, until it does not** |

## 6. Why These Three and Not Others

Every accepted worksheet clears the four questions in §2 in the same way, and it is worth showing the pattern rather than asserting it.

| | **CCW-01 · 8.3.6** | **CCW-02 · 2.2.7** | **CCW-03 · 3.4.2** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Q0 — legitimate constraint** | Firmware field cap, verified by attempted set on all nine | Interface offers no strong protocol, verified from the negotiation | Platform blocking breaks the application; documented business dependency |
| **Q1 — addresses the same risk** | The risk is derivation and undetected use. A credential no human knows, valid once, alerting on every use | The risk is observation on the path. **The path is removed** | The risk is relocation. **Every destination is removed** |
| **Q2 — above and beyond** | Vaulting, single-use release, post-use rotation, 15-minute adjudication and quarterly reconciliation are **not required of Marketa by any other requirement** for this account | Full session recording of every management session and monthly rule attestation are **beyond** the isolation Requirement 1 already obliges | 100% session recording and a weekly 10% review are **beyond** the logging and monitoring Requirement 10 obliges |
| **Q3 — commensurate** | Four characters of length traded for a credential that exists for one bounded window and cannot be used unseen | A weak protocol on a two-endpoint isolated segment reached only through a multi-factor broker | A clipboard that functions in a session with no reachable destination and an inspected egress |

**Question 2 is where the work is.** In each case the control had to include something Marketa was not already obliged to do, because a control assembled from existing PCI DSS obligations compensates for nothing — the entity owed it anyway.

## 7. The Two That Were Refused

### 7.1 8.6.2 — refused June 2026

**8.6.2** obliges passwords for application and system accounts that can be used for interactive login **not to be hard-coded** in scripts, configuration files or bespoke and custom source code. Two credentials remain embedded — `batch-icq` in a Korn shell wrapper on CDE-05 and `gl-extract` in a Java properties file on CDE-04.

| Proposed limb | Why it failed |
|---|---|
| Monthly credential rotation under two-person change control | **Fails Q1.** 8.6.2 does not prohibit stale credentials; it prohibits hard-coded ones. A credential rotated monthly and written back into the same file is a hard-coded credential with a fresh value |
| Restricted repository with read access limited to three named individuals, every read logged | **Fails Q2.** File permissions and access logging are already owed under Requirements 7 and 10 |
| File integrity monitoring alerting on change and on unexpected read | **Fails Q2.** Already owed under 11.5.2 |
| Single-source-host authentication restriction and out-of-window alerting | **Fails Q1.** Both address use of the credential. **Neither addresses readability by anyone with configuration access** to the component |
| Interactive use governed as a documented 8.6.1 exception with full attribution | **Fails Q2.** Already owed under 8.6.1 |
| **Determination** | **Refused.** Every proposed limb was either already required elsewhere or did not address the risk the requirement addresses |

**The intent of 8.6.2 is that the secret is not present in a readable file. No arrangement of permissions, monitoring, alerting or rotation makes a secret absent from a file it is in.** Marketa could have produced a worksheet; a competent assessor would have rejected it, and an assessor who accepted it would have been wrong.

**8.6.2 is Not in Place.** R-17 held at High 16, remediation **2027-01-31**, re-assessment **2027-02-18**.

### 7.2 11.3.1.2 — refused July 2026

**11.3.1.2** obliges internal vulnerability scans to be performed via authenticated scanning. Authenticated scanning is complete on 62 of 71 components and impossible on the nine vendor-locked appliances.

| Proposed limb | Why it failed |
|---|---|
| More frequent **unauthenticated** scanning — weekly rather than quarterly | **Fails Q1, decisively.** An unauthenticated scan sees a service banner and infers; an authenticated scan enumerates what is installed. **More frequent unauthenticated scanning detects a different class of vulnerability. It is not a substitute; it is a different test** |
| Monthly firmware version reconciliation against the vendor's current release | **Fails Q1.** A firmware version tells you which release is running, not what is unfixed in it |
| Quarterly vendor patch attestation under 12.8.5 | **Fails Q1 and Q2.** A vendor attestation is a statement about the vendor's process, not an observation of Marketa's nine devices — and provider assurance is already owed under Requirement 12.8 |
| The vendor advisory feed carried as a named 6.3.1 source | **Fails Q2.** Already owed under 6.3.1 |
| Network isolation of the appliance population | **Fails Q1 and Q2.** Isolation bounds the consequence of a compromise and says nothing about whether the appliance is compromised — **and it is already required by Requirement 1** |
| **Determination** | **Refused on two of the four tests; question 3 was never reached** |

Marketa also argued the requirement's own documentation allowance — that systems unable to accept credentials are documented — and that argument failed on a different distinction: **the allowance addresses a system that cannot accept credentials, not a system whose credentials the entity has not obtained.** The first is a property of technology; the second is a property of a contract.

**11.3.1.2 is Not in Place.** R-36 held at High 16, remediation **2027-01-31** under **CAP-06**, re-assessment **2027-02-18**.

### 7.3 What distinguishes the three from the two

One sentence, and it is Q1:

> **The three accepted controls address the risk their requirement addresses. The two refused ones address a neighbouring risk and were assembled largely from obligations Marketa already owed.**

| Accepted | Refused |
|---|---|
| CCW-01 makes a short credential un-derivable and its use un-hidden — **the risk 8.3.6 is about** | The 8.6.2 measures make an embedded credential harder to reach and its misuse more visible — **not the risk 8.6.2 is about, which is presence in a readable file** |
| CCW-02 removes the network path — **the risk 2.2.7 is about** | The 11.3.1.2 measures scan more often, from outside — **not the risk 11.3.1.2 is about, which is knowing what is installed** |
| CCW-03 removes the destinations — **the risk 3.4.2 is about** | |

The pattern is that an accepted compensating control **attacks the requirement's risk from a different direction**, while a refused one **reduces an adjacent risk and hopes the adjacency is close enough.** It never is.

**Two Not in Place findings in this programme, and both are places where a compensating control was available to be argued and was refused.** The programme declined to spend the assessment's credibility on the two controls where the gap is unambiguous, and the three that were accepted are stronger for it.

## 8. The Observation the Programme Would Rather Not Make

Say it plainly:

> **Two of the three accepted compensating controls, and one of the two Not in Place findings, all trace to the same nine devices and the same support agreement.**

| Entry | Requirement | Root |
|---|---|---|
| **CCW-01** | 8.3.6 | The nine vendor-locked appliances — firmware field cap |
| **CCW-02** | 2.2.7 | The nine vendor-locked appliances — management interface protocol |
| **11.3.1.2 Not in Place** | 11.3.1.2 | The nine vendor-locked appliances — **no administrative credential release, no agent installation rights** |

Three of the five exceptional dispositions in a 306-requirement assessment sit on **nine devices out of seventy-one**, under one support agreement with one vendor. Add the sampling refusal at **S-12** — the population whose standardisation could not be demonstrated, which is the same nine — and the count is four. Add the two customized-approach account classes that cannot be gated because they authenticate locally in appliance firmware, and the nine devices are present in five separate places in this assessment.

### 8.1 What that concentration means

| Reading | Position |
|---|---|
| It is a technology problem | **Only partly.** The firmware caps and the protocol support are technology. The credential release and the agent installation rights are **contract terms**, and the constraint that produced the Not in Place finding is commercial |
| It is a procurement outcome | **Yes.** Nobody negotiated for administrative credential release, because at the time of procurement nothing required it. Requirement 11.3.1.2 was future-dated |
| It is a concentration risk | **Yes, and it is the important reading.** A single support agreement holds up three exceptional dispositions. **A control that depends on a contract clause has a renewal date** |
| It is remediable | Partly. The **CAP-06** amendment executed 2027-01-08 delivers credential release and agent installation rights and closes the finding. **It does not change firmware.** CCW-01 and CCW-02 both survive |

### 8.2 The consequence for the compensating-control count

**The compensating-control count stays at 3 through the re-assessment.**

| Date | Position |
|---|---|
| 2026-12-11 | 3 compensating controls, 2 Not in Place findings |
| 2027-02-18 re-assessment | 3 compensating controls, 0 Not in Place findings |

**The remediation removed the finding, not the constraint.** That sentence is the honest summary of the whole 2027 remediation and it belongs in this document rather than only in the one that reports the re-assessment. Route B under CAP-06 — replacement of the appliance class — is not needed for the finding and is **retained on the 2027 roadmap anyway**, precisely because a control that depends on a contract clause has a renewal date and the firmware constraints do not go away when the clause is signed.

## 9. What These Worksheets Do Not Establish

| Claim | Position |
|---|---|
| That a compensating control makes a requirement met as stated | **No.** It records that the requirement is **not** met as stated, that a legitimate constraint prevents it, and that a different control addresses the same risk. The disposition is In Place **with a compensating control**, and the ROC distinguishes the two |
| That three accepted worksheets is a good outcome | Three is three. **The number to watch is that two of them are the same nine devices** |
| That the two refusals were forced on Marketa | Both were Marketa's own conclusions before they were the assessor's. 05.07 §5.3 and 06.05 §5.5 record the reasoning, written months before fieldwork |
| That a worksheet accepted in 2026 will be accepted in 2027 | **No.** Each is re-argued and re-validated at every assessment, and the 2028 assessor rotation will re-examine all three from scratch |
| That the compensating controls reduce the register entries they touch | They do not. **R-36 is held at High 16** until CAP-06 closes, and the accommodations on the nine devices are recorded as accommodations |
| That a compensating control is available for the customized approach | **No.** The two cannot be combined for the same requirement, which is why 8.3.9 has no fallback and why a disagreement about its objective would have been a third Not in Place finding |
