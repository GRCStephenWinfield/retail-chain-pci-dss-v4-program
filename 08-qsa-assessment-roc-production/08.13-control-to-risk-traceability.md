## Purpose

03.12 mapped all **44** baseline risks forward to the controls that would treat them. 04.12 and 05.12 recorded that nothing moved, because a control published in July has no operating history. 06.12 released six entries on a year of operation. 07.12 released five more, two of them on exit criteria written six months in advance.

**Phase 08 is different from all of them, and the difference is worth stating before any movement is claimed.**

> **Phase 08 builds no controls.** It contributes one thing the register has never had: **an independent test of the same controls, performed by somebody with no stake in the answer, against a defined population, in a defined window.** Fifteen business days, 42 sampled components, 28 stores, 96 terminals, 45 change records, 47 interviews, 34 independent testing actions, and **1,654 indexed evidence artefacts**. That is corroboration, and corroboration is a different input from operation.

Seven entries move. Four are held explicitly, and the holds are stated with the same care as the movements. The document answers five questions and refuses to blur them:

| Question | Where |
|---|---|
| What does an assessment contribute to a risk register, and what can it not contribute? | §1 |
| Which entries **move**, and on what evidence? | §3 |
| Which entries are **held**, and why is that not an omission? | §4 |
| Is it true that **every movement in this programme has been a likelihood movement**? | §5 |
| Does the register reconcile to the published Phase 09 close of **0 High · 13 Moderate · 31 Low**? | §6 — **and the answer is no** |

And the discipline that governs the movements, stated before any of them:

> **A rating moves on measured evidence, never on an event.** The assessment is an event. **R-17 and R-36 do not move because a report was issued; they move because two requirements that were not met are met, were re-tested by the assessor on 2027-02-18, and met exit criteria published in Phase 05 and Phase 06 before anybody knew whether they would be met.** The other five move on evidence the assessment produced about controls that were already running.

## 1. What Phase 08 Contributes, and What It Does Not

| Contribution | What it is | What it is not |
|---|---|---|
| **Independent corroboration** | An assessor tested Marketa's controls against the standard's own testing procedures, chose its own samples, and performed **34 testing actions of its own** | **Not new operating history.** The controls ran for a year; the assessment observed them for fifteen days |
| **Remediation of two findings** | **CAP-07** and **CAP-06**, completed 2027-01-29 and 2027-01-30, re-tested 2027-02-18 | **Not a change to the constraint.** CCW-01 and CCW-02 survive on the same nine appliances |
| **A measured evidence position** | **186 of 214** evidence requests satisfied from artefacts that already existed | Not a statement that the evidence is complete — **28 items required new production**, and 6 were re-requested |
| **A store-estate observation at scale** | **28 stores**, **96 terminals**, three assessors over three days, with **store 0417** deliberately included by the assessor | Not a segmentation test. That is **11.4.5** and its next execution is **OI-06-10**, dated 2027-05-31 |
| **17 assessor observations** | Matters raised that affected **no disposition** | **Not findings**, and not closed. They are Phase 09's inheritance |

**Phase 08 is primary for no risk in the register.** Every risk it touches is owned by the phase that built the control; this phase either corroborates that control or, in two cases, records that the treatment finally completed. That is why the movement table below has ten columns of arithmetic and a basis column that names somebody else's control in every row.

## 2. How to Read the Mapping

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Corroborated** | An independent assessor tested the control and reached the same conclusion Marketa had. Corroboration can support a movement; it cannot create one where the exposure is unchanged |
| **Completed** | The treatment the register named as the exit condition has been executed and verified. This is what happened to **R-17** and **R-36**, and to nothing else in the phase |
| **Movement** | A change in likelihood or impact score, justified by measured evidence, with the evidence named |
| **Held** | The rating is unchanged. **Held is a determination, not an absence of one**, and every explicit hold carries its reason in §4 |
| **Changed shape** | The residual is the same score and a different exposure. **R-36 is the only instance in the programme**, and it is stated rather than smoothed |

### 2.1 What each assessment instrument can and cannot support

The assessment is not one input. It is seven, and they differ in what they are capable of proving about a risk rating. Stating that before §3 prevents the commonest error in post-assessment risk reporting — **treating a clean report as evidence for everything at once.**

| Instrument | What it can support | What it cannot support |
|---|---|---|
| **Sampling across 18 populations** | That a control operates consistently across a population the entity did not choose | That the unsampled remainder is identical. **Four populations were sampled at or below 5.8%** — S-04, S-05, S-17 and S-18 |
| **47 interviews across 39 individuals** | That the people who operate a control understand it and describe it consistently when asked separately | That they would do the same thing under pressure at 03:00 |
| **61 process observations** | That a process performed under observation matches its documentation | That it is performed the same way when nobody is watching. **This is why the assessor's observation of 96 terminals argues for a movement on R-02 and does not carry one — §3.3** |
| **34 independent assessor testing actions** | That specific claims are true when tested by somebody with no stake in the answer — the 1.5.1 netflow review, the 5.3.3 port-state examination | That the environment resists an adversary. **An assessor tests controls against a standard; a penetration tester tests an environment against an attacker**, and those are different engagements |
| **214 evidence requests** | That the entity can produce, on demand, the record of a control it says it operates | That the record is complete. It measures retrievability, which is exactly and only what **R-42** describes |
| **The 28-store sweep** | That a control implemented centrally survives contact with 482 stores and the people in them | That segmentation holds. **That is 11.4.5, and the next execution is OI-06-10** |
| **The 2027-02-18 re-assessment** | That two specific remediations were executed and verified, and that 14 related sub-requirements did not break | That the remediated state has an operating history. **It has eighteen days** |

### 3.1 R-17 and R-36 — the only two that were waiting on a requirement

These are the register's last two High entries, both held at **16** since they were raised, both unchanged for twelve months, **neither ever accepted by the Chief Financial Officer**, and both held by the same rule: **an accommodation never lowers a rating.** Between them they carried eleven interim measures across 2026 — six around two credentials and five around nine appliances — and not one of those measures moved either score by a point.

| | **R-17** | **R-36** |
|---|---|---|
| What moved it | The credential is **absent from the file**, which is what 8.6.2 obliges and what no accommodation could deliver | Marketa can **observe the installed and unremediated state** of nine components, which is what 11.3.1.2 obliges and what no accommodation could deliver |
| Likelihood 4 → 2 | The exposure route — a readable secret in a file reachable by anyone with configuration access — no longer exists | The blind spot — nine components whose vulnerability state was unknown — no longer exists |
| Impact held at 4 | **Both accounts still reach settlement-path data.** Removing the credential from a file does not change what the account can do if it is ever obtained by another route | **The nine appliances are still in the assessed population, in 482 stores, connected to the store back-office fabric.** Visibility changes the probability of an unremediated flaw persisting; it does not change what a compromise of one would mean |
| Why not lower than 2 | Ninety-three vault-brokered credentials and thirty-one workload identities are a strong architecture, and a vault is a system that can fail | **The right to scan is a contract term with a renewal date** |
| Residual | Moderate 8 | Moderate 8, **and a different exposure from the one that was scored in 2026** |

**R-36's residual changing shape is the honest way to report the CAP-06 outcome.** In 2026 the risk was that Marketa could not see inside nine devices. In 2027 the risk is that Marketa's ability to see inside them depends on a clause somebody negotiated once and will negotiate again. Those are not the same risk at the same score by coincidence — **the score is the same because the consequence is the same and the likelihood improved by the same amount as the dependency worsened**, and the register records the substitution rather than presenting the movement as pure gain. Route B — replacement of the appliance class — stays on the 2027 roadmap for exactly that reason.

|---|---|---|

That is how every contested movement in this portfolio has been handled — R-27's return-to-High condition in Phase 07, R-14's 90-day clock reset on 2026-07-03, R-42's expiring likelihood in §6.1. **A movement with a stated reversal condition is a movement somebody can hold you to.**

Twenty-six of the register's entries were touched by the assessment in the ordinary sense that the controls treating them were tested. Four are held **explicitly**, because in each case there is a plausible argument for a movement and the programme is declining to make it.

| Risk | Rating | Why it is held |
|---|---|---|
| **R-43** — incident procedures not exercised against the scenarios this estate produces | **Held** | **The incident response plan has still never handled a confirmed compromise of account data.** 512 incident records, 0 SEV-1, 5 SEV-2, none of them a breach. An assessment is not an incident, and a QSA testing 12.10 tests whether the plan exists, covers the required elements and has been exercised — **not whether it works on the worst morning**. 06.13 and 07.13 both warned in advance that use is not proof, and this phase does not release what two phases held |
| **R-22** — customer-initiated account data | **Held** | The controls met their **first October–January peak**, which is the evidence the entry has been waiting for since it was raised. **The complete peak dataset closes 2027-02-01** and the analysis belongs to Phase 09 — **OI-07-04**. | **R-07** — account data in the messaging estate | **Held** | Identical reasoning to R-22, and the two were held together in Phase 07 for the same reason. **The peak completes after the assessment and the measurement belongs to the phase that can see all of it** |

The general form, so that a reader can apply it to the rest of the register: **an assessment corroborates a control; it does not supply the operating evidence a rating moves on.** Where the register is waiting for a season, a penetration test or an incident, an assessor's opinion is not a substitute for any of them — and the four entries above are the four places where saying so costs the programme something.

### 4.1 The rest of the register — touched, corroborated, and held

The four above are held **explicitly** because each has a plausible argument for movement. The remainder of the register was touched in the ordinary sense — the controls treating those entries were among the 306 the assessor tested — and held without argument. The reason is one sentence and it applies to all of them:

> **An assessment tells you that a control exists, operates and can be evidenced. The register scores exposure, and exposure is unchanged by a second party agreeing that the control is there.**

| Category of held entry | Why the assessment does not move it |
|---|---|
| Entries whose treating controls were assessed **In Place** and which had already moved on operating evidence in Phase 06 or Phase 07 | The movement has been made. **Corroboration is not a second reduction** |
| Entries whose treating controls were assessed **In Place** and which are held pending an operating cycle — a season, a peak, a second penetration test | The cycle is what they are waiting for, and the assessment is not it |
| Entries treating exposures the standard does not fully reach — provider concentration, insider access, design trade-offs priced in Phase 03 | **A clean assessment of Requirement 12.8 does not reduce the consequence of a Truvance compromise**, and 06.10 §8 says so at length |
| Entries whose treatment is another phase's open item | Eleven open items carry dates from **2027-01-31** to 2027-06-30, **one of which — OI-06-04 — is already 18 days overdue at the phase close**. **A rating does not move on a plan, and it certainly does not move on a late one** |

## 5. Every Movement Is a Likelihood Movement

**Every movement in this phase is a likelihood movement.** The programme has reduced an impact score **three times in twelve months, and names all three**: **R-10** in Phase 06, where the suspension rule recorded in advance turned a provider lapse from an improvised argument under deadline pressure into a defined change in the assessed position; and **R-14** and **R-31** in Phase 07, where the path the segmentation test actually proved was Z7 to Z5 rather than into the cardholder data environment, and where the historical account-data corpus was destroyed and evidenced rather than merely controlled. In each case something changed about the **consequence**, and the reason is on the record in the phase that made the change. **Impact has never been reduced in this programme as a way of moving a score that likelihood would not move**, and Phase 08 reduces none.

The Phase 08 claim is checkable, which is why it is made in this form. In the table at §3, **the From I and To I columns are identical in all seven rows.** R-17 holds at 4, R-36 holds at 4, R-13 holds at 3, R-32 holds at 4, R-41 holds at 2, R-42 holds at 3, R-38 holds at 2.

### 5.1 Why impact does not fall

| Reason | Statement |
|---|---|
| **Impact is calibrated on consequence, and the consequence is set by the business** | The register's impact dimension is **cardholder data exposure, brand-programme penalties, and business interruption**. Those are properties of a **$4.2B** retailer processing **68.4M** card transactions a year across 482 stores. **No control Marketa builds makes a compromise cheaper** |
| **Controls change probability, not magnitude** | A vault, an authenticated scan, an inspection round and a joiner-mover-leaver process all reduce the chance of an event. None of them reduces what the event would cost if it happened |
| **The exceptions are exactly the controls that bound consequence, and they are rare** | The only mechanisms that genuinely reduce impact are architectural: **P2PE, tokenization and DTMF masking**, which reduced the impact of a store, e-commerce or call-centre compromise by removing the primary account number from the path. **Those decisions belong to Phase 02, they are recorded there as scope reduction rather than as risk-score adjustments**, and the register's impact scores were set afterwards, against the reduced estate |
| **A falling impact score is the easiest way to flatter a register** | Impact is the harder number to defend and the easier number to argue down, because it is a judgement about a hypothetical. A programme that reduces impact scores as its controls mature produces a register that improves twice for every control it builds |

### 5.2 What the discipline costs

It is not free, and the cost is visible in this phase. **R-17 and R-36 both come off High and land at Moderate 8 rather than at Low**, because with impact held at 4, likelihood would have to fall to 1 to reach the Low band — and likelihood 1 means *the event is not reasonably foreseeable*, which is not a claim either entry can support. **Two requirements that were Not in Place and are now In Place still leave the register carrying two Moderate entries**, and a programme willing to move impact could have reported both as Low. The three impact reductions the programme has made were each argued on **a change in consequence that had actually occurred** — a suspension rule that converts a provider lapse into a defined position, a proved path that ran Z7 to Z5 rather than into the cardholder data environment, a destroyed and evidenced corpus. **Nothing of that kind has occurred here.** Neither the settlement-path reach of two accounts nor the presence of nine appliances in 482 stores changed on 2027-02-18.

The same discipline produces the register's single most challengeable number, and it is stated here rather than left for a reader to find. **R-42 is the only entry the register has ever carried at likelihood 1, and it earns it in the narrowest possible way.** The risk describes a specific event on a specific date — evidence unavailable to an assessor in November 2026 — and that date passed with **214 of 214 requests answered and none unsatisfied**. It returns to 2 on **1 January 2027**, when a new evidence year begins with no history behind it. **A likelihood of 1 that expires is the only kind this register will carry**, and **§6.1 records the reversion actually happening** rather than leaving a temporary score to become permanent by inattention.

### 6.3 The forecast this phase corrects

**R-17 and R-36 are still on the register as Moderate entries at the Phase 09 close. They are two of the thirteen** the forecast allows for — and this phase declines to reconcile the composition of the remaining eleven, because **it does not reconcile.**

03.12 §6 named thirteen Moderate→Low entries. **Four of them — R-10, R-19, R-26 and R-34 — already reached Low in Phase 06**, leaving nine. Two of the nine, **R-22 and R-23**, and several of the rest are scored **3 × 4**, and a 3 × 4 entry moving on likelihood alone reaches **2 × 4 = 8**, which is Moderate. **Under the discipline §5 has just spent a section defending, several of the entries the forecast moves to Low cannot get there.**

This phase does not resolve that. It records it, and hands Phase 09 the reconciliation with a warning: **on the arithmetic visible from here, the published close of 0 · 13 · 31 is unlikely to be met, and the shortfall will be in the Moderate band.** Phase 09 reports the actual close against the published forecast, whichever way it falls.

**06.12 §7 forecast both R-17 and R-36 reaching Low, and that forecast was wrong.** It was made when the only visible route out of High was the closure of the requirement each entry was held by, and it assumed that closing the requirement would carry the entry all the way down. It does not. **A remediation that closes a requirement takes a risk to Moderate when impact is held at 4, and leaves it there** — because likelihood 2 is as low as either entry can support, and 2 × 4 = 8 is Moderate by the model 03.10 published.

| Statement | Where it was made | Status |
|---|---|---|
| R-17 and R-36 move **High → Low** on remediation | 06.12 §7, movement band table | **Wrong, and corrected here** |
| The close reconciles at **0 High · 13 Moderate · 31 Low** | 03.12 §6, restated in 04.12, 05.12, 06.12, 07.12 | **Published, and NOT reconciled here.** Phase 09 reports against it |
| The eleven Moderate entries leaving for Low | Nine forecast in 03.12 §6, plus two unidentified | **Under-determined.** Several of the nine are 3 × 4 entries that cannot reach Low on likelihood alone |

**The close position is a forecast, not a commitment, and this phase stops treating it as arithmetic that has already been checked.** This phase corrects an earlier phase's forecast in the open rather than quietly restating it, which is the same treatment every other published number in this programme has had — the 46-hour customized-approach estimate against 51 actual, the 90-day R-14 exit clock reset on 2026-07-03, and the awareness denominator that counts 611 people who are not yet late.

## 7. Reverse Traceability — Does the Assessment Produce Anything the Register Lacks?

The forward map asks whether every risk has a control. The reverse test asks whether the assessment surfaced any exposure the register does not already describe. **ADR-0005** obliges a new entry when discovery **disproves an assumption**, not whenever a control or an assessor produces a finding.

| Candidate | Test | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| **1.2.8** — two of nine network security control ruleset backups on an unrestricted file share, found during fieldwork | Was an assumption disproved? **Yes, narrowly** — that backup destinations inherit the protection of their source. Would a new entry describe something the register does not? **R-32** is a boundary-affecting change classified as routine and **R-42** is evidence handling. The exposure is an instance of the first | **No entry raised.** Recorded as an instance under R-32; corrected **2026-11-05** and disclosed under **ADR-0029** |
| **5.3.3** — USB mass-storage capability enabled on **31 of 71** components while policy prohibits removable media | Was an assumption disproved? **Yes** — that a policy prohibition describes the estate's capability. Would a new entry describe something the register does not? The requirement is **In Place** on the EDR device-control module, so the exposure is governed | **No entry raised.** The disproof changed a determination rather than an exposure, and the lesson is recorded in **08.08 §4** |
| **The 17 assessor observations** | Do any of them describe an exposure the register does not carry? | **None raised an entry in this phase.** They **are not closed**, they affected no disposition, and they pass to **Phase 09** as an inheritance rather than as a backlog somebody has already discounted |
| **The customized approach limitation at 8.3.9** | Is *the approach does not carry forward* a new risk? | **No entry raised.** It is a property of the mechanism, recorded in **08.06** and in the 2027 calendar, and it becomes a Phase 09 planning obligation when the assessor rotates in **2028** |

Four candidates, four tests, **no new entry**. The register remains at **44**, on the ground 05.12 §6.1 stated and 06.12 §6.1 applied: **a register that grows by one entry every time a control produces a finding stops describing exposure and starts describing activity.**

## 8. What This Traceability Does Not Prove

| Limitation | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **An assessment is fifteen days** | The assessor observed a fifteen-business-day window across a sampled population. **Five of the seven movements rest on evidence from a full operating year that the assessment corroborated**, and two rest on remediation the assessor re-tested on a single day |
| **Corroboration is not independence from the entity's evidence** | **1,284 of 1,654** indexed artefacts are documents Marketa produced. The assessor tested them and performed **34** independent actions of its own, and the ratio is the honest description of what an assessment is |
| **The two completed treatments are the newest things in the register** | R-17 and R-36 move on remediation completed in the last two days of January and re-tested eighteen days later. **Neither has an operating history at its new score**, and Phase 09 reports the first one |
| **R-36's residual is a contract** | The credential-release right has a renewal date. **A rating that depends on a commercial term is a rating with a diary entry**, and Route B remains on the 2027 roadmap because of it |
| **Four holds are judgements, and judgements can be wrong in either direction** | **R-43 in particular could be argued down** — the plan has been exercised eleven times and tested by an exercise designed to break it. It is held because the event it exists for has not happened, and that is a judgement rather than a calculation |
| **The register moving is not the environment changing** | Five of the seven movements are the register catching up with controls that have been running since the spring. **Nothing about the estate changed in November because an assessor was in it**, and a reader who treats the movement table as a description of 2027's environment has read it backwards |
