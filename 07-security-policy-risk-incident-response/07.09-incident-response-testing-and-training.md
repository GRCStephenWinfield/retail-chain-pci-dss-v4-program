## Purpose

07.08 delivered a plan. This document delivers the three things that decide whether the plan is real: **somebody is available at three in the morning (12.10.3)**, **those people have been trained recently enough for the training to still be true (12.10.4 and 12.10.4.1)**, and **the plan has been tested against a scenario that could embarrass it (12.10.2)**.

The middle one is where **TRA-09** lives. 12.10.4.1 is one of the nine requirements that hand the entity the frequency and then ask for the analysis, and 07.03 gave TRA-09 a register identity, an owner, a review date and a trigger set. This document gives it its substance: the asset, the threat, the frequency, why not a shorter one, the trigger set, the detective backstop, and the determination with an accepting authority and a date.

The last one is where the portfolio's standing rule applies. **A tabletop that finds nothing was not a test.** Marketa's 2026 exercise found six things and disproved one of the plan's load-bearing assumptions, and §5 reports the disproof first.

## 2. 12.10.3 — Designated Personnel, Available 24/7

### 2.1 The roster

| Tier | Population | Rotation | Response commitment | Authority held |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Duty Incident Commander** | **4** — Naomi Bhatt, Owen Castellanos, Marcus Hale, and a fourth nominated senior manager | One week in four | Contactable within 15 minutes at any hour | Declare any severity including SEV-1; commission forensics; make the NOT-1 determination with the General Counsel |
| **On-call security responder** | **6** security operations analysts | One week in six | **Acknowledge within 15 minutes**, 24/7 | Declare up to SEV-2 without approval; invoke any playbook's pre-authorised containment |
| **Domain on-call — e-commerce** | 5 engineers | One week in five | 30 minutes out of hours; 15 at P1 | The four payment-page containment options, including tightening the Content Security Policy at the edge unilaterally |

| **Named backstop** | Naomi Bhatt | Standing | — | The roster never has a gap, because the CISO is the terminal escalation and holds no rotation exemption |

**Twelve individuals hold the two tiers that 12.10.3 is actually about**, and each of them is named in the plan, in the contact register and in the roster publication, with a personal mobile number verified monthly.

### 2.2 Northbridge, and the distinction 12.10.3 turns on

Marketa outsources after-hours first-line monitoring and triage to **Northbridge Managed Services**, 22:00 to 06:00, across the SIEM covering all 71 assessed components, under a signed 12.8.5 responsibility matrix.

> **Northbridge is not a 12.10.3 designated responder, and Marketa does not present it as one.**

The requirement asks for *specific personnel* designated by the entity to respond to suspected or confirmed incidents. Northbridge detects, triages against an agreed use-case set, and escalates. **Every incident decision is Marketa's**, the matrix says so in the row that matters, and a missed detection inside a covered use case is Northbridge's while a coverage gap is Marketa's. An entity that answers 12.10.3 with "we have a managed SOC" has answered a different question, and it is one of the more common ways this sub-requirement is over-claimed.

What Northbridge does discharge is the thing that makes the 12.10.3 roster workable: at 03:00 there is a human watching, so the on-call responder is woken by an escalation from a person who has already looked, rather than by a pager firing into an empty room.

### 2.3 Proving availability rather than asserting it

| Instrument | Cadence | 2026 result |
|---|---|---|
| **Roster coverage audit** | Monthly | **52 weeks with no uncovered hour** across both tiers, verified against the published rota and the acknowledgement records |
| **Northbridge handover test** — an injected P2 case at 03:00, unannounced | Quarterly | **4 of 4 passed**; slowest acknowledgement **6 minutes** |
| **Unannounced out-of-hours activation test** — a genuine page to the on-call responder requiring a real declaration and a real containment decision | Quarterly | **4 executed.** Acknowledgements at **4, 9 and 11 minutes**, all inside the 15-minute commitment. **The fourth is reported in §2.4** |
| Contact register verification | Monthly | 12 of 12 cycles; two failures found and corrected; **one failure missed and found by the annual test instead** — §5.3 |

### 2.4 The test that did not go cleanly

On the **2026-08-19** unannounced activation test, at 02:47, the primary on-call responder did not acknowledge. The secondary acknowledged at **12 minutes**, inside the commitment, and the declaration and containment decision that followed were both correct.

Marketa reports this rather than the three clean results, for the same reason 06.03 reports four undetected techniques rather than forty-two detected ones. The cause was mundane and instructive: the responder's device was in a do-not-disturb mode that the paging application's critical-alert exemption had lost at an operating system update, silently, three weeks earlier. **Nothing was wrong with the roster, the process or the person.** The remediation is a monthly automated critical-alert delivery test to every roster device, which is now the thirteenth row of the contact register verification, and the finding is carried as **OI-07-02**.

### 3.2 TRA-09 — the targeted risk analysis for 12.10.4.1

Performed under **PS-1**, which obliges six elements and returns an analysis missing any of them.

| # | Element | TRA-09's content |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **The asset and the consequence** | The asset is the response capability itself — the ability of a named human, at any hour, to recognise a suspected incident, declare it, contain it without destroying the evidence, and start a 24-hour contractual clock correctly. The consequence of the capability degrading is not a missed alert; it is **a correctly detected incident handled wrongly.** Marketa's own evidence for that consequence is not hypothetical: two of nine call-centre agents would have deleted a recording containing spoken PAN, and one of twelve store managers would have removed a suspected tampered terminal before reporting it. **Both instincts destroy the evidence of the incident, and both belong to people who were trying to help** |
| **2** | **The threats and their likelihood in Marketa's environment** | E-commerce skimming against pages Marketa serves but whose scripts it does not all control — **R-01**'s subject, and the class in which the year's three page-integrity incidents fell. POI tampering and substitution across 1,914 terminals in 482 stores staffed by a workforce that grows by 6,800 people every October. Destructive malware interrupting trading — **R-20**. Account data arriving where nobody expects it — four real instances, **R-31** and **R-22**. Provider-side compromise, where the incident begins in an environment Marketa cannot audit. The likelihoods are not generic: **the roster turns over, the estate is seasonal, and two of the four incident classes above occurred in this operating period** |
| **3** | **The frequency, and why not a shorter one** | **Semi-annual for the core team of 12; annual for the extended roster of 47.** A shorter cadence was examined explicitly. **Quarterly for the core team was costed and rejected**: twelve people times four cycles times a half-day is 24 person-days a year of the same twelve people who are also the on-call roster, and the analysis concluded that the marginal knowledge retention between six months and three months is smaller than the marginal loss of on-call availability the training itself consumes. **Semi-annual for the extended roster was examined and rejected** on a different ground — the extended roster's function is recognition and escalation, not decision-making, and that knowledge is reinforced continuously by AW-1, the fortnightly huddle card and the incident population itself, rather than by a module |
| **4** | **What would have to change for the frequency to become inadequate** — the trigger set | **T1** — any **Critical** penetration test finding. **T2** — any confirmed security incident at SEV-1, or any incident where the response itself is found deficient at stage 8. **T3** — any change to the response team roster exceeding **25%** of either tier. **T4** — any material change to the plan, meaning a new playbook or a changed notification obligation. **T5** — any 12.10.7 detection in a class not already covered by **P-1 to P-4** |
| **5** | **The detective backstop** — what detects a failure between runs | The **quarterly unannounced out-of-hours activation test**, which is a live measurement of whether the training holds at 02:47 rather than at 10:00 in a classroom; the **quarterly Northbridge handover test**; the stage-8 review of every declared incident, which asks whether the response was correct and not only whether the outcome was; and the **annual tabletop**, which is the only instrument that tests the parts of the plan no real incident has yet reached |
| **6** | **The determination, the accepting authority and the next review date** | **Determined:** semi-annual core, annual extended, with T1–T5 event triggers. **Accepted by:** Naomi Bhatt, CISO. **Owner:** Marcus Hale. **Next review:** **2027-07-14**, staggered deliberately away from the other thirteen analyses |

### 3.3 The trigger that fired

**T1 fired on 2026-09-16**, when Ironwood reached an unauthenticated administrative console on a cardholder data environment component from an assumed-compromise position in the administrative zone. The finding was Critical, the stop-and-notify was invoked at 14:20, and a 12.10 incident record was opened rather than a defect ticket.

The consequence for TRA-09 was mechanical and is recorded as such: **the core team's second 2026 training cycle was brought forward from December to 2026-10-07**, and its playbook drill was re-selected to **IR-P5** and **IR-P10** — unauthorised access, and unauthorised administrative action — against the September incident used as live case material rather than against a constructed scenario.

**An analysis whose triggers never fire is an analysis nobody is testing against.** TRA-09's fired once, in its first year, and it changed a date and a syllabus rather than producing a note.

## 4. 12.10.2 — the Annual Review

12.10.2 has two limbs and they are separable: the plan is **reviewed and updated as needed**, and the plan is **tested including all elements listed in 12.10.1**. An entity can pass the first and fail the second, and the more common failure is passing the second with an exercise that tests three elements and asserts the other four.

| Attribute | 2026 position |
|---|---|
| Review window | **2026-06-29 to 2026-06-30** |
| Performed by | Naomi Bhatt with Owen Castellanos, Marcus Hale and Frank Mueller |
| Inputs | The year's **512 incident records**; the six tabletop findings; the four unannounced activation tests; the 19 penetration test findings; the 21 critical security control failures; the four Phase 02 unexpected-PAN findings; the Retail and Hospitality ISAC threat briefing; the 44-entry risk register |
| Output | Plan **v3.0**, approved 2026-07-15. **Nine amendments**, of which six came from the tabletop and the activation tests and three from industry developments — the modification history is reported in **07.10 §6**, because 12.10.6 is the requirement it evidences |
| Sections examined and deliberately unchanged | **11**, each with a reason |

| **2 — Incident response procedures with containment and mitigation by incident type** | **Live in TT-2026-01** for IR-P1 and IR-P2; **drilled separately** across all ten playbooks in the training cycles | **Tested. One finding — TTF-5** |

## 5. TT-2026-01 — the Annual Test

### 5.1 The exercise

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Identifier | **TT-2026-01** |
| Date | **2026-06-24**, 09:00 to 13:15 |
| Format | Facilitated tabletop with injects on a compressed clock — four hours of exercise time representing 30 hours of incident time |
| **Scenario selection** | **Designed by Rosa Delgado, Director of Internal Audit, and not disclosed to any participant in advance** — including the Incident Commander, including the CISO |
| Facilitator | Rosa Delgado, with an independent scribe from the compliance team |
| Attendees | **17** — Naomi Bhatt, Owen Castellanos, Marcus Hale, Trevor Kim, Sonia Rendell, Elena Marchetti, Frank Mueller, Adaeze Nwosu, Bill Traynor, Raymond Voss, two security operations analysts from the on-call roster, two e-commerce engineers, one network engineer, one payment operations analyst, and one store district manager |
| Who was **not** in the room | **Sable Ridge Assurance.** The QSA received the exercise report and the findings; it did not have a seat. An exercise designed to expose weaknesses is a worse exercise when the assessor is watching people expose them |
| Observers | Priya Raghunathan, Chair of the Audit Committee, for the final hour |

> **ADR-0027 — The exercise scenario is chosen by somebody who did not write the plan.**
>
> Marketa's 2025 exercise was designed by the security team, against a scenario the security team considered likely, and the plan performed well. The 2026 exercise was designed by Internal Audit with a standing brief to find the seam. The decision was recorded because it has a cost: **the CISO cannot prepare, the exercise may make the security function look bad in front of the Audit Committee Chair, and that is the point.** An organisation that only tests scenarios its security team thought of has tested its security team's imagination.

### 5.2 The scenario, and what it disproved

**The inject at 09:00 was not an alert.** It was a telephone call from Cardinal Merchant Bank at 07:40 on a Tuesday in November, reporting that an issuer's fraud analytics had identified approximately **1,900 accounts with a common point of purchase at Marketa's e-commerce channel** across a six-week window. No Marketa detection had fired. No alert existed. The first Marketa knew of a possible compromise was somebody else's evidence.

That choice was deliberate on Rosa Delgado's part, and it is the reason the exercise found what it found. **Every scenario the security team had ever built started with a Marketa alert**, and a plan tested only against its own detections is tested against the cases where it already works.

> ### The disproof
>
> **The plan's implicit claim was that Marketa could scope an account data compromise on its own evidence. It cannot.**
>
> Asked to identify which transactions and which accounts were potentially affected on the named templates in the named window, the room reached the correct answer in fifty-five minutes and the answer was: **Marketa does not hold that information.** Marketa stores tokens and truncated PAN. The token-to-account mapping is Truvance's, the six-week enumeration would be a bulk incident-driven request into a provider Marketa cannot audit, and — the part nobody in the room knew until Frank Mueller opened the contract — **the Truvance agreement carries no response-time commitment for it.**
>
> This is the price of the scope reduction, arriving in the one place the programme had not looked for it. **604 to 71 was achieved by putting the primary account number somewhere else. It did not put the obligation to answer questions about the primary account number somewhere else**, and an acquirer asking a merchant to scope a common-point-of-purchase report will not accept "our provider holds that" as a scoping answer. 06.10 §8 stated the price in assurance terms. TT-2026-01 stated it in operational terms: **on the morning it matters, Marketa's ability to answer is a third party's service level, and the service level does not exist.**

### 5.3 The six findings

| ID | Finding | Class | What changed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TTF-1** | **Marketa cannot enumerate potentially affected accounts from its own records, and the provider agreement carries no response-time commitment for the enumeration.** Fifty-five minutes of exercise time reached a determination that the answer was unobtainable within Marketa's control | **The disproof** | An operational incident runbook agreed with Truvance's incident team, naming a contact, a request format and a target; and **OI-07-01** — a contractual response-time commitment for incident-driven token resolution, to be negotiated at the **2027-03-31** AOC anniversary and renewal |
| **TTF-2** | **The determination took 9 hours 20 minutes of exercise clock.** The room tried to establish whether a compromise had occurred *before* notifying, on an instinct that a notification should carry a conclusion | Timing | Plan v3.0 states that **NOT-1 may and must be discharged on an `undetermined` determination**, that *undetermined* is a permitted answer, and that the Incident Commander records why a conclusion was not reached. The outer bound from suspicion to notification is now stated as **48 hours** and is a plan obligation |
| **TTF-3** | **The General Counsel's after-hours number routed to a voicemail box that had been full since March.** The monthly contact verification had passed every cycle | Contact strategy | The verification now calls **the out-of-hours number, at a randomised hour**, and requires a human acknowledgement. **A control that only tests the path that works will pass forever** |
| **TTF-4** | **Two participants believed Marketa engages a PCI Forensic Investigator directly and selects the firm.** Under a brand mandate the direction comes through the acquirer | Brand procedures | Annex A extended with a one-page *who decides what* table covering PFI engagement, brand direction and Marketa's cooperation obligation under **C10** |
| **TTF-5** | **The pre-authorised payment-page containment options were bound to a tamper alert, not to an incident.** With no alert, nobody was certain who could authorise serving the minimal payment template | Containment | Pre-authorisation re-expressed against **incident severity** rather than alert severity. **IR-P2**'s options are now available at any SEV-2 or above however the incident arrived |
| **TTF-6** | **The scribe role, the store and MOTO continuity position, and the flow-record enumeration all performed as designed** and required no change | Confirmation | Recorded, because an exercise that reports only failures gives the reader no way to calibrate the failures it reports |

### 5.4 What the exercise cost, and what it did not prove

Seventeen people for four and a quarter hours is roughly **9 person-days**, plus five days of Internal Audit design time. The Steering Committee approved it as a recurring annual cost.

| It does not prove | Because |
|---|---|
| That the plan works | It proves that seventeen people who knew they were in an exercise, at 09:00, with the right people already in the room, reached defensible decisions. **Nobody was tired, nobody was missing, and nothing was actually broken** |
| That the timings are achievable | The clock was compressed and the injects were scripted. **TTF-2's 9 hours 20 minutes is an exercise measurement, and the real number is unknown and is probably worse** |
| That TTF-1 is fixed | An operational runbook with a provider's incident team is a good thing to have and it is not a contractual commitment. **OI-07-01 does not close until a renewal Marketa has not yet negotiated** |
| That the scenario was the right scenario | It was one scenario, chosen by one person, for one exercise. The 2027 exercise will be designed by Internal Audit again, and against something else |

## 7. What This Document Does Not Claim

| Limitation | Statement |
|---|---|
| **A tabletop is not an incident** | TT-2026-01 tested judgement under scripted conditions. **The plan has never been activated for a confirmed compromise of account data**, and the exercise is the closest Marketa has come to finding out what that would be like |
| **The frequency in TRA-09 is a judgement, not a measurement** | Semi-annual is defensible and it is not proved. The trigger set and the quarterly activation tests are the only mechanisms that test it, and one of the triggers has fired once |
| **Availability is measured on four tests** | Four unannounced out-of-hours activations in a year is a small sample of 8,760 hours, and one of the four exposed a failure mode nobody had modelled |
| **TTF-1 remains open** | The most important finding of the year's most important exercise is treated by a runbook and a renewal negotiation. **It is not closed and this document does not present it as closed** |
| **The exercise was designed to find something** | It did, which is evidence about the design as much as about the plan. A reader should treat the absence of findings in any future exercise as a question about the exercise, not as a result |
