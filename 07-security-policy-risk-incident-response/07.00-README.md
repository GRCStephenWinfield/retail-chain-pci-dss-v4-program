## What this phase is

**Requirement 12** — the governance spine — and the last build phase before Sable Ridge arrives. It closes the policy suite, consolidates all fourteen targeted risk analyses into one register, delivers awareness and screening, performs the **12.5.2 annual scope confirmation**, and formally documents the incident response programme including **12.10.7 — PAN found where it is not expected**.

It is also where the register makes its last move before assessment, and where the programme decides what it will walk in carrying.

## The decision that defines the phase

Three weeks before fieldwork, the Steering Committee was offered a way out. A late compensating-control argument might have moved one or both of the programme's **Not in Place** findings to In Place. There was time to attempt it.

**They declined** (DEC-710). The CISO's reasoning:

> "We refused a compensating control for 8.6.2 in June and for 11.3.1.2 in July, on the record, for reasons that have not changed. Arguing one now because the assessment is three weeks away would tell the assessor exactly what our earlier refusals were worth."

The CFO — who signs the AOC under ADR-0002 — asked what he would be signing. Recorded verbatim so nobody could later claim surprise:

> "A non-compliant Attestation of Compliance, with two requirements recorded as Not in Place and a remediation date of 31 January 2027, and a re-assessment on 18 February 2027 that we expect to pass. That is what we have. It is also what we told the Audit Committee in March we would rather have than the alternative."

## The two risks that came down — on criteria written six months earlier

The register enters the assessment at **2 High · 22 Moderate · 20 Low**. Five risks moved in this phase; two of them matter more than the others.

**R-14** and **R-31** were the two risks **raised on evidence** during the programme — one by a failed segmentation test, one by a discovery scan that found account data nobody expected. Both were given **exit criteria in writing, in advance, before anyone knew whether they would be met.**

| Risk | Exit criterion, published in | Met |
|---|---|---|
| **R-14** — segmentation not effective | **90 days of clean drift detection** (Phase 03). The clock was reset once, on 2026-07-03, when two further divergences appeared — and the reset was honoured rather than argued away | **Completed 2026-10-01** |
| **R-31** — account data in unstructured stores | **A second full discovery run returning nothing** (Phase 03) — not the cleanup of the first | **Ran 2026-08-17 → 08-28. Returned clean** |

A risk that comes down on a criterion set six months earlier is a different kind of claim from one that comes down because a phase ended. Both came down on their own terms, and neither criterion was quietly relaxed on the way.

**R-27's movement is the thinnest of the five**, and carries a recorded dissent and a return-to-High condition. A movement nobody objected to and a movement whose objection was written down are different artefacts.

## The fourteen targeted risk analyses, consolidated

v4 lets an entity define its own frequency for a number of requirements — and obliges it to justify each one in a **targeted risk analysis under 12.3.1**. Marketa has **fourteen**, and this phase puts them in one register for the first time.

| Type | Count | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **12.3.1** | 9 | An entity-defined frequency a specific requirement permits |
| **12.3.2** | 1 | The analysis underpinning the customized approach at 8.3.9 |
| **Elective** | 4 | Analyses Marketa wrote where the standard did **not** compel one — 6.3.3, 11.4.5, 12.8.4, 12.5.2 |

The electives exist for a reason worth stating: **a frequency nobody can justify is a frequency nobody will defend at fieldwork.** Writing the reason down when you did not have to is cheaper than improvising it in November.

Fourteen analyses, each reviewed at least every 12 months, is a real annual maintenance burden — plus the separate **12.3.3** cryptographic review (four cipher suites removed) and **12.3.4** technology review (eleven items approaching end of life). The phase says so rather than presenting the register as a finished artefact.

## 12.10.7 — the limb entities skip

The requirement has three parts: identify PAN in an unexpected location, securely delete or migrate it, and **determine how it got there and remediate the process gap that put it there**.

The first two are easy and everybody does them. The third is the requirement.

Marketa's position is unusual: **the procedures were written in Phase 02, in response to four real discoveries, months before 12.10.7 was formally documented as a control.** Each of P-1 to P-4 exists because somebody asked how the data arrived, not just how to delete it — media (from the call recordings), unstructured document stores (from the dispute share), orphaned assets (from the decommissioned host), and inbound customer-initiated data (from the emailed spreadsheet).

That is the right way round. The far more common artefact is a beautifully written procedure that has never been exercised.

## The tabletop that found six things

**TT-2026-01**, 2026-06-24, 17 attendees. Under **ADR-0027** the scenario was set by **somebody who did not write the plan** — because an author testing their own plan tests the parts they already thought about.

The scenario was deliberately awkward: a **common-point-of-purchase alert arriving from the acquirer**, so the response begins with Marketa already behind and an external clock running. Determination took **9 hours 20 minutes** against a plan that assumed four — not for technical reasons, but because nobody had exercised cross-channel reconciliation against a token namespace held by a third party while counsel needed an answer.

Six findings, including one that mattered a lot: **the 24-hour notification clock runs from "determination", and nobody had defined what determination meant.**

**A tabletop that finds nothing was not a test.**

## The honest position

Internal Audit's note for the file, recorded at the readiness review:

> "In eleven months this programme has raised two risks on evidence, refused two compensating controls, reported a failed ASV scan to its acquirer, failed a segmentation test and published the fact, and is now walking into an assessment with two findings it identified itself. I have no assurance concerns about the accuracy of what is being presented. **That is not the same as saying everything is fixed, and the report should not read as though it is.**"

Both remaining findings are dated **2027-01-31** against a re-assessment on **2027-02-18**. There is **no float** in that, and it is stated rather than disguised.
