## Purpose

The targeted risk analysis is the mechanism v4.0 introduced to let an entity choose its own frequency for a control and still be assessable. Before v4, a requirement either stated a frequency or it did not, and where it did not, the entity did whatever it had always done. **v4.0.1 permits the choice and prices it: you may set the frequency, and you must be able to show the analysis that produced it.**

Marketa performs **fourteen**. Ten are compelled — nine by a requirement that explicitly permits an entity-defined frequency and refers to 12.3.1, and one by the customized approach under 12.3.2. **Four are elective**, performed against requirements that compel no analysis at all, because the underlying judgement is a risk decision and a judgement with no written reason is indistinguishable from a habit.

This document is the consolidated register. It presents all fourteen in one table, explains the difference between the two types and why the electives exist, delivers the two annual technology reviews under **12.3.3** and **12.3.4**, and states plainly what fourteen analyses cost to maintain every year.

**Two of the fourteen are delivered in this phase**: **TRA-09** for 12.10.4.1, the incident response training frequency, whose analysis is here and whose programme is in the second half of this phase; and the elective **TRA-14** for 12.5.2, the definition of a significant change, which 07.06 depends on.

## 1. The Two Types, and the Third Category Marketa Added

| Type | The standard's position | What the analysis has to answer | Marketa's count |
|---|---|---|---|
| **12.3.1 — entity-defined frequency** | A specific requirement permits the entity to determine the frequency of an activity, **and directs that the frequency be supported by a targeted risk analysis performed in accordance with 12.3.1** | Why this frequency, and **why not a shorter one**. What would have to change for it to become inadequate. What detects a failure between runs | **9** |
| **12.3.2 — customized approach** | Where an entity meets the **Customized Approach Objective** by a means other than the defined approach, it performs a targeted risk analysis for that specific requirement, maintains a controls matrix, and the assessor derives testing procedures | The objective, quoted. How the implemented control meets it. Why the defined approach was not used. The controls matrix reference | **1** |
| **Elective** | The standard compels nothing. The requirement either states its own frequency or leaves a judgement unremarked | The same six elements, applied to a judgement the entity was not obliged to write down | **4** |

The distinction between the first two is not a formality. **A 12.3.1 analysis justifies a number. A 12.3.2 analysis justifies a control.** The first can be wrong and still leave a compliant entity — a frequency set too long is a finding against the analysis, not against the requirement's existence. The second, if wrong, means a requirement is not met at all, because there is no defined-approach implementation underneath it. That is why Marketa runs exactly one customized approach, and why 05.06 is one of the longest documents in the portfolio.

### 1.1 Why the four electives exist

| Elective | The requirement's own position | Why Marketa analysed it anyway |
|---|---|---|
| **TRA-11 — 6.3.3** | Critical and high-security patches within one month; **other** patches within an entity-defined "appropriate" time frame. **6.3.3 does not cross-reference 12.3.1**, so no analysis is compelled — 04.06 §3.1 records that determination explicitly | The 90-day and 180-day bands are a risk decision affecting every component in the estate, and they had to be reconciled with **TRA-07**'s remediation windows for non-high, non-critical vulnerabilities. Two entity-defined timeframes that disagree is a finding waiting to happen |
| **TRA-12 — 11.4.5** | Segmentation testing **at least once every 12 months and after any change** to segmentation controls or methods. The frequency is stated; nothing is left to the entity | Marketa asked whether annual is sufficient **for an estate of 482 stores maintained by field engineering**. The answer was that the calendar frequency stands and the change trigger carries the load — and the analysis is what makes that a determination rather than an assumption |
| **TRA-13 — 12.8.4** | Monitor providers' compliance status **at least once every 12 months**. A floor, not a choice | The floor is annual and Marketa moved to quarterly with monthly verification. **A control tighter than the requirement still needs a reason**, or it is cost with no argument |
| **TRA-14 — 12.5.2** | Scope confirmed at least every 12 months **and upon significant change**. The standard does not define "significant change" | The definition **is** the requirement, in practice. An entity that never writes it down has defined it as nothing, because a trigger nobody can recognise never fires. **SIG-1 to SIG-10** is the answer and this analysis is its basis |

**TRA-12 is the elective that concluded "no change".** It examined a shorter cadence, costed it, and determined that the calendar frequency was already right. That is a legitimate outcome and it is recorded as one — but the programme's own standard, PS-1, carries the warning that an analysis reaching that conclusion without examining an alternative is not an analysis. TRA-12 examined a six-monthly cadence explicitly, and the reason it rejected it is that **11.4.6's six-month cadence is a service-provider requirement**; adopting it voluntarily would have committed Marketa to an obligation it does not have, on a workload it could not sustain across 482 stores, in exchange for coverage the change trigger already provides.

### 2.1 Reconciling the count

Phase 06's summary recorded **nine of fourteen complete** at its close, and a reader who counts the register's "Delivered in" column reaches twelve. Both are correct and the difference is worth stating rather than smoothing.

| Basis | Count at Phase 06 close | What it counts |
|---|---|---|
| **Standalone 12.3.1 analyses published under their own TRA identifier** | **9** — TRA-01 to TRA-08 and TRA-13 | The figure 06.12 and 06.13 report |
| **Analyses performed, in any form** | **12** | Adds **TRA-10**, whose analysis is 05.06 in full under the identifier `TRA-12.3.2-8.3.9`; **TRA-11**, whose rationale is recorded in 04.06 §3.1 and was completed as a full elective analysis alongside TRA-07; and **TRA-12**, recorded within the testing programme in 06.11 |
| **The register** | **14** | Adds the two this phase delivers — **TRA-09** and **TRA-14** |

**The consolidation is the work, not the arithmetic.** Before this document, three of the fourteen existed inside the documents that produced them, with no separate identifier, no owner recorded as an analysis owner, and no review date anybody was accountable for. An analysis that lives inside another document is an analysis that will not be reviewed, because nothing raises it. **All fourteen now have an ID, an owner, a review date and a trigger set, and that is what 12.3.1's "reviewed at least every 12 months" limb actually requires.**

## 3. What Every Analysis Carries

PS-1, the targeted risk analysis standard, obliges six elements. An analysis missing any of them is returned rather than accepted.

| # | Element | Why it is mandatory |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The asset and the consequence** — what account data or system the control protects, and what happens if it does not operate | Without it the analysis has no scale, and every frequency looks equally defensible |
| 2 | **The threats and their likelihood in Marketa's environment** — not in general | 482 stores, 1,914 terminals, a hosted iframe and a seasonal workforce produce different likelihoods from a generic model |
| 3 | **The frequency, and why not a shorter one** | This is the whole question. An analysis that justifies the chosen frequency without examining a shorter one has not been performed |
| 4 | **What would have to change for the frequency to become inadequate** — the trigger set | The mechanism that makes the analysis a living document rather than an annual artefact |
| 5 | **The detective backstop** — what detects a failure of the control between runs | A frequency is a gap. The analysis must say what watches the gap |
| 6 | **The determination, the accepting authority and the next review date** | Accountability with a name and a date on it |

### 3.1 Trigger sets, and the one that fired

Element 4 is the element most often written as boilerplate. Marketa's triggers are specific, and they are tested by the fact that one of them fired and changed something.

| Analysis | A representative trigger | Status |
|---|---|---|
| TRA-05 — POI inspection | A tamper finding at more than two terminals in one cycle, or any finding at a store inspected within the previous 30 days | Not fired. **Six seal discrepancies in Q3, all at distinct stores, all cleared as maintenance** — below the trigger, and the procedure was tightened anyway |
| TRA-06 — periodic log review | Any component in the monthly population producing a detection that the daily population would have caught within hours | Not fired |
| TRA-07 — other vulnerabilities | The same finding surviving two consecutive quarterly scans | **Fired 43 times.** Every instance escalated and closed. A trigger firing 43 times is a trigger doing its job, not a control failing |
| **TRA-08 — payment-page detection** | **A proof-of-life test in which the mechanism's detection time exceeds its own median by a stated margin** | **FIRED.** The November proof-of-life test produced a slower result than the first; the matrix rotation frequency was doubled, field digest sampling raised to 1 in 10 for mobile profiles, and the analysis review was **brought forward to 2027-02-10** — which is why TRA-08's review date is four months ahead of its cohort |
| TRA-09 — IR training | Any Critical penetration test finding, any confirmed incident, or any change to the response team roster exceeding 25% | **Fired once**, on the September Critical. The core team training cycle was brought forward |
| TRA-14 — significant change | Any change that was **not** classified as significant and subsequently required a scope revisit | **Fired once**, on the Q1 marketing subdomain repoint — see 07.06 §5 |

**An analysis whose triggers never fire is an analysis nobody is testing against.** Four of fourteen trigger sets fired in the operating period, and the two that produced the most change — TRA-08's and TRA-14's — both fired on evidence the programme would rather not have generated.

## 4. Cryptographic Review — 12.3.3

12.3.3 obliges cryptographic cipher suites and protocols in use to be **reviewed at least once every 12 months**, including an up-to-date inventory of all cryptographic cipher suites and protocols in use, active monitoring of industry trends regarding continued viability, and **a documented strategy to respond to anticipated changes** in cryptographic vulnerabilities.

| Element | 2026 position |
|---|---|
| Review performed | 2026-06-22, by Trevor Kim with the cryptographic key custodians |
| Inventory scope | All 71 assessed components; the 4.2.1.1 trusted key and certificate inventory; the ten key classes **K-01 to K-10**; the P2PE and tokenization boundaries where the cryptography is a provider's |
| Protocols in use | TLS 1.2 and TLS 1.3 only. **TLS 1.0 and 1.1 removed in the period** as a SIG-7 significant change |
| Cipher suites | An allow-list rather than a deny-list. Suites are enumerated per termination point and reconciled to the inventory, not inferred from a default |
| Suites removed in the period | Four, on the ordinary trajectory of the industry rather than on any incident |
| Hash and signature position | SHA-1 not present on any in-scope path; certificate signature algorithms enumerated per certificate rather than per issuer |
| Provider-held cryptography | **Verition** operates the P2PE decryption environment; **Truvance** holds the token vault. Marketa holds **no decryption capability** — condition **P2PE-C6** — and the review verifies the condition rather than the algorithm |
| **The anticipated-change strategy** | The requirement's least-performed limb. Marketa's strategy names three anticipated changes, the trigger that would start each response, the inventory query that identifies the affected population, and the owner |

### 4.1 The three anticipated changes

| Anticipated change | Trigger that starts the response | Affected population, from the inventory | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deprecation pressure on **TLS 1.2** in favour of 1.3 | A brand, acquirer or provider announcement, or a Council bulletin | 61 of 71 components terminate or originate TLS; 19 are provider-dependent | Trevor Kim |
| Migration pressure from classical public-key cryptography toward post-quantum algorithms | Published, ratified algorithm standards reaching the platform vendors Marketa actually runs | Every certificate in the 4.2.1.1 inventory; the store estate is the long pole because of **CON-01** and **CON-02** | Trevor Kim |
| A cipher suite or construction moving from "discouraged" to "broken" without notice | A credible published break, or a vendor emergency advisory | Determined by inventory query at the time. **The response depends on being able to answer "where do we use it" in hours rather than weeks**, which is why the inventory is per-termination-point | Trevor Kim |

The third row is the point of 12.3.3. The requirement is not really asking whether the cipher suites are good today; it is asking whether the entity would know what to change on the morning one of them stops being good. **An inventory recorded per issuer or per product cannot answer that. An inventory recorded per termination point can.**

## 5. Technology Currency Review — 12.3.4

12.3.4 obliges hardware and software technologies in use to be **reviewed at least once every 12 months**, including analysis that technologies continue to receive security fixes from vendors promptly, analysis of whether any technology is no longer supported or approaching end of support, and a plan — approved by executive management — to remediate outdated technologies.

| Element | 2026 position |
|---|---|
| Review performed | 2026-06-29, by Trevor Kim with the six baseline owners |
| Population | The **71** assessed components across **BL-WIN 18 · BL-LNX 15 · BL-NET 11 · BL-AWS 6 · BL-DB 3 · BL-APP 12 · 6 un-baselined SaaS control planes** |
| Components on a currently supported release | **66 of 71** |
| Components with a vendor end-of-support date **inside 24 months** | **11**, each with a named successor and a scheduled window outside the freeze |
| Components on a release no longer receiving security fixes | **0** |
| **Components where vendor support exists but does not deliver what the programme needs** | **9** — the POS back-office appliances behind **CON-03** |
| Remediation plan | Approved by the PCI Steering Committee, chaired by the CFO |

### 5.1 The nine appliances, seen from 12.3.4 rather than from 11.3.1.2

The nine vendor-locked POS back-office appliances are supported. The vendor issues firmware, publishes advisories and honours the agreement. **They pass 12.3.4's literal test and fail its purpose**, and the technology review is where that gets recorded in its own right rather than as a consequence of the scanning finding.

| Question 12.3.4 asks | Answer for the nine |
|---|---|
| Does the technology continue to receive security fixes from the vendor promptly? | **Yes.** Firmware is issued on a published cadence and applied |
| Is it approaching or past end of support? | **No** |
| Is there a plan for outdated technology? | **Not applicable — it is not outdated** |
| *The question 12.3.4 does not ask* | **Whether Marketa can verify what is installed on it.** It cannot: the support agreement provides neither administrative credential release nor agent installation rights, which is **11.3.1.2 Not in Place** and **CAP-06** |

The distinction is worth isolating because it is the kind of thing an annual review can launder. A technology review that reports "66 of 71 supported, 0 unsupported" and stops is accurate and useless. **The nine appliances are the most important row in this review and they do not appear in its headline figure**, which is why the row exists.

## 6. The Annual Maintenance Burden, Stated Plainly

Fourteen analyses, two annual technology reviews, and a policy suite of seventeen documents. This is what it costs every year, in perpetuity, and the programme states it rather than presenting the analyses as a one-time deliverable.

| Activity | Frequency | Effort, per cycle | Who cannot delegate it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Review of the fourteen targeted risk analyses | At least every 12 months each | Roughly **3 to 5 working days per analysis** where the environment has changed; 1 day where it has not | The named owner. **Six different people own the fourteen** |
| Verification that each trigger set is still the right trigger set | With the review | Included above — and it is the part that gets skipped | The owner |
| **12.3.3** cryptographic review | Annual | 4 to 6 days, most of it inventory reconciliation rather than analysis | Trevor Kim |
| **12.3.4** technology currency review | Annual | 3 to 4 days across six baseline owners | Trevor Kim |
| Consolidation and register maintenance | Continuous, formally annual | Roughly 0.15 FTE | Owen Castellanos |
| Steering Committee approval of any changed determination | On change | Per instance | Naomi Bhatt |

Three honest observations follow from that table.

**Fourteen is a lot, and four of them Marketa chose.** Ten are compelled. The other four exist because the programme decided that a judgement worth making is worth writing down, and each one is now a recurring annual obligation with an owner and a date. That is a deliberate, permanent cost accepted for a benefit — a defensible answer when the assessor asks — and a reader is entitled to notice that Marketa manufactured 40% of its own analysis burden.

**The review is where these fail, not the drafting.** Every one of the fourteen was written by somebody who cared about it, in the phase that built the control. The failure mode is year three: the owner has changed role, the environment has moved, the analysis still says six-monthly, and nobody has asked whether six-monthly is still right. **The staggered review calendar and the named owner exist for year three, not for this year.**

**A frequency chosen without an analysis fails 12.3.1 even when the frequency is correct.** This is one of the most common v4 findings and it is worth restating in the document that consolidates the analyses. Quarterly POI inspection would have been the right answer whether or not TRA-05 existed. Without TRA-05, it is a habit with a number attached, and the requirement is not satisfied by being accidentally right.

## 8. What This Register Does Not Establish

| Limitation | Statement |
|---|---|
| **An analysis is a justification, not a measurement** | Fourteen documents establish that Marketa can explain every entity-defined frequency in its programme. They do not establish that any of the frequencies is right. **The trigger sets are the only mechanism that tests them, and four of fourteen fired** |
| **Three of the fourteen have been reviewed once** | TRA-09 and TRA-14 are published here for the first time; TRA-08's first review has been pulled forward because its trigger fired. **A first review is not a review cycle** |
| **The electives can be quietly dropped** | Nothing in the standard would notice if TRA-11 to TRA-14 disappeared. They are protected only by being in this register with an owner and a date, which is the entire reason the register exists |
| **12.3.3's strategy is untested** | Three anticipated changes with triggers and owners is a plan. **No cryptographic emergency occurred in the period**, so the plan's response time is an estimate, not a measurement |
| **12.3.4 passes on a technicality for nine components** | Supported, patched, advised — and unverifiable. The requirement's own test is met and the programme's purpose is not, and §5.1 says so rather than letting the headline figure carry it |
