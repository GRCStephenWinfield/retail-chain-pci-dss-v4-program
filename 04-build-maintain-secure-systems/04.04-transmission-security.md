## Purpose

Requirement 4 — *protect cardholder data with strong cryptography during transmission over open, public networks* — across Marketa's e-commerce tier, its CDE and its messaging estate. This document covers **4.1.1** and **4.1.2**, **4.2.1**, **4.2.1.1**, **4.2.1.2** and **4.2.2**.

Requirement 4 is where Marketa's architecture stops helping. Scope reduction removes account data from storage; it does not remove it from the wire. Every one of the three exception flows in 04.02 §2 crosses a network Marketa does not control, and the payment page that hosts the Truvance iframe is served by Marketa over the public internet to 15.7 million e-commerce transactions a year.

It is also the requirement Marketa most recently and most clearly breached. **Finding PAN-04 put 34 account numbers into email**, inbound from customers and then internally in a spreadsheet, and 4.2.2 admits no exception for internal mail and none for a customer who started it.

## 1. What Crosses an Open, Public Network

4.2.1 attaches to transmissions of PAN over open, public networks. The determination has to be made flow by flow, because the answer is not the same as "leaves the building."

| Flow | Carries PAN? | Open public network? | Protection |
|---|---|---|---|
| POI terminal to Verition, all 1,914 lanes | Yes, but **encrypted by the SRED device before it leaves the terminal** | Yes — store broadband and the SD-WAN underlay | P2PE point-to-point encryption plus TLS on the transport. Marketa holds no decryption key |
| Consumer browser to the Truvance iframe | Yes | Yes | TLS terminated by **Truvance**, not Marketa. Marketa's obligation is that the page framing it is served securely and that nothing on the page can intercept the field — 6.4.3 and 11.6.1 |
| Consumer browser to Marketa's storefront and payment page template | No PAN | Yes | TLS 1.2 minimum, TLS 1.3 preferred; HSTS with preload; the §3 cipher standard |
| **CDE-05 to Cardinal Merchant Bank** — settlement, interchange and exception files | **Yes**, in the exception subset | Yes | Mutually authenticated TLS 1.3 **and** OpenPGP payload encryption under K-01. Two independent layers, because a file that fails in transit is retried and a retry is a second exposure |
| **CDE-05 to Truvance** — token and integration APIs | Yes, on exception paths | Yes | Mutually authenticated TLS 1.3 with pinned issuers under K-04 |
| CDE-03 and CDE-06 to the Truvance dispute portal | **Yes** — the rendered imagery | Yes | TLS 1.3 with pinned issuers; render-only session; the 3.4.2 controls in 04.02 §6 |
| Contact-centre SIP trunk to Cadence | No — suppression is upstream of Marketa | Yes | SIP over TLS with SRTP media encryption. Condition MOTO-C2 monitors that the suppression point stays upstream |
| Z1 to Z2 replication | Truncated PAN and cardholder name | Traverses a provider backbone | IPsec with IKEv2, treated as an open network irrespective of the carrier's assurances |
| Store back-office to corporate | No account data | Yes | SD-WAN with IPsec; out of the CDE by segmentation, per Phase 03 |

The Z1-to-Z2 row records a decision. A private circuit or a cloud provider's backbone is frequently argued to be a non-public network. Marketa's position is that **the entity does not control the path, therefore the entity treats it as open** — the cost of encrypting it is small, and the argument saved is one nobody has to have in November.

## 3. Protocol and Cipher Standards — 4.2.1

4.2.1 obliges strong cryptography and security protocols, with four properties: only trusted keys and certificates are accepted; certificates used to safeguard PAN in transit are confirmed valid and not expired or revoked; the protocol in use supports only secure versions and configurations and **does not support fallback to an insecure version**; and the encryption strength is appropriate for the methodology.

| Setting | Standard | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum protocol | **TLS 1.2.** TLS 1.3 preferred and negotiated first | Every listener in the assessed population and every internet-facing listener in the e-commerce tier |
| Prohibited protocols | SSL 2.0, SSL 3.0, TLS 1.0, TLS 1.1 — **absent, not merely deprioritised**. Disabled estate-wide on 2026-04-30 | All |
| Fallback | **No downgrade path.** TLS_FALLBACK_SCSV honoured; no version-tolerant retry logic in any Marketa client; renegotiation disabled | All |
| Cipher suites | TLS 1.3: `TLS_AES_128_GCM_SHA256`, `TLS_AES_256_GCM_SHA384`, `TLS_CHACHA20_POLY1305_SHA256`. TLS 1.2: ECDHE with AES-GCM or ChaCha20-Poly1305 only. **No CBC-mode suites. No static RSA key exchange**, so every session has forward secrecy | All |
| Key exchange, signatures and certificate keys | X25519, secp256r1, secp384r1; SHA-256 or stronger with SHA-1 signatures refused; ECDSA P-256 preferred and RSA 2048-bit minimum where a counterparty requires RSA | All |
| Compression | Disabled | All |
| TLS 1.3 early data | **0-RTT disabled on every payment-related path.** Early data is replayable, and a replayable request on an exception refund path is a business-logic exposure as well as a cryptographic one | Payment page templates; CDE-05; CDE-07 |
| HSTS | `max-age` one year, `includeSubDomains`, preload, on all 6 serving domains | E-commerce tier |
| OCSP | Stapling enabled; must-staple on the payment page certificates | E-commerce tier |
| Mutual authentication | Required on every provider and acquirer flow: CDE-05 to Cardinal, CDE-05 to Truvance, CDE-03 to the dispute portal | CDE flows |
| Certificate validation | Full chain validation with hostname verification and revocation checking. **No client anywhere in the estate is configured to skip verification** | All, asserted by a source-code rule and a runtime scan |

The last row is worth its own sentence. The single most common way strong cryptography stops protecting anything is a client configured to ignore certificate errors, usually added during an integration test and never removed. Marketa treats it as a build-breaking condition in the static analysis rule set and as a scanned condition at runtime, which is why 04.07 §5 lists "insecure use of cryptography" among the 6.2.4 attack classes with a named enforcement mechanism rather than a coding guideline.

### 3.1 How the standard is verified

| Check | Method | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Protocol and cipher conformance on every internet-facing listener | Active TLS scan of the full external attack surface | Weekly, plus quarterly ASV scanning under 11.3.2 |
| Protocol and cipher conformance on internal listeners in the assessed population | Active scan from an adjacent segment | Weekly, as CA-5 in 04.01 §3 |
| Client-side configuration | Source-code rule in the build pipeline plus runtime configuration assertion | Every build; daily |
| Fallback behaviour | Deliberate downgrade attempt against a sample of listeners | Quarterly |
| Provider-side posture | Truvance, Cadence and Cardinal endpoints scanned from Marketa's side and the result recorded in the quarterly TPSP review | Quarterly |

The provider-side row is a small point of method. Marketa cannot configure a provider's endpoint, but it can observe it, and observing it converts a 12.8.5 assurance statement into something with a date and a result attached.

## 4. The Trusted Keys and Certificates Inventory — 4.2.1.1

4.2.1.1 is one of the future-dated requirements now in force. It obliges an inventory of the entity's trusted keys and certificates used to protect PAN during transmission.

The requirement is narrow and the temptation is to build something narrow. Marketa built the wide version and then marked the narrow subset inside it, because a list of 31 certificates with no denominator cannot be shown to be complete.

| Population | Count | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Certificates discovered across the whole estate | **1,140** | Every X.509 certificate found by any of the four discovery sources |
| Certificates on the 71 assessed components | **238** | The Requirement 2 and Requirement 4 population |
| **Certificates used to safeguard PAN during transmission over open public networks** | **31** | **The 4.2.1.1 inventory proper.** CDE-05's client and server certificates for Cardinal and Truvance, the dispute-portal trust set, the payment page template certificates, and the Z1-to-Z2 IPsec certificates |
| Trusted root and intermediate certificates on Z1 and Z2 components | **9** | The curated trust store — see §4.2 |
| Certificates on the automated 90-day renewal path | **935 of 1,140 (82%)** | ACME through CT-16 |
| Certificates requiring a manual renewal step | **205** | Appliances, provider-issued client certificates, and the internal CA's own chain |

Each entry in the 4.2.1.1 inventory carries: identifier and fingerprint, subject and issuer, key algorithm and size, the flow it protects, the component it is installed on, the named owner, the issue and expiry dates, the renewal method, and the date of the last reconciliation.

### 4.1 The first inventory found things

Building the inventory is a control in itself, and the first pass produced findings that no policy statement would have produced.

| Finding | Count | Disposition |
|---|---|---|
| Certificates on assessed components with no recorded owner | **17** | Owners assigned within ten working days. Four turned out to belong to services nobody was still consuming; those services were decommissioned under procedure P-3 |
| Certificates expired but still installed on a listening service | **3** | All three on internal management interfaces that clients had been configured to accept regardless — which is the exact failure mode §3's last row exists to prevent. Replaced; the clients' validation was re-enabled |
| Self-signed certificates on a vendor appliance management interface | **1** | Registered as **SCS-EX-03**'s companion; the appliance cannot accept an externally issued certificate. Access restricted to the management VLAN and the fingerprint pinned in the management client |
| Certificates issued by a CA not on Marketa's authorised issuer list | **2** | Both legitimate — a provider-issued client certificate and a test certificate left in a staging configuration. The issuer list was corrected for the first and the second was removed |
| Wildcard certificates in use on internet-facing services | **6** | Retained, with the constraint recorded: **no wildcard certificate is used on any payment page template.** A wildcard's private key on any host is a private key for every host in the domain |

### 4.2 Trust-store curation

The default operating-system trust store on a general-purpose server trusts on the order of 140 root certificate authorities, most of them irrelevant to anything the server will ever connect to. That is 140 organisations any one of which can issue a certificate a Marketa CDE component will accept.

On Z1 and Z2 components the default store is replaced with a curated store of **9 roots**: the two public issuers Marketa uses, Marketa's internal root, the four roots required by Cardinal, Truvance and Cadence, and two operating-system update infrastructure roots. The store is deployed as a managed artefact, versioned in CT-30, and asserted daily by CA-2.

**This is the strongest single control in this document** and it costs almost nothing. It converts "the certificate validates" from a statement about the global CA ecosystem into a statement about seven named organisations Marketa has a relationship with.

## 5. Certificate Lifecycle and Expiry

The lifecycle runs discovery into inventory, inventory into a named owner and a renewal method, and renewal into a monthly reconciliation whose disagreements are treated as substitution until proven otherwise. Four discovery sources, deliberately — the same discipline the 12.5.1 inventory reconciliation uses, and for the same reason: in Phase 02 the CMDB alone missed 19 systems.

| Source | Finds |
|---|---|
| Active network scan of every listener in the estate and the external attack surface | What is actually presented on the wire |
| Certificate Transparency log monitoring for all Marketa domains | Publicly issued certificates, including any Marketa did not request |
| CT-16 issuance records, internal CA and ACME | What Marketa believes it issued |
| Endpoint agent certificate-store inventory | Certificates installed but not currently presented, which is where the three expired ones were found |

**Expiry monitoring** alerts the named owner at 90, 30, 14, 7 and 1 days. Below 7 days the alert also goes to the Security Operations Manager; at 1 day it becomes an incident with a bridge. The escalation ladder exists because an expiry is the one certificate failure with a known date, and an outage caused by something with a known date is an operational embarrassment rather than a security event.

Internal certificates were originally outside the alerting scope. **They were brought in after the K-03 rotation in 04.03 §5.1 missed an appliance certificate by six days**, which caused no outage only because the consuming client had been configured to accept it anyway — a defect that took two problems to reveal and would have taken only one to exploit.

## 6. Wireless Transmitting PAN — 4.2.1.2

4.2.1.2 obliges wireless networks transmitting PAN or connected to the CDE to use industry best practices for strong cryptography in authentication and transmission.

| Question | Determination |
|---|---|
| Does any Marketa wireless network transmit PAN? | **No.** All 1,914 lanes are wired to the store payment-device VLAN. No POI device in the estate operates over wireless, and no mobile or line-buster acceptance is deployed |
| Is any wireless network connected to the CDE? | **No.** Recorded in 03.06 and re-established by the 2026-06-26 segmentation re-test |
| So does 4.2.1.2 engage? | On a strict reading, no. **Marketa applies the standard anyway**, for the reason given in 04.01 §9: connectivity is a configuration state, and on 2026-05-15 a wireless network reached somewhere the design said it could not |
| What is the standard if it ever did engage? | WPA3-Enterprise, or WPA2-Enterprise with 802.1X and AES-CCMP where hardware requires it. Certificate-based EAP-TLS authentication. **Pre-shared keys are not acceptable on any network that could transmit PAN or reach the CDE**, under any circumstances, without exception |
| The 214-device legacy PSK population | Transmits no account data and is confined to a dedicated internet-only VLAN with explicit deny to the operations, payment-device and management VLANs. 4.2.1.2 does not reach it; **R-15** and **CAP-05** govern it, and 2.3.2 is not met for it |

The 1,612 authorised access points across the estate run WPA2-Enterprise or WPA3-Enterprise with 802.1X. The wireless design, the SSID-to-VLAN policy and the rogue-access-point detection under 11.2.1 and 11.2.2 are Phase 03's, in 03.06, and are referenced rather than restated.

## 7. End-User Messaging Technologies — 4.2.2

### 7.1 What the requirement actually says

4.2.2 obliges PAN to be **secured with strong cryptography whenever it is sent by end-user messaging technologies** — email, instant messaging, SMS, chat and equivalents.

Marketa's policy is stricter than the requirement, and the distinction is stated because it matters to how the control is designed. **The requirement permits PAN in email if it is strongly encrypted. Marketa's policy prohibits PAN in end-user messaging entirely.** Prohibition is the simpler way to satisfy 4.2.2, it is the only way that survives a workforce of 34,000 plus 6,800 seasonal colleagues, and it means the control can be a technical block rather than a judgement about whether a particular encryption arrangement was adequate.

### 7.2 What PAN-04 was

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| What happened | Between October and November 2025, **34 customers** emailed their own card details, unprompted, to a store's published special-orders address. A store manager collated them into `special-orders-holiday-2025.xlsx` and sent it to a district manager and a corporate shared mailbox so the orders could be keyed through the compliant MOTO channel before the seasonal cut-off |
| Copies | **11**, once forwards, mailbox copies and journal copies were counted, across 3 mailboxes and 2 archives |
| Why 4.2.2 was breached twice | Inbound, by the 34 customers, and internally, when the spreadsheet was forwarded. **4.2.2 admits no exception for internal mail and none for a customer who started it** |
| What was not wrong | The payments themselves were taken correctly through the documented MOTO channel with the secure session. No undocumented acceptance channel was created. The failure was entirely in what happened to the data before it reached the channel |
| Root cause | No defined, trained and tooled procedure for handling account data received unsolicited from a customer, and **no technical detection of PAN anywhere in the messaging estate** |
| Handling | Treated as a process failure, not a disciplinary matter, by an explicit and recorded decision of the CISO |

### 7.3 The DLP control, live 2026-04-10

| Element | Implementation |
|---|---|
| Coverage | Corporate email inbound, outbound and internal; the chat and collaboration platform; the SMS gateway; ticketing-system attachments; the 482 store special-orders mailboxes and the corporate customer-service shared mailboxes |
| Detection | Luhn-validated candidate account numbers with brand-prefix matching, over message bodies, common attachment formats and archive contents, with proximity rules for expiry dates and cardholder names to reduce false positives on order references and product codes |
| **Inbound action** | **Quarantine.** The message is held, the recipient is not exposed to it, and the sender receives an automated reply explaining how to pay securely and providing a Truvance-hosted payment link |
| **Outbound and internal action** | **Block.** The sender is notified with the reason and the procedure P-4 instruction; the security operations team is notified simultaneously |
| Response | Procedure **P-4** — do not forward, do not quote, do not print; report within one hour; the team purges every copy including journals with e-discovery evidence; the customer receives a safe alternative. **Reporting is never penalised**, and that instruction is repeated in the 12.6.3 awareness content |
| Evidence | Quarantine and block logs to CT-18, retained under 10.5.1; monthly reporting to the PCI Working Group |

### 7.4 Operating results, 2026-04-10 to 2026-06-30

| Measure | Value | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| Inbound messages quarantined, of which true positives | **61**, of which **44** | An average of **five a week**, from customers nobody asked. This is the number that justifies the control |
| Inbound false positives | 17 | Order references, a supplier's 16-digit part number series, and one loyalty-card range. All three patterns tuned by 2026-05-20 |
| Outbound or internal messages blocked, of which true positives | **9**, of which **3** | Two colleagues forwarding a customer's message to ask what to do with it — precisely the PAN-04 behaviour, caught before it multiplied. One was a security analyst testing the control |
| Median time from quarantine to customer response | 3.4 hours | Against a four-hour target |
| Copies requiring e-discovery purge | 0 | Because nothing was delivered |
| Store special-order prepayments completed through the Truvance payment link | 1,284 | **The compliant path is the fast path**, which is the only kind of control that survives a seasonal peak |

### 7.5 What the DLP control does not catch

Stating the limits is part of the control.

| Limitation | Consequence | What covers it |
|---|---|---|
| **A photograph of a card is not a Luhn-valid digit string.** A customer who photographs their card and attaches the image passes every pattern rule | The most likely residual route for account data into the messaging estate | Procedure P-4's human route; awareness content specifically covering photographed cards; **an image-OCR DLP capability is an open item for Phase 06** |
| Encrypted or password-protected attachments cannot be inspected | A blind spot by construction | Password-protected attachments from external senders are quarantined for review as a category, regardless of content |
| A PAN split across two messages, or written in words | Defeats pattern matching | Accepted residual. Named rather than engineered around |
| Personal devices and personal accounts | Outside the tenant | Acceptable-use policy under 12.6, and the store special-order payment link that removes the reason to improvise |

**R-07** — account data arriving in the messaging estate and coming to rest — is Low 6 in the register, and it is Low because of this control rather than because the event is rare. Forty-four inbound events in twelve weeks is not rare. **R-22** — customer-initiated account data arriving by email, web form free-text or special-order note — remains Moderate 9, because the web form free-text limb is not covered by messaging DLP and is handled by input validation in 04.07.
