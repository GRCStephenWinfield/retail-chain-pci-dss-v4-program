## Purpose

02.07 identified **63 connected-to and security-impacting components** by applying the administrative-path test across the estate. This document enumerates them: each one, its function, why it is in scope, and the requirements that consequently apply to it.

It also records the systems that were **considered and excluded**, with the reasoning for each. An exclusion without a recorded reason is not a determination — it is an omission that happens to be defensible, and the assessor cannot tell the difference.

This is the document a QSA will interrogate hardest. Merchants routinely under-scope here, and the reason is structural rather than dishonest: the connected-to population contains no card data, produces no revenue, belongs to infrastructure teams rather than payment teams, and is the only part of the assessed environment where the argument for inclusion has to be constructed rather than observed. Nobody argues about the payment database. Everybody argues about the backup server.

## 1. What "In Scope" Means for a Connected-To System

A common and expensive misunderstanding is that connected-to systems attract the full requirement set exactly as CDE systems do. They do not, and neither do they attract a token subset chosen by the entity.

| Position | Correct? | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Every PCI DSS requirement applies to every connected-to system | No | Requirement 3 obligations attach to account data. A domain controller that holds none has no 3.5.1 obligation |
| No requirement applies unless the system holds account data | **Emphatically no** | This is the under-scoping error. Requirements 2, 5, 6, 7, 8, 10, 11 and 12 attach to system components in scope, not to card data |
| The applicable requirements are determined by what the system *is* and what it *does* | **Yes** | Each of the 63 carries a requirement set derived from its function. The mapping is in §3 to §15 and is consolidated in 02.09 |

The requirement families that attach to **every** one of the 63, without exception, are the ones that make the system defensible as a component of a secure environment:

| Family | Obligation on a connected-to system |
|---|---|
| **2.2.x** | Configuration standards applied; vendor defaults removed; only necessary services enabled |
| **5.2 / 5.3** | Anti-malware deployed and current, or a documented determination that the system type is not commonly affected, reviewed under 5.2.3.1 |
| **6.3.3** | Security patches applied — critical and high-risk within one month |
| **7.2.x** | Access by business need to know; privileges reviewed at least every six months under **7.2.4** |
| **8.2.x / 8.3.x** | Unique IDs, no shared accounts, **12-character passwords with numeric and alphabetic characters under 8.3.6** |
| **8.4.2 / 8.4.3** | **MFA for all access into the CDE** and for all remote network access originating outside the network |
| **8.6.1–8.6.3** | Governance of application and system accounts — the future-dated requirements that bite hardest on infrastructure |
| **10.2.x / 10.3.x** | Audit logs captured, protected from alteration, and forwarded to a component the local administrator cannot alter |
| **10.4.1.1** | Included in the **automated** log review mechanism |
| **11.3.1 / 11.3.1.1 / 11.3.1.2** | Quarterly internal vulnerability scanning, **authenticated**, with all other applicable vulnerabilities managed |
| **12.5.1** | Present in the inventory of in-scope system components, with a description of function or use |

**11.3.1.2 is the requirement that produced one of the two Not in Place findings in the eventual ROC.** Authenticated internal scanning could not be completed on **9 of the 71** assessed components — vendor-locked POS back-office appliances covered by constraint **CON-03** — and it is recorded here, at the point the obligation attaches, rather than being left as a surprise in Phase 08.

Every one of them can affect the security of the cardholder data environment. Not one of them had ever appeared in a Marketa scope document.

## 6. Group G4 — Logging, Monitoring and Detection (5)

A compromised detection estate does not attack the cardholder data environment. It removes the organisation's ability to know that something else has. Requirement **10.3.2** obliges audit logs to be protected from modification, and a SIEM administrator who can delete an index is the modification path that requirement contemplates.

| ID | Component | Zone / location | Why in scope | Requirements beyond the common set |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **CT-18** | SIEM correlation platform, including the automated review engine | Z3 / Columbus | Receives every CDE audit log and performs the **10.4.1.1** automated review | **10.2.x, 10.3.1–10.3.4, 10.4.1, 10.4.1.1, 10.4.2, 10.7.2, 10.7.3, 12.10.5** |
| **CT-19** | SIEM log storage and archive tier | Z3 / Columbus and Ashburn | Holds twelve months of audit history, three months immediately available | **10.5.1**, 10.3.2, 10.3.3, 9.4.x for archive media |
| **CT-20** | Log collection forwarders serving Z1 and Z2 | Z1 / Z2 boundary | The path by which CDE logs leave the CDE. A forwarder that is silenced produces a clean-looking environment | 10.2.x, 10.3.2, **10.7.2 failure detection and response** |
| **CT-21** | File integrity monitoring console | Z3 / Columbus | Baselines and alerts on change to CDE system files and to critical configuration | **11.5.2**, 10.3.4, 12.10.5 |
| **CT-22** | Network intrusion detection and prevention management console | Z3 / Columbus | Manages the sensors at the Z1 and Z2 boundaries | **11.5.1, 11.5.1.1**, 12.10.5 |

**10.7.2 is the requirement that makes CT-20 interesting.** Failure of a critical security control system must be detected, alerted and responded to promptly, and a log forwarder that stops forwarding is precisely that failure. Marketa's mechanism is a heartbeat reconciliation: every CDE component's expected log volume is modelled, and a component whose volume falls outside its band raises an alert regardless of whether the forwarder reports itself healthy. A silent agent reports nothing, including its own silence.

**A note on mixed-mode virtualisation, recorded because it is the decision that kept this group to five components.** Where CDE and non-CDE workloads share a hypervisor, the hypervisor and every guest on it are in scope — the isolation between guests is a segmentation control and must be tested as one. Marketa avoided the argument entirely: the six Columbus CDE components run on **dedicated physical hardware in dedicated cabinets**, and the Z2 CDE workloads run in a dedicated VPC in a dedicated account. The decision was made in Phase 03's architecture work and its scoping consequence is recorded here, because a merchant who virtualises the CDE alongside general workloads acquires a hypervisor estate in scope and an isolation control to prove, and usually discovers both during fieldwork.

| **CT-56** | Tag management console | Vendor-hosted | Can load arbitrary third-party script into a page. Source of all three unauthorised scripts in 02.05 §5.3; **removed from payment templates as the primary mitigation** and retained in scope because the capability persists elsewhere on the estate | **6.4.3**, 7.2.x, 12.8.x for the agency holding rights |
| **CT-57** | Payment-page change and tamper detection service | Z3 / vendor-hosted | The **11.6.1** mechanism itself. Compromise removes the detection, which is how an undetected page modification becomes a long-lived one | **11.6.1**, 10.7.2, 12.10.5 |

CT-57 is in scope on the same reasoning as CT-18 and CT-21: a detection control that fails silently converts a detectable compromise into an undetectable one. Its health is monitored under 10.7.2 and its detection capability is tested quarterly by controlled modification, per condition ECOM-C6 in 02.05 §8.

## 14. Group G12 — Contact-Centre Telephony Boundary (3)

| ID | Component | Zone / location | Why in scope | Requirements beyond the common set |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **CT-58** | Session border controller — Austin | Z9 / Austin | The component whose position relative to Cadence determines whether Marketa's telephony carries account data. A routing change here moves the suppression point — condition **MOTO-C2** | **1.2.x**, 2.2.x, 11.4.5 where it enforces a boundary, 10.2.x |
| **CT-59** | SIP media routing gateway to Cadence | Z9 / Austin | Carries the masked media and the signalling that establishes the secure session | As CT-58 |
| **CT-60** | Call recording platform management node | Z9 / Austin | Controls what is recorded and retained. The component behind finding **PAN-01**, and the enforcement point for the 13-month retention rule and condition **MOTO-C7** | **3.2.1**, 7.2.x, 9.4.x, 10.2.x, 12.10.7 detection support |

CT-60 is in scope for a reason worth stating explicitly: it holds **no account data today**, and it is in scope because it is the control point that decides whether it holds any tomorrow. A configuration change disabling the pause, or a retention change reintroducing an archive, is a scope event executed from a console that would otherwise be unassessed.

## 15. Group G13 — Core Network Foundation Services (3)

| ID | Component | Zone / location | Why in scope | Requirements beyond the common set |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **CT-61** | Authoritative internal DNS service | Z3 / Columbus | CDE components resolve provider and internal endpoints through it. A poisoned record redirects a payment integration | 2.2.x, 1.3.x, 10.2.x |
| **CT-62** | Network time service | Z3 / Columbus | Supplies the time on which every audit log in the CDE depends | **10.6.1, 10.6.2, 10.6.3** |
| **CT-63** | IP address management and DHCP service for Z1 and Z3 | Z3 / Columbus | Assigns addressing within the CDE and the connected-to zone; an address reassignment is a boundary event | 1.2.x, 2.2.x, 10.2.x |

CT-62 is in scope on the strength of **10.6.x** alone. Requirement 10.6 obliges time synchronisation from an accepted source, with time data protected and access to time settings restricted. An unsynchronised or manipulable time source makes every log in the CDE unreliable as evidence — including, in the worst case, the logs an incident response would depend on.

## 16. Considered and Excluded

An exclusion without a recorded reason is not a determination. Each of the following was examined under the administrative-path test and the five out-of-scope criteria, and each carries the condition on which its exclusion depends.

| System or population | Why it was considered | Determination | Condition the exclusion depends on |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Order management system** | Receives tokens and order data from all three channels; talks to Z1 | **Out of scope.** Tokens only; no CDE-initiated trust; the flow is Z1-outbound to Z8 through a named, unidirectional interface with no administrative capability | TOK-C1 to TOK-C5 |
| **Enterprise data warehouse and BI estate** | Holds token and last-four data for 68.4M transactions a year | **Out of scope.** No PAN found by discovery; no CDE connectivity; no administrative path | TOK-C2; annual discovery re-run |
| **Loyalty platform** | Holds 16-digit Luhn-valid member IDs that look exactly like PANs | **Out of scope.** A loyalty number is not a brand account number. Verified against the scheme specification in 02.02 §3.2 | Loyalty scheme number format unchanged |
| **Corporate email and collaboration estate** | Finding **PAN-04** occurred in it | **Out of scope as a system population**, with the DLP control plane CT-25 in scope. The estate is a place account data can arrive, not a component of the CDE. Bringing 34,000 mailboxes into scope would be an unassessable population and would not improve the control | DLP enforcement operating; 12.10.7 procedure P-4 |
| **Configuration management database** | Supplies the tags that produced the 604 | **Out of scope**, with a qualification. It has no CDE path and no security impact, but its unreliability as an inventory is recorded as a **12.5.1** observation and it is reconciled monthly against the assessed register | Monthly reconciliation performed |
| **IT service management and ticketing platform** | Holds change records for CDE changes; approvals flow through it | **Out of scope.** It records changes; it cannot execute them. No credential, no connectivity, no execution path. **Reconsidered twice** — if a ticket approval were wired to trigger an automated deployment, it would enter scope immediately | No automation path from ticket state to CDE change |
| **Distribution centre systems — 96 across 4 sites** | 8 carried a "payment-adjacent" tag in the 604 | **Out of scope.** Discovery found no account data; the tag traced to a 2019 project and had no data behind it | Annual discovery re-run |
| **Store timeclock, workforce management and inventory systems** | Sit in Z5 alongside the store servers | **Out of scope.** No account data, no path to Z1 or Z2, no administrative capability over anything that has one | SEG-C1, SEG-C4, SEG-C6 |
| **Digital signage and in-store music** | In store, on a store network | **Out of scope**, on a dedicated VLAN with internet-only egress. Recorded specifically because "it is only a screen" is the kind of reasoning that puts an unmanaged Linux host on a payment network | VLAN separation verified in the CT-48 policy; tested under 11.4.5 |
| **Building management, CCTV and physical access control** | CCTV covers the Columbus CDE cabinets; access control governs entry | **Out of scope as system components.** They are **in the assessment** under Requirement 9 — 9.3.1, 9.3.2 and 9.5.1 evidence comes from them — which is a different obligation from being an assessed component. The distinction is the same one 02.04 draws for the POI estate | No network path to Z1, Z2 or Z3; verified |
| **Corporate end-user laptops — approximately 1,100** | Administrators use them to start administrative sessions | **Out of scope**, because the session terminates on a Z3 jump host with MFA and no clipboard or drive redirection. **This is the single largest exclusion by count and the most conditional one.** Remove the jump-host constraint and 1,100 endpoints enter the assessment | CT-08 to CT-12 operating as designed; SEG-C5; RCH-02 remaining closed |
| **Test and development environments** | Mirror the CDE application stack | **Out of scope.** Carry no live account data — verified by discovery — are separately segmented, and have no path into Z1 or Z2. **6.5.5 obliges removal of test data and accounts before production**, which is a Phase 04 control | No live account data; segmentation from Z1 and Z2 |
| **Northbridge's own SOC platform** | Monitors Marketa's estate | **Out of Marketa's scope; in Northbridge's.** Marketa assesses the gateway CT-13 through which Northbridge reaches Z3, and obtains assurance over Northbridge under **12.8.4** | Northbridge validation current; responsibility matrix maintained |
| **Marketa's HR, payroll and finance ERP** | Finance receives settlement output | **Out of scope.** Receives reconciled totals and tokens, not account data; no CDE connectivity | TOK-C2 |
| **Store guest wireless — Z7** | Physically present at 482 sites | **Out of scope, subject to proof of separation.** Configuration evidence is insufficient. This exclusion is the one the penetration test was pointed at, and Phase 03 reports what it found | **SEG-C4** — and this is the condition that failed |

Two of these deserve a closing remark. **The corporate laptop exclusion is the largest single reduction in this document and it hangs on one architectural property** — that no administrative session reaches Z1 or Z2 except through a jump host. That property was true when tested, was violated by finding RCH-02 for the 14 hosts on a legacy management VLAN until 2026-02-09, and is verified quarterly. **And the guest wireless exclusion is recorded as conditional in the strongest terms available**, because the programme wrote it knowing the test had not yet been run.
