## Purpose

A register that lists risks and a programme that implements controls are two documents that can each look complete while failing to meet in the middle. This document forces them to meet.

It maps every one of the **44** baseline risks in 03.11 to the control or controls that treat it, the PCI DSS requirement or requirements those controls discharge, the phase that delivers them, and the residual position expected at programme close. It then runs the test in the opposite direction — **which controls treat no risk?** — because a control that treats nothing is either unnecessary or the register is incomplete, and both answers are worth having.

It ends by naming the risks that are **structural**: exposures that no control in this programme will reduce to Low, because they are properties of what Marketa is rather than defects in how Marketa operates.

## 1. What Traceability Has To Prove

| Test | Question | Where it is answered |
|---|---|---|
| **Forward coverage** | Is every risk treated by at least one named control? | §3 — all 44 |
| **High-risk coverage** | Is every High risk treated, by controls that are delivered and dated, with no reliance on acceptance? | §4 — all 9 |
| **Reverse coverage** | Does every control in the programme treat at least one risk? | §5 |
| **Residual honesty** | Does the expected end position reconcile, and does it admit what will not reduce? | §6 and §7 |
| **Delivery reality** | Is the treating control actually delivered by a phase with an owner and a date, or is it an intention? | The delivering-phase column throughout |

The fourth test is the one that catches an over-confident programme. It is easy to map a risk to a requirement; the requirement is a statement of what should be true. Mapping a risk to a **control, in a phase, with an owner** is a commitment that can be missed, and this document is written so that a missed commitment is visible.

## 2. How to Read the Mapping

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Risk** | The entry ID and baseline rating from 03.11 |
| **Treating control(s)** | The specific mechanisms — named standards, assertions, procedures, architectural properties or providers — not the requirement number |
| **PCI requirement(s)** | What those controls discharge in the assessment |
| **Delivers** | The phase that builds or evidences the control. Phase 03 controls are operating now; later phases are commitments |
| **Residual at close** | The rating expected at the Phase 09 close position, stated here so that Phase 09 reports against a prediction rather than against a blank page |

Where a control already exists and is operating, the mapping says so. Where it does not, the mapping names the phase, and **that gap is the honest content of this document**: at 2026-07-15 the programme is three months from QSA fieldwork and a meaningful number of these controls have implementation dates rather than operating histories.

## 5. Reverse Traceability — Controls That Treat No Risk

A forward map can be complete and still be wrong, because it only tests the risks it started with. The reverse test asks the harder question.

> **A control that treats no risk is either unnecessary, or the register is incomplete. There is no third answer, and "it is required by the standard" is not one of them — a requirement exists because somebody identified a risk, and if Marketa's register cannot name it, Marketa has not understood the requirement.**

The reverse analysis was run across the Phase 03 control set — the 14 NSC standards, the 15 boundaries, the 31 named flows, the 6 drift assertions, the change classification list, the 1.2.7 review cycle and the 2 exceptions — plus the control commitments carried from Phases 01 and 02. **Five** controls mapped to no register entry on the first pass.

| Control with no initial mapping | Investigation | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| **1.4.5** — limitation of internal IP address and routing information disclosure | Mapped to nothing, yet **SEG-PT-03** and **SEG-PT-06** were both 1.4.5 findings and both accelerated reconnaissance | **Mapping error.** Corrected: 1.4.5 treats **R-03** and **R-27** as a reconnaissance-suppression control. The register was right; the map was thin |
| **NSC-11** — time from CT-62 only | Appeared to be housekeeping | **Mapping error.** Corrected: NSC-11 is a primary treating control for **R-44**, which exists precisely because the forensic record depends on it |
| **NF-07** — single-endpoint patch pull replacing the Z1 egress path to a Z4 proxy | Delivered as the remediation for finding RCH-03, with no register entry behind it | **Mapping error.** Corrected: NF-07 treats **R-27** and **R-30**. The RCH-03 class — a boundary crossing hidden behind a business-purpose label — is what R-32 is about |
| **B-15** — no internet gateway attached to the CDE VPC | Structural isolation with no obvious risk line | **Mapping error.** Corrected: B-15 is the outbound-restriction control treating **R-26** and, with 1.3.2, the exfiltration limb of **R-27** |
| **NSC-EX-01's compensating position** — SNMPv2c read-only with a rotated string and a polling ACL | Treats no risk; it **accommodates** one | **Not a treatment.** Recorded as a constraint accommodation. The risk it accommodates is **R-15**, and the accommodation does not reduce it — only **CAP-05** does. **Four of the five were failures of the map, not of the register.** The fifth is a genuine distinction the programme now maintains: an accommodation keeps an unmeetable standard under management; it does not reduce the exposure, and the register should not show it as though it did.

## 7. The Thirteen That Stay Moderate

Thirteen entries are expected to be **Moderate at close** and no lower. Eight of them are **structural** — the exposure is a property of what Marketa is rather than a defect in how it operates — and five stay Moderate for a stated, dated reason. Naming both sets is the difference between a programme that has finished and a programme that pretends to have finished.

### 7.1 The eight structural risks

| Risk | Why it is structural | The floor, and what would move it |
|---|---|---|
| **R-03** — store network flatness | Inside a store, Z5, Z6 and Z7 are separated by **one switch's configuration**. A second enforcement plane means a second switch fabric in 482 stockrooms, rejected on cost and space in 03.04 §4 with the residual risk recorded — **and the residual risk materialised** | Floor: Moderate. Only a hardware architecture change moves it, and that is a multi-year capital decision, not a compliance action |
| **R-02** — POI tampering and substitution | 1,914 devices on public counters in 482 buildings, inspected by colleagues whose population turns over by 6,800 each season. Inspection is a detective control with a human in it, at scale | Floor: Moderate. Reduced by inspection quality and device telemetry; never eliminated while the devices are reachable by the public |
| **R-05** — P2PE condition failure | The largest scope reduction in the programme rests on **operational** conditions across 482 sites, not on cryptography. A contractor with a patch cable can invalidate it at one store, and did, three times, in February 2026 | Floor: Moderate. Reduced by DA-2, quarterly verification and contractor procedure; the failure mode is entropy and entropy does not stop |
| **R-09** — TPSP concentration | One provider is hosted checkout, gateway and the single token vault across all three channels. **No control Marketa can build reduces this**; only a second provider does, and that is a commercial and architectural decision measured in years | Floor: Moderate. Moved only by diversification or by an exit that would itself be a programme |
| **R-13** — seasonal workforce | Up to 6,800 people onboarded at speed during the change freeze, at peak volume, with access to stores, POI devices and store systems. The business needs them and needs them fast | Floor: Moderate. Reduced by time-bounded accounts and automatic expiry; not eliminated while the seasonal model exists |
| **R-27** — administrative-services concentration | A deliberate design choice under principle **SP-4**. Concentrating every crossing into one zone makes the boundary enumerable and testable **and** makes one zone worth seven paths into the CDE. The trade is real in both directions | Floor: Moderate. This is the price of a testable architecture, and the register prices it rather than hiding it |
| **R-20** — destructive malware and business interruption | A national retailer with 482 trading sites and a fourteen-week peak has an availability exposure that no security control removes. Recovery competes with trading | Floor: Moderate. Reduced by segmentation, EDR, backup and store-and-forward trading; bounded by the calendar |
| **R-25** — cloud boundary changed by an API call | In the cloud, a boundary change is one authorised call completing in under a second with no outage and no symptom. IaC-only management and continuous plan-diff make it visible; they do not make it hard | Floor: Moderate. This is a property of the platform, not of Marketa's configuration of it |

### 7.2 The five that stay Moderate for a dated reason

These are not structural in the same sense. Each has a specific, identifiable thing that would move it, and in each case that thing does not happen inside this programme.

| Risk | Why it does not reach Low at close | What would move it, and when |
|---|---|---|
| **R-01** — payment-page script injection | 6.4.3 and 11.6.1 will be operating and alerting within 24 hours, which is a strong treatment. It does not change the fact that Marketa serves a page into a browser it does not control, on a channel carrying 15.7M transactions | Nothing available to Marketa reduces the impact component below 5. Only a sustained clean operating record reduces likelihood, and one year is not sustained |
| **R-06** — unstructured account data recurring | Quarterly discovery, DLP and procedure P-2 will have run for a full cycle. The estate still creates new repositories faster than any programme retires them | Two consecutive clean full-estate discovery years, plus repository ownership at 100%. Realistically FY2028 |
| **R-12** — insider misuse in disputes | Non-savable in-session rendering removes the copy path; least privilege and 10.2.1 logging remove the anonymity. The residual is a person with a legitimate reason to see PAN | Elimination of the visible-PAN workflow entirely, which depends on issuer-side dispute imagery Marketa does not control |
| **R-15** — the 214-device legacy pre-shared key | **CAP-05 is a hardware replacement programme across 138 stores that does not complete inside this compliance year.** A register showing this as Low would be describing a plan rather than an estate | CAP-05 closure in the FY2027 store refresh. Tracked to Phase 09 and beyond |
| **R-30** — patching against the freeze | Risk-ranked patching and a one-month window will be operating and evidenced. **Constraint CON-01 does not go away** — the estate freezes for four months every year, during its highest-volume quarter | Nothing. The freeze is a business decision the programme does not have standing to change, and the emergency patch path is a mitigation rather than a removal |

Four further entries reduce to Low but should be read as *maintained* rather than *solved* — **R-04** drift, **R-19** decommissioned assets, **R-32** change misclassification and **R-33** scope creep. Each is Low only for as long as its control keeps running, and each is a re-run of a failure the programme has already experienced once.

## 8. What This Traceability Does Not Prove

| Limitation | Consequence |
|---|---|
| A mapping is not evidence of operation | This document proves that a control is **assigned** to a risk. Phase 06 and Phase 08 prove that it **ran** |
| Residual predictions are predictions | The Phase 09 close position is a target, published in July so that Phase 09 reports against a commitment rather than against a blank page. Missing it is a legitimate outcome and will be reported as one |
| Coverage is not sufficiency | Every risk has a control. Whether the control is strong enough is a judgement the QSA, the penetration testers and Internal Audit each test differently |
| The register bounds the analysis | Reverse traceability tests the map against the register. **It cannot find a risk nobody thought of** — that is what testing, discovery and independent review are for, and both **‡** entries are what it looks like when they work |
