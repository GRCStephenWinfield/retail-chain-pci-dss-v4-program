## What this phase is

Two things. It builds the segmentation that keeps 482 store systems out of scope — and then it **tests that segmentation and fails**. It also baselines the **44-risk register** that every later phase moves against.

Segmentation is not a PCI DSS requirement. It is an optional means of reducing scope, and the standard is unambiguous about the price: if you use it, **11.4.5** requires it to be verified by penetration testing at least annually and after any change to segmentation controls. Phase 02 verified it by reading rule sets. This phase found out what that was worth.

## The test failed. That is the most valuable thing in this portfolio.

> **2026-05-15.** Ironwood Security Labs associated with the **guest wireless network at store 0417** — a network any customer can join — and reached the **store back-office VLAN in nineteen minutes.**

The root cause was a trunk port template in store build image **SB-4.1**, released in November 2024, which had silently lost its allowed-VLAN list. The fault sat in the estate for **eighteen months**. It survived a store build process that deployed it faithfully, periodic configuration reviews, and a March 2026 design review that concluded segmentation was verified.

Two preconditions were needed. The template fault was **latent at 37 stores**; the guest SSID had been moved to local switching at **one**, to fix a latency complaint in September 2025. They coincided at store 0417.

Marketa refused to report this as a single-store issue. The Chair's minute:

> **"'Only one store' is not to appear in any reporting of this finding."**

The tester never reached the cardholder data environment — the Z5-to-CDE boundary held. That is not a mitigation. Under **ADR-0006**, the out-of-scope classification of 482 store systems rested on precisely the boundary that failed.

## The distinction the phase turns on

**The re-test proves the boundary holds today. It does not prove the process that let it rot is fixed.**

Only operating history proves that, which is why R-14 stays High with a 90-day clean-operation exit criterion. In the first 31 days the drift control observed **14,942 store-days** and found **11 divergences at 9 stores**, of which **2 were segmentation-determining** — one from a contractor's stock switch image *during re-test fieldwork*, one from another guest SSID moved to local switching.

Neither created a live path. Both **reset the R-14 clock** to 2026-07-03, earliest exit 2026-09-30.

A clock that never resets is not a clock.

## Why the test found it: ADR-0009

The sample was **14 stores — under 3%**. It found the fault because one stratum was deliberately composed of **stores with a recent local network change**: sites where someone had solved a local problem in a way the standard build did not anticipate.

The Ironwood lead, at the readout:

> "If you had given us the standard build and a list of well-maintained sites, we would have passed you, and the fault would still be sitting at thirty-seven stores."

| Band | Count | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| **High** | **9** | Score ≥ 15 — none may remain untreated at programme close |
| **Moderate** | **21** | Score 8–12 |
| **Low** | **14** | Score ≤ 6 |
| **Total** | **44** | Expected at close: **0 High · 13 Moderate · 31 Low** |

Impact is scored on three dimensions, not one — **cardholder data exposure, brand-programme penalties, and business interruption**. A retailer's worst case is a forensic investigation, card reissuance and brand assessments arriving *at the same time* as the operational disruption of a compromised payment estate.

**Two of the nine Highs got there by being raised on evidence**, not by being scored high at identification: **R-14** (the segmentation test) and **R-31** (the Phase 02 discovery). The register marks them, because as the Chair put it:

> "Seven of these we predicted. Two of them we got wrong and were told so. A reader deserves to know which is which, because it tells them how much to trust the seven."

## The honest position

Segmentation is now **tested**, not merely configured — and the test had to fail first for that sentence to mean anything.

What is *not* yet established is that the process which allowed a build image to silently drop an allowed-VLAN list for eighteen months has been fixed. The drift control is six weeks old. It has already caught two segmentation-determining divergences, which is encouraging about the control and sobering about the estate.

**R-14 remains High.** It comes down on 90 days of clean operation, and the clock has already been reset once.
