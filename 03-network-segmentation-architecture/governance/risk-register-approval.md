## 2. Scoring calibration

Every risk was scored by its owner and then **re-scored blind** by a second reviewer, with divergences of more than one band adjudicated at this meeting. Eleven risks diverged; nine were resolved in favour of the higher score.

The impact dimension is deliberately three-part — **cardholder data exposure, brand-programme penalties, and business interruption**. A member asked why business interruption belongs in a data-protection register. Marchetti's answer was recorded:

> "Because if the payment estate is compromised at a retailer, the forensic investigation and the remediation happen during trading. The exposure and the outage arrive together. A register that scores only the disclosure is pricing half the event."

## 3. The two risks that were raised rather than scored

**R-14** and **R-31** are both High, and both got there by being **raised on evidence** — one from a penetration test, one from a discovery scan. Neither was scored High at identification.

The Chair asked the register owners to be explicit about this in every report, because the two are qualitatively different from the other seven Highs:

> "Seven of these we predicted. Two of them we got wrong and were told so. A reader deserves to know which is which, because it tells them how much to trust the seven."

## 5. Determination

The baseline register of **44 risks** is **approved**. It is reviewed quarterly, on significant change, and at the annual 12.5.2 scope confirmation.
