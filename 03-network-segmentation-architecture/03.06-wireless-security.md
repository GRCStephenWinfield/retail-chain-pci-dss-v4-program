## Purpose

Marketa broadcasts wireless in 482 stores, four distribution centres and two offices. None of it is in the cardholder data environment, and the standard obliges Marketa to prove that rather than assert it, and to perform several wireless requirements **whether or not** wireless is used in the CDE.

This document covers **1.3.3** — network security controls between wireless networks and the CDE; **2.3.1** and **2.3.2** — wireless vendor defaults and encryption key management, including the applicability determination that most merchants skip; **11.2.1** — quarterly testing for the presence of authorised and unauthorised wireless access points; and **11.2.2** — the inventory of authorised access points with business justification.

It also records a known weakness before anybody finds it: **214 devices in the store estate remain on a legacy pre-shared key**, a remnant of a 2019 deployment, and that remnant becomes corrective action **CAP-05** later in the programme. It is written here, in the design document, because a weakness the programme names in advance is a managed weakness and a weakness an assessor names first is a finding.

## 2. Wireless and the CDE — 1.3.3

Requirement 1.3.3 obliges network security controls to be installed between all wireless networks and the CDE, **regardless of whether the wireless network is itself a CDE**, such that wireless traffic to the CDE is denied or, where necessary for a business purpose, only authorised traffic is permitted.

The "regardless" is the operative word. A merchant whose wireless is nowhere near the CDE still owes this requirement, because the point is to prove the separation rather than to describe it.

| Wireless network | Business need for CDE access | Enforcement | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Store guest — Z7 | **None** | Anchored to CT-49 and tunnelled to a dedicated internet break-out; client isolation enabled; no local VLAN inside the store; no route to any Marketa zone | **B-09** |
| Store colleague — Z5 VLAN 20 | **None.** Reaches the store server and named Z3 endpoints only | Dynamic VLAN assignment at CT-49; VLAN ACL at the store switch SVI; SD-WAN per-flow policy at the WAN edge | B-06 |
| Store legacy PSK — VLAN 40 | **None** | Mapped to the media VLAN; internet egress only through the store WAN edge; explicit deny to VLANs 10, 20, 30 and 99 | B-08 |
| Corporate — Z4 | **None.** Administrative access reaches Z3 jump hosts only, through B-04, with MFA | Perimeter firewall CT-44; no route from Z4 to Z1 or Z2 | B-04 |
| Corporate guest | **None** | Separate SSID, separate VLAN, internet-only break-out, no route to Z4 | B-05 |
| Distribution centre — Z4 | **None** | As corporate | B-04 |

Every row says the same thing, and the interesting question is what evidences it. Configuration evidences the intent — that is **E2**. What evidences the fact is the **11.4.5** test, which included wireless starting positions at every tested site.

**The test found a path from the store guest wireless VLAN to the store back-office VLAN at one store.** That is a failure of B-09 and it is reported in 03.07. It is worth being precise about which requirement it implicates: the tester reached **Z5**, not the CDE, so **1.3.3 was not breached** — no wireless network obtained a path to Z1 or Z2. What was breached is the segmentation claim that keeps 482 store servers out of scope, which is a **11.4.5** and **12.5.2** matter rather than a 1.3.3 matter. The distinction is not a mitigation and 03.07 §7 refuses to use it as one; it is simply the accurate mapping of a finding to a requirement.

## 3. Wireless Defaults and Encryption Keys — 2.3.1 and 2.3.2

### 3.1 The applicability determination

Requirements 2.3.1 and 2.3.2 are scoped by their own wording: they apply **for wireless environments connected to the CDE or transmitting account data**. Marketa performed the applicability determination explicitly rather than assuming, and recorded it, because a requirement quietly treated as applicable produces confused evidence and a requirement quietly treated as not applicable produces a finding.

| Question | Determination | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Is any wireless environment **connected to the CDE**? | **No** | Nine-zone model; reachability computation; 11.4.5 test result. No wireless network has a permitted or observed path to Z1 or Z2 |
| Does any wireless environment **transmit account data**? | **No** | POI devices are wired on Z6 by PIM requirement; the e-commerce and MOTO channels do not traverse Marketa wireless; lane-level capture testing found zero clear-text PAN anywhere in the store estate |
| Are 2.3.1 and 2.3.2 therefore applicable? | **Not applicable as written** | Recorded in the ROC with the reasoning, alongside the 12.5.2.1 and 11.4.6 not-applicable determinations |
| Does Marketa apply them anyway? | **Yes, as an internal standard, with evidence** | §3.2 and §3.3. The controls are cheap, the evidence is produced anyway, and the determination that they are not applicable rests on a boundary that has now failed once |

That last row is a deliberate position. An entity that argues a requirement is not applicable and then does not implement the control has bet the argument. Marketa implements the controls, evidences them, and presents the applicability determination as the assessor's to accept or reject. If the assessor takes a different view of any wireless environment's relationship to the CDE, the evidence already exists.

### 3.2 Vendor defaults — 2.3.1

| Default | Position |
|---|---|
| Default wireless encryption keys | Replaced at deployment on every network. The legacy PSK in §6 is a **deployment-set** key, not a vendor default, which is a distinction that makes it a 2.3.2 problem rather than a 2.3.1 one |
| Default SSIDs | Removed; no vendor-branded or default SSID is broadcast anywhere in the estate |
| Access point administrative credentials | No local administrative credential on any access point. Access points are controller-managed; the controllers authenticate administrators through central AAA with MFA under NSC-02 and NSC-03 |
| SNMP community strings | Default strings removed. SNMPv3 with authPriv on the controllers; SNMPv2c persists on 496 store-estate appliances under exception **NSC-EX-01**, with a non-default community, read-only, ACL-restricted to CT-48 |
| Default certificates | Replaced with certificates issued by the internal certificate authority CT-16 |
| Management interfaces | Access point and controller management on VLAN 99, reachable only from CT-49, CT-48 and named Z3 monitoring endpoints |
| Wi-Fi Protected Setup, unauthenticated provisioning, vendor cloud onboarding | Disabled estate-wide |
| Broadcast and management protocols | Peer-to-peer client traffic blocked on every guest SSID; multicast forwarding restricted; mDNS and SSDP not bridged between SSIDs |

### 3.3 Encryption key management — 2.3.2

Requirement 2.3.2 obliges wireless encryption keys to be changed whenever personnel with knowledge of the key leave the company or the role, and whenever a key is suspected or known to be compromised.

For an 802.1X network the requirement is largely structural: there is no shared key to know. EAP-TLS derives per-session keys from a per-device certificate, and a departing colleague takes no key with them because there was never one to take. Revocation is a certificate operation at CT-16, not a network operation, and it takes effect for one device.

| Network | Key model | 2.3.2 exposure | Change trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| Store colleague, corporate, distribution centre | **EAP-TLS**, per-device certificate, per-session keys | **None.** No human holds a key | Certificate revocation on device retirement or compromise; annual certificate rotation |
| Store guest, corporate guest | Open with a captive portal; no shared key | None | — |
| **Store legacy device wireless** | **WPA2-PSK, a single static key across 214 devices at 138 stores** | **The whole of Marketa's 2.3.2 exposure lives here** | §6 |

> **This is why 2.3.2 exists, and it is worth restating in Marketa's own terms.** A pre-shared key is a credential held by every device that uses it and by every person who has ever configured one. It cannot be revoked for one holder. It cannot be attributed to one holder. There is no record of who knows it. When somebody who knows it leaves, the only compliant response is to change it — which, across 214 devices in 138 stores, is a field operation. **The requirement is not onerous. The architecture that makes it onerous is the problem.**

## 4. Guest Wireless — the Design and the Weakness

The full store design is in 03.04. The wireless-side position is summarised here because it is the boundary the test broke.

| Property | Design intent | Enforcement | Layers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Guest traffic never exists as a local VLAN in a store | Centrally switched — encapsulated at the access point, tunnelled to CT-49, broken out to the internet in Columbus | CT-49 SSID configuration | **One** |
| The access point uplink carries only the VLANs it needs | Trunk with an explicit allowed-VLAN list of 20 and 99 | CT-48 store build image, `ROLE-AP` port template | **One** |
| Guest clients cannot see one another | Client isolation enabled on the guest SSID | CT-49 | One |
| Guest bandwidth is bounded | Per-client rate limiting at the controller | CT-49 | One |
| Captive portal terms accepted before egress | Portal at the break-out | CT-49 | One |

Two properties in that table have a **single** enforcement layer each, and the isolation depends on both holding simultaneously at every one of 482 sites. **At store 0417 both failed at once** — the build image had dropped the allowed-VLAN list, and a local change had moved the guest SSID to local switching to resolve a latency complaint. 03.04 §5 sets out the sequence; 03.07 sets out what a tester did with it.

The wireless-specific lesson, recorded here rather than in the test report: **an SSID's switching mode is a segmentation control.** It looks like a performance setting. It appears in the wireless controller's radio-frequency configuration alongside band steering and channel width. Nobody classified it as a network security control, and it decides whether an untrusted network exists inside a building. It is now on the segmentation-affecting change list in 03.03 §4.1, and it is asserted daily across all 482 sites.

## 5. Detection of Unauthorised Access Points — 11.2.1

Requirement 11.2.1 obliges the entity to test for the presence of wireless access points and to detect and identify **all authorised and unauthorised** wireless access points **at least once every three months**, covering all system components and facilities; to maintain the methods used; and to invoke incident response under **12.10.5** when unauthorised access points are detected.

The requirement applies **whether or not wireless is used in the CDE**, and the reason is the threat it exists to counter: not a badly configured corporate access point, but a cheap one plugged into a network port in a stockroom by somebody who wanted better coverage, or by somebody who wanted a way back in.

### 5.1 Marketa's detection design

| Layer | Method | Coverage | Cadence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Continuous over-the-air monitoring** | The 1,612 managed access points scan off-channel and report every radio they hear to CT-49, which classifies each against the authorised inventory | All 482 stores, 4 DCs, 2 offices — everywhere a managed access point exists | **Continuous** |
| **Wired-side correlation** | Every unclassified radio heard over the air is correlated against the switch MAC address tables at CT-48 to determine whether it is connected to a Marketa network or is simply a neighbour | All 482 stores | Continuous |
| **Quarterly attested scan** | A structured quarterly scan producing an attested, dated record for each facility, covering the frequency bands in use and the ones not in use | **All 488 facilities** | **Quarterly** — the 11.2.1 obligation |
| **Physical and logical inspection** | Store Operations includes a network cabinet inspection in the quarterly 9.5.1 device inspection round: unexpected devices, unexpected cables, unexpected ports in use | All 482 stores | Quarterly |
| **Port-state assertion** | Every unused switch port asserted to be administratively shut and assigned to VLAN 999; any port transitioning from shut to active without a change record raises an alert | All 482 stores | **Daily** |

The wired-side correlation is the layer that makes the over-the-air data usable. A store in a shopping centre hears dozens of radios that are nothing to do with Marketa, and a detection programme that treats every unknown SSID as a rogue produces an alert stream nobody reads. **The question is not "is there an unknown radio?" — it is "is there an unknown radio attached to my network?"**, and answering it requires the switch tables as well as the air.

### 5.2 Results, and what to do about a detection

| Quarter | Facilities scanned | Unknown radios observed | **Attached to a Marketa network** | Disposition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 2026 | 488 | 4,112 | **2** | One consumer access point in a distribution centre office, installed by a supervisor for personal device coverage. One travel router in a store stockroom, left by a contractor. Both removed within 24 hours; **12.10.5** incident procedure invoked for both; the DC case produced a disciplinary referral and an all-staff reminder |
| Q2 2026 | 488 | 4,367 | **1** | A wireless print server enabled in ad-hoc mode by default at a store. Feature disabled, device re-homed to the wired media VLAN, procurement standard updated to exclude wireless-capable peripherals |
| Q3 2026 | 488 | 4,290 | 0 | — |
| Q4 2026 | 488 | Reported in Phase 06 | Reported in Phase 06 | — |

Three unauthorised access points in a year, all found, all removed inside a day, none with any path to the CDE. The programme reports them rather than reporting a clean quarter, because **the value of 11.2.1 is the three, and an entity reporting zero detections across a 482-site retail estate is more likely to have a detection problem than a rogue-AP problem.**

The response procedure is short and is exercised rather than written: classify within an hour — is it attached to a Marketa network, and where; contain within two by administratively shutting the switch port, which the wired-side correlation has already identified; invoke the **12.10.5** incident procedure immediately, recording the event as a security incident rather than as a network fault; determine within 24 hours what the device could reach and whether anything did; remove it physically at the next store visit once containment is confirmed; and complete root cause within five working days, against two questions — why was the port live, and why was the device available.

## 6. The Legacy Pre-Shared Key Remnant — 214 Devices

### 6.1 What it is

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| Devices | **214** |
| Stores affected | **138** |
| Device types | Digital signage media players, in-store music appliances, and environmental and refrigeration sensors |
| SSID | A dedicated legacy device SSID, mapped to VLAN 40 |
| Authentication | **WPA2-PSK** — a single static pre-shared key, common to all 214 devices |
| Key set | At deployment, in **2019**. **Never rotated** |
| Who knows the key | Unbounded. It was distributed to installation contractors, appears in a 2019 deployment runbook, and is stored in the configuration of every device that uses it — recoverable from any one of them |
| Network position | VLAN 40; internet egress only through the store WAN edge; explicit deny to VLANs 10, 20, 30 and 99 |
| Path to the CDE | None |
| PCI requirement engaged | **2.3.2** as an internal standard; **1.2.6** insecure-service register; **11.2.2** inventory |
| Programme disposition | **Known weakness. Becomes corrective action CAP-05** |

### 6.2 Why it exists, and why it has not been fixed yet

The devices predate Marketa's 802.1X estate. They have no certificate store, no supplicant capable of EAP-TLS, and in several cases no configuration interface beyond a vendor mobile app. There is no configuration change that moves them onto the enterprise network; there is only replacement, and replacement of 214 appliances across 138 stores is a field programme rather than a change.

The interim controls are real and they are not sufficient:

| Control in place | What it achieves | What it does not achieve |
|---|---|---|
| VLAN 40 confinement, internet egress only | The devices cannot reach the store operations VLAN, the POI VLAN or the management VLAN | Nothing, if a VLAN mapping is wrong — which is exactly the failure mode 03.04 §5 documents |
| Explicit deny at the VLAN 40 SVI | A second layer inside the store | It is enforced by the same switch configuration that failed at store 0417 |
| MAC allow-list on the legacy SSID | Raises the effort of joining | Trivially defeated. Recorded as a hurdle, not a control |
| Client isolation on the legacy SSID | Devices cannot reach one another | Does not affect what they can reach off-VLAN |
| Inventory maintained under 11.2.2 | The population is known and is shrinking as devices fail and are replaced with wired equivalents | Does not reduce the exposure of the devices still deployed |

### 6.3 Why the programme names it now

Three reasons, stated plainly.

**A static key shared by 214 devices and an unbounded population of people is a credential without an owner.** It cannot be revoked for one holder, attributed to one holder, or changed without a field operation. Every 2.3.2 change trigger — a departure, a role change, a suspicion — has been met with nothing since 2019.

**The devices are unmanaged Linux appliances on a store network.** 02.08 §16 excluded digital signage from scope with a specific warning attached: *"it is only a screen" is the kind of reasoning that puts an unmanaged Linux host on a payment network*. The exclusion is correct on the current architecture and it is conditional on the VLAN separation holding — which is the same condition, enforced by the same switch configuration, as the one that failed at store 0417.

**Its containment depends on the boundary this phase has just proved can fail.** That is the argument for treating it as a live weakness rather than as technical debt. The 214 devices are safe because a VLAN mapping is correct in 138 stores, and Phase 03 has demonstrated that a VLAN mapping was not correct in one.

| Aspect | Position |
|---|---|
| Current risk rating | Moderate, in the Phase 03 baseline register |
| Corrective action | **CAP-05** — eliminate the legacy pre-shared key population |
| Approach | Replace with wired equivalents at natural refresh and at any store network visit; certificate-capable replacements where wireless is genuinely required; retire the legacy SSID when the count reaches zero |
| Interim obligation | Key rotated on a defined cycle from 2026-08, accepting that rotation requires touching each device; population reported monthly; any device failing to check in for 30 days removed from the allow-list |
| Owner | Trevor Kim, with Adaeze Nwosu for the field execution |
| Tracking | Phase 06 corrective action register; closure reported in Phase 09 |

## 7. Inventory of Authorised Access Points — 11.2.2

Requirement 11.2.2 obliges an inventory of authorised wireless access points, including a documented business justification for each.

| Inventory attribute | Held? | Source of truth |
|---|---|---|
| Make, model and hardware revision | Yes | CT-49 controller inventory, reconciled to procurement records |
| MAC address of each radio | Yes | CT-49 |
| Serial number | Yes | CT-49, reconciled to the asset register |
| Physical location — facility, floor, position | Yes | Site survey records, updated on any move |
| Switch and port it is connected to | Yes | CT-48 correlation — the attribute that makes 11.2.1 containment possible in minutes |
| SSIDs broadcast and the VLAN each maps to | Yes | CT-49 configuration extract |
| **Business justification** | Yes | Per site, by SSID class — customer amenity, colleague operations, legacy device connectivity |
| Firmware version | Yes | CT-49 telemetry |
| Authorised-by reference | Yes | Change record for the installation |

| Population | Count | Business justification |
|---|---|---|
| Store access points | **1,388** | Colleague handheld operations and the customer guest amenity. Coverage sized by store format from site survey |
| Distribution centre access points | **164** | Warehouse handheld and forklift terminal operations across four sites |
| Office access points | **60** | Staff computing and visitor access at Columbus and Austin |
| **Total authorised** | **1,612** | — |
| Unauthorised detected in 2026 | **3** | All removed; §5.2 |

The inventory is reconciled monthly against the controller's live view and against the asset register, on the same discipline as the 12.5.1 component inventory: **an inventory that is not reconciled is a list, and a list cannot tell you that something has been added.**

## 8. What Wireless Costs Marketa in Scope

Store guest wireless keeps itself out of scope and, by extension, **482 store servers** — because a failure of **B-09** puts the store estate on the reachable side of the boundary, which is condition **SEG-C4**. Store colleague wireless keeps the handheld and workforce device population out on VLAN and flow policy. The legacy pre-shared key network keeps 214 appliances out on VLAN 40 separation, for as long as **CAP-05** remains open. Corporate and distribution centre wireless inherit the Z4 position and, through it, the ~1,100-laptop exclusion that rests on the jump-host property at **B-04**.

Every one of those conditions is a configuration property, in a network Marketa does not physically control, at sites members of the public walk into. That is why wireless carries its own requirements in PCI DSS, and why the 11.4.5 test used wireless as its primary starting position.
