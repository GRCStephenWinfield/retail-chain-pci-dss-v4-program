The honest headline. Marketa told its acquirer it did not meet two PCI DSS requirements — twenty days before it had to tell it anything — and then met them. Neither finding was discovered by the assessor: both were on Marketa’s own register months before fieldwork, and in both cases a compensating control was drafted and withdrawn rather than argued. The compliant re-assessment of 2027-02-18 did not withdraw the non-compliant attestation.

And the register missed its own published forecast by three entries. Under this register’s discipline — a rating moves on likelihood unless the consequence changed, and a likelihood of 1 is reserved for events that are not reasonably foreseeable — an entry scored 3 × 4 reaches 2 × 4 = 8 and stops. Eight is a floor. The Phase 03 forecast moved thirteen entries across a boundary the arithmetic does not permit. The close is 0 · 16 · 28 against a published 0 · 13 · 31, and the final phase publishes the arithmetic rather than the excuse.

Where the non-compliance actually came from

Three of the five recorded exceptions trace to the same nine devices and the same support agreement — the 11.3.1.2 finding, CCW-01 at 8.3.6, CCW-02 at 2.2.7 — and those nine were also the only population the assessor was not permitted to sample, because standardisation could not be demonstrated. One commercial decision, signed years before the programme started, produced more assessed exceptions than every control gap in the estate combined. Not a compliance failure — a contract nobody had read as a security control.

And the remediation removed the finding, not the constraint. The Verition amendment of 2027-01-08 granted credential release, not firmware change. CCW-01 and CCW-02 both survive the re-assessment and the compensating-control count stays at three. R-36’s residual did not disappear — it changed shape, from technical to contractual, and a control that depends on a clause has a renewal date.

PCI DSS is contractual, not statutory. The PCI Security Standards Council writes the standard and does not enforce it — enforcement runs from the card brands to the acquirer to the merchant, and Marketa’s counterparty is Cardinal Merchant Bank, N.A. There is no PCI DSS certification for a merchant; a Report on Compliance and an Attestation of Compliance are a point-in-time attestation about an assessed period, and no attestation is a determination of compliance with any law. A QSA’s opinion binds neither an acquirer nor a card brand.
