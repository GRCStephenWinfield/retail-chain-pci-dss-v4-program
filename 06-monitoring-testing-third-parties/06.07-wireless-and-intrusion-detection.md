## 2. Wireless Access Point Detection — 11.2.1

11.2.1 obliges testing for the presence of wireless access points and the detection and identification of **all authorised and unauthorised** access points at least once every three months, covering all system components and facilities, with the methods maintained and **12.10.5** incident response invoked when an unauthorised access point is detected.

**The requirement applies whether or not wireless is used in the CDE.** Marketa has no wireless network with a permitted path to Z1 or Z2, and owes this requirement in full anyway, because the threat it addresses is not a badly configured corporate access point. It is a cheap one plugged into a network port in a stockroom.

The detection design — continuous over-the-air monitoring across 1,612 managed access points, wired-side correlation against the CT-48 switch tables, a structured quarterly attested scan of all 488 facilities, physical inspection inside the 9.5.1 round, and daily port-state assertion — is set out in **03.06 §5.1** and is not repeated. The operative point from that design, which governs how the results below should be read: **the question is not whether there is an unknown radio, but whether there is an unknown radio attached to Marketa's network.** A store in a shopping centre hears dozens of radios that belong to other tenants.

### 2.1 The year

| Quarter | Facilities scanned | Unknown radios observed | **Attached to a Marketa network** | Disposition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Q1 2026** | 488 | 4,112 | **2** | A consumer access point in a distribution centre office, installed by a supervisor for personal device coverage; and a travel router left in a store stockroom by a contractor. Both removed inside 24 hours; **12.10.5** invoked for both; the DC case produced a disciplinary referral and an all-staff reminder |
| **Q2 2026** | 488 | 4,367 | **1** | A wireless print server enabled in ad-hoc mode by default at a store. Feature disabled, device re-homed to the wired media VLAN, procurement standard updated to exclude wireless-capable peripherals |
| **Q3 2026** | 488 | 4,290 | **0** | — |
| **Q4 2026** | **488** | **4,186** | **0** | — |
| **Year** | **1,952 facility-scans** | **16,955** | **3** | **All removed inside 24 hours; none with any path to the CDE** |

Four complete quarterly cycles across every facility, with no sampling. **Three unauthorised access points in a year across 482 stores, four distribution centres and two offices**, all detected, all removed within a day.

The programme reports the three rather than reporting three clean quarters, on the position 03.06 established and this document repeats without softening: **an entity reporting zero detections across a 482-site retail estate is more likely to have a detection problem than a rogue access-point problem.** Q3 and Q4 returning zero is only credible in the context of Q1 and Q2 returning three.

Q4's cycle ran on 2026-11-09, inside the seasonal freeze and inside the QSA fieldwork window. That is deliberate. The freeze stops change; it does not stop detection, and a quarterly cycle that skips the quarter with the highest store footfall, the most temporary colleagues and the most contractor activity would be skipping the quarter that matters.

### 2.2 The response procedure, and what it is measured on

The procedure is short and is exercised rather than written: classify within an hour, containment within two by shutting the switch port the wired-side correlation has already identified, **12.10.5** invoked immediately as a security incident rather than a network fault, a determination within 24 hours of what the device could reach and whether anything did, physical removal at the next store visit once containment is confirmed, and root cause within five working days against two questions — why was the port live, and why was the device available.

| Measure across the three detections | Result |
|---|---|
| Median time from over-the-air observation to classification | **22 minutes** |
| Median time from classification to port shut | **31 minutes** |
| 12.10.5 incident record opened | 3 of 3 |
| Reachability determination completed within 24 hours | 3 of 3. **In all three cases the device's VLAN had no path to Z1 or Z2** |
| Root cause completed within 5 working days | 3 of 3 |
| Procedure changes produced | **2** — the procurement standard excluding wireless-capable peripherals, and a contractor equipment sign-out and sign-in check at store visits |

The second procedure change is the one that matters more. A travel router left in a stockroom is not a malicious act; it is a contractor forgetting a device. The control that prevents it is an equipment reconciliation at the end of a site visit, which is a store operations process and not a network one.

## 4. The Legacy Pre-Shared Key Remnant — CAP-05 Status

**214 devices at 138 stores sharing one static WPA2-PSK set in 2019 and never rotated.** The full analysis — why it exists, why the interim controls are real and insufficient, and why the programme named it in a design document rather than waiting for an assessor to find it — is **03.06 §6**. This document carries the corrective action's status, because CAP-05 is tracked in Phase 06.

| Field | Position |
|---|---|
| Identifier | **CAP-05** — eliminate the legacy pre-shared key population |
| Risk | **R-15** — 3 × 4 = 12, Moderate |
| Requirement engaged | **2.3.2** as an internal standard — the applicability determination in 03.06 §3.1 concluded 2.3.1 and 2.3.2 are not applicable as written, and Marketa implements them anyway; **1.2.6** insecure-service register; **11.2.2** inventory |
| Owner | Trevor Kim, with Adaeze Nwosu for field execution |
| Population at Phase 03 baseline, 2026-07-15 | **214 devices, 138 stores** |

| Population at freeze start, 2026-09-30 | **203 devices, 133 stores** — 11 replaced with wired equivalents at natural refresh and at store network visits |
| Position through the freeze | **Frozen at 203.** CON-01 stops store-side change from October; replacement resumes February 2027 |
| Devices failing to check in for 30 days and removed from the allow-list | 6 across the period |
| Target | Legacy SSID retired when the count reaches zero |
| Reported | Monthly to the PCI Steering Committee; closure reported in Phase 09 |

The rotation is recorded as a completed obligation rather than as a resolution. **Rotating a key that 214 devices share, and that an unbounded population of installation contractors has known since 2019, replaces one unattributable shared credential with a newer unattributable shared credential.** It discharges the 2.3.2 change trigger that had gone unmet for seven years. It does not change the property that makes the arrangement a weakness, and CAP-05 stays open until the population is zero.

## 5. Intrusion Detection and Prevention — 11.5.1

11.5.1 obliges intrusion-detection and/or intrusion-prevention techniques to detect and/or prevent intrusions, with **all traffic monitored at the perimeter of the CDE**, **all traffic monitored at critical points within the CDE**, personnel alerted to suspected compromises, and all engines, baselines and signatures kept up to date.

Four limbs. The second is the one entities under-deliver, because perimeter monitoring is the obvious reading and "critical points within" requires the entity to decide what those are.

### 5.1 Where the sensors are

| Position | Type | What it sees | 11.5.1 limb |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Z1 CDE core perimeter — north and south** | Inline prevention at CT-43 and CT-44, with detection-mode inspection of the decrypted path | All traffic entering and leaving the Columbus CDE, in both directions | **Perimeter** |
| **Z2 CDE cloud perimeter** | Traffic mirroring from the CDE VPC to an inspection tier, plus inline controls at CT-50 | All traffic entering and leaving the Z2 CDE VPC, including cross-region standby replication | **Perimeter** |
| **The Z3-to-Z1 crossing** | Inline prevention | The only routed path into the Columbus CDE — the concentration that **R-27** prices | **Critical point within** |
| **The CDE-05 external interface** | Inline prevention plus protocol-aware inspection | The file-transfer and API path to Cardinal and Truvance, on which flow F-04 carries full PAN | **Critical point within** |
| **Between the Z1 application and data tiers** | Detection, from a tap | East-west traffic inside the CDE — CDE-01 to CDE-02, CDE-04 to CDE-05 | **Critical point within** |
| **Z2 inter-subnet** | Detection, from mirrored traffic | CDE-07 to CDE-08 and the egress path to Truvance | **Critical point within** |
| **The CDE-06 desktop pool egress** | Inline prevention | The path from the secure desktop pool where dispute images render | **Critical point within** |
| Store estate and Z5 boundary | Detection at the WAN aggregation layer | Not a CDE limb; carried because R-14 exists | Beyond the requirement |

**Marketa's definition of a critical point within the CDE**, recorded so it can be challenged: any point at which traffic crosses between components of differing account-data relationship, any point at which traffic leaves the CDE for a third party, and any point at which an administrative path enters. That definition produces the five internal positions above. An entity that monitors only the perimeter would have two of the eight.

### 5.2 Alerting personnel

| Element | Position |
|---|---|
| Route | Sensors alert into CT-22, which forwards to CT-18 for correlation with the 187-rule detection set. **An intrusion alert is a case in the same adjudication queue as everything else**, not a separate console with a separate watcher |
| Severity | Prevention events on a CDE path are P1 and page the on-call responder immediately; detection-only events on internal positions are P2 |
| Coverage | 24 hours — Marketa security operations 06:00–22:00, Northbridge 22:00–06:00 under the 12.8.5 matrix |
| Volume in the period | 1,914 sensor alerts reaching adjudication; **31** escalated to a 12.10 incident; **0** confirmed intrusions into the CDE |
| Prevention actions taken | 2,706 blocks at the perimeters, overwhelmingly commodity internet scanning against the external interfaces |

Routing intrusion alerts into the same adjudication queue is a deliberate design choice, and it is the reason the alerts get read. A dedicated intrusion-detection console with its own watcher is a second queue, and a second queue is a queue somebody is not looking at while they look at the first.

### 5.3 Engines, baselines and signatures kept up to date

| Element | Cadence | Assurance |
|---|---|---|
| Signature and rule feeds | Automatic, at least daily | **Feed age asserted continuously; a sensor whose feed is more than 48 hours old is a CSC-2 failure under 10.7.2** |
| Engine software | Within the vendor's supported range; security releases within one month under **NSC-14** | Version reconciliation monthly |
| Behavioural baselines | Recomputed weekly; re-baselined after any significant change affecting the monitored path | Baseline age monitored |
| **Efficacy** | **Weekly synthetic signature test traffic** injected at each sensor position, which must alert | This is the assurance that matters. A sensor with current signatures and a broken inspection path reports healthy |
| Quarterly validation | Controlled execution with Ironwood Security Labs — 06.03 §4.4 | 42 of 46 techniques detected across the whole detection estate |

One efficacy test failed in the period: the Z2 inter-subnet mirroring session stopped after a cloud network change, and the sensor saw no traffic for **31 hours** while reporting itself healthy. It was found by the weekly synthetic test rather than by the sensor's own health reporting, and it is recorded in the 10.7.2 failure register as the single **CSC-2** entry in 06.04 §5.

### 5.4 11.5.1.1 is a service-provider requirement

**11.5.1.1 — detecting, alerting on and/or preventing and addressing covert malware communication channels, such as DNS tunnelling — applies to service providers only.** Marketa is a merchant. The requirement is **not part of Marketa's applicable requirement set and was never among the 306** applicable sub-requirements assessed.

As with 10.7.1 in 06.04, this is not a Not Applicable determination. Service-provider-only requirements were excluded from the applicable population at scoping. **The ROC's four Not Applicable entries are merchant-applicable sub-requirements determined not to apply to Marketa's environment**, and Phase 08 names them.

Marketa implements covert-channel detection anyway, voluntarily, and records the outcome honestly: **the quarterly controlled-execution validation in 06.03 §4.4 found that a DNS-based exfiltration channel from the store estate was one of the four techniques no rule detected.** A rule now exists. The voluntary control failed its first real test, and reporting that is more useful than reporting that the control is in place.

## 6. Change Detection on Critical Files — 11.5.2

11.5.2 obliges a change-detection mechanism — for example, file integrity monitoring — deployed to alert personnel to unauthorised modification, including changes, additions and deletions, of critical files, and configured to perform critical file comparisons **at least once weekly**.

### 6.1 Distinguishing 11.5.2 from 10.3.4

| | **11.5.2** | **10.3.4** |
|---|---|---|
| Applies to | **Critical files** — system binaries, configuration, content | **Audit logs** |
| Obligation | Alert on unauthorised modification, addition or deletion | Existing log data cannot be changed without an alert |
| Cadence | **At least weekly comparison** | No stated frequency; the obligation is that change alerts |
| Delivered in | This document | **06.02 §5** |

One deployment of CT-21 serves both, and both are evidenced separately, because an assessor testing 11.5.2 will ask for the weekly comparison record and an assessor testing 10.3.4 will ask what protects the log store.

### 6.2 What counts as a critical file

The requirement does not enumerate critical files, and an entity that monitors everything produces an alert stream nobody reads. Marketa's definition is by function, per platform, approved by the platform owner and version-controlled.

| Class | Examples | Population |
|---|---|---|
| **System binaries and libraries** | Operating system executables, shared libraries, kernel modules, drivers | All 71 |
| **Security-relevant configuration** | Authentication and authorisation configuration, audit policy, host firewall rules, agent configuration, cryptographic configuration and trust stores | All 71 |
| **Application binaries and configuration** | Deployed application code, connection and integration configuration, scheduled task and service definitions | The 8 CDE components and the 20 connected-to components running Marketa applications |
| **Boundary configuration** | Network security control rule sets, VLAN and interface configuration, routing policy | CT-43 to CT-51 and the store estate boundary, monitored through configuration-state comparison rather than a file agent |
| **Payment-page content and templates** | The 6 templates and the publication path | Monitored here as **files at rest**. The consumer-browser view is **11.6.1 in 06.09**, and they are not the same thing |
| Explicitly excluded | Log files, temporary directories, package caches, application data, user profile data | Excluded by named rule with an owner, not by silence. **43 exclusion rules**, reviewed quarterly |

The payment-page row carries the distinction most worth stating in this phase. **File integrity monitoring proves that the template on the origin server has not changed. It says nothing about what a consumer's browser assembled**, because a third-party script loaded at run time from a vendor's content delivery network never touches Marketa's file system. That is why 11.6.1 exists as a separate requirement, why it is detective where 6.4.3 is preventive, and why it is delivered in 06.09 rather than absorbed here.

### 6.3 How it operates

| Element | Position |
|---|---|
| Mechanism | CT-21 agents on all 71 assessed components; configuration-state comparison for the appliance and network classes that cannot host an agent |
| **Comparison cadence** | **Real-time on the 8 CDE components and the 20 connected-to components running Marketa applications**; **daily** full comparison on the remainder of the 71; **weekly** full baseline comparison across every monitored object on every component. The weekly full comparison is the 11.5.2 obligation and it runs even where real-time monitoring is in place, because a real-time agent that has stopped reports nothing in real time |
| Baseline approval | Baselines derived from the Phase 04 build standards, approved by the platform owner; a baseline change requires a change record |
| Change correlation | Every detection is automatically correlated against approved change records and against the patch deployment stream from CT-26 before it reaches a human |
| Alerting | Uncorrelated modification on a CDE component is **P1**; on a connected-to component, **P2** |
| Coverage assurance | A **deliberate benign change on a canary path**, weekly, on every monitored component, which must alert. This is the **CSC-3** secondary detection in 06.04 §4.3 |
| The 9 CON-03 appliances | **No agent is possible.** Monitored by configuration-state comparison from the management console and by firmware version reconciliation. Recorded as a coverage limitation, consistent with 06.05 §5 |

### 6.4 What it found

| Disposition | Count in the period | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Correlated to an approved change or patch deployment | 41,208 | Closed automatically; sampled monthly by an analyst to confirm the correlation is sound |
| Uncorrelated, benign cause established | **312** | Vendor installer behaviour, agent self-updates, application-generated configuration writes. Each produced either an exclusion rule with an owner or a baseline correction |
| Uncorrelated, **control condition** | **9** | A configuration reverted outside change control on 4 occasions; an agent stopped on 3; a baseline not re-established after a change on 2 |
| **Escalated to a 12.10 incident** | **2** | Both on connected-to components; both determined to be unauthorised administrative action by an engineer working outside change control, not compromise. Both produced disciplinary and process outcomes |
| Confirmed unauthorised modification by an external party | **0** | — |
| Canary tests failed | **2 of 3,692** | Both agent-state issues, both recorded as **CSC-3** failures in the 10.7.2 register |

The 312 benign uncorrelated detections are the interesting number. Each one had to be examined and each one produced either an exclusion rule or a baseline correction — which is the work that keeps a file integrity monitoring deployment usable. A deployment where 312 unexplained detections are left unexamined becomes a deployment where the two real control conditions are invisible inside the noise, and that is the ordinary fate of file integrity monitoring in most estates.
