## Purpose

03.12 mapped all **44** baseline risks forward to the controls that would treat them. 04.12 and 05.12 did the same job backwards for their phases, and both recorded that **nothing in the register moved**, because a control published in July has no operating history and a control with no operating history does not lower a rating.

**Phase 06 is the phase where that changes**, and the reason is the whole subject of the phase.

> **Phase 06 does not build many controls. It establishes whether the controls the programme built actually run.** Its evidence is 365 daily review records, 4 quarterly scan cycles, 4 ASV attestations, 1,952 facility-scans, 19 penetration test findings from two independent engagements, 214 tamper-detection alerts, and 36 provider validation verifications. **That is operating history, and operating history is the thing the earlier phases said they were waiting for.**

Six entries move. Twenty-two are held, and each holding is stated with its reason. The document answers five questions and refuses to blur them.

| Question | Where |
|---|---|
| Which of the 44 does Phase 06 touch, and with what? | §3 |
| Which entries **move**, and on what evidence? | §4 |
| Which entries are **held**, and why is that not an omission? | §5 |
| Does every control Phase 06 built treat a risk the register contains? | §6 |
| Does the expected close position still reconcile to **0 High · 13 Moderate · 31 Low**? | §7 |

And the discipline that governs the movements, stated before any of them:

> **A rating moves on measured operating evidence, never on delivery.** R-01 moves because 6.4.3 and 11.6.1 have both operated and one of them detected three real conditions in 140 days. R-09 moves because a year of monitoring caught a provider's AOC scope-statement change before the provider raised it. **R-36 does not move**, because the requirement it treats is Not in Place and the accommodations around it were explicitly refused as compensating controls.

## 2. How to Read the Mapping

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Primary** | Phase 06 delivers the control on which the risk's treatment principally depends. If this phase's control fails, the risk is substantially untreated |
| **Contributory** | Phase 06 delivers a control that reduces the risk, where the principal treatment sits in another phase |
| **Accommodation** | Phase 06 delivers measures that reduce consequence without treating the cause, **and says so.** An accommodation never lowers a rating |
| **Movement** | A change in the register's likelihood or impact score, justified by measured operating evidence, with the evidence named |
| **Held** | The rating is unchanged. **Held is a determination, not an absence of one**, and every held entry carries its reason in §5 |

### 4.1 The one that the phase exists for

R-01 has been the register's highest entry since the baseline, at **20**, the only entry at both likelihood 4 and impact 5. Four documents across two phases declined to move it, in almost the same words each time:

| Document | What it said |
|---|---|
| **04.10 §13** | "**High** at 2026-07-15... The controls are strong and **three months old**. Six of 38 scripts are assured by detection rather than prevention, **11.6.1 is not yet live**" |
| **06.03 §8** | "The substantive detective control is **11.6.1 in 06.09**. **High until 11.6.1 has operated for a full cycle**" |
| **06.06 §8** | "**An ASV scan does not detect a script injected into a page served correctly by a healthy host.**... That control is **11.6.1 in 06.09**" |
| **06.07 §8** | "**Not treated here.** File integrity monitoring proves the template at rest is unchanged and cannot see a run-time third-party script" |

**Every one of those conditions is now discharged**, and the discharge is not a delivery date. It is 140 days of operation, 1.29 million synthetic evaluations, 4.1 million real-browser digests, three incidents — two of which were Marketa's own failures, which is the harder detection case — and a detector that was deliberately tested twice and reported the slower of the two results.

**The portfolio held R-01 at High for two phases specifically so that this movement would mean something.** Moving it in Phase 04 on the strength of a published inventory would have been the easier and less defensible position, and 04.10 §13 says why in advance.

## 6. Reverse Traceability — Controls That Treat No Risk

### 6.1 Two findings tested against ADR-0005, and why neither raised a register entry

**ADR-0005** obliges a new risk entry when discovery **disproves an assumption**, not whenever a control produces a finding. Phase 06 produced two candidates and both were tested.

| Candidate | Test | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| **APP-PT-01** — an unauthenticated administrative interface reachable from Z3 on a CDE component | Was an assumption disproved? **The assumption that authentication enforced at a proxy is authentication enforced by the application** — which was never a written assumption, and which no register entry rests on. Would a new entry describe something **R-27** and **R-32** do not? R-27 is the concentration that made Z3 the launch point; R-32 is a boundary-affecting change classified as routine. **The finding is an instance of both** | **No entry raised.** Recorded as an instance under R-27 and R-32; the treatment is the SIG-4 amendment and the listener assertion |
| **The SCR-29 vendor change notification arriving four days late** | Was an assumption disproved? **Yes, arguably** — the assumption that a contractual change-notification duty would perform. Would a new entry describe something **R-09** and **R-11** do not? R-09 is provider concentration; **R-11 is a responsibility gap such that an event is seen by neither party**, and a notification duty that does not perform is precisely that gap | **No entry raised.** Recorded as an instance under R-11, with **OI-06-08** as the contract remedy. The register remains at **44** |

The second determination is the closer call and is recorded at length for that reason. A contractual duty that fails is a real disproof, and the argument for raising an entry was made in the working group. It was rejected on the ground 05.12 §6.1 states: **a register that grows by one entry every time a control produces a finding stops describing exposure and starts describing activity.** The exposure — a provider changing something and Marketa learning late — is already entry R-11, and R-11 is held at Low 6 **precisely because it was tested and found wanting once**.

The Phase 09 close is reported after the **2027-02-18** re-assessment, and that sequencing is why it reconciles. **The register and the ROC disagree at a point in time because they measure different things on different dates**, and the programme states the sequence rather than smoothing the numbers — the same position 05.12 §7 took for R-17 and this phase now takes for R-36 as well.

## 8. What This Traceability Does Not Prove

| Limitation | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **A year of operation is one year** | Every movement in §4 rests on a single operating cycle. **11.6.1 has 140 days**, not a year, and its movement of R-01 is the largest single change in the register on the shortest evidence base in it. Stated here rather than left for a reader to compute |
| **A control that found nothing may be a good control or a blind one** | TAM-7 produced no alerts. The 60-day pre-expiry watch never fired. Thirty-six MT-1 verifications detected no status change. **Each is reported as a null result**, and a null result is only interpretable alongside the instrument's proof-of-life evidence |
| **The movements depend on parties Marketa does not employ** | R-09's residual rests on six providers' continued validation and on notification duties that failed once. R-01's rests on a monitoring provider's availability, which produced three CSC-10 failures in 140 days |
| **The register bounds the analysis** | The reverse test in §6 found one control that arrived before its risk did. **It cannot find a risk nobody thought of.** R-14 and R-31 are what it looks like when testing and discovery find one instead — and Phase 06's near-miss is the TPSP-06 omission, found by a control looking for something else |
| **Held is a judgement, and judgements can be wrong in either direction** | Six entries are held with reasons in §5. **R-28 in particular could be argued down** — the mechanism found its own failures four times and fixed them each time. It is held because the same mechanism keeps finding more, and that is a judgement rather than a calculation |
| **Phase 06 cannot close what Phase 08 owns** | **R-36's residual depends on a contract negotiation**, R-17's on a vendor release and an ERP upgrade, R-14's on a second annual segmentation test in 2027. Phase 06 tested everything it can test and says so rather than claiming the residual |
