## Purpose

Requirement **12.8** — the management of third-party service providers with which account data is shared, or which could affect the security of the cardholder data environment — executed from the merchant's side, across **six** providers.

| Sub-requirement | Obligation |
|---|---|
| **12.8.1** | A **list of all TPSPs** with which account data is shared or that could affect its security is maintained, **including a description of the service provided** |
| **12.8.2** | **Written agreements** are maintained with TPSPs, **including acknowledgements from the TPSP that they are responsible for the security of account data they possess or otherwise store, process or transmit on behalf of the entity, or to the extent that they could impact the security of the entity's cardholder data environment** |
| **12.8.3** | An **established process** is implemented for engaging TPSPs, **including proper due diligence prior to engagement** |
| **12.8.4** | A **programme is implemented to monitor TPSPs' PCI DSS compliance status at least once every 12 months** |
| **12.8.5** | **Information is maintained about which PCI DSS requirements are managed by each TPSP, which are managed by the entity, and any that are shared** |
| 12.9.1 / **12.9.2** | **Service-provider requirements.** Never within Marketa's 306. **12.9.2 is the provider-side duty Marketa's 12.8.5 relies on** — §7 |

**01.09 defined the register and the process. This document executes them for a full year, across six providers rather than five**, and states the structural conclusion the programme has been deferring since Phase 02.

> **Marketa's scope reduction — 604 systems to 71, a fall of 88.2% — did not remove the risk of holding card data. It converted a technical control problem into a third-party assurance problem, and this is the document where that bill arrives.**

## 1. Why This Document Is Harder Than It Looks

Requirement 12.8 reads like procurement hygiene. Keep a list, sign contracts, do diligence, check the AOCs, write down who does what. Five obligations, all administrative, none technical.

That reading is why 12.8 is under-built at most merchants, and it is wrong at Marketa for a specific and measurable reason.

| Fact | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **The PAN never touches a Marketa server on any of the three acceptance channels** | Every technical control protecting the PAN is operated by somebody else |
| **1,914 lanes and 482 store servers are out of the CDE because Verition's P2PE solution is listed by the Council** | That reduction rests on a listing Marketa cannot audit, held by a party Marketa cannot assess |
| **246 AWS e-commerce workloads are out of the CDE because the card fields live in a Truvance-origin iframe** | That reduction rests on the same-origin policy — which is real and enforced by the browser — **and** on Truvance's own environment being secure, which is not |
| **310 call-centre agent desktops are out of the CDE because Cadence suppresses DTMF upstream of Marketa** | That reduction rests on a suppression point in a provider's telephony platform |
| **Truvance is gateway, hosted checkout and the single token vault across all three channels simultaneously** | **One provider failure affects every channel at once.** That is **R-09**, rated **High 15** |
| Two of the six providers hold no PCI validation at all | Their requirements are assessed inside Marketa's own ROC — which is the standard's provision, and it means Sable Ridge tests them and Marketa carries the finding |

**Three providers now sit between Marketa and its ability to accept payment at all.** Verition on the card-present channel, Truvance on all three, Cadence on MOTO. Remove any one of them and a channel stops, and in Truvance's case the phrase "a channel" understates it: the token vault is single, and it is theirs.

Marketa made that architecture deliberately, and 02.10 records why: the alternative is holding 68.4 million primary account numbers a year across 482 stores, a call centre and a storefront. **The trade is correct and it is not free.** What it bought was a 71-component assessment. What it cost is that the most consequential controls in the payment architecture are exercised by parties whose evidence Marketa can read, corroborate and contract for, and cannot test.

### 2.1 What is deliberately not in the register

A register that contains only inclusions cannot be reviewed, because there is nothing to check the boundary against. 01.09 §1 records the excluded categories, and the quarterly TPSP forum re-tests them. **One category changed during the year and one was re-tested and confirmed.**

| Category | Position at 2026-12-31 | Change in the period |
|---|---|---|
| Merchandise vendors, freight, payroll, general SaaS with no CDE connectivity | Excluded | None |
| Facilities and maintenance at non-CDE sites | Excluded; contractors entering Columbus CDE areas are handled under Requirement 9 visitor controls | None |
| **Co-location and hosting providers for in-scope components** | **Now included — TPSP-06** | **The change.** The Phase 01 test asked whether the provider touches account data. **The right question is whether it could affect the security of the CDE** |
| Marketing agencies | Excluded **only** where they hold no ability to publish to payment-page templates | Re-tested after the 6.4.3 finding. **Payment-template publication no longer exists as a capability in the tag console**, so no agency currently meets the security-impact limb. The exclusion is now conditional on a permission state that is asserted rather than assumed |
| **The 11.6.1 monitoring provider** | **Assessed and excluded from the 12.8.1 register; carried in vendor risk management** | New relationship, 2026-08. It receives no account data, holds no CDE access and cannot alter a Marketa system. **It could, however, fail to report a compromise**, so its availability is governed as **CSC-10** under 10.7.2 and its service terms carry a notification obligation. The determination is recorded so a reviewer can disagree with it |

The last row is the honest one. A monitoring provider that goes quiet does not affect the security of the CDE; it affects Marketa's knowledge of it. **That is a real dependency and it did not meet the 12.8.1 test**, so it is governed elsewhere rather than added to the register to look thorough. Diluting the register until nobody can see which six relationships matter is the failure 01.09 §1 warns about.

### 3.1 ADR-0023 — provider evidence is graded, and a grade is not a control

| Field | Position |
|---|---|
| **Decision** | **ADR-0023 — Every item of third-party assurance evidence carries an explicit grade, and a grade is a statement about the evidence, not about the control.** No provider assurance artefact may be recorded as satisfying a Marketa obligation without a named requirement, a named evidence item, and a grade |
| Grades | **E1** — Marketa's own direct observation or record. **E2** — a provider statement corroborated by an independent Marketa observation. **E3** — a provider statement or third-party report, uncorroborated |
| Context | Extends **ADR-0016**, which established the rule for physical security at Ashburn, to all six providers and all requirement areas. A SOC 2 Type II report, an AOC, a NAID certificate and a vendor attestation are four different kinds of evidence about four different things, and the folder they sit in makes them look identical |
| Rationale | The failure this prevents is not fraud. It is **a reviewer in 2029 who cannot tell which claims Marketa verified and which it accepted** |
| Consequence 1 | The §6 matrix carries an evidence grade per provider per control area, and **§6.3 reports the grade distribution rather than only the coverage** |
| Consequence 2 | **Where the grade would otherwise be E3 on a control Marketa depends on, Marketa installs its own mechanism** — the cage reader at Ashburn, the certificate-to-inventory reconciliation for Halberd, the quarterly handover test for Northbridge, the interface behaviour profile for Truvance |
| Rejected alternative | Recording assurance as met or not met. It is faster, it produces a green register, and it cannot answer the only question a reviewer asks: *how do you know?* |

### 4.2 The honest position on TPSP-06 and 12.8.3

**12.8.3 requires due diligence prior to engagement. For TPSP-06 that did not happen, because the provider was never classified as a TPSP.**

The programme states that rather than presenting the 2026-10-16 review as if it were pre-engagement diligence.

| Question | Position |
|---|---|
| Is 12.8.3 met? | **Yes** — 12.8.3 obliges an established process including pre-engagement diligence. The process exists, is documented, and was applied to every relationship classified as a TPSP. **No provider was engaged without diligence after the process was established** |
| Then what failed? | **The classification, not the process.** The process was never invoked because the relationship was never recognised as being in its scope. That is a 12.8.1 failure with a 12.8.3 shadow, and it is recorded as such |
| What was done for TPSP-06 | A **due-diligence-equivalent review** completed 2026-10-16 covering every stage of §4.1 except the pre-engagement sequencing, which cannot be recovered. The review found the provider's controls adequate and corroborated by Marketa's own 2026-04-22 site inspection |
| The durable change | **The trigger for the 12.8 process is now the classification decision, not the procurement event.** Any supplier newly classified as a TPSP — whether at engagement, at a service change, or at a register review — enters the process, and where diligence cannot precede engagement because the relationship pre-dates the classification, a **due-diligence-equivalent review is completed within 60 days** and the sequencing gap is recorded |
| Would Marketa argue this differently to an assessor? | **No.** The reasoning above is what was put to Sable Ridge, and it is in the ROC evidence set with the classification record attached |

## 5. TRA-12.8.4 — Monitoring Provider Compliance Status

12.8.4 obliges a programme to monitor TPSPs' PCI DSS compliance status **at least once every 12 months**. The requirement sets a floor and, unlike 11.6.1 or 10.4.2.1, **it does not reference 12.3.1** — it names a fixed minimum rather than an entity-defined frequency.

Marketa performed a targeted risk analysis anyway. It is **elective**, it is **#13 of the programme's fourteen**, and the reason for performing it is stated first because an elective analysis with no reason is decoration.

> **The question the analysis exists to answer: is annual monitoring sufficient for a merchant whose entire payment architecture rests on three providers' validation, when a validation can lapse, be withdrawn, or have its scope narrowed at any point in the eleven months between reviews?**

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Analysis ID | **TRA-12.8.4** |
| Requirement | 12.8.4 — monitoring of TPSP PCI DSS compliance status |
| Performed under | **12.3.1**, **electively** — 12.8.4 states a fixed 12-month minimum and does not delegate the frequency to the entity |
| Owner | Owen Castellanos, Payment Card Compliance Manager |
| Contributors | Elena Marchetti, Frank Mueller, Naomi Bhatt, Adaeze Nwosu, Bill Traynor, Marcus Hale |
| Approver | Naomi Bhatt, Chief Information Security Officer |
| Date performed | **2026-06-30** |
| Date approved | **2026-07-08** |

| **Determination** | **The 12-month review is retained in full for every provider and is not the whole programme.** Monitoring is **tiered by what the provider's validation holds up**: **MT-1** monthly status verification plus the annual review for the three providers whose validation supports a scope reduction; **MT-2** quarterly performance and matrix review plus the annual review for the provider delivering a live security function without PCI validation; **MT-3** annual review with event triggers and per-transaction evidence for the two providers whose service is bounded and evidenced by artefacts Marketa itself produces |

### 5.1 Element 1 — Assets being protected

| Asset | Description |
|---|---|
| **The three scope reductions** | 1,914 lanes and 482 store servers (Verition); 246 AWS e-commerce workloads and the whole e-commerce card path (Truvance); 310 agent desktops and the MOTO channel (Cadence). **Between them, the difference between a 71-component assessment and a 604-component one** |
| **The token vault** | Single, held by Truvance, across all three channels. Marketa holds tokens and **no detokenisation capability**, which is a control and a dependency in the same sentence |
| **68.4M transactions a year** | 50.6M card-present, 15.7M e-commerce, 2.1M MOTO. Every one passes through at least one of the three |
| **The 71-component assessed population's boundary** | Six assessed connected-to components sit in a building Marketa does not run |
| **The detection capability for two thirds of each day** | Northbridge covers 22:00 to 06:00 across the SIEM covering all 71 components |
| **The evidence chain for media destruction** | 9.4.6 and 9.4.7 rest on certificates Marketa reconciles against an inventory Marketa maintains |

### 5.2 Element 2 — Threats

| Threat | Realisation in Marketa's environment |
|---|---|
| **A validation lapses silently between annual reviews** | **The dominant threat, and it is R-10 at Moderate 12.** An AOC has an anniversary; a listing can be withdrawn at any time. A merchant reviewing annually can be relying on a lapsed basis for up to eleven months without knowing |
| **A validation's scope narrows without lapsing** | The subtler version, and the one that actually occurred — §5.7. A provider re-issues an AOC covering a different product boundary. **The status is still "validated"; the service Marketa consumes may no longer be inside it** |
| **A provider is compromised** | Truvance holds the token-to-PAN mapping for all three channels. **This is R-09 at High 15**, and no Marketa control reduces its impact component |
| **A provider changes a subservice organisation** | Hosting, connectivity or key management changes downstream, altering the environment the AOC described |
| **A responsibility gap opens at a service change** | Both parties assume the other operates a control. **R-11.** The gap does not announce itself; it is discovered during an incident |
| **The provider performs its service and Marketa's dependent obligation lapses** | The mirror threat, and the one merchants under-weight: Verition's solution stays listed and a store deploys a POI on the wrong VLAN. **The provider is compliant and the scope reduction is void** |
| **A provider's service degrades without failing** | Northbridge acknowledging alerts inside the service level and investigating them thinly. Validation status says nothing about this |

### 5.3 Element 3 — Factors contributing to likelihood and impact

| Factor | Direction | Weight | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Three providers hold up scope reductions covering 533 of the 604 originally in-scope systems** | **Increases impact** | **High** | 02.10. A lapse suspends a scope reduction; it does not delete it, and the affected environment becomes in-scope until validation is restored |
| **Truvance is gateway, hosted page and single token vault simultaneously** | **Increases impact** | **High** | **R-09 at High 15.** Concentration is a design decision made for good reasons and it is not diversifiable in the near term — §8 |
| Validation evidence for the three MT-1 providers is **independently publishable** — brand registries and the Council's P2PE list | **Reduces likelihood of undetected lapse** | **High** | The status can be verified without the provider's cooperation, at any frequency, at effectively zero cost. **This factor is what makes monthly verification affordable** |
| The provider population is **small and stable** — six providers, one addition in twelve months | Reduces likelihood | High | §4.3 |
| Contractual notification duties exist for validation change, compromise and material service change | Reduces likelihood | Moderate | **And they have failed once in the period** — the SCR-29 vendor change notification in 06.09 §9.1 arrived four days late. A contractual duty is a claim on a counterparty's diligence |
| **Two providers hold no PCI validation**, so there is no external status to monitor | Changes the instrument rather than the level | High | Their requirements are inside Marketa's ROC; the monitoring instrument is performance evidence and Marketa's own assessment, not a status check |
| AOC anniversaries are **distributed across the year** — March, May, September, February, June | Reduces the value of a single annual review date | Moderate | An annual programme anchored to one date leaves some providers reviewed 15 months after their last evidence |
| Marketa **cannot audit** a Level 1 provider | Bounds what any frequency can achieve | **High** | The analysis must not conclude that more frequent monitoring substitutes for assurance Marketa cannot obtain. **It buys currency, not depth** |
| The cost of a status check is minutes; the cost of an annual review is days | Enables tiering | High | The two activities are different in kind, and treating them as one forces a single cadence on both |

### 5.4 Element 4 — The resultant analysis and its justification

| Candidate | Argument for | Argument against | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Annual review only — the requirement's floor** | Compliant. Lowest cost. Defensible on the standard's plain words | Leaves up to eleven months in which a withdrawn listing, a lapsed AOC or a narrowed scope statement supports a scope reduction that no longer has a basis. **R-10 exists precisely because this window is real** | **Rejected as the whole programme; retained as its backbone** |
| **Quarterly full review of all six** | Uniform, simple to describe | A full review is a reading of the AOC and its scope statement, a reconciliation of the matrix, and a conversation with the relationship owner. Performed quarterly across six providers it becomes a form-filling exercise, and the annual review's depth is what actually finds things — §5.7 | **Rejected** |
| **Continuous automated status verification for all six** | Fastest detection of a status change | **Two providers have no external status to verify.** Applying a status-check instrument to a provider with no published status produces a green indicator that measures nothing | **Rejected as uniform; adopted for the three where it works** |
| **Tiered — MT-1, MT-2, MT-3 — with the annual review retained in full for all six** | Matches the instrument to what the provider's assurance actually consists of. Monthly verification where a published status exists and a scope reduction depends on it; quarterly performance review where the service is a live security function with no validation; annual plus event triggers where the evidence is produced per transaction by Marketa itself | Three tiers is more to operate and to explain than one cadence | **Adopted** |

**Determination**, in four parts:

| Tier | Providers | What happens, and how often |
|---|---|---|
| **MT-1** | **TPSP-01 Truvance · TPSP-02 Cadence · TPSP-03 Verition** | **Monthly** verification of published validation status against the authoritative source — the Visa Global Registry and Mastercard SDP list for the two AOC-holders, the Council's list of validated P2PE solutions for Verition — recorded with a dated capture. **Plus a 60-day pre-expiry watch** on every AOC and attestation date. **Plus** the full annual review on the provider's own anniversary, not on a Marketa calendar date |

| **All tiers** | All six | **Six event triggers** force an out-of-cycle review — §5.6. And the **annual 12.8.4 review is never skipped for any provider in any tier**, because the tiering adds to the floor and does not replace it |

| Field | Position |
|---|---|
| **Decision** | **ADR-0024 — TPSP monitoring is tiered by what the provider's validation holds up, above the 12-month floor, and the annual review is never displaced by the tier.** A provider whose published validation supports a Marketa scope reduction is verified monthly; a provider delivering a live security function with no validation is reviewed quarterly on performance; a provider whose service is evidenced per transaction by Marketa's own records is reviewed annually with event triggers |
| Why an ADR and not just an analysis outcome | 12.8.4 states a fixed minimum and delegates nothing. **An entity that does more than a requirement demands should record why, so that a successor does not quietly revert to the floor** on the reasonable ground that the floor is what the standard says |
| Rejected alternative | A single uniform cadence for all six. It either over-serves the two providers with no external status to check, or under-serves the three whose status can change on any day without notice |

The sentence that makes this a determination rather than a preference: **monitoring frequency buys currency, not depth.** A monthly registry check tells Marketa that a provider's status has not changed. It tells Marketa nothing new about the provider's environment. The annual review is where the AOC's scope statement is read, the matrix is reconciled and the relationship owner is questioned, and **the tiering exists so that the annual review can stay deep rather than becoming the only thing that happens.**

## 6. Written Agreements and Responsibility — 12.8.2 and 12.8.5

### 6.1 The 12.8.2 position

12.8.2 obliges written agreements **including the TPSP's acknowledgement that it is responsible for the security of account data it possesses or otherwise stores, processes or transmits on behalf of the entity, or to the extent that it could impact the security of the entity's CDE.**

The second limb of that acknowledgement is the one entities omit from their contract templates. **Northbridge and the Ashburn provider never touch account data.** A security schedule drafted only around possession would leave both without an acknowledgement, and 12.8.2 would be unmet for two of six providers.

## 7. The Provider Side — 12.9.2, and Why It Matters Here

**Requirement 12.9 is a service-provider requirement.** 12.9.1 obliges a provider to acknowledge in writing its responsibility for the security of account data it possesses or could affect; 12.9.2 obliges it to support a customer's request for information to meet 12.8.4 and 12.8.5. Neither binds Marketa. **Neither was ever within the 306**, and neither is among the ROC's four Not Applicable entries — the same determination 05.08 §6.2 records.

They matter to this document for one reason: **12.9.2 is the mechanism that makes 12.8.5 obtainable rather than aspirational.**

| Marketa's obligation | The provider-side duty it depends on | What Marketa does when the duty is a provider's rather than Marketa's |
|---|---|---|
| **12.8.4** — monitor compliance status annually | **12.9.2** — the provider supports the request for validation information | The right to request is written into the 12.8.2 agreement, so Marketa's entitlement is contractual and does not depend on the provider being subject to 12.9.2 at all |
| **12.8.5** — know which requirements each party manages | **12.9.2** — the provider supports the request for the responsibility matrix | **No matrix is treated as complete until the provider has signed it.** A matrix Marketa wrote and the provider never saw is a Marketa opinion |
| **12.8.2** — hold the acknowledgement | **12.9.1** — the provider makes it | Held on file for TPSP-01, TPSP-02 and TPSP-03. **TPSP-04, TPSP-05 and TPSP-06 are not PCI-validated service providers subject to 12.9**, so their acknowledgements are contractual undertakings in the security schedule rather than 12.9.1 acknowledgements, and the register says which is which |

The distinction in the last row is the kind of precision that costs nothing to record and is impossible to reconstruct later. **Three of Marketa's six providers give an acknowledgement because a requirement obliges them to; three give it because a contract does.** Both satisfy 12.8.2. Only one of them survives the provider deciding it no longer wishes to.

## 8. Where the Bill Arrives

Phase 02 reduced the assessed population from 604 systems to 71 — **a fall of 88.2%** — through P2PE, tokenization and DTMF masking. 02.10 recorded the reasoning and the alternatives considered. Phase 03 built the segmentation the reduction depends on. Phases 04 and 05 controlled what remained.

**None of that removed the risk. It moved the risk to a place where Marketa's instruments are contractual rather than technical.**

| What Marketa gave up | What Marketa got | Where it is governed now |
|---|---|---|
| The ability to apply its own controls to the PAN | 68.4M primary account numbers a year that never touch a Marketa system | **12.8.2, 12.8.4, 12.8.5** |
| The ability to test the environment holding the data | A 71-component assessment instead of a 604-component one | **The provider's AOC, and Marketa's reading of its scope statement** |
| Diversity of provider | A single token vault serving three channels, which is simpler, cheaper and materially more secure than three | **R-09 at High 15**, and no Marketa control reduces its impact |
| Direct control of the decryption environment | 1,914 lanes out of the CDE | **A Council listing and a PIM Marketa must adhere to** |
| Control of the recording path | 310 agent desktops out of the CDE | **A suppression point in a provider's platform, asserted daily** |

Four statements the programme makes without softening:

**Marketa cannot audit its largest provider.** Truvance is a Level 1 service provider validated by its own QSA. Marketa's rights are to an AOC, a scope statement, a responsibility matrix, notification duties and answers to questions. It has no right of audit, no right of inspection, and no realistic prospect of obtaining one. **That is the ordinary position of a merchant relative to a payment provider**, and it is stated because the alternative — implying that a merchant's diligence reaches inside a provider — is the most common overstatement in third-party assurance writing.

**Concentration is not a defect to be remediated. It is a design choice with a residual.** 03.11 rates R-09 at High 15 and 05.12 records that no Marketa control reduces it below Moderate. Diversifying the token vault would mean two vaults, two integrations, two key domains and two failure modes, and would raise the aggregate risk while lowering the concentration. **The register carries the concentration as a residual rather than pretending it can be engineered away.**

**Exit is possible and it is not fast.** Marketa holds tokens, not PANs, and cannot detokenise. Moving providers means a coordinated re-tokenisation exercise executed by the outgoing and incoming providers across a vault covering three channels. The exit position is documented, was tested for feasibility during the 2026 renewal, and is measured in quarters. **A dependency you cannot leave inside a quarter is a dependency, whatever the contract calls it.**

**And the reduction was still right.** The comparison is not "outsource or control it perfectly". It is "hold 68.4 million PANs across 482 stores staffed by a workforce that grows by 6,800 people every October, or hold none of them and govern six relationships properly". **The second is the better risk position by a wide margin, and this document is what the second one costs.**
