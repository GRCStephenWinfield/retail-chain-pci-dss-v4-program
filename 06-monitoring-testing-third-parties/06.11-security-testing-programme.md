## Purpose

Requirement 11 is delivered across six documents in this phase and two in Phase 03. **This document is the one that makes them a programme rather than eight activities.** It states what is tested, by whom, how often, on whose selection, and what the year produced when the results are put side by side.

It also does the thing a testing programme document usually does not, and §5 is the reason it exists: **it states what is not tested, and why.**

| Question | Where |
|---|---|
| What are the instruments, and which requirement does each discharge? | §1 |
| When does each run? | §2 |
| Who selects the sample, and how is independence preserved? | §3 |
| What did the whole year produce, in one reconciled position? | §4 |
| Which control found which finding? | §4.2 |
| **What is not tested?** | **§5** |

Nothing in this document re-delivers a requirement. Every position below is evidenced in the document named against it.

## 1. The Instruments

### 1.1 Why eight instruments and not three

| Question only that instrument answers | Instrument |
|---|---|
| *What does the internet see, judged against the ASV Program Guide's passing criteria?* | ASV scanning |
| *What is actually installed on a component, including what a service banner does not disclose?* | Authenticated internal scanning |
| *Can a lower-trust zone reach a governed zone in practice, as deployed, at a real store?* | Segmentation penetration testing |
| *Can a competent adversary chain findings into an outcome no single finding produces?* | Application penetration testing |
| *Is there a radio attached to Marketa's network that nobody authorised?* | Wireless detection |
| *Has a file on a component changed without an approved change?* | Change detection |
| *Is the page a customer receives the page Marketa authorised?* | **Payment-page tamper detection** |
| *Would any of this have fired?* | Controlled execution |

**No instrument answers another's question.** The clearest demonstration in the year is that a passing ASV scan, a clean authenticated scan, a clean file-integrity comparison and a passing segmentation test were all true of Marketa's payment pages on 2026-09-29 — and **none of them would have seen the vendor script change that 11.6.1 detected in eleven minutes.**

### 2.1 What the calendar is designed around

| Constraint | Effect on the calendar |
|---|---|
| **CON-01 — the October-to-January seasonal freeze** | Store-side **change** stops. **Testing does not.** Q4 scanning, the Q4 wireless cycle and the Q4 ASV scan all run inside it. What the freeze does is convert a remediation into a signed exception — one deferral in Q4, recorded as **VE-19** |
| **QSA fieldwork 2026-11-02 → 11-20** | Every annual instrument must have completed and been remediated before it. **The application test is in September so that its re-test closes in October**, not because September is convenient |
| Segmentation must precede application testing | The application test's scope depends on the segmentation result. May and June before September — 06.08 §1 |
| Q4 activity concentrates in the highest-risk trading period | Deliberate. **A quarterly cycle that skips the quarter with the most footfall, the most temporary colleagues and the most contractor activity would be skipping the quarter that matters** |
| Provider evidence anniversaries are distributed | 12.8.4 monitoring is anchored to each provider's own anniversary rather than to a Marketa calendar date — 06.10 §5.4 |

## 3. Independence and Selection

11.3.1, 11.3.2.1, 11.4.2, 11.4.3 and 11.4.5 all require **organisational independence**, and none of them requires an external party. The requirement is routinely misread in both directions — entities that engage a QSA for work no QSA is needed for, and entities that treat "our own team did it" as automatically independent.

| Instrument | Where independence comes from | What would break it |
|---|---|---|
| ASV scanning | External, PCI SSC-approved, **and structurally**: the Attestation of Scan Compliance is issued to Cardinal independently of Marketa. **Marketa cannot choose which results are transmitted** | Marketa standing between the ASV and the acquirer |
| Internal scanning | **The scanning team does not administer the scanned systems.** Marcus Hale's team scans; Trevor Kim's team operates. Neither can suppress the other's output. Scan configurations, target lists and exclusion sets are version-controlled and reviewed quarterly by a third party — Owen Castellanos | The scanning team gaining administrative responsibility for the estate, or an exclusion being added as a scanner setting rather than as a change with an approver |
| Penetration testing | External, independent of the design and build teams, **and independent of the QSA**. Sable Ridge neither selects nor reviews Ironwood's work | The same firm designing the segmentation and testing it |
| Wireless detection | Network engineering runs the technical limb; **store operations runs the physical limb inside the 9.5.1 round**, so the team that owns the network is not the only team looking at it | Both limbs owned by one function |
| Change detection | Baselines approved by platform owners; **exclusions named with an owner**, 43 of them, reviewed quarterly; a weekly canary change validates the mechanism from outside itself | Exclusions added silently by the monitored team |
| **11.6.1** | Measurement from outside Marketa's networks on infrastructure Marketa does not run — **ADR-0021**; and the detector proved quarterly by a test **designed by security operations and not disclosed to e-commerce engineering in advance** — ADR-0022 | Monitoring the page from inside the estate |
| Adjudication | Internal Audit re-performs 40 cases a quarter independently. **10 disagreements in 160** | A re-performance that never disagrees |

### 3.1 Who selects the sample — ADR-0009

Every instrument that samples has to answer one question: **who chose what got tested?** An entity that selects its own sample is testing its own opinion of its estate.

> **Marketa proposes strata. The tester selects within them. At least one stratum targets known messiness.**

That is **ADR-0009**, taken in April 2026 before the segmentation test and applied across the programme since. Its origin is the sentence the Ironwood lead said at the readout:

> "If you had given us the standard build and a list of well-maintained sites, we would have passed you, and the fault would still be sitting at thirty-seven stores."

| Instrument | Population | Sampling rule in practice |
|---|---|---|
| Segmentation test, May | 482 stores | **14 stores, 2.9%**, selected by Ironwood from Marketa-proposed strata including a **recent-local-network-change stratum**. **SEG-PT-01 was found in that stratum** |
| Segmentation re-test, June | 482 stores | **41 stores, 8.5%** — all 37 latent sites plus **4 selected by Ironwood with no Marketa input** |
| Confirmatory limb, September | 482 stores | **11 stores**, Ironwood-selected from strata, including stores with a local network change since June |
| Application test | 9 applications, 1,204 request parameters, 6 templates | **No sampling.** Full enumeration of the authorisation matrix and the parameter set |
| Internal scanning | 71 components | **No sampling. 71 of 71 every quarter**, four quarters — and the exclusions are four named plugin-level exclusions, not host exclusions |
| ASV scanning | 57–58 addresses, 31–32 hostnames | No sampling; scope reconciled from four independent sources immediately before each scan |
| Wireless detection | 488 facilities | **No sampling. 488 of 488 every quarter**, 1,952 facility-scans in the year |
| 9.5.1 device inspection | 1,914 terminals | No sampling; 7,622 inspections at 99.56% coverage — 05.09 |
| **11.6.1** | 6 templates | **No sampling on the synthetic limb** — all 192 permutations every cycle. **1 in 20 on the field limb**, raised to 1 in 10 for mobile profiles after PoL-2 |

Six of the nine instruments do not sample at all, and that is a property of the estate rather than a virtue: **71 components, 488 facilities, 6 templates and 9 applications are small enough to enumerate.** The one population too large to enumerate is the 482 stores, and it is the population where both the sampling rule and the programme's only failed test live.

### 4.2 What found what

The most useful reading of the year is not the finding count. It is which instrument found which class of thing, because that is what tells an entity where its blind spots are.

| Class of finding | Found by | Would any other instrument have found it? |
|---|---|---|
| A deployed configuration diverging from a correct design across an estate | **Segmentation penetration testing**, in a deliberately messy stratum | The daily configuration assertion **now** would; it did not exist before the test that produced it |
| An application surface with no authentication | **Application penetration testing** | **No.** Four controls saw the change and none asked the question — 06.08 §5.6 |
| An internet-facing host nobody had scoped | **ASV scanning** | The four-source scope reconciliation would have, at the next quarterly cycle, which was scheduled after the scan |
| A missing library patch inside a host | **Authenticated internal scanning** | Nothing else. An unauthenticated scan of a fully patched host and one missing eleven updates can produce identical output |
| A consumer access point plugged into a store network | **Wireless detection**, wired-side correlated | The daily port-state assertion would flag the port; not the radio |
| An engineer changing a configuration outside change control | **Change detection**, 11.5.2 | The 10.4.1 daily review, from the log side, for some classes |
| **A third-party script changing after Marketa authorised it** | **11.6.1, and only 11.6.1** | **No. None of the other seven instruments can see it** |
| A detection rule that would not have fired | **Controlled execution** | Nothing. This is the only instrument that measures the absence of detection |
| An adjudication closed on a correlating record that did not cover the action | **Internal Audit re-performance** | Nothing. Automation cannot audit its own dispositions |

**Two rows in that table have "no" in the third column, and they are the two newest instruments in the programme.**

## 5. What Is Not Tested

A testing programme described only by what it covers invites the reader to assume the complement is empty. It is not. Every entry below is a real limit on what the year's results can support, with what compensates for it and what would be required to close it.

### 5.1 ADR-0025 — publish the untested surface

| Field | Position |
|---|---|
| **Decision** | **ADR-0025 — The testing programme publishes what it does not test, with the reason, the compensating control and the condition that would close it.** The untested surface is a standing section of this document and is reported to the Steering Committee annually alongside the coverage figures |
| Context | Every testing report in the programme carries a limitations section. **Nothing collected them**, so a reader could see each instrument's limits and never the shape of the whole gap |
| Rationale | **Coverage figures are read as completeness.** "488 of 488 facilities, 71 of 71 components, 19 of 19 findings remediated" is an accurate description of what was tested and an invitation to assume the complement is empty. Thirteen rows say it is not |
| Consequence | Three of the thirteen rows produced actions: the phishing and pretext programme proposed for 2027; **CAP-06** as the only route to closing the appliance row; and the acceptance of the physical row recorded against R-38 rather than left unstated |
| Rejected alternative | Keeping the limitations distributed across eight documents. It is technically complete and practically invisible |
