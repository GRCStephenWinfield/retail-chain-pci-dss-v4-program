## What this phase is

PCI Requirements **10, 11 and 12.8/12.9** — logging, monitoring, testing, and the third parties who now operate most of the controls Marketa gave away in Phase 02. It contains the payoff the portfolio has been building toward since Phase 02, the second of the two Critical penetration test findings, the second of the two **Not in Place** findings, and the moment the bill for scope reduction arrives.

## The payoff: R-01 finally moves

Phase 04 implemented **6.4.3** in full — inventoried all 38 payment-page scripts, removed three nobody had authorised, and closed the tag-manager publication path entirely. And it **held R-01 at High 20 anyway**. Four separate documents said why: 6.4.3 is preventive, and a preventive control alone leaves open the path where an authorised third-party script is modified at its origin *after* deployment.

**11.6.1 closes that path.** Synthetic browser agents fetch the six payment templates **from outside Marketa's network**, across 192 permutations of geography, device class and network path, and compare the received HTTP headers, DOM and script content against a baseline reconciled monthly to the 6.4.3 inventory. Alerting inside **24 hours** against a requirement floor of weekly, under **TRA-11.6.1**.

| | **6.4.3** — preventive | **11.6.1** — detective |
|---|---|---|
| Delivered | Phase 04 | **This phase** |
| Does | Authorise each script, bind integrity at deployment, inventory with justification | Continuously evaluate what the customer actually received |
| **Cannot** | Detect modification at a third-party origin after deployment | **Prevent** anything |

**Together the control is complete, and R-01 moves from High 20 to Moderate 10** — likelihood 4 → 2, **impact held at 5**. A skimming script on a payment page is a card-data compromise whatever else is true; only the likelihood moved.

The proof that both halves were needed is concrete rather than argued: a vendor released a new version of an authorised script **before** the contractual notification arrived. **6.4.3 alone would have permitted it** — the script was authorised and its integrity method was a contract. 11.6.1 saw the change.

## The design decision behind 11.6.1

The cheaper implementation compares the deployed bundle against source control from inside the estate. Marketa rejected it (**ADR-0021**):

> "That tells you what we published. It does not tell you what the customer received. Those are the same thing right up until the moment they are not, and the moment they are not is the only moment this control exists for."

Everything that makes digital skimming hard to catch lives in that gap: a compromised CDN edge, a third-party origin serving different content by geography, an injection conditioned on device class. The requirement's own wording — *as received by the consumer browser* — points straight at it.

## The second Critical

The **September application penetration test** produced **11 findings — 1 Critical, 3 High, 4 Medium, 3 Low**. The Critical was an **unauthenticated administrative interface on the chargeback application**, a CDE component. It had never had an authentication check applied to it, which is worse than one that failed: nothing broke, because nothing had ever been there.

Marketa then did something it did not have to. Rather than fix, retest and record "no evidence of unauthorised access", it performed **forensic enumeration of prior access** across the exposure window (**DEC-608**, 06.08 §6):

> "'No evidence of access' obtained by not looking is not a finding. It is a sentence."

| Engagement | Findings | Critical | High | Medium | Low |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Segmentation test — Phase 03, May | 8 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 2 |
| Application test — September | 11 | 1 | 3 | 4 | 3 |
| **Programme total** | **19** | **2** | **5** | **7** | **5** |

**All 19 remediated and independently retested.** None closed on Marketa's assertion. Both Criticals were found by testers, not by Marketa — and the portfolio writes it that way.

## The second Not in Place

**11.3.1.2** obliges internal vulnerability scans to be performed via **authenticated** scanning. Marketa achieved it on **62 of 71** components. The remaining **nine** are the vendor-locked store back-office appliances that cannot accept credentialed assessment.

The requirement offers a route for systems unable to accept credentials. Marketa argued it and **the QSA rejected the argument**, on a distinction worth recording: *a system technically unable to accept credentials* is not the same as *an entity contractually unable to obtain them*. **No compensating control was argued** — the same discipline the portfolio applied to 8.6.2 in Phase 05.

Recorded as **Not in Place, dated 2027-01-31**, becoming **CAP-06**. **R-36 held at High 16.**

Both Not in Place findings — 8.6.2 from Phase 05 and 11.3.1.2 from this phase — share a remediation date and a re-assessment window **with no float**. That is stated rather than disguised.

## The quarter that failed

Four quarterly **ASV** scans. **Q1 failed on three findings and was rescanned clean in 11 days.** Q2, Q3 and Q4 passed first time.

The failure is the most useful evidence in the phase. Sable Ridge is both QSA and ASV under the documented independence position in **ADR-0003**, and the obvious objection to a same-firm arrangement is that scan findings get softened to help the ROC. **A failure reported to the acquirer as a failure** — rather than quietly rescanned and reported as a pass — is the structural answer to that objection.

Root cause: a marketing subdomain repointed to a stale-image edge host with no scope-delta review. It fed directly into the significant-change definition from Phase 04.

## Where the bill for scope reduction arrives

Phase 02 cut scope by **88.2%** and said plainly that **scope reduction is not risk reduction** — the data still exists, it is just held by someone else. This is where that caveat becomes a workload.

**Six providers**, up from five after Phase 05 added the Ashburn co-location as TPSP-06. Three of them — Truvance, Cadence and Verition — sit between Marketa and its ability to accept payment **at all**. The register carries that concentration as a **structural residual that does not reduce**; it is the shape of the architecture, not a gap in it.

The annual review found a **Truvance AOC scope statement that did not clearly cover a service Marketa relies on**. Clarified and re-issued — and the lesson recorded: *an AOC is evidence about the services it names, and only those.*

What cannot be delegated is responsibility. A provider can operate a control; it cannot hold Marketa's obligation, and the AOC is still signed by Marketa's CFO.

## The honest position

Nineteen findings, nineteen fixes, nineteen retests. As the CISO recorded at the readout:

> "That tells us we can respond. It does not tell us what the twentieth would have been. That is what next year's test is for."

Two risks remain High, both held by Not in Place findings with the same date and no float before re-assessment. The detective controls that now carry much of the programme are weeks old, and **ADR-0022** exists because the way they fail is quietly: a quarter with zero alerts is treated as a defect to investigate, not as a success.
