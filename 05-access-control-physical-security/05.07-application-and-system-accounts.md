## Purpose

Requirement **8.6** is entirely future-dated. All three sub-requirements were best practice until 31 March 2025 and are mandatory for the first time in Marketa's 2026 assessment.

| Sub-requirement | Obligation |
|---|---|
| **8.6.1** | Where accounts used by systems or applications **can be used for interactive login**, interactive use is prevented unless needed for an exceptional circumstance, limited to the time needed, justified in writing, explicitly approved by management, with individual identity confirmed before access and **every action attributable to an individual** |
| **8.6.2** | Passwords or passphrases for any application and system account that can be used for interactive login are **not hard-coded in scripts, configuration files or bespoke and custom source code** |
| **8.6.3** | Passwords or passphrases for application and system accounts are changed **periodically at a frequency defined by a targeted risk analysis** under 12.3.1, and on suspicion or confirmation of compromise, and are **constructed with complexity appropriate to that frequency** |

Together with **7.2.5** — application and system accounts assigned and managed at least privilege — this document governs the **142 non-human accounts** operating across the 71 assessed system components. It also delivers **TRA-8.6.3**, the second of the two targeted risk analyses arising in the Requirement 7 and 8 workstream.

This is where **R-17** is treated: *application and system accounts hold embedded, non-rotatable credentials in legacy batch integrations, with interactive use possible and no individual attribution* — **High 16**, one of the nine High risks in the register.

And it is where one of the programme's **two Not in Place findings** originates.

> **8.6.2 is assessed Not in Place. Two legacy batch integrations still authenticate with credentials embedded in a script and a configuration file. Remediation is dated 2027-01-31.**

§5 states it in full, without softening, per **ADR-0004** — the programme permits no "Not Tested" finding and no gap dressed as something it is not.

## 1. Why This Population Is Different

Every control in 05.04 and 05.05 rests on a person: a joiner record, a manager, an MFA factor, a review packet with a name on it. **None of that exists for an application account.** There is no HR record to trigger a leaver event, no human to present a second factor, no manager to attest at a review, and — critically — nothing that fails visibly when the account is over-privileged, because the integration keeps working.

| Property | User accounts | Application and system accounts |
|---|---|---|
| Lifecycle trigger | HR events | **The decommissioning of an integration nobody records as an event** |
| Second factor | MFA on all 20 paths | **Not possible.** A batch job cannot tap a security key |
| Accountability | The named human | **Only by attribution to the owning integration and its named human owner** |
| Failure signal when over-privileged | A person notices they can see something odd | **None. The integration works** |
| Failure signal when under-privileged | Immediate and loud | Immediate and loud — which is why the grant is always generous at build time |
| Review | Six-monthly, by a manager who knows the person | Six-monthly under **TRA-7.2.5.1**, by an owner who may never have met the integration |

The last row is the crux. **A service account is granted whatever makes the integration work on the day it is built, and nothing subsequently removes what turned out to be unnecessary.** That is privilege accretion, it is silent, and it is why 8.6 exists.

## 2. Least Privilege for Non-Human Accounts — 7.2.5

7.2.5 obliges application and system accounts and related access privileges to be assigned and managed based on **the least privileges necessary**, with access limited to the systems, applications and processes **specifically required**.

| Rule | Implementation | Verified |
|---|---|---|
| One account, one purpose | An account serves one integration. A second integration needing similar access receives its own account, even where the entitlements are identical | Asserted at each 7.2.5.1 review; **6 accounts disabled** in the first cycle as duplicates or obsolete |
| Named human owner | Every account has a **named individual** owner, not a team. An account without an owner has no reviewer | **9 accounts** re-assigned to a named owner at the first cycle |
| Entitlement scoped to the specific process | Not "read the database" but the specific objects, the specific operations, and where the platform supports it the specific source host and time window | **17 entitlement sets narrowed** at the first cycle |
| Source constraint | Where the platform supports it, the account authenticates only from the host or role that runs the integration | 118 of 142 |
| Time constraint | Where the integration runs on a schedule, authentication outside the window **alerts** | 61 of 142 — the scheduled population |
| No account holds an entitlement it has not exercised in 180 days without re-justification | Exercised-privilege reporting from CT-18 | First report produced 2026-07-08; **22 unexercised entitlements** identified for the next cycle |

The exercised-privilege report is the control that will do the most work over time and has the least evidence behind it today, because it has run once. It answers the question the review cannot: not "should this account have this right" — to which the owner will usually say yes — but "has this account ever used this right", to which the logs give an answer the owner cannot argue with.

Ninety-three vault-brokered credentials is the number that makes 8.6.2 tractable. **A credential that is retrieved at run time from a vault, over an authenticated and attested channel, is not present in any script, any configuration file or any repository** — which is what 8.6.2 asks for. Thirty-one cloud workload identities go further and are the strongest row in the table: there is no credential to embed, hard-code, leak, rotate or audit, because short-lived credentials are minted by the platform for the workload's identity. **The most effective control against hard-coded credentials is an architecture in which no long-lived credential exists.**

Two do not fit either pattern, and they are §5.

## 4. Interactive Use — 8.6.1

8.6.1 applies only where an application or system account **can be used for interactive login**. Where interactive use is impossible, the requirement's conditions do not bite — but "impossible" has to be established rather than assumed.

| Control | Implementation | Population |
|---|---|---|
| Interactive use **prevented** | Deny-interactive-logon policy at the platform, no assigned shell on Unix components, no console rights, and where the platform supports neither, network-layer denial of the interactive service from every source | **136 of 142** |
| Verified rather than assumed | Quarterly test: attempt interactive login with each account's credential, from an authorised administrative position, and confirm refusal | 4 cycles; **136 of 136 refused each cycle** |
| **Documented exceptions** | 6 accounts where interactive use is genuinely needed for an exceptional circumstance | **6 of 142** — below |

| Exception | Account | Why interactive use is necessary | Controls |
|---|---|---|---|
| **INT-01** | CT-38 hypervisor vendor maintenance account | The vendor's cluster maintenance procedure requires a shell on the host during a firmware operation | Disabled by default; activated only in an approved window under 8.2.7; CT-11 brokered and recorded; **individual identity of the human confirmed before the credential is released**, and the session attributed to that person |
| **INT-02** | CT-39 storage array maintenance account | As INT-01 | As INT-01 |
| **INT-03** | CT-31 scanning credential validation | Authenticated scanning credentials must be validated interactively against a target before a scan cycle, or a failed credential silently produces an unauthenticated scan reported as authenticated | As INT-01. **This exception exists because of the 11.3.1.2 finding** — a scan that silently degrades to unauthenticated is worse than no scan |
| **INT-04** | CDE-05 managed-file-transfer recovery account | Used interactively during failure recovery of a settlement transfer, where the automated path is the thing that has failed | As INT-01, with Elena Marchetti notified on every activation |
| **INT-05** | **CDE-05 `batch-icq` interchange qualification account** | The vendor's batch wrapper requires the account to hold an interactive shell. Marketa cannot remove it without breaking the package | As INT-01, **plus** the measures in §5.3. **This is one of the two 8.6.2 accounts** |
| **INT-06** | **CDE-04 `gl-extract` general ledger extract account** | The ERP interface library initialises an interactive session | As INT-01, **plus** the measures in §5.3. **This is the other** |

Activations in the period: **INT-01** 2, **INT-02** 1, **INT-03** 12 (once per scan cycle per scanner), **INT-04** 0, **INT-05** 3, **INT-06** 1. **Nineteen activations, nineteen recorded sessions, nineteen named humans.** Zero interactive authentications occurred outside an approved activation window across all six accounts.

Individual attributability is the condition entities most often fail here, and it is worth stating precisely how Marketa satisfies it. The account is shared in the sense that more than one person may at different times cause it to be used. **It is never shared in the sense that matters**, because the credential is never held by a person: it is released by CT-11 to an authenticated, MFA-verified individual, for a bounded window, and the whole session is recorded. Every action taken by the account during that window is attributable to exactly one named human, which is what 8.6.1 requires.

### 5.2 The two integrations

| | **`batch-icq`** | **`gl-extract`** |
|---|---|---|
| What it does | Nightly interchange qualification batch — recalculates interchange categorisation against Cardinal's returned settlement records | Nightly general ledger extract from settlement reconciliation to the finance ERP |
| Component | **CDE-05**, Payment Integration Gateway | **CDE-04**, Settlement Reconciliation Engine |

### 5.3 What was done in the meantime, and what it does not achieve

| Interim measure | Effect |
|---|---|
| Both files held in a **restricted repository** with read access limited to 3 named individuals, every read logged | Removes the source-repository exposure, which is usually the larger of the two |
| **File integrity monitoring** under 11.5.2 on both files, alerting on any change and on any read by an unexpected principal | Detects tampering and unexpected access |
| Account authentication restricted to the **single source host** running the batch | An attacker holding the credential cannot use it from anywhere else |
| **Out-of-window authentication alerts** — the batches run 02:10 to 02:40 and 03:15 to 03:50; any authentication outside those windows is a **P2** | Detects use of the credential for anything other than its purpose |
| Interactive use governed as **INT-05** and **INT-06** — disabled by default, activated only in an approved window, CT-11 brokered and recorded | Every interactive session is attributable to a named human |
| **Monthly credential rotation** under change control, with a two-person change | Bounds the age of a credential that may have been exposed |

Results in the period: **0 unauthorised file reads, 0 out-of-window authentications, 4 file-integrity alerts** — all four from the approved monthly rotations.

**None of this makes 8.6.2 met, and the programme says so in those words.**

Two arguments were made internally for a softer treatment, and both were refused.

**"Rotate it monthly and it is effectively controlled."** 8.6.2 does not prohibit stale credentials; it prohibits **hard-coded** ones. A credential rotated monthly and written back into the same shell script is a hard-coded credential with a fresh value. Monthly rotation is a genuine risk reduction and it is not compliance with the requirement.

**"Write a Compensating Controls Worksheet."** A compensating control must meet the **intent and rigour** of the original requirement. The intent of 8.6.2 is that the secret is **not present in a readable file**. No arrangement of permissions, monitoring, alerting or rotation makes a secret absent from a file it is in. Marketa could have produced a worksheet; a competent QSA would have rejected it, and a QSA who accepted it would have been wrong. **The programme declined to spend the assessment's credibility on the one control where the gap is unambiguous.**

So it is recorded as **Not in Place**, with a named owner, a dated plan, and a re-assessment on **2027-02-18**.

### 5.5 The preventive control, so it cannot recur

Interim measures address two known accounts. The control that matters is the one preventing a third.

| Control | Implementation | Coverage at 2026-07-15 |
|---|---|---|
| **Secret scanning in the pipeline** | Every commit to any repository building or configuring an in-scope component is scanned for credential patterns; a match **fails the build** | All 14 in-scope repositories; **7 blocks** in the period, all developer test values |
| Pre-commit hooks | Local scanning before the commit reaches the server | Deployed to all in-scope development environments |
| **Secret scanning at rest** | Weekly scan across configuration management content, deployment artefacts and the file systems of all 71 components, using the same pattern set as the PAN discovery tooling in 02.02 | 71 of 71; **the two known accounts are the only findings**, and they appear in every scan by design as a standing reminder |
| Architectural default | New integrations use a cloud workload identity where the platform permits, and CT-14 retrieval where it does not. **Static credentials in a new integration require an architecture exception approved by Naomi Bhatt** | Zero exceptions requested since April 2026 |

The at-rest scan deliberately continues to report the two known accounts rather than suppressing them with an exclusion. An exclusion is how a known finding becomes an unknown one.

## 6. TRA-8.6.3 — Frequency and Complexity of Password Changes for Application and System Accounts

**8.6.3** requires passwords for application and system accounts to be changed periodically **at a frequency defined in the entity's targeted risk analysis performed according to 12.3.1**, and upon suspicion or confirmation of compromise, and to be **constructed with sufficient complexity appropriate for how frequently the entity changes them**.

The two parameters are coupled by design: **a credential changed rarely must be stronger than one changed often.** An analysis that sets a frequency without setting a matching complexity has answered half the requirement.

### 6.1 Element 1 — Assets being protected

The 8 CDE components and the account data processed and transmitted through them; the 63 connected-to and security-impacting components; the controls those components operate — segmentation at CT-43 to CT-51, key material at CT-14 to CT-17, detection at CT-18 to CT-22, recoverability at CT-34 to CT-37; and the **142 accounts** themselves, which are simultaneously an asset and a control surface.

### 6.2 Element 2 — Threats the requirement protects against

| Threat | Realisation |
|---|---|
| **Credential disclosure through a file, log, backup or repository** | The realised threat. **Two accounts are in this state now** and are the 8.6.2 finding |
| **Credential disclosure through a departing human** | An engineer who saw a credential during a build in 2019 still knows it if it has never changed |
| **Undetected long-lived compromise** | A credential obtained once and used quietly for years; the defining property of a non-rotating machine credential |
| **Offline derivation** | A hash obtained from a backup, a memory dump or a compromised component, attacked offline where lockout does not apply |
| **Blast radius on shared use** | A credential reused across integrations converts one exposure into several — prevented architecturally by the one-account-one-purpose rule in §2, and re-verified at each review |

### 6.3 Element 3 — Factors contributing to likelihood and impact

| Factor | Direction | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| **31 of 142** hold no static credential at all | **Strongly reduces** likelihood and impact | Cloud workload identities. Nothing to steal, nothing to rotate |
| **93 of 142** are vault-brokered and **never visible to a human** | Reduces | Retrieved at run time over an authenticated channel; not present in any file or repository |
| **2 of 142** are embedded in a script and a configuration file | **Strongly increases** | The 8.6.2 finding; **R-17** at High 16 |
| Rotation of a machine credential is **automatable and invisible to users** | Reduces the cost of frequency | The pathologies in 05.06 §4 — predictable increments, written credentials, help-desk load — **do not apply to machines**. This is the decisive asymmetry between 8.3.9 and 8.6.3 |
| Rotation of a machine credential **can break a production integration** | Increases the cost of frequency | The genuine constraint. A failed rotation on a settlement path at 02:10 is a payment incident |
| **7 of 142** run on platforms with a password length maximum below 32 characters | Increases | Appliance and legacy platforms; complexity is capped, so frequency must compensate |
| Detection coverage — out-of-window and out-of-source alerting on 61 scheduled accounts | Reduces impact | Operating; extended to all 142 where the platform permits |

### 6.4 Element 4 — The determination and its justification

Because rotation of a machine credential imposes no human cost, the constraint is **operational risk**, not usability. The determination is therefore made per class, trading frequency against complexity and against the fragility of the integration.

| Class | Accounts | **Change frequency** | **Complexity** | Justification |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **ASA-03** — cloud workload identities | 31 | **Continuous** — credentials minted per session, maximum lifetime **1 hour** | Cryptographic; not a password | No static credential exists. The strongest position available and the architectural default for anything new |
| **ASA-04** — certificate and mTLS credentials | 8 of 14 | Per the **cryptoperiod** in 04.03 §5 | Key strength per 04.03 | Governed by key management, not by 8.6.3's password provisions |
| **ASA-05** — assessment credentials | 6 | **30 days**, automated | **40 characters**, full character set, machine-generated | The highest-privilege credentials in the estate. A scanning credential is an authenticated foothold on everything it scans, and the shortest interval is warranted |
| **ASA-01, ASA-02, ASA-06** — service, database, backup and agent accounts | 78 | **90 days**, automated through CT-14 with an automatic rollback on integration failure | **32 characters**, full character set, machine-generated | Vault-brokered and never human-visible; rotation is a non-event. 90 days bounds the value of a credential exposed through a backup or memory artefact |
| **ASA-04** — API secrets | 6 of 14 | **180 days**, or the provider's shortest supported interval where longer | **40 characters** or the provider's maximum | Constrained by provider rotation windows and coordinated at the quarterly TPSP review. Complexity compensates for the longer interval |
| **ASA-07** — appliance and vendor-embedded accounts | 9 | **180 days**, manual under two-person change control | **20 characters** — the platform maximum on 7 of the 9 | The clearest instance of the frequency–complexity trade: where complexity is capped by the platform, the interval cannot also be relaxed. 180 days is set by the vendor maintenance window, not by preference |
| **ASA-08** — batch and file-transfer accounts, vault-brokered | 2 | **90 days**, automated | **32 characters** | As ASA-01 |
| **ASA-08** — the **2 embedded credentials** | **2** | **30 days**, manual under two-person change control | **32 characters** | **An interim risk-reduction measure, not compliance with 8.6.2.** The shortest interval the change process supports on a settlement-path component. §5.3 |
| **All classes** | 142 | **Immediately on suspicion or confirmation of compromise**, on the departure or role change of any human who has had sight of the credential, and on completion of any third-party engagement touching it | — | The event-driven half of 8.6.3, and the half that matters most |

**Automated rotation with rollback** is what makes 90 days safe on a settlement path. The vault performs the change, verifies the integration authenticates with the new value, and restores the previous value if verification fails — so a failed rotation is a retry rather than an outage. Rotations executed in the period: **214**, of which **3 rolled back** — two an integration with a cached credential, one a platform that required a service restart. All three succeeded on retry after the underlying cause was corrected.

Reviewed at least once every 12 months, next due **2027-05-13**, by Trevor Kim with Marcus Hale, approved by Naomi Bhatt. The review tests whether the class populations have moved, whether more accounts can be migrated to workload identity or certificate authentication, and whether the platform maxima constraining ASA-07 have been lifted by a firmware release.

### 6.6 Element 6 — Triggers for an updated analysis before the annual date

| Trigger | Effect |
|---|---|
| **The 8.6.2 remediation completes on 2027-01-31, or slips** | Re-analyse. The highest-impact factor in §6.3 changes state either way, and the 30-day interim interval on those two accounts ceases to be needed or ceases to be sufficient |
