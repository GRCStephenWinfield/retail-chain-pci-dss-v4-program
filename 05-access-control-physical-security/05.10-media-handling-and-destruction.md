## Purpose

Requirement **9.4** governs media: securing it, classifying it, moving it, counting it and destroying it. It is the part of Requirement 9 that most entities treat as a formality, and it is the part that produced Marketa's second-largest unexpected PAN finding.

| Sub-requirement | The obligation, stated plainly | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **9.4.1** | All media with cardholder data is **physically secured** | §3 |
| **9.4.1.1** | **Offline media backups** with cardholder data are stored in a **secure location** | §3.2 |
| **9.4.1.2** | The security of the location holding offline media backups is **reviewed at least once every 12 months** | §3.3 |
| **9.4.2** | All media with cardholder data is **classified** in accordance with the sensitivity of the data | §4 |
| **9.4.3** | Media with cardholder data **sent outside the facility** is secured, and sent by **secured courier or other delivery method that can be accurately tracked** | §5 |
| **9.4.4** | Media with cardholder data sent outside the facility is **approved by management** before being moved | §5.2 |
| **9.4.5** | **Inventory logs** of all electronic media with cardholder data are maintained | §6 |
| **9.4.5.1** | Inventories of electronic media with cardholder data are **conducted at least once every 12 months** | §6.2 |
| **9.4.6** | **Hard-copy materials** with cardholder data are **destroyed when no longer needed** for business or legal reasons — cross-cut shredded, incinerated or pulped so the data cannot be reconstructed, and secured in containers until destruction | §7 |
| **9.4.7** | **Electronic media** with cardholder data is **destroyed when no longer needed** — rendered unrecoverable by destruction or secure wiping | §8 |

The honest position is set out at the front rather than at the back, because it frames everything below.

> **Marketa's media population is small precisely because Marketa does not store account data.** P2PE removes account data from the store estate, tokenisation removes it from the enterprise estate, and DTMF masking removes it from the contact centre. What remains is a short list, and a short list is easy to control.
>
> **It is also easy to stop counting.** Finding **PAN-02** — 2,187 scanned dispute documents on a Columbus file share, with 33 copies in the Ashburn backup estate — was created by a legitimate business process, held for years, and appeared in no media inventory because nobody classified a file share as media and nobody classified dispute paperwork as containing cardholder data. **This requirement family exists to stop that recurring**, and §7.2 is what happened to the paper it came from.

## 1. What Counts as Media Here

9.4 applies to media **with cardholder data**. Determining what falls inside that phrase is the first control, not a preliminary to it, because everything downstream — securing, classifying, tracking, inventorying, destroying — applies to the population this determination produces.

| Population | Media with cardholder data? | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **Offline backup volumes of CDE-02 and CDE-08** | **Yes** | They hold the truncated-PAN and token estate and, historically, restorable copies of it. Treated as containing cardholder data whether or not a given volume does |
| **Decommissioned drives, flash modules and SSDs** from any assessed component | **Yes, by default** | A drive from a CDE or connected-to component is treated as holding account data unless it is proved otherwise, and proving otherwise costs more than destroying it |
| **Decommissioned POI devices** | **Yes, by default** | They hold key material and may hold cached transaction artefacts. Returned or destroyed per the PIM — 02.04, PIM-09 |
| **The legacy hard-copy dispute file** | **Yes** | The paper originals behind PAN-02. §7.2 |
| **SIEM archive media at CT-19** | **Treated as yes, conservatively** | Log content is redacted of PAN at emission under 04.07 §8, so no account data is expected. It is classified and inventoried anyway, because "expected" is what PAN-02 was |
| **Store merchant copies of receipts** | **No** | Receipts print **no more than the last four digits**, verified across every receipt variant at 96 lanes in 02.04 §6. A last-four receipt is not media with cardholder data. Recorded as a **determination**, not an omission, and re-tested annually |
| **Store paper — refund vouchers, price overrides, end-of-day** | **No** | Same basis; no account number is printed on any store paper artefact |
| **Signed sales drafts** | **Not produced** | Cardholder verification is on-device under EMV. Marketa's stores generate no signed paper draft bearing an account number |
| **Hard copy of a dispute document** | **Not produced** | Printing, saving and exporting are disabled in the CDE-06 session — 05.01 §7. The population is **zero by construction**, and 9.4.6 exists to keep it there |
| **Employee, customer and commercial paper generally** | No | Not in scope of 9.4; governed by the General Counsel's records policy |

The receipt determination is the one an assessor will test, and it is worth being explicit about why Marketa states it rather than staying silent. **482 stores generate a very large volume of paper every day.** If any of it carried an account number, 9.4.6 would become a 482-site destruction programme with containers, collections, certificates and a chain of custody. It does not, and the evidence for that is 3,840 test transactions across five acceptance modes with every receipt variant examined — not a policy saying receipts should be truncated.

## 2. The Six Controls

| ID | Control | Requirement anchor |
|---|---|---|
| **MED-1** | **Every media item in the population is registered, classified and located.** An item with no register entry is treated as lost | 9.4.1, 9.4.2, 9.4.5 |
| **MED-2** | **Media at rest lives in one of three named secure locations**, each inside a designated sensitive area or a provider vault under contract | 9.4.1, 9.4.1.1 |
| **MED-3** | **Nothing moves without prior management approval and a manifest.** The courier cannot collect without one | 9.4.3, 9.4.4 |
| **MED-4** | **Annual physical inventory and annual review of the storage location**, both with a named performer and a dated record | 9.4.1.2, 9.4.5.1 |
| **MED-5** | **Destruction is performed by a certified vendor, evidenced by a certificate listing serial numbers**, and reconciled against Marketa's own disposal manifest | 9.4.6, 9.4.7 |
| **MED-6** | **A drive that leaves an assessed component does not leave Marketa intact.** No failed media is returned to a vendor under warranty; it is destroyed and the non-return is contractual | 9.4.7 |

MED-6 is a policy choice stricter than the requirement and it is the cheapest strong control in this document. A failed drive is the one item in the estate that cannot be securely wiped — wiping requires the drive to work — and it is also the item vendors most want back. Marketa's supply contracts for storage, servers and appliances all include non-return of failed media, agreed at procurement rather than negotiated at the point of failure.

### 3.1 The three locations

| Location | Site | Contains | Physical control | Access |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The media and evidence store, SA-5** | Columbus HQ | Offline backup volumes; assessment evidence media | Designated sensitive area under 05.08 §2; badged, individually authorised, video-covered; media held in a fire-rated safe requiring a **second badge and a combination** | **6** individuals to the room; **3** hold the safe combination |
| **The Ashburn vault** | Ashburn co-location | The alternate copy of the offline volume set | Provider-operated vault under contract, inside the facility whose entry controls are attested under 05.08 §6.1; Marketa's items in a sealed, numbered case | Marketa's **2** escorting individuals plus provider vault staff, whose handling is logged by the provider |
| **The disposal cage** | Columbus data centre, inside SA-2 | Media awaiting collection by Halberd | Locked cage inside a designated sensitive area; **sealed, tamper-evident, numbered containers**; container seals recorded on the disposal manifest | **4** individuals |

Media awaiting destruction is the population most often left in a corridor, on a trolley or in an unlocked room "until the collection". Marketa's rule is that **media awaiting destruction is media**, subject to the same location, classification, inventory and access controls as media in use, from the moment it is removed from a component to the moment a certificate names it.

## 4. Classification — 9.4.2

9.4.2 obliges media with cardholder data to be classified in accordance with the sensitivity of the data. The purpose is that anybody handling an item can determine how sensitive it is **without opening it**, which means the classification has to be on the outside.

| Class | Meaning | Applied to | Physical marking | Handling |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **M-CD** | Contains, or is treated as containing, **cardholder data** | Offline backup volumes; decommissioned drives and flash from assessed components; decommissioned POI devices; the legacy hard-copy dispute file | Red label bearing the class, the register identifier and the owning system; the sealed transport case carries the same | Secure location only; movement requires 9.4.4 approval; destruction by MED-5 only; never leaves Marketa intact |
| **M-INSCOPE** | In-scope media with **no cardholder data expected** | SIEM archive media; configuration backups of connected-to components | Amber label with the register identifier | Secure location; movement requires approval; destroyed under MED-5 |
| **M-INTERNAL** | Everything else | General corporate media | Standard asset label | Corporate records policy; outside 9.4 |

Two conventions make the scheme work in practice. **Classification is applied to the container as well as the item**, so a sealed case of mixed media carries the highest class it holds. And **an item of unknown provenance is M-CD by default** — a drive found in a cupboard is treated as containing account data until a register entry says otherwise, and the resolution of the unknown is destruction rather than investigation.

## 5. Moving Media — 9.4.3 and 9.4.4

### 5.1 How media leaves a facility

| Control | Implementation |
|---|---|
| **Secured** | Items are sealed in numbered, tamper-evident transport cases. Contents are encrypted at rest in every case; the classification label is on the outside; the key never travels with the media |
| **Secured courier or accurately trackable method** | A single bonded courier under contract, with a scanned chain-of-custody manifest, per-leg tracking, and a **two-person handover at both ends** — one Marketa individual and one courier individual, each signing |
| **No general carrier, no post, no personal transport** | Stated as a prohibition. A media movement performed in an employee's car is not a trackable method however reliable the employee |
| Reconciliation on receipt | The receiving end reconciles the case seal number, the item count and every item identifier against the manifest before the courier leaves |
| Exception handling | A seal discrepancy, a count discrepancy or a late delivery beyond the manifest window is a security incident under 12.10.1, not a logistics query |

### 5.2 Management approval before movement — 9.4.4

| Control | Implementation |
|---|---|
| Who approves | **Trevor Kim** or a single named delegate for backup rotations; **Elena Marchetti** for anything containing dispute or settlement material; **Naomi Bhatt** for any movement outside the two standing routes |
| When | **Before** the item leaves the secure location. The approval is a precondition of the manifest, and the courier's collection order is generated from the approved manifest — **no manifest, no collection** |
| What is approved | The items by identifier, the classification, the origin, the destination, the route and the business reason |
| Evidence | The approved manifest, retained 12 months with the movement log |

| Route | Movements | Items | Approved in advance | Discrepancies |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Columbus SA-5 → Ashburn vault, offline backup rotation | **12** | 24 volumes | **12 of 12** | **0** |

One movement was late. The February rotation arrived at Ashburn seven hours beyond the manifest window because of weather. Marketa's counter-measure is the same one in each case — make the reconciliation a **completion criterion** of the activity rather than a task that follows it.

## 7. Hard-Copy Destruction — 9.4.6

### 7.1 The standing regime

| Control | Implementation |
|---|---|
| **Destroyed when no longer needed** | Retention for each hard-copy class is set in the 3.2.1 retention schedule with a named owner. There is no "keep indefinitely" class in the payment estate |
| **Method** | **Cross-cut shredding** to a particle size that prevents reconstruction, performed by Halberd at collection; incineration is available for bound or laminated material and was used once |
| **Secured containers until destruction** | Locked consoles at Columbus HQ and Austin with a slot that does not permit retrieval, emptied on a scheduled cycle by Halberd under chain of custody; the disposal cage in SA-2 for bulk material |
| **The population, however** | Is **zero by construction** for current operations. Printing is disabled in the CDE-06 dispute session; no signed paper draft is generated; store paper carries no more than the last four digits — §1 |

The consoles are retained despite the population being zero, and that is deliberate. **9.4.6 is a control against the paper that should not exist**, and an estate with no destruction capability at all offers a colleague with an accidental printout exactly two options, one of which is a general waste bin.

### 7.2 The PAN-02 paper

Finding **PAN-02** was a legacy chargeback dispute file share holding **2,187 scanned documents** containing PAN, cardholder name and expiry, remediated in Phase 02. The scans were the electronic instance. They had originals.

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| What was found | **611 physical dispute documents** in two archive boxes in the Columbus HQ records room — the originals from which part of the PAN-02 share had been scanned, retained after scanning because nobody's procedure said to destroy them |
| How it was found | Not by the discovery scan, which reads systems. It was found by **asking where the scans came from**, during the 12.10.7 response to PAN-02 in 02.03 |
| Retention basis | None. The dispute cases were closed between 2017 and 2021; the retention schedule required destruction at case closure plus 24 months |
| Interim securing | Boxes moved to SA-5, sealed, registered as M-CD, access restricted to three individuals, from 2026-02-19 |
| **Destruction** | Cross-cut shredded by **Halberd Data Destruction** under witnessed collection on **2026-03-19**, certificate **HDD-2026-0114**, itemised by box and document count |
| Witness | Owen Castellanos attended the collection; the shred was performed on site in the mobile unit rather than at a facility, at Marketa's request |
| Verified | Records room re-swept by Facilities and Payment Operations jointly; **0 further documents** found |

> **This is what 9.4.6 is for.** Not the tidy destruction of paper an entity knows about, but the paper generated by a process that ended years ago, retained by nobody's decision, in a room nobody thought of as holding account data. The scans were the finding. **The paper was the thing the finding was made of**, and it survived four years of an organisation believing it did not store card numbers.

### 7.3 Why the preventive control matters more than the destruction

| Preventive control | Effect |
|---|---|
| Printing, saving, exporting and screen capture disabled in every CDE-06 dispute session, verified by quarterly test rather than by configuration setting | The current dispute process cannot generate the artefact PAN-02 was made of |
| The 3.2.1 retention schedule names an owner and a disposal action **per repository**, including physical ones | The records room is now a repository with an owner |
| Quarterly discovery under 12.10.7 covers unstructured stores; the physical analogue is a **records-room review at each annual media inventory** | A physical sweep is the only discovery method that works on paper |
| Awareness content under 12.6.3 names the specific behaviour — do not print, do not photograph, do not take it home | Behaviour, not policy |

### 8.1 Halberd Data Destruction

| Attribute | Value |

### 8.2 The certificates, and the reconciliation

The certificate is the 9.4.7 evidence, and a certificate that says "one pallet of IT equipment" evidences nothing. Marketa's contract requires the certificate to list **every item by serial number**, and Marketa reconciles that list against its own disposal manifest, which was generated from the media register when the items entered the disposal cage.

| Collection | Date | Items | Certificate | Reconciliation result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | 2026-01-22 | 84 drives and SSDs | HDD-2026-0041 | **One discrepancy** — a drive on the manifest absent from the certificate |
| **2** | **2026-03-19** | **611 hard-copy documents** — the PAN-02 originals | **HDD-2026-0114** | Clean; witnessed by Owen Castellanos |
| **3** | 2026-04-16 | 96 decommissioned POI devices | HDD-2026-0159 | Clean |
| **4** | 2026-05-21 | 71 appliance flash modules | HDD-2026-0203 | Clean |
| **5** | 2026-06-18 | 31 backup volumes past retention | HDD-2026-0247 | **One discrepancy** — a serial transcribed incorrectly on the certificate |
| **6** | 2026-07-09 | 130 drives, SSDs and mixed media | HDD-2026-0288 | Clean |
| **Total** | | **412 items destroyed** ⁠— 214 drives and SSDs, 96 POI devices, 71 flash modules, 31 volumes; plus the 611 hard-copy documents | **6 certificates** | **2 discrepancies, both resolved** |

The two discrepancies are the reason the reconciliation exists.

**The missing drive** on collection 1 was found still in the disposal cage — it had been placed in a second container that was not sealed with the others and therefore not collected. It was destroyed on collection 3 under HDD-2026-0159 and the manifest was corrected. **A drive that a certificate does not name has not been destroyed**, whatever the pallet weight says, and the interval between the manifest asserting the item was gone and the reconciliation establishing it was not is precisely the window an entity without this control never sees.

**The transcription error** on collection 5 was a single character in a serial number. Halberd corrected and re-issued the certificate. Marketa's position, put to Halberd in writing, is that a corrected certificate is acceptable and an uncorrected one is not, because **the certificate is the only record that survives the item**.

### 8.4 POI device disposal

Ninety-six POI devices were decommissioned and destroyed in the period. They are handled under **PIM-09** as well as 9.4.7, because a decommissioned POI is simultaneously electronic media, a device holding key material, and — as 05.09 §3.2 observes about the spare pool — exactly the hardware a substitution attack would prefer to obtain.

| Step | Control |
|---|---|
| Removal from service | Recorded as a chain-of-custody event under **POI-2**; the register entry moves to "withdrawn" and the serial can no longer be activated |
| Transport to the disposal cage | Sealed numbered case, manifest, approved movement under 9.4.4 |
| Key material | Zeroised on withdrawal; Verition notified per the PIM |
| Destruction | Physical destruction by Halberd, **witnessed**, serialised on the certificate |
| Reconciliation | Certificate serials reconciled to the POI register, which is the same register that supports 9.5.1.1. **96 of 96 matched** |
| What is never done | A decommissioned POI is never sold, donated, returned to a general vendor pool or used for training |

**9.4.1 — In Place.** All 101 registered media items in one of three named secure locations, each inside a designated sensitive area or a contracted provider vault. **9.4.1.1 — In Place**, 34 offline volumes in a fire-rated safe under dual control in SA-5 with the alternate set in the Ashburn vault. **9.4.1.2 — In Place**, review performed 2026-06-30 with two findings, both corrected. **9.4.2 — In Place**, three classes, marked on the item and on the container, with unknown provenance defaulting to M-CD. **9.4.3 — In Place**, 18 movements, all by bonded courier under a scanned chain-of-custody manifest with two-person handover, zero discrepancies. **9.4.4 — In Place**, 18 of 18 approved in advance by a named manager, with the manifest as a precondition of collection. **9.4.5 — In Place**, a register of 101 items with location and movement history. **9.4.5.1 — In Place**, full physical inventory performed 2026-06-30, 100 of 101 located on the first pass, the exception located in four hours and its cause corrected. **9.4.6 — In Place**, with a current hard-copy population of zero by construction and the 611 legacy documents destroyed under certificate HDD-2026-0114. **9.4.7 — In Place**, 412 items destroyed across six certified collections, all M-CD collections witnessed, with two reconciliation discrepancies found and resolved.

| Claim | Position |
|---|---|
| That a small media population means a low residual risk | It means a **controllable** population. **PAN-02 was in this population and nobody knew**, and the 611 paper originals sat in a records room for four years while the organisation correctly believed it did not store card numbers |
