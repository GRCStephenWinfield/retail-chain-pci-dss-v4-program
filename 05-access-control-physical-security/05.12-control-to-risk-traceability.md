## Purpose

03.12 mapped all **44** baseline risks forward to the controls that would treat them and named the phase that would deliver each. 04.12 did the same job backwards for Phase 04. This document does it for Phase 05: it takes the controls **Requirements 7, 8 and 9 actually produced**, maps them to the register, states what each achieves, and states what it does not.

It answers four questions and refuses to blur them.

| Question | Where |
|---|---|
| Which of the 44 risks does Phase 05 touch, and with what? | §3 |
| Which risks does Phase 05 **reduce**, and which does it merely **hold**? | §4 and §5 |
| Does every control Phase 05 built treat a risk the register contains? | §6 |
| Does the expected close position still reconcile to **0 High · 13 Moderate · 31 Low**? | §7 |

And the same discipline Phase 04 stated is restated here rather than quietly abandoned, because Phase 05 has more temptation to abandon it than Phase 04 did.

> **Nothing in the register moves in this document.**
>
> The baseline in 03.11 is dated **2026-07-15**. So is this phase. Phase 05 delivered the largest single workstream in the programme — 8.4.2 across 145 identities and 20 access paths — and the most operationally demanding one — 9.5.1 across 1,914 terminals. Both are months old. **A control with no operating history does not lower a rating**, and the mirror of ADR-0005 applies to this phase exactly as it applied to the last.
>
> Two entries make the discipline uncomfortable rather than merely tidy. **R-17 stays High 16** while one of its treating requirements is **Not in Place**. **R-13 stays Moderate 12** on a control that has never met a season. Both are stated as such.

## 2. How to Read the Mapping

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Baseline** | The rating in 03.11 at 2026-07-15 |
| **Phase 05 contribution** | The specific mechanisms built in this phase — named controls, not requirement numbers |
| **Standing** | **Primary** where Phase 05 delivers the control that moves the risk · **Contributory** where Phase 05 strengthens a risk whose decisive control is elsewhere · **Accommodation** where Phase 05 makes an unmeetable position manageable without reducing the exposure |

Phase 04's version of this section observed that a control published today has only its own existence as evidence. Phase 05 has slightly more than that — **7,622 completed POI inspections, 214 credential rotations, two access review cycles, 9,640 MFA challenges a week, 1,281 visitor badges, 6 destruction certificates** — and it is still not enough, for three reasons that are specific to this phase.

**The operating evidence is short and the cycles are long.** A control assessed on a six-monthly cycle has run twice. A quarterly inspection regime has run four times, and one of those four ran under observation. Two data points and four data points are not records.

**The two hardest entries have external dependencies.** R-17 cannot move until a vendor ships release 9.4 and an ERP interface upgrade lands, both of which fall the far side of the freeze. R-13 cannot move until a season happens.

**The most informative results were failures.** Two of nine repair-personnel verification tests failed. Seventeen cabinet-open events had no work order. Eleven visitor records had no exit time. One backup volume was in the wrong place. Two of six destruction certificates needed correction. **A phase whose own controls produced five findings about themselves has not earned a rating reduction; it has earned the right to be believed about the sixth.**

## 6. Reverse Traceability — Controls That Treat No Risk

The forward map only tests the risks it started with. 03.12 §5 set the rule the programme applies to the answer, and 04.12 §6 applied it to Phase 04:

> **A control that treats no risk is either unnecessary, or the register is incomplete. "It is required by the standard" is not a third answer — a requirement exists because somebody identified a risk, and if Marketa's register cannot name it, Marketa has not understood the requirement.**

The reverse analysis was run across the Phase 05 control set in §1. **Seven** controls mapped to no register entry on the first pass.

### 6.1 The TPSP-06 finding, tested against ADR-0005, and why no risk was raised

05.08 §6.2 found that the Ashburn co-location provider — a party performing three of Marketa's Requirement 9 controls — was absent from the third-party service provider register. **ADR-0005** obliges the programme to raise a risk when discovery disproves an assumption, and the finding was tested against it deliberately rather than filed as a correction.

| Test | Answer |
|---|---|
| Was an assumption in the register disproved? | **No.** No entry asserts that the TPSP register is complete. **R-10** and **R-11** both exist precisely because provider assurance can fail |
| Was a new exposure discovered? | **No.** The provider's physical controls were operating throughout, were attested, and were corroborated by Marketa's own site inspection on 2026-04-22 |
| Was a control found to be ineffective? | **No.** A governance omission was found in the register that lists the parties over which assurance is obtained |
| Would a new entry describe something R-10 and R-11 do not? | **No.** R-10 is reliance on a provider whose basis has lapsed; R-11 is a responsibility gap. **This finding is an instance of both, not a third thing** |
| **Conclusion** | **No entry raised.** The finding is recorded as an instance under R-10 and R-11, the corrective action is **OI-05-02**, and the register remains at **44** |

That determination is stated at length because the opposite determination would have been easy and wrong. **ADR-0005 raises a risk when an assumption is disproved, not whenever something is found.** A register that grows by one entry every time a control produces a finding stops describing exposure and starts describing activity.

## 8. What This Traceability Does Not Prove

| Limitation | Consequence |
|---|---|
| A mapping is not evidence of operation | This document proves a Phase 05 control is **assigned** to a risk and describes what it produced in its first months. Phase 06 and Phase 08 prove it **ran** for a year |
| A prediction is not a result | The close position is a target published in July. **R-17's place in it depends on a vendor release and an ERP upgrade Marketa does not control** |
| Coverage is not sufficiency | Every risk Phase 05 touches has a named control. Whether the control is strong enough is a judgement the QSA, Ironwood and Rosa Delgado each test differently — and two of nine unannounced tests already said no |
| The register bounds the analysis | The reverse test found seven controls with no mapping and corrected five of them. **It cannot find a risk nobody thought of.** R-14 and R-31 are what it looks like when testing and discovery find one instead |
| A control with human operators is not evidenced by its design | 9.5.1.2 is executed by approximately 29,600 people. The design is in 05.09 §6; the evidence that it is performed as designed is 341 dwell-time flags, 1,204 photograph rejections and nine unannounced tests, and all three are first-year numbers |
| Phase 05 cannot close what Phase 06 owns | R-02's movement waits on operating history, R-05's on the segmentation and TPSP work, R-17's on a remediation date, R-13's on a season. **Phase 05 built everything it can and says so rather than claiming the residual** |
