## Purpose

05.01 defined the model. This document is the model instantiated: **the role catalogue**.

Twenty-four roles — **RC-01 to RC-24** — covering all **71 assessed system components**, held as **134 role assignments** by **118 distinct individuals** out of a workforce of about **34,000**. Each role carries a defined entitlement set, a named approver under **7.2.3**, a privileged or standard classification, and a review cycle.

It also discharges **7.2.6**, the requirement that governs who may query a repository of stored account data directly. That requirement is small, easily overlooked, and is the precise control that finding **PAN-02** did not have.

The catalogue is published in full rather than summarised. A role model that cannot be read is a role model that cannot be reviewed, and 05.03's entire mechanism depends on a reviewer being able to hold a role's definition in their head long enough to decide whether a person should hold it.

It is **not** a risk to the CDE, because no seasonal job classification makes any catalogue role available. That is a scoping outcome rather than an access control achievement, and it is stated here so that the risk is treated where it actually lives.

## 2. How the Catalogue Is Read

In both catalogue tables, **Components** names which of the 71 the role reaches — where a group is named, the role reaches every member of it. **Privilege** distinguishes a role that can alter configuration, grant access, execute code, read data at rest or change what another control does, from a **Standard** role that can only use a function the application already exposes. **Holders** are named individuals at 2026-07-15. **Approver** is the authorised person who must approve an assignment under **7.2.3** — never the requester and never the implementer, per ACP-5. **Data element** is the maximum representation of an account number the role may see, per 05.01 §6.

RC-08 is where ACP-7 is visible as a design decision rather than a slogan. Seven people administer the operating systems the CDE runs on. **None of them is authorised to read account data**, and the model does not treat that as an inconsistency to be resolved. A platform administrator can, with effort and by leaving evidence, reach data on a system they administer; the point of the separation is that doing so is an unauthorised act with a name attached rather than a routine use of a granted privilege. Detection of that act is Requirement 10's job, in Phase 06, and the two controls are designed as a pair.

RC-06 is the companion decision. Application support can reconfigure the dispute application; it cannot open a case and read its imagery, because case assignment is a separate authorisation that support staff do not hold.

Three rows carry a constraint stronger than the requirement compels, and each has a specific reason.

**RC-10 — dual authorisation on every action.** An MFA service whose administrative console can enrol a new factor for an existing user is a complete authentication bypass for anyone who reaches it. 02.08 §3 flagged CT-05 on exactly this basis. Three administrators, no unilateral action, every enrolment change carrying two identities.

**RC-15 — dual control and split knowledge.** Requirement 3.7 obliges it for key-custodian roles; the catalogue implements it as a property of the role rather than a procedure the role is expected to follow.

**RC-21 — six holders, countersigned by the compliance manager.** This is the direct residue of the Phase 04 finding. Tag-console publication accounts went from **19 to 6** under **ADR-0011**, and payment-template publication was removed from the tag console entirely. RC-21 is the six, and the countersignature exists because the population that can alter a payment page is the population that mattered most in that finding.

## 5. Approval — 7.2.3

7.2.3 requires that required privileges are approved by authorised personnel. Marketa reads "authorised personnel" as a named individual with the standing to refuse, and encodes that in the catalogue rather than in a workflow tool's configuration.

| Approval class | Roles | Approver | Second signature | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Full-PAN authorisation** | RC-02, RC-03 | **Elena Marchetti personally, in writing** | Naomi Bhatt notified; Rosa Delgado informed | Cannot be delegated to a queue, a team lead or an automated entitlement request |
| Executive | RC-04 | Naomi Bhatt | — | The VP cannot approve her own access |
| Infrastructure privileged | RC-08, RC-13, RC-17 to RC-19, RC-23, RC-24 | Trevor Kim or Marcus Hale | — | Platform ownership |
| **Concentrated privileged** | RC-06, RC-07, RC-09, RC-14, RC-16, RC-20, RC-21, RC-22 | Function owner | **Countersignature required** | Each of these can affect a control another requirement depends on |
| **Authentication and key material** | RC-10, RC-12, RC-15 | Naomi Bhatt | Dual authorisation at the point of action, not only at grant | Compromise of these roles defeats Requirement 8 or Requirement 3 wholesale |

Under **ACP-5**, the requester, the approver and the implementer are three different people. The catalogue enforces this structurally: an identity administrator holding **RC-09** provisions role membership but cannot approve it, and cannot grant themselves any role including their own. The one deliberate exception is emergency break-glass, which is covered in 05.04 §4 and 05.05 §9 and is a **documented exception under 8.5.1**, not a hole in ACP-5.

Approvals in the period: **147 role assignment requests**, of which **134 approved and remain active**, **9 approved and subsequently revoked at a review**, and **4 refused** — three because a nearby existing role already carried the entitlement, one because the requester wanted RC-07 to run a monthly report and the report was rebuilt as a programmatic extract instead. That last refusal is the model working: **the answer to "I need to query the database" is usually a report, and the requirement that forces you to ask is 7.2.6.**

## 6. Privileged Versus Standard

The catalogue's split between privileged and standard is not cosmetic. The two classes are provisioned, authenticated, brokered, logged and reviewed differently.

| Property | Standard access | Privileged access |
|---|---|---|
| Standing rights | The role's entitlements are held continuously during employment in the function | **Zero standing rights.** Membership of the privileged group is granted at check-out and removed at check-in |
| Duration | Continuous | Time-bounded per check-out; default **4 hours**, maximum 12, extension requires a fresh approval |
| Authentication | MFA on every path into the CDE — 8.4.2 | MFA on every path, **plus** re-authentication at check-out — 8.4.1 |
| Session record | Application-level audit | **Full session recording** through CT-11 |
| Review cycle | Six-monthly under 7.2.4 | Six-monthly under 7.2.4, **plus** monthly check-out pattern review by the role owner |
| Revocation on role change | Within one working day | **Immediate** — vault enrolment removed before the HR change is processed downstream |

Check-out activity in the measurement period, 2026-04-10 to 2026-07-15: **2,847 privileged check-outs** across 76 assignments, median duration **51 minutes** against a 4-hour default, **31 extensions** requested and 29 approved. **Nine check-outs carried no change or incident reference** and were raised with the role owner; all nine were legitimate diagnostic work performed without a ticket, and the corrective action was to make the reference field mandatory rather than expected.

## 8. Querying Repositories of Stored Account Data — 7.2.6

7.2.6 obliges that all user access to query repositories of stored account data is restricted to programmatic methods with role-based permissions, and that **only responsible administrators can directly access or query those repositories**. It is a short requirement with a sharp edge, and it is the requirement PAN-02 violated in spirit long before v4 wrote it down.

Marketa holds no full PAN at rest. It does hold **cardholder data** — truncated PAN and cardholder name — and 7.2.6 attaches to that.

| Repository | What it holds | Programmatic access | Direct query right | Controls on the direct right |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **CDE-02** — Payment Operations Database | Tokens, truncated PAN, cardholder name, approval codes, settlement state | CDE-01 only, through the application's role model. No user holds a database credential | **RC-07 — 3 individuals** | Check-out through CT-11 with a stated purpose; read-only by default; **write requires a change record**; every statement logged and shipped to CT-18; session recorded |
| **CDE-08** — Refunds Datastore | Tokens, last four, refund state | CDE-07 only | **RC-07 — 3 individuals** | As CDE-02 |
| **CT-35 / CT-36** — backup storage holding CDE datasets | Backup images of the above; the component that held the 33 copies in finding PAN-02 | None. Backups are not queryable in place | **RC-20 — 5 individuals**, restore only | A restore of CDE data requires a **second approval per event** from Elena Marchetti; restore targets are restricted to Z1 or Z2 |
| **CT-19** — SIEM log archive | Audit history; by design contains no PAN | RC-17, RC-24 read-only through the SIEM's own role model | RC-17 senior tier — 4 individuals | 12.10.7 procedure applies if PAN is ever found here; **none has been** |

The operational rule that makes 7.2.6 real rather than declarative: **there is no shared read-only reporting credential on CDE-02.** Every historical request for one — six in the build period — was met by building or extending a programmatic report on CDE-01. This is slower for the requester and it is the whole point of the requirement, because a reporting credential is a query right that has been granted to everyone who can find the password.

Ad-hoc direct queries executed in the period: **41**, all by RC-07 holders, all with a stated purpose, **38** in support of a change or incident and **3** in support of the QSA's evidence requests. Median session **14 minutes**. Zero queries returned an untruncated PAN, which is unsurprising, because none is stored.

## 9. Keeping the Catalogue Small Enough to Review

05.01 §8 set out the rules. This is what they produced.

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Roles at first draft, February 2026 | **41** |
| Roles after rationalisation, April 2026 | **24** |
| Proposals refused for having fewer than three plausible holders | **7** |
| Combined roles created | **0** — a person needing two functions receives two roles |
| Individuals holding two roles | **16** |
| Individuals holding three or more | **0** |
| Time for one reviewer to review one role definition | **11 minutes** measured in the second cycle — the number that determines whether 05.03 is a control or a ritual |

The last row is the justification for the whole approach. Twenty-four roles at eleven minutes is about four and a half hours of genuine reviewer attention per cycle, distributed across ten role owners. The 41-role draft would have been closer to eight hours each, and the several-hundred-role model that individual grants would have produced is not reviewable at all — which is how organisations arrive at a six-monthly review that is a signature on a spreadsheet nobody read.

**ADR-0013** records the trade explicitly: a coarser catalogue with a little unused entitlement, deliberately preferred over a finer catalogue that cannot be attested. The counter-evidence to watch for is a role whose holders use only part of it, and the monthly check-out pattern review in §6 is the mechanism that would surface it.

## 10. What This Catalogue Does Not Claim

| Claim | Position |
|---|---|
| That 24 roles is the correct granularity | It is the granularity that is reviewable. §9 and ADR-0013 record the trade and the evidence that would overturn it |
| That 118 people is the complete access population | It is the complete population holding a **catalogue role**. Twenty-seven external individuals hold provider access under 8.2.7 (05.04 §8) and application and system accounts are governed separately (05.07) |
| That 7.2.6 removes the risk of a database administrator reading data | Three people can query CDE-02 with approval and a recording. The control is attribution and constraint, not prevention — the same honest position as 05.01 §7 |
| That the catalogue is stable | It is four months old. The first rationalisation cycle falls due in April 2027 and no role has yet been retired |
